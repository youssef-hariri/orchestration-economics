## Phase 0: Market Intelligence Gathering (Days 1-14) — Expanded Research Design

**Do not proceed to Tier 1 until Phase 0 is complete. This phase gates all downstream decisions.**

### Consumer Behavior Research — Methodologically Rigorous

**Survey design** (150-250 respondents, stratified):

Instead of "100-200 respondents," use this structure:

- **Income stratification**: 
  - Segment 1: Bottom 40% (mass market, price-primary, convenience stores)
  - Segment 2: Middle 40% (growth cohort, willing to pay for quality/convenience, modern trade)
  - Segment 3: Top 20% (premium, willing to pay for experience/authenticity/health, specialty/online)
  - Interview 50-75 per segment (ensures you capture willingness-to-pay variation)
  - *Why*: Option A (authentic) works for Segments 2-3; Option C (social) works for Segment 2; Option B (health) works primarily for Segment 3. Mass market (Segment 1) may not be viable at premium price points.

- **Consumption occasion mapping**:
  - Morning commute (6-9am): portability, speed of consumption
  - Office/school break (10am-2pm): social acceptance, mess factor, shelf-stable storage
  - Post-workout: protein/energy recovery, indulgence vs. guilt
  - Evening social (5-11pm): sharing experience, packaging visibility, occasion-specificity
  - Weekend family time: bulk purchase, variety, entertainment value
  - *Action*: Ask "which of these occasions do you snack most?" and "what do you buy for each?" — this determines product format and distribution strategy.

- **Flavor & ingredient deep-dive**:
  - Spice tolerance scale (mild → extreme heat); regional variation
  - Sweet vs. savory preference
  - Acceptance of fermented/umami flavors (varies dramatically by culture)
  - Allergen & dietary restrictions: gluten-free, vegan, dairy-free, religious restrictions (halal, kosher, Hindu vegetarian)
  - *Critical*: Ask "would you buy this IF it were [locally-sourced / organic / high-protein / low-sugar]?" to test positioning relevance per segment.

- **Current competitive usage**:
  - "What snacks do you buy now?" (brand, product, price paid, where purchased)
  - "Why did you choose that brand?" (taste, habit, convenience, price, trust, family tradition)
  - "What snack do you wish existed?" (open-ended; often reveals unmet needs)
  - "What would make you switch brands?" (this is your differentiation target)

- **Sampling methodology**: 
  - Urban (metro) vs. suburban vs. small town (if market spans multiple geographies)
  - Age cohorts: 18-25, 25-35, 35-50, 50+ (snacking behavior differs; younger = trial-friendly; older = habit-loyal)
  - Gender (snacking occasions sometimes gender-specific)
  - *Example for India*: If launching in Mumbai + Tier-2 cities, interview 80 Mumbai respondents (metro) + 70 Tier-2 (different retail/consumption patterns).

**Retail channel audit** (structured, not exploratory):

Instead of "visit 15-20 retail points," use this framework:

- **Channel breakdown** (visit 5-7 per channel):
  - Modern trade (supermarket/hypermarket): shelf space availability, planogram flexibility, margin expectations (20-30%), velocity benchmarks for snack category
  - Traditional independent retailers (kirana, mom-and-pop): margin expectations (25-40%), willingness to stock new brands (often higher than modern trade, but volume lower)
  - E-commerce (if applicable): platform availability (Amazon, Flipkart, DTC), minimum order values, category commissions (15-25%), fulfillment logistics
  - Specialty retailers (health shops, ethnic markets, convenience chains): margin, customer profile, exclusivity potential
  - *Why this matters*: If you choose Option A (authentic local), independent retailers are allies; if Option C (social/shareable), modern trade is critical. Your retail audit must confirm that your chosen option's channels are viable.

- **Competitive shelf audit** (for each channel):
  - **Top 3 competing snacks** (by shelf space, brand visibility, price):
    - Price per 40g equivalent
    - Packaging format (individual vs. multi-pack)
    - Packaging messaging (e.g., "made with real [ingredient]," "no artificial colors," "authentic taste")
    - Flavor variants (how many SKUs per brand)
    - In-stock rate (is the product consistently available?)
  - **Market gaps**: If all competitors are sweet, savory may be underserved; if all are mass-market brands, premium/authentic positioning may be available
  - *Action item*: Document this in a competitive matrix so you can claim a specific niche, not generic "authenticity"

- **Retailer interviews** (3-5 per channel type):
  - "How many new snack brands do you trial per year?" (acceptance rate)
  - "What makes a snack succeed on your shelf?" (velocity, margin, turnover)
  - "What's the biggest barrier to stocking a new brand?" (minimum order, payment terms, consumer pull, space constraints)
  - *Critical question*: "Would you give shelf space to a new [local/health/social-format] snack if it had [customer demand / sampling support / promotional budget]?" — This tells you if retail partnership is achievable.

### Culinary & Ingredient Research

**Local food expert interviews** (5-8 experts per market):

- **Identify** (not brainstorm, but document):
  - Iconic flavor combinations specific to the region (e.g., if India: chole-bhature spices, South Indian sambar flavors, street chaat masala)
  - Seasonal ingredients (monsoon vs. summer varieties; availability and cost variation)
  - Festive/occasion-based foods (Lunar New Year, Diwali, Ramadan, etc.) that could inform limited editions
  - Taboo ingredients (e.g., beef in Hindu-majority India, pork in Muslim markets, nuts in certain allergen-conscious markets)

- **Supply chain feasibility**:
  - Which ingredients are locally-produced vs. imported? Cost variance?
  - Which have reliable suppliers? (This is critical; supply disruption kills a brand faster than bad taste)
  - Which require certification (organic, halal, fair-trade) and what's the cost premium?
  - *Example*: If Option A requires locally-sourced chili, confirm sourcing cost ($2-4/kg) vs. imported ($1.50/kg) — this impacts margin.

### OPTION A: Authentic Local Flavor Hero
*Best if: Consumer research shows nostalgia/authenticity gap; retailers willing to stock local-positioned brands; local ingredient sourcing is cost-viable*

**Differentiation Lock (Address Commoditization Risk)**

Before designing, confirm your differentiation is NOT just "authentic taste" (hard to verify, commoditizable):

- **Angle 1: Ingredient Story + Sustainability**
  - *Not*: "Authentic chaat masala taste" (vs. Lay's, PepsiCo local brands)

- **Angle 2: Unique Format/Consumption Experience**
  - *Not*: "Chips with local spice blend"

  - *Not*: "Available year-round"
  - *Instead*: "Made with [seasonal ingredient]; limited edition [season]" (scarcity + occasion tie-in)
  - Requires: Seasonal ingredient availability; commitment to limited production runs
  - Cost: Lower per-unit margin (smaller batches), but higher perceived value

**Choose ONE differentiation angle; do not try all three.**

**Three-Product Architecture**

**Product 1: Signature Savory Snack** (Hero product)
- **Base**: Corn or rice (sourced locally if cost-neutral; otherwise commodity sourcing is fine — story is the differentiator, not ingredient cost)
- **Flavor**: One iconic local combination identified in Phase 0 culinary research
  - *Example for India (South)*: Sambar spice blend (cumin, coriander, fenugreek, chili, asafetida)
  - *Example for Southeast Asia*: Shrimp-garlic-lime (common in Thai/Vietnamese street snacks)

- **Validation**: >50% of segment 2/3 should express interest in the variant in Phase 0 (not a hypothetical 80%; these people have committed purchase intent)
- **Format**: Same pack sizes as Product 1 (individual + family)
- **Purpose**: Repeat-purchase trigger (customer tries hero, variant gives reason to return; limits commoditization)

**Product 3: Occasion-Specific Limited Edition** (not a permanent SKU)
- **Tie-in**: Festival, season, or daypart identified in Phase 0

- **Differentiation**: Packaging design + limited quantity messaging ("only [month X]")
- **Retail strategy**: Does NOT count as a separate SKU for retailer negotiation; positioned as "limited-time promotional format"

**Why this works**:
- Defensible positioning (authentic + story + occasion-tie-in is harder to copy than flavor alone)
- Margin profile supports premium pricing for segment 2/3
- Retail complexity is low (3 formats, not 10 flavor variants)
- Repeat purchase mechanism is clear (Product 2 variant + seasonal re-engagement)

### Pre-Launch Testing (Days 46-60)

**Sampling strategy — Bias-controlled**

Instead of "distribute 500-1000 at transit hubs," use this sampling approach:

- **Sample size**: 800-1,200 units (larger to hit repeat-purchase targets)
- **Distribution locations** (match your target segment + consumption occasion):
  - *If targeting Segment 2*: Office parks, co-working spaces, gyms, community centers, modern trade (supermarkets)
  - *If targeting Segment 3*: Premium gyms, specialty retail stores, e-commerce platforms, coffee shops
  - *If targeting all segments*: Mix urban metros (office parks, malls) + tier-2 cities (community centers, independent retail hubs)
  - *Do NOT oversample* (e.g., if mass market is 60% of your target, don't sample only at office parks where you'll find only affluent professionals)

- **Sample pack design**:
  - Include: Individual product sample (40-50g), branded brochure (why the product exists), QR code for feedback survey
  - No coupon attached (this biases toward price-conscious triers, not true trial intent)
  - Instead: Offer discount on first purchase IF survey completed (creates data capture without distortion)

- **Feedback loop** (critical; most plans skip this):
  - Post-sample QR code or SMS link with **5-minute survey**:
    - "Would you buy this product?" (Yes/No)
    - "At what price would you consider it?" (price sensitivity)
    - "Where would you prefer to buy it?" (retail channel preference)
    - "Which aspect did you like most?" (flavor, packaging, convenience, story)
    - Open-ended: "What would make you buy this regularly?"
  - Target response rate: 25-30% (800 samples → 200-240 responses, statistically useful)
  - *Analysis*: If <15% say "Yes, would buy," Option choice was wrong; pivot or exit. If 25%+ say yes, proceed.

**Retailer partnership negotiation** (structured approach)

Instead of "secure 10-15 retail doors," use this three-stage negotiation:

**Stage 1: Retailer Identification & Approach (Days 46-55)**
- **Modern trade first** (supermarkets, chain convenience stores):
  - Identify 10-12 target stores (mix of store sizes: flagship, standard, neighborhood)
  - Identify category manager (not store manager; category manager controls shelf)
  - Approach with: "We've completed consumer research showing [X%] of shoppers want [specific value prop]. We're seeking 2-week trial placement with sampling support."
  - *Frame as low-risk*: "Limited time trial; we provide sampler staff; you keep 35% margin; if velocity is strong, we move to permanent listing"

- **Independent retail second** (if Option A is chosen):
  - Identify 15-20 independent kirana stores, local grocers, specialty shops

### 2.2 Strategic Inventory & Buffer Stock Strategy

**Objective**: Maintain adequate buffer inventory for critical items without overcommitting capital.

#### Framework: Risk-Based Inventory Allocation

**Step 1: Classify inventory needs by risk tier**

**Tier A: Strategic Buffer Stock** (for high-risk, high-criticality suppliers)
- Suppliers in high-risk geographies (Brazil, India, Vietnam) OR low on-time delivery history
- Components with long lead times (>45 days)
- Components with high substitution complexity (custom specifications)
- **Stock target**: 60-90 days of supply (higher cost, but reduces disruption risk)
- *Rationale*: If supplier goes down, you have 2-3 months to find alternative

**Example**: Vietnam fasteners supplier (25% of volume, 45-day lead time, single-source)
- Annual consumption: 1,000 units
- Safety stock: 150-180 units (60-90 days) = ~$15K-20K inventory value
- Cost of capital (holding cost): ~8-10% annually = $1.2K-2K/year
- Cost of disruption (if supplier fails): Production halt = $100K+ loss per week
- **Decision**: Justified; hold 75-day buffer

**Tier B: Operational Buffer Stock** (for moderate-risk suppliers)
- Suppliers in medium-risk geographies OR 85-95% on-time delivery history
- Components with moderate lead times (20-45 days)
- **Stock target**: 30-45 days of supply
- *Rationale*: Normal safety stock for forecast error + moderate supply risk

**Tier C: Minimal Buffer Stock** (for low-risk, reliable suppliers)
- Suppliers in low-risk geographies AND >95% on-time delivery history
- Components with short lead times (<20 days)
- **Stock target**: 15-20 days of supply
- *Rationale*: Frequent orders; supplier stability = lower risk

**Step 2: Calculate optimal buffer stock levels**

Use this formula for each component:

**Safety Stock = Z × σ_d × √L**

Where:
- Z = Service level factor (e.g., 1.65 for 95% service level; 2.33 for 99%)
- σ_d = Standard deviation of daily demand (from historical sales data)
- L = Lead time in days

**Example**:
- Component: Vietnam fasteners; annual demand = 1,000 units = ~3 units/day
- Historical demand variation (σ_d): ±0.5 units/day (some days 2, some 4)
- Lead time: 45 days
- Service level target: 95% (acceptable 5% risk of stockout) → Z = 1.65

**Safety Stock = 1.65 × 0.5 × √45 = 1.65 × 0.5 × 6.7 = ~5.5 units**

Wait—this seems low. Why? Because this formula assumes supplier is reliable (predictable lead time). For high-risk suppliers, add **lead time variability buffer**:

**Adjusted Safety Stock = Basic formula + Lead time variability buffer**
- If Vietnam supplier's lead time varies 45-60 days (±15 days variability):
- Lead time buffer = 15 days × 3 units/day = 45 units
- **Total Safety Stock = 5.5 + 45 = ~50 units** (handles both demand and supply variability)

**Step 3: Inventory investment prioritization**

Rank by inventory cost × disruption risk:

| Component | Supplier | Annual Demand | Unit Cost | Annual Consumption $$ | Buffer Days | Buffer Stock $ | Disruption Risk Cost | ROI of Buffer Stock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fasteners | Vietnam | 1,000 | $15 | $15K | 75 | $1,875 | $100K/week | Excellent |
| Minerals | India | 500 units | $40 | $20K | 60 | $3,300 | $80K/week | Excellent |
| Precision parts | Germany | 200 | $100 | $20K | 20 | $1,100 | $40K/week | Good |
| **Total** | | | | | | **$6,275** | | **Justified** |

**Decision rule**: If (Disruption Risk Cost × Lead Time / 365) > Buffer Stock holding cost, buffer is justified.

- Fasteners: ($100K × 45/365) = $12.3K/year disruption cost >> $1,200/year holding cost = **Justified**
- Minerals: ($80K × 60/365) = $13.2K/year disruption cost >> $3,300/year holding cost = **Justified**
- Precision parts: ($40K × 30/365) = $3.3K/year disruption cost ≈ $1,100/year holding cost = **Justified** (but lower priority)

**Step 4: Storage & management**

**Inventory rotation**:
- Use FIFO (first-in, first-out) to prevent obsolescence
- For items with short shelf life (raw materials): Monitor expiration; rotate with production schedule

**Location strategy**:
- Store strategic buffer stock at different locations (reduce single-site risk):
  - 50% at your primary manufacturing facility
  - 50% at a contract warehouse near key supplier (closer to source; faster replenishment)
  - OR: Split between two geographic regions (if multi-site operations)

**Cost management**:
- Negotiate consignment inventory terms (supplier holds stock; you pay when used)
- Vendor-managed inventory (supplier manages buffer on your site; reduces your cash outlay)

#### Step 2: Multi-Modal Transportation Strategy

**Tier 2: Standard Components** (Regular-volume, 45-day lead time acceptable)

**Tier 3: Bulk, Non-Urgent Materials** (Commodity inputs, long lead time acceptable)
- **Primary**: Ocean freight (cheapest)
- **Alternative routes**: Willing to use alternate ports (LA → Oakland → Houston) to avoid congestion
- **Negotiation**: Flexible port designation (don't specify single port; let forwarder choose uncongested option)

**Strategic negotiations**:

**With freight forwarders/3PLs**:
- Establish master service agreement naming 2-3 backup carriers for each route

- Request visibility into port congestion/labor issues (early warning system)

**With ocean carriers**:
- Negotiate space allocation agreement (guaranteed container slots during peak seasons)
- Build relationship with carrier operations (so you're deprioritized less during disruptions)

**With trucking/rail providers**:
- Establish agreement for surge capacity (20% above normal volume) at agreed premium price

| Customs clearance | Within 2 days of arrival | Check documentation completeness; contact broker |
