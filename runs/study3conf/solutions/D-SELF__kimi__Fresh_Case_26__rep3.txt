**GLOBAL CULTURAL SEASONAL CAMPAIGN FRAMEWORK**  
*Authentic Seasons: Governance, Representation, and Cross-Border Risk Management for Faith-Based Marketing*  
**2026 Deployment Edition**

---

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This framework governs the brand’s 2026 seasonal calendar across Ramadan (GCC, North Africa, Southeast Asia, and global diaspora), Lunar New Year (Greater China, Vietnam, Korea, Mongolia, and global diaspora), and Diwali (South Asia, Indo-Caribbean, Indo-Fijian, and global diaspora). It replaces fragmented, agency-led execution with a **“Glocal” Governance Architecture**: centrally orchestrated non-negotiables, locally authored creative expression, and real-time cross-border scrutiny.

The objective is to move from *seasonal advertising* to **relational cultural accountability**. The brand enters sacred seasons as an invited guest, bound by legally enforceable operational rails, hardened regulatory shields, and deep ethnographic fidelity. Key innovations for 2026 include: a **Five-Gate Pre-Launch Protocol** with native-language creative supremacy; an **Agency Consequence Ladder** codified in all statements of work; **Satellite Nodes** to protect small markets from creative orphaning; a **Cross-Platform Digital Drift Protocol** to manage screenshot collapse across borders; and a **Post-Campaign Restitution Mechanism** that transforms errors into accountable repair.

## 1. CORE PRINCIPLES FOR AUTHENTIC REPRESENTATION  
*(Legally Binding in All Agency SOWs)*

All campaigns, regardless of market or agency, must adhere to the following principles. They are codified in every creative brief and agency contract.

| Principle | Operational Definition | Food-Category Implication |
|-----------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| **1.1 Sacred vs. Secular Distinction** | Spiritual practice is the protagonist; the product is the enabler. Fasting is not universal—children, the elderly, pregnant and menstruating women, travelers, and the ill are exempt. Do not equate piety with fasting, nor film exemption contexts as narrative drama. Ancestral rites are sacred obligations, not festive backdrops. | Ramadan: Product placement occurs in iftar/suhoor context, never interrupting prayer or ablution. Diwali: Food is *prasad* or religious offering, never "party catering." Lunar New Year: *Charye* and *cúng* are altar rituals; do not stage them as rustic table-setting. |
| **1.2 Hyper-Local Specificity** | Intra-regional variation equals cross-regional variation. There is no single “Muslim world,” “Asian,” or “South Asian” visual language. Dialect, dress, and utensils must be ethnically specific, not nationally generic. | Mandi for Najd; *bazin* for Libya; kolak for Java; *bánh chưng* (North Vietnam) vs. *bánh tét* (South); *murukku* for Tamil communities; *adhirasam* for Karnataka; *tteokguk* for Korea. |
| **1.3 Community Voice & Economic Justice** | Co-creators retain IP attribution and negotiated revenue share (5–15% of net production budget). They are not “talent for rent.” Community members must never serve as visual “proof” of brand virtue. | No parachute celebrity chefs into community kitchens. Feature Surabaya *pesantren* cooks, Chennai *halwais*, or Fez *dada* with editorial equity and billing. |
| **1.4 Symbolic Integrity & Theological Precision** | Iconography is never decorative. The crescent-and-star is an Ottoman/political symbol rejected by many Salafi, Ibadi, and African/Asian communities. The *diya* is sacred; do not animate it into a product shape. | No adhan, Quranic verses, names of Allah or the Prophet, Gayatri Mantra, or Buddhist *dharani* as sonic branding. No animation of deities. No Kaaba or Kotel imagery. No “Orientalist” typefaces or dragon-reductionism. |
| **1.5 Transparency, Intent & Labor Dignity** | Commercial campaigns must not disguise themselves as philanthropy. Publish audited donation metrics. Acknowledge invisible migrant, feminized, and lower-caste labor without exploitative “savior” framing. | Avoid “Ramadan charity-washing.” Gulf iftars must not erase the South Asian, Southeast Asian, or African cooks who prepared the food. Indian *mithai* campaigns must not erase *halwai* laborers. |
| **1.6 Zero-Assumption & Intersectional Nuance** | Markets contain divergent ethnoreligious minorities, sects, and caste/class strata. Never default to upper-caste, North Indian, fair-skinned, Han Chinese, or Sunni Arab aesthetics. | Diwali: Treat Dalit-Bahujan, Tamil, Bengali (Kali Puja), Jain, and Sikh (Bandi Chhor Divas) observances as parallel, never subsidiary. Ramadan: Account for Javanese, Minangkabau, Sundanese, Acehnese, Afro-Arab, and Amazigh distinctions. |
| **1.7 Sectarian, Interfaith & Caste Neutrality** | Do not privilege one Islamic *madhhab* or sect, one Hindu tradition, or one Christian denomination over another. Avoid imagery that implies sectarian or caste supremacy. Acknowledge multi-religious dynamics where authentic, but never fabricate harmony for brand optics. | In the Gulf, avoid erasing Shia, Ibadi, and Afro-Arab Muslim communities. In India, do not depict Diwali as exclusively Hindu where Sikh, Jain, secular, and Muslim neighbors coexist. |
| **1.8 Tiered Intersectional Safeguards** | Disability, LGBTQ+, and non-traditional family structures must be reflected in diaspora markets (UK, US, Canada, Australia) where community partners consent. In MENA and South Asian domestic markets, do not force representation that endangers local partners. | Diaspora Lunar New Year may feature queer family structures; domestic Gulf Ramadan must not out local participants. Cast disabled practitioners in diaspora Diwali content with dignity, never as inspirational props. |

### Anti-Stereotype Operational Mandates
The following tropes are **prohibited** in all 2026 campaigns:
- **The “Oppressed vs. Liberated” Frame:** Never juxtapose a “conservative” local practice against a “modern” product as implicit rescue.
- **The “Magical Grandmother”:** Elder women are not vessels of timeless wisdom for brand extraction; they are co-authors with named expertise.
- **The “Multicultural Rainbow”:** Do not stage forced interfaith iftars or Diwali parties unless they reflect documented, organic community relationships.
- **The “Exotic Laborer”:** Migrant cooks, domestic workers, or *halwais* may be acknowledged with dignity and credit, but never as colorful backdrop without narrative agency.
- **The “National Costume” Reduction:** Do not dress non-Chinese Asian talent in *qipao*-derived styling for Lunar New Year; do not put all South Asian women in *lehengas* for Diwali.

## 2. REGIONAL CUSTOMIZATION GUIDELINES  
*(The “Glocal” Creative Playbook)*

### A. Ramadan

| Region | Cultural Spine | Creative Guardrails | Food & Ritual Specifics |
|--------|----------------|---------------------|-------------------------|
| **Gulf (KSA, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain)** | *Hospitality as Sacred Duty (Diwaniya/Majlis) & Zakat* | **Internal variation is mandatory.** Najd (Riyadh) ≠ Hijaz (Jeddah/Mecca) ≠ Eastern Province. UAE/Bahrain cosmopolitan registers differ from Oman’s maritime/Ibadi identity. Modesty norms vary by family/emirate; consult local gender advisors. **Sectarian note:** Bahrain and Eastern Province have significant Shia populations; avoid state-Sunni framing as “normative.” Oman’s Ibadi tradition is distinct; do not impose generic Sunni tropes. **Audio:** No percussion during adhan. In Salafi-influenced markets, instrumental music may be rejected entirely; use ambient sound with theological clearance. | Mandi, harees, qatayef, jareesh. **Zakat and Sadaqah are theological pillars distinct from hospitality; do not conflate brand iftars with Islamic charitable obligation.** Women host large, visible women-only iftars; erasing women from public Ramadan imagery is a prohibited stereotype. Acknowledge South Asian, Filipino, and African kitchen staff through credit lines or narrative presence, not as extras. |
| **North Africa (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Egypt, Libya)** | *Night Worship (Qiyam/Tarawih), Amazigh Heritage & Urban Communal Memory* | **Do not frame North Africa as “liberal” contrast to a “conservative” Gulf.** Ramadan nights are spiritually charged (*Tarawih*). **Amazigh dimension is mandatory for Morocco, Algeria, and Libya:** Tamazight language, *tafaska* traditions, Atlas/Rif/Kabyle/Nafusa distinctions. Egypt’s *mosalsalat* and *fawazeer* create unique serialized storytelling expectations. **Libya-specific:** Distinguish Tripolitanian, Cyrenaican, and Fezzan customs; *bazin* and *asida* are distinct from general Maghrebi staples. | Harira and *chebakia* (Morocco), *chorba frik* (Algeria), *brik* (Tunisia), kunafa (Egypt), *asida* and *bazin* (Libya). The *mesaharaty* is primarily Egyptian/Levantine; do not paste it across Morocco or Algeria. Darija dialects dominate domestic spheres, not Modern Standard Arabic. |
| **Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Malaysia, Brunei, Singapore, Southern Thailand, Mindanao)** | *Gotong-Royong & Iftar as Pluralist Practice* | Align with MUI (Indonesia), JAKIM (Malaysia), and Brunei’s MIB, recognizing these bodies hold different orientations. **Anti-stereotype:** The “multicultural iftar” is authentic to Singapore and urban Malaysia only if it avoids state-harmony propaganda tropes. **Indonesia:** Distinguish Javanese *santri/abangan/priyayi* streams, Minangkabau matriliny, Aceh’s Shafi’i-Sharia context, and Bugis/Makassar identities. **Southern Thailand (Pattani):** Observe Malay-Muslim minority context; use Pattani Malay sensitivity. **Mindanao:** Observe Bangsamoro identity (Maranao, Maguindanao, Tausug); do not import Indonesian or Malaysian templates uncritically. | Kolak, bubur suro, *buka puasa* at *pasar Ramadan* (Indonesia), *nasi kerabu* (Malaysia), *tiyula itum* (Tausug). Emphasize *ngabuburit*. Distinguish Javanese *santri* traditions from Minangkabau or Acehnese practices. |
| **Diaspora (UK, US, Canada, Australia, France, Caribbean)** | *Identity Affirmation Under Scrutiny* | Diaspora audiences face heavier cross-border scrutiny. **British Bangladeshi Sylheti traditions differ from British Pakistani Punjabi or Arab norms.** Afro-Caribbean Muslims and West African Muslims in France/UK have distinct Ramadan textures. Avoid Arab-centrism when targeting South Asian or African diasporas. | Feature working-class and feminist iftar collectives, not only nuclear families. Acknowledge mosque kitchens and volunteer labor as central to diaspora Ramadan. |

Zodiac may differ (Cat vs. Rabbit). *Lì xì* presentation differs from Chinese *hóngbāo*. ## 3. MULTI-AGENCY COORDINATION PROTOCOLS

### 3.1 Governance Structure

| Body | Composition | Authority |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **Global Cultural Council (GCC)** | Global CMO, Global Comms Director, Chief Diversity Officer, Regional GMs, Resident Cultural Practitioners and Scholars from target communities (minimum 51% non-Western heritage). | Veto power over any campaign. Sets Sacred/Secular boundary. Mandates IP/revenue-share terms. |
| **Regional Cultural Nodes** | Local agency lead + Brand Marketing Director + Local Religious/Cultural Advisor with lived community credibility (vetted per §3.2). | Creative ownership and localization. Approves local executions. Escalates cross-border assets. |
| **Cross-Market Sensitivity Cell** | Diaspora strategists and community reviewers with lived experience from non-target markets (e.g., Malaysian Tamil reviews Indian Diwali; British Bangladeshi reviews Gulf Ramadan). | Identifies unintended cross-border offense before launch. |
| **Satellite Nodes** | For Libya, Brunei, Mindanao, Southern Thailand, and Indo-Caribbean markets: a Tier-1 Regional Node agency must subcontract a vetted local boutique, embedding them in fortnightly huddles with **veto rights on cultural accuracy**. | Prevents small-market orphaning. Satellite leads report directly to the GCC for cultural matters, bypassing Tier-1 filtration. |

### 3.2 Vetting, Credentialing & Independence Standards

All Cultural Advisory Board (CAB) members, community co-creators, and “lived experience” reviewers must meet the following standards:

| Criterion | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Independence** | No current employment by the executing agency or brand. No political party or lobbying affiliation in the target market. |
| **Community Standing** | Recognized practitioner, historian, or organizer with 5+ years of documented community work; or published scholar in relevant ethnography/theology. Must be fluent in the target market’s dominant dialect and script. |
| **Compensation** | Paid at senior consultant day rates with Global South parity. Travel and per diem covered. Charged to **global brand overhead**, not regional P&L. |
| **Term Limits** | 18-month terms to prevent capture; 6-month cooling-off before re-engagement. |
| **Conflict Arbitration** | If a CAB member vetoes creative on theological or cultural grounds, the Regional Node escalates to the GCC within 24 hours. The GCC convenes a binding mediation panel (CAB rep + agency rep + independent GCC scholar). The panel’s decision is final. |
| **KOL Extremism Vetting** | Before Gate 5, all community influencers and digital leaders must pass a third-party background screen for fundamentalist, sectarian, hate-aligned, or fraud affiliations. Results shared with GCC only. |

### 3.3 The “One Brief, Many Authors” System

1. **Global Narrative Spine (75-day lead):** The GCC issues a single-page “Seasonal Belief Statement” defining emotional territory and theological red lines. It does not contain scripts or visuals. **For Ramadan, this must be reviewed by a *madhhab*-aware fiqh advisor** (Maliki for North Africa; Shafi’i for Southeast Asia; Hanbali influence in the Gulf; Ja’fari sensitivity for Shia markets; Ibadi for Oman).
2. **Local Creative Manifesto (60-day lead):** Each agency submits a 12-slide manifesto: (a) historical ritual root, (b) food semiotics and ethnic specificity, (c) competitive landscape, (d) risk map including intra-regional minorities and sects, (e) labor visibility plan, (f) anti-stereotype compliance checklist.
3. **Shared Asset Library & “Forbidden Zones”:** A centralized DAM (e.g., Bynder or Frame.io) with mandatory metadata fields: (i) approved market vector, (ii) dialect/script tag, (iii) sectarian flag, (iv) diaspora leakage risk (Low/Medium/High), (v) original-language creative rationale, (vi) native-language audio transcript. **Absolute Red Lines:** No adhan in commercial contexts. No Quran verses, names of Allah, or Prophet Muhammad in brand contexts. No Gayatri Mantra or Buddhist *dharani* as sonic branding. No animation of deities. No Kaaba imagery. No pigs or alcohol in Ramadan markets. No beef in any Diwali market. No sacred thread (*janeu*) as generic Hindu symbolism.
4. **Cultural Shadowing (Mandatory):** Before filming, at least two creative team members per campaign must attend an actual ritual (iftar, *charye*, *puja*) in the target community with no recording devices, documented by CAB attestation.

### 3.4 Cross-Agency Review Cycles

- **Fortnightly Global Huddle:** All agency leads present work-in-progress. The rule is *“Show your neighbor’s market first.”* Presentations must include native-language creative samples with live translation; English-only presentation of non-English creative is prohibited.
- **Asset Tagging:** Every asset carries its approved market vector and metadata. No asset may be lifted for another market without GCC re-approval.

### 3.5 Agency Consequence Ladder
Codified in every agency SOW:
- **First Gate Failure:** Mandatory re-brief and cultural retraining (charged to agency).
- **Second Failure:** 25% fee holdback and GCC oversight of all creative for 12 months.
- **Red-Line Violation (post-launch):** Immediate termination for cause, clawback of production fees, and blacklisting from subsequent seasonal pitches.

### 3.6 Native-Language Creative Supremacy
The Grandmother Test, CAB review, and Diaspora Red Team stress tests must be conducted in the **creative’s original dialect and script**. English translations may be provided for observer comprehension, but cultural accuracy is judged solely on native-language reception.

## 4. PRE-LAUNCH APPROVAL SAFEGUARDS  
*(The “Five-Gate” Protocol)*

No campaign may proceed to paid media without sequential passage. Gates 1–3 run in parallel (Days T-60 to T-45); Gates 4–5 are sequential (Days T-30 to T-3).

### Gate 1: Cultural Advisory Board (CAB) Sign-Off
- **Who:** Minimum 3 independently vetted CAB members per market.
- **Test:** The **“Grandmother Test”** — Show the rough cut to 2 elder practitioners from distinct ethnic/sectarian backgrounds **in the original dialect/script** without brand context. If both agree it feels “made by us,” it passes. If either feels it is “made about us” or “made to sell to us,” it fails.
- **Intersectional Review:** Verify that diaspora assets comply with §1.8 and that domestic-market assets do not endanger local participants.
- **Deliverable:** Signed **Cultural Accuracy Certificate (CAC)** with dissenting opinions annexed if applicable.

### Gate 2: Regulatory, Religious, Sectarian & Labor Compliance
- **Who:** Local legal counsel + national halal/religious certification bodies + sectarian advisor where relevant.
- **Regulatory Passport:** Every campaign must obtain a country-specific memo listing: (a) applicable media law statute, (b) relevant religious authority or fatwa body, (c) required pre-clearance certificate, and (d) criminal liability exposure rating (**High:** KSA, Pakistan, Indonesia, India; **Medium:** UAE, Malaysia; **Low:** Singapore, UK, US).
- **Fatwa Risk Register:** In KSA, Indonesia, Malaysia, and Pakistan, retain a local mufti or fiqh board liaison (separate from the creative CAB) who issues a documented **“No Objection”** letter addressing sectarian neutrality.
- **Caste Representation Audit (India only):** Before Diwali execution, commission a Dalit-Bahujan-led organization to certify: (a) no savarna-default casting, (b) labor visibility includes subaltern workers, and (c) no caste-supremacist symbolism (e.g., *janeu* presented as generic Hindu practice).
- **China Religious Decoding Protocol:** All Lunar New Year assets must be reviewed by a PRC-licensed cultural consultant to reframe ancestral rites as “family reunion heritage” and scrub visuals that trigger CAC religious filters.
- **UU ITE Blasphemy Risk Register (Indonesia):** Verify no content risks criminal liability under Indonesia’s Electronic Information and Transactions Law for sectarian or religious offense.
- **Compliance Clearance Memo:** Deliverable includes sectarian neutrality attestation and labor visibility confirmation.

### Gate 3: Diaspora Red Team & Cross-Border Stress Test
- **Who:** Cross-Market Sensitivity Cell + diaspora focus groups in UK, US, Canada, and Australia (minimum 8 participants per group, segmented by ethnicity/sect).
- **Test:** 
  - Will a Malaysian student in London find the Indonesian ad appropriative?
  - Will a Pakistani-American find the Gulf ad too Arab-centric?
  - Will an Indo-Caribbean Canadian find the Indian ad erasing their tradition?
- **Deliverable:** **Cross-Border Risk Assessment (CBRA)** with leakage-risk rating and mitigation script.

### Gate 4: Global Executive Review
- **Who:** GCC. 48-hour review window.
- **Test:** Does this violate any of the 8 Core Principles? Does it risk shareholder value through global backlash? Does the labor visibility plan meet minimum standards?
- **Deliverable:** **Executive Approval Stamp** with conditional riders if applicable.

### Gate 5: Community Preview & Pre-Briefing
- **Who:** KOLs, religious community digital leaders, and food influencers from target and high-risk diaspora markets.
- **Test:** 72 hours before public launch, brief trusted voices with cultural background notes and approved talking points so they can defend the campaign if misinterpreted.
- **Deliverable:** **Influencer/Leader Pre-Alignment Log** with signed NDAs and briefing acknowledgments.
