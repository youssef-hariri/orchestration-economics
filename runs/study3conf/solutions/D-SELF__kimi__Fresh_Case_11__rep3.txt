**FINANCIAL-OPERATIONS STRATEGY: CROSS-BORDER REMITTANCE CORRIDORS (2026–2028)**  
*Mid-Size Payments Firm | EU–Gulf–Southeast Asia–Latin America*

---

## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The global remittance market has bifurcated into high-velocity, open-API corridors and fragmented, capital-controlled jurisdictions. A mid-size firm can no longer afford a “license-first, everywhere” model. This strategy replaces geographic breadth with **regulatory authenticity and capital concentration**.

**The Board is asked to approve a hub-and-spoke operating model that concentrates 65% of incremental capital and 75% of engineering bandwidth on two value-accretive corridors—Gulf→Philippines and EU→Brazil (B2B only)—while converting all other markets to zero-balance-sheet technology-export or SaaS arrangements.**

| Strategic Pillar | Board Decision |
|---|---|
| **Core Corridors** | Own licenses and direct settlement in UAE→Philippines and EU→Brazil B2B only. |
| **Conditional Markets** | capped, partner-dependent presence in Indonesia, Mexico, and KSA with strict volume/fee gates. |
| **SaaS / Zero-Capital** | Vietnam repositioned to fixed-fee software licensing; zero FX or settlement exposure. |
| **Exit** | Complete exit from Argentina (no referral, no partnership). Intra-LatAm netting suspended. |
| **Compliance** | Deploy a $4.5–6.0m Compliance Orchestration Layer (COL) with parallel legacy stacks. |
| **Treasury** | Two primary nodes (Amsterdam, Dubai); Singapore downgraded to liaison status. No cross-region netting. |

**Financial Targets (Risk-Adjusted, 2026–2028)**
- **Gulf→Philippines:** 2.0–2.8% net margin; 22–28% ROE.
- **EU→Brazil (B2B):** 0.5–1.0% net margin; 10–14% ROE.
- **Capital-light markets:** Fee-based or capped at 8–12% ROE.
- **Portfolio Rule:** No new capital commitment to any corridor projecting <8% risk-adjusted ROE.

---

## II. STRATEGIC NORTH STAR

1. **License Authenticity:** Every entity either holds a direct, defensible regulatory authorization for the activity it performs, or operates as a non-regulated software vendor with strict contractual firewalls. No regulatory fiction.
2. **Capital Concentration:** Direct ownership of settlement risk and FX float is limited to corridors where unit margins justify fixed-cost drag.
3. **Balance-Sheet Purity:** Zero overnight exposure to non-convertible or trapped currencies (IDR, VND, ARS). Zero intraday gap risk from cross-time-zone netting.
4. **Kill Criteria:** Explicit volume and margin gates suspend further investment if thresholds are missed.

### Tier 3: SaaS, Harvest, or Exit (Zero Incremental Capital)

## V. COMPLIANCE & DATA-GOVERNANCE FABRIC

### A. Compliance Orchestration Layer (COL)
- **Budget:** $4.5–6.0m over 18 months (build + maintain).
- **Design:** Single orchestration backbone with jurisdiction-specific rule modules.
- **Reality Check:** eIDAS 2.0 wallet penetration will be <45% in 2026. Budget for **parallel legacy KYC stacks** (video-ID + bank-based AIS fallback) for 18–24 months.

### B. KYC & Identity by Corridor

| Market | 2026 Regulatory Requirement | Technical Response | Data-Governance Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EU** | PSD3/PSR + AMLR risk-based QDD; eIDAS 2.0 wallets | Parallel stack: Wallet API + AIS fallback + legacy video-ID | GDPR Schrems II: DTIA, encryption, SCCs for all non-adequate transfers |
| **UAE** | UAE Pass / passport + visa; biometric >USD 3,000 | Government API hooks; in-region liveness detection | PII processed in UAE; no biometric export without CBUAE justification |
| **KSA (via Bank)** | Absher validation; SAMA XML >SAR 50k | Partner manages validation and filing; we provide data formatting only | Zero Saudi PII on foreign servers; in-Kingdom data residency mandatory |
| **Brazil** | CPF (Receita Federal); tiered Pix KYC | Real-time CPF; auto-escalation at BRL 1,000 / 10,000 | BCB localization: 5-year in-country log retention |
| **Mexico** | CURP + RFC via RENAPO/SAT | Batch + real-time validation | Deferred unless IFPE pursued |
| **Philippines** | PhilSys ID preferred; biometric >PHP 50,000 | PhilSys API; biometric hashes stored locally | BSP data-residency compliance |
| **Indonesia** | NIK via Dukcapil; in-country data only | In-country micro-service; zero PII cross-border | Localized cloud/DR; $1.5–2.5m fixed cost |

### C. Capital-Control & Settlement Engine
- **Brazil:** Auto-route Pix >BRL 5,000 to BRL float; reject if liquidity insufficient.
- **Vietnam:** Engine tracks partner-settled T+1 status but **does not automate same-day FX**; SBV batch processes prevent STP.
- **Indonesia:** Engine blocks IDR accumulation; triggers partner-managed settlement via independent FX sourcing.
- **KSA:** If direct license ever obtained (post-gate), engine auto-files SAMA XML >SAR 50k. Until then, partner bears filing obligation.

### D. Sanctions & AML
- **Single vendor backbone** (Dow Jones/LSEG) with regional name-variant dictionaries (Arabic patronymics, Tagalog/Latin maternal-paternal surnames).
- **Dual screening:** At origin (EU/Gulf) and pre-settlement (local hub).
- **Corridor-specific typologies:** Gulf→SEA (structuring across beneficiaries); EU→LatAm (crypto off-ramp round-tripping). Localized ML model tuning included in COL budget.

## VI. TREASURY, SETTLEMENT & FX STRATEGY

### A. Treasury Hub Design
Eliminate the Singapore liquidity node. Consolidate to **two primary 24-hour nodes**:

1. **Amsterdam (EUR Hub):** EUR liquidity, ECB reserve management, multi-currency Nostro (USD, GBP, CHF). Covers EU origination and EU→LatAm payables.
2. **Dubai (USD/AED Hub):** USD working capital for Gulf origination. AED peg eliminates FX hedging cost. Covers Asian morning payouts via USD Nostro with ASEAN partner banks.

**Singapore:** Reduced to partner liaison and audit-trail backup. No primary liquidity function.

### B. Netting Protocol: Bilateral, Same-Region Only
**Cross-region multi-lateral netting is suspended.** Time-zone gaps between Asian payables and European/LatAm receivables create gap risk requiring costly intraday liquidity lines (150–600 bps), which erase theoretical savings.

- **Amsterdam:** Net EU→Brazil against EU→Mexico receivables within the EUR book.
- **Dubai:** Net Gulf→PH payables against Gulf→ID partner-swap receivables within the USD book.
- No cross-region netting until real-time 24-hour settlement rails exist.

### C. Local-Currency Settlement & Pre-Funding

| Market | Requirement | Operational Model | Revised Economics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brazil** | BRL mandatory for Pix >BRL 5,000 | Onshore float; 48-hour ML-driven pre-funding | Net float yield: **20–40 bps** (after IOF, local tax, BRL depreciation) |
| **Philippines** | PHP via InstaPay/PESONet | PHP Nostro; 1.5x ADV buffer | Core corridor; prioritize STP automation |
| **Mexico** | MXN via SPEI | **Deferred.** If IFPE obtained, MXN pool with intra-day overdraft | Conditional on Brazil B2B gate |
| **Indonesia** | IDR via BI-FAST | **Partner-managed.** Partner independently sources USD/IDR and settles IDR | Zero IDR on our balance sheet; fee: 35–50 bps |
| **Vietnam** | VND mandatory inbound | **Partner-managed.** SaaS model: zero VND touch | Zero overnight VND risk |

### D. FX Risk Management
- **Pegged (AED/USD):** No hedge.
- **Liquid (EUR/USD, USD/PHP):** 24-hour rolling forwards.
- **Restricted (USD/IDR, USD/VND):** Instant conversion by partner; zero overnight open position.
- **BRL (EU→Brazil):** Partial hedge via B2B receivables matching; net float yield treated as minor additive, not strategic pillar.

- **KSA Evaluation:** If Gulf-originated volume via TSP exceeds $75m/month for 6 months, present business case for direct SAMA PSP license to Board. **Until then, remain TSP only.**

### Phase 3: Strategic Optionality (Months 18–36)
- Evaluate acquisition of Mexican IFPE or Colombian PI **only if** standalone licensed volumes in LatAm justify the regulatory capital.
- Reassess intra-LatAm netting if Brazil and Mexico each sustain >$50m/month with bilateral regulatory approval.
