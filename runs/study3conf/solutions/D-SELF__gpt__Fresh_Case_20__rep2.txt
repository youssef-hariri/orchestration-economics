# 2026 Global Monetization and Product-Governance Strategy

## Executive summary

The publisher should operate one global game and product family with a shared gameplay, content, account, social, and competitive foundation, supported by a configurable regional monetization and compliance layer.

The commercial model should transition from dependence on paid randomness to a diversified, deterministic foundation:

- Direct-purchase cosmetics and bundles;
- Known-content battle passes;
- Noncompetitive subscriptions;
- Story expansions and fixed-value content;
- Earnable and purchasable premium currency;
- Fixed-value social gifting;
- Choice packs, crafting, and collection milestones;
- Paid randomized rewards only for approved adults in approved markets.

The central principle is **functional parity, not identical stores**:

> Players should have comparable access to gameplay, events, social participation, competitive viability, and cosmetic quality, even where the lawful method of acquiring rewards differs.

Paid randomized rewards must not be required for core progression, story access, social participation, ranked access, or competitive viability. Belgium and the Netherlands should use deterministic monetization by default. China should be treated as a separately governed operating and data environment where required. Gulf markets should be reviewed country by country.

This model may reduce short-term revenue from some collectors and high-spending players. It is intended to improve durable, risk-adjusted profitability by broadening payer participation, reducing refunds and disputes, lowering regulatory and advocacy exposure, improving revenue predictability, and reducing dependence on vulnerable high-spending cohorts.

All jurisdictional conclusions in this document are operating assumptions or internal standards, not legal opinions. Local counsel must validate the exact mechanic, reward, payment flow, age model, data arrangement, advertising, platform treatment, and operating structure before launch or material change.

---

## 1. Global principles

The following are binding company standards:

1. **Core viability:** The core game, meaningful events, social participation, and competitive play must be viable without purchasing randomized rewards.
2. **Minor protection:** Paid randomized rewards are unavailable to minors globally. If adult status is unknown or disputed, the deterministic configuration applies.
3. **Competitive integrity:** Paid products must not provide exclusive or disproportionate competitive advantages.
4. **Deterministic foundation:** Direct purchases, known-content passes, subscriptions, expansions, and earnable progression are the primary economic foundation.
5. **Conservative defaults:** Paid randomness is disabled by default in Belgium and the Netherlands pending written approval of the precise design.
6. **Transparency:** Where randomness exists, the company applies a common disclosure standard at least as strong as the applicable local requirement.
7. **No circumvention:** Gifting, trading, account migration, payment flows, and cross-platform entitlements must not bypass legal, age, pricing, or regional restrictions.
8. **Protective analytics:** Spending-risk signals may trigger limits, cooling-off, support, or self-exclusion, never more aggressive monetization.
9. **Auditability:** Offers, probabilities, approvals, advertisements, entitlements, and eligibility decisions must be versioned and recoverable.
10. **Evidence-based localization:** Cultural adaptations require local research and review, not assumptions based solely on country labels.
11. **Reversibility:** Every high-risk feature must have a tested deterministic fallback and remote suspension capability.
12. **Revenue quality:** Product decisions must consider retention, payer diversification, refunds, support, compliance, reputational exposure, and player health—not bookings alone.

## 2. One global game with regional configuration

### 2.1 Universal game elements

The following should remain common wherever legally possible:

- Core gameplay and progression rules;
- Character, item, and content identities;
- Story and seasonal event structure;
- Earnable progression;
- Account identity;
- Friends, guilds, and social systems;
- Matchmaking and competitive rules;
- Accessibility and safety features;
- Brand standards;
- Analytics definitions.

Regional monetization differences must not create inferior game quality, delayed access to meaningful gameplay, or competitive disadvantage.

### 2.2 Jurisdiction-specific configuration

The following may vary by country, age status, platform, payment context, or operating entity:

- Reward acquisition method;
- Price and currency;
- Payment methods and tax treatment;
- Probability disclosures;
- Gifting and transfer rights;
- Age eligibility;
- Event reward route;
- Advertising language and presentation;
- Refund flow;
- Content availability required by law;
- Local customer support and moderation.

A shared event may therefore use the same story, quests, characters, and social activities globally while delivering fixed bundles in one market and an approved randomized offer in another.

### 2.3 Three-layer operating model

**Global service layer:** gameplay, content identity, account systems, social features, competitive systems, and common safety standards.

**Regional policy layer:** offer eligibility, pricing, disclosures, gifting, age controls, refunds, and local monetization rules.

**Local operating enclaves:** separate publishing, data, infrastructure, moderation, payment, or support environments where required, particularly in China.

### 2.4 Complexity budget

Each regional exception must be justified as:

- Legally required;
- Required by a platform or payment provider;
- Necessary for player protection;
- Commercially material;
- Supported by local cultural research; or
- Temporary pending legal clarification.

Quarterly governance should review the number of exceptions, offer schemas, client builds, local environments, support workflows, and associated cost. Unnecessary variants should be retired.

## 3. Functional parity standard

### Mandatory parity

The following must not vary adversely by region except where law requires it:

- Core gameplay access;
- Competitive viability;
- Ranked and matchmaking rules;
- Story participation;
- Social and guild participation;
- Safety tools;
- Quality and functionality of regional substitutes.

### Target parity

The publisher should seek comparable:

- Cosmetic quality;
- Event participation;
- Expected reward value;
- Time-to-acquisition;
- Collection opportunity;
- Social visibility;
- Customer support;
- Refund access.

Parity assessment must consider more than expected value. Variance, prestige, completion difficulty, exclusivity, and perceived status also matter.

### Permitted variation

Regional differences may affect:

- Random versus deterministic acquisition;
- Price and currency;
- Payment method;
- Disclosure format;
- Gifting and transfer;
- Age eligibility;
- Event reward route;
- Local advertising;
- Content restrictions required by law.

### Prohibited divergence

Do not create:

- Inferior-quality substitutes;
- Region-based competitive disadvantages;
- Delayed access to meaningful gameplay;
- Stigmatizing regional presentations;
- Cross-border mechanisms that enable circumvention.

Maintain a regional parity matrix covering gameplay, social participation, competitive viability, cosmetic quality, event access, time-to-acquisition, price-adjusted value, gifting, and refunds.

## 4. Jurisdiction and age controls

### 4.1 Jurisdiction of service

Adopt a written hierarchy, subject to local legal validation, using:

1. Mandatory operating entity or local-service jurisdiction;
2. Verified residence where legally relevant;
3. Account and storefront country;
4. Payment jurisdiction;
5. Physical location where legally necessary;
6. Device locale only for language and presentation.

IP address and VPN signals may support fraud or circumvention detection but should not alone determine legal eligibility.

The policy must address:

- Travel and relocation;
- Expatriates;
- Family accounts and shared devices;
- Foreign payment cards;
- VPN use;
- Cross-platform accounts;
- Country changes;
- Conflicting signals;
- Existing entitlements after relocation.

Restrictions must be defined separately for the purchaser, recipient, account, transaction, item, transfer, and competitive use. When classification is uncertain, use the more protective deterministic configuration and provide a transparent support and appeal process.

### 4.2 Age determination

If adult status cannot be established through an approved, privacy-proportionate method, the player receives the deterministic catalog and cannot purchase paid randomness.

Do not collect government identification globally by default. Depending on local requirements, age assurance may use:

- Declared age;
- Platform parental controls;
- Parent or guardian consent;
- Local verification methods;
- Account-level protective controls.

Behavioral signals may trigger protective review but must not be used to make opaque child classifications or to increase monetization pressure.

The age policy must define:

- Approved signals by market;
- Shared-family-account procedures;
- Parent verification;
- Treatment of minors using adult accounts;
- Correction and appeal rights;
- Data retention and deletion;
- Account recovery;
- Maximum dispute-resolution time.

## 5. Monetization portfolio

### 5.1 Direct cosmetics

Make direct cosmetics the principal scalable product:

- Skins;
- Emotes;
- Animations;
- Housing;
- Profile effects;
- Banners;
- Titles;
- Cosmetic loadouts;
- Social personalization.

Each offer must show contents, price, ownership status, duration, rerun policy, and applicable restrictions. Cosmetics must not impair visibility, hit recognition, audio cues, or other competitive information.

A direct cosmetic may still be high risk if it uses deceptive scarcity, targets minors, creates social pressure, or affects competitive readability.

### 5.2 Battle passes

Use free and paid tracks with known contents.

Requirements:

- Full reward track visible before purchase;
- Reasonable completion requirements;
- No essential character, weapon, story, or ranked access only in the paid track;
- Clear expiration and rollover rules;
- No mandatory renewal;
- Transparent earn-back currency;
- No excessive daily-play pressure;
- Regional affordability testing.

### 5.3 Subscriptions

Offer fixed, noncompetitive benefits such as:

- Cosmetic allowances;
- Housing and profile customization;
- Storage or loadout capacity;
- Convenience features;
- Monthly known-value items.

Clearly disclose renewal, price, cancellation, entitlement changes, and refund terms.

### 5.4 Expansions and fixed-value bundles

Use story chapters, cosmetic collections, housing packs, and themed bundles.

Each bundle must identify:

- Every included item;
- Permanence or expiry;
- Alternative acquisition routes;
- Rerun policy;
- Price and taxes;
- Refund terms;
- Platform and regional restrictions.

### 5.5 Premium currency

Use as few denominations as practical. Display:

- Local-currency price;
- Currency amount;
- Approximate real-money value;
- Taxes or fees where required;
- Remaining balance where useful.

Avoid packages that routinely leave players with large unusable balances.

### 5.6 Paid randomized rewards

Where legally approved, paid randomness must be:

- Available only to adults;
- Nonessential to progression, story, social participation, and competition;
- Nontransferable unless separately approved;
- Fully disclosed before purchase;
- Server-randomized;
- Covered by duplicate conversion, exchange, or guarantee rules;
- Independently tested;
- Remotely suspendable;
- Subject to a sunset and renewal review.

Paid randomness should remain a declining minority of net revenue. It may be enabled only where it is legally cleared, player-safe, operationally auditable, and commercially justified after compliance, support, refund, and reputational costs.

### 5.7 Free and earned randomness

Free or earned random rewards are not automatically outside the risk framework.

Global policy:

- The exact mechanic must be approved in the country requirements register;
- Deterministic or selectable rewards are the default in Belgium, the Netherlands, China, and other uncertain jurisdictions;
- Earned currency cannot bypass paid-randomness restrictions;
- Free rewards cannot use misleading near-miss or escalation design;
- Youth, social-pressure, advertising, and behavioral-risk reviews still apply;
- A transition from free or earned randomness to paid randomness requires separate approval and disclosure.

## 6. Regional adaptations

### Belgium

Default to:

- No paid randomized rewards;
- No indirect purchase through premium currency;
- No related trading, cash-out, or marketplace functionality;
- Direct-purchase cosmetics;
- Fixed-content bundles;
- Selectable reward tokens;
- Deterministic event tracks.

Counsel must review payment, chance, transferability, economic value, reward type, and advertising presentation for any proposed random mechanic. Free or earned randomness must not be presumed safe without review.

### Netherlands

Use the same conservative default:

- Disable paid randomized rewards pending written advice on the exact mechanic;
- Avoid tradable or externally monetizable randomized items;
- Restrict cross-border gifting and transfer;
- Offer direct-purchase and fixed-selection substitutes.

Public communications should not state that every loot box is universally prohibited. The analysis depends on the specific mechanic and reward characteristics.

### Korea

Where permitted, provide localized, pre-purchase disclosure of:

- Applicable reward pools;
- Item-level or category-level probabilities as required;
- Tier and rarity rates;
- Pity and guarantee rules;
- Duplicate treatment;
- Rate changes after draws;
- Event dates and reset conditions;
- Maximum-cost or exchange information where applicable.

The displayed table, live configuration, approval record, and offer metadata must share one immutable version identifier. Rate changes require controlled publication and auditable change history.

### China

Operate through the approved local structure and separately review:

- Publishing and operating permissions;
- Content and event approvals;
- Randomized-item disclosures;
- Real-name, minor, playtime, and spending controls;
- Virtual-currency and payment requirements;
- Advertising and livestream rules;
- Data localization and cross-border transfers;
- Local moderation and support.

Use deterministic alternatives where interpretation or approval is uncertain. China’s financial model must include local publishing, operating, infrastructure, moderation, support, data, and compliance costs.

### Japan

Use a mixed collection model:

- Transparent adult randomized rewards where approved;
- Direct-purchase bundles;
- Clear rates, ceilings, pity, exchange, duplicate, and reset rules;
- Accurate “limited” and “exclusive” claims;
- Published rerun policies.

Adopt an internal prohibition on completion-based mechanics in which separately randomized items unlock an additional prize unless counsel specifically approves the design. This is a company risk policy, not a universal legal conclusion.

### Gulf markets

Review Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, and Oman separately.

Default to:

- Direct cosmetics;
- Known-value bundles;
- Housing and personalization;
- Fixed-value subscriptions;
- Fixed-content social gifts;
- Deterministic event rewards.

Avoid gambling-coded language and presentation by default, including “jackpot,” “bet,” “casino,” and “spin.” Document separately whether each restriction is legal, platform-based, culturally motivated, or reputational.

Review clothing, symbols, music, imagery, event timing, Arabic localization, right-to-left UX, gifting, public status displays, and family controls.

### Brazil

Use:

- Local-currency prices;
- Small entry tiers;
- Familiar payment methods;
- Affordable passes;
- Earnable premium currency;
- Direct cosmetics;
- Portuguese disclosures and support;
- Strong minor and parental controls.

Test affordability, payment success, refunds, chargebacks, support, taxes, and contribution margin rather than copying US or Japanese price points.

### United States

Use a mixed model with conservative standards:

- Direct products and passes as primary offers;
- Global probability disclosure for randomized products;
- Honest scarcity and countdowns;
- Clear subscriptions and cancellation;
- No misleading “free” claims;
- Disclosed influencer and affiliate relationships;
- State-sensitive privacy, youth, gambling, consumer-protection, advertising, and platform review.

## 7. Competitive integrity

The publisher should not market the game as “non-pay-to-win” unless defined internal tests are met.

Competitive characters, weapons, abilities, upgrades, and materials must be:

- Earnable through reasonable play;
- Available through a predictable route if sold;
- Not exclusive to randomized purchases;
- Available without excessive seasonal delay;
- Subject to equivalent ranked and matchmaking rules.

Before launch, establish measurable thresholds for:

- Maximum nonpayer time to competitive viability;
- Maximum progression-speed difference between nonpayers and payers;
- Maximum ranked-access delay;
- Maximum early-access period;
- Acceptable performance differences after controlling for skill, mode, playtime, and account age;
- Cosmetic readability.

Progression skips, convenience products, subscriptions, early access, extra attempts, and social features must be reviewed for indirect competitive impact.

## 8. Disclosure and probability assurance

Where randomness exists, disclose before payment:

- Complete reward pool or applicable categories;
- Item-level probabilities where required;
- Tier and rarity rates;
- Whether rates change after each draw;
- Pity, guarantee, and exchange rules;
- Duplicate handling;
- Featured-item conditions;
- Event phases and pool changes;
- Expiry and reset rules;
- Local-currency price and taxes;
- Effective date and offer version.

Disclosure must be:

- In-game;
- Localized;
- Mobile-readable;
- Available before purchase;
- Consistent with advertising and store listings;
- Supported by an accessible history of material changes.

Controls must include:

- Server-side randomization;
- Versioned probability tables;
- Automated probability-total checks;
- Immutable deployment logs;
- Independent statistical testing;
- Displayed-versus-executed-rate reconciliation;
- Automated launch gating;
- Immediate kill switches.

A material mismatch suspends the offer, stops further purchases, preserves evidence, identifies affected players, and triggers refund or remediation review. Disclosure is necessary but does not cure excessive pressure, unaffordable pricing, pay-to-win effects, or harmful design.

---

## 9. Gifting, transfers, and social monetization

Gifting should default to fixed-content, nontransferable cosmetics.

Controls must include:

- Recipient eligibility checks;
- Sender confirmation;
- Daily and monthly limits;
- Fraud and chargeback monitoring;
- Cancellation or dispute rules;
- Minor restrictions;
- Cross-border restrictions;
- No public minor-spending or gifting rankings.

Do not gift by default:

- Randomized rewards;
- Premium currency;
- Competitive power;
- Cash-like or tradable items;
- Externally redeemable items;
- Products that could bypass regional restrictions or prices.

Prohibit:

- Guild spending quotas;
- Influencer pressure campaigns;
- Spending or gifting leaderboards;
- Events implying that spending proves friendship, loyalty, status, or belonging;
- Public exposure of minors’ spending or gifting activity.

## 10. Pricing, payments, refunds, and player protection

### Pricing

Use a regional value framework based on:

- Purchasing power;
- Local price conventions;
- Taxes;
- Store and payment fees;
- Fraud losses;
- Exchange-rate volatility;
- Competitive benchmarks;
- Refund obligations;
- Expected lifetime value.

Do not use inferred vulnerability to personalize prices, urgency, scarcity, or spending limits.

### Payments and refunds

Provide:

- Local currencies;
- Relevant payment methods;
- Purchase authentication;
- Receipts and purchase history;
- Clear recurring-payment terms;
- Country- and platform-specific refund flows;
- Fraud controls that distinguish travel from systematic circumvention.

Maintain a refund matrix covering:

- Digital-content withdrawal rights;
- Immediate delivery and consent;
- Minor and unauthorized purchases;
- Technical errors;
- Duplicate charges;
- Chargebacks;
- Restoration or removal of refunded items.

Do not use a universal “no refunds after opening” policy.

### Player protection

Implement globally:

- Age-appropriate onboarding;
- Parent and guardian controls;
- Spending caps and alerts;
- Cooling-off prompts;
- Self-exclusion or monetization lock;
- Purchase history;
- Easy support access;
- No paid randomness for minors;
- No high-pressure notifications to minors;
- No deceptive near-miss presentations;
- Easy subscription cancellation.

Use protective intervention bands:

1. Informational reminder;
2. Cooling-off prompt;
3. Temporary purchase hold;
4. Spending limit or monetization lock;
5. Human support escalation.

Thresholds must be validated, explainable, privacy-reviewed, audited for disparate impact, and used only for protection. Players must have correction and appeal routes.

## 11. Product, legal, cultural, and data governance

### 11.1 Monetization Review Board

The board may block, suspend, or require redesign of any offer. It includes:

- Product;
- Legal and regulatory affairs;
- Finance and tax;
- Economy design;
- Trust and safety;
- Child safety;
- Privacy and data protection;
- Security and fraud;
- Player support;
- Localization and cultural review;
- Communications;
- Regional operations.

### 11.2 Accountability

Assign:

- Global accountable executive;
- Global product owner;
- Global legal owner;
- Named regional legal owners;
- Policy-engine and entitlement owner;
- Trust and player-protection owner;
- Privacy owner;
- Audit owner;
- Regional operating owners.

### 11.3 Decision rights

- A legal objection blocks launch where a required legal condition is unresolved;
- A child-safety or privacy objection blocks launch where required controls are absent;
- Commercial objectives cannot override non-negotiable safety or compliance standards;
- A regional legal owner may block operation in that jurisdiction;
- The accountable executive resolves remaining commercial or design disputes;
- Any override requires written rationale, documented risk acceptance, and board notification.

### 11.4 Risk classification

Risk is based on impact, not product label.

Assess:

- Randomness;
- Transferability;
- Competitive impact;
- Minor exposure;
- Social pressure;
- Personalized targeting;
- Price magnitude;
- Scarcity;
- Regulatory uncertainty;
- Potential player harm.

Tier 1 products may use a standard checklist. Tier 2 requires legal, economy, privacy, trust, and cultural review. Tier 3—including paid randomness, tradable items, prize-like events, competitive power, minor-facing monetization, cross-border gifting, and personalized urgency—requires written legal advice, independent assurance, executive approval, rollback testing, and a sunset date.

### 11.5 Legal requirements register

For every jurisdiction and material mechanic, record:

- Requirement or issue;
- Classification: legal, platform, internal standard, risk default, cultural recommendation, or open issue;
- Source or counsel opinion;
- Confidence level;
- Effective date;
- Product implication;
- Technical owner;
- Legal owner;
- Re-review trigger;
- Default treatment if unresolved.

## 12. Financial transition and legacy migration

### 12.1 Financial model

Before changing the current economy, prepare conservative, base, and upside scenarios by region covering:

- DAU and MAU;
- Payer conversion;
- Median spend and ARPPU;
- Deterministic and randomized revenue mix;
- Pass and subscription adoption;
- Revenue concentration;
- Refunds and chargebacks;
- Payment success;
- Content and art capacity;
- Localization;
- Support;
- Compliance;
- Platform fees and taxes;
- Local operating costs;
- Churn and retention;
- Contribution margin.

\[
\text{Incremental contribution}
=
\text{Net revenue}
-
\text{content cost}
-
\text{store and payment costs}
-
\text{taxes}
-
\text{refunds and chargebacks}
-
\text{support cost}
-
\text{compliance cost}
-
\text{incremental churn impact}
\]

Set targets only after establishing the baseline. Targets should cover deterministic revenue share, randomized revenue share, top-spender concentration, payer retention, contribution margin, and player-health outcomes.

### 12.2 Commercial gate for paid randomness

Paid randomness may be enabled only where it has:

- Legal clearance;
- Player-protection compliance;
- Probability and entitlement integrity;
- Acceptable competitive impact;
- Acceptable concentration, refund, complaint, and reputational risk;
- Positive or strategically justified risk-adjusted contribution;
- A tested deterministic fallback.

### 12.3 Legacy entitlements

Before migration, publish rules for:

- Existing premium currency;
- Unopened randomized items;
- Pity and guarantee progress;
- Temporary event tokens;
- Regionally restricted items;
- Cross-platform balances;
- Future transfer or gifting rights;
- Refunds and chargebacks.

Paid value should remain usable or be converted transparently. Pity progress should be preserved or converted into clearly equivalent deterministic value. Unopened items should be honored, converted, or refunded under a disclosed rule. Migration must be tested across regions, platforms, account states, and local operating enclaves.

## 13. KPIs and measurement

Every KPI must have a definition, owner, data source, baseline, target or acceptable range, review cadence, threshold, required action, privacy classification, and retention period.

### Commercial

- Net revenue per DAU and MAU;
- Payer and first-time-payer conversion;
- 30-, 90-, and 180-day payer retention;
- Median payer spend and ARPPU;
- Deterministic and randomized revenue share;
- Pass completion and renewal;
- Subscription renewal;
- Regional contribution margin;
- Payment success;
- Revenue concentration among the top 1%, 5%, and 10%.

### Player health

- Refund and chargeback rate;
- Unauthorized and minor-purchase rate;
- Spending-limit and self-exclusion use;
- Purchase-burst frequency;
- Cooling-off interventions;
- Complaints per 1,000 payers;
- Regret or pressure reports;
- Negative monetization sentiment;
- Churn after major events;
- High-risk-cohort revenue share;
- False-positive and appeal rates for protective interventions.

### Fairness and competition

- Win rate by spend cohort, adjusted for skill, mode, playtime, and account age;
- Time to competitive viability;
- Ranked-access differential;
- Progression-speed differential;
- Cosmetic readability complaints;
- Meaningful event content available without payment;
- Regional event and cosmetic parity;
- Price-adjusted value comparisons.

### Compliance and operations

- Displayed-to-actual probability match rate;
- Disclosure defects;
- Age-control failures;
- Approval completeness;
- Open legal exceptions;
- Refund-resolution time;
- Audit findings;
- Localization defects;
- Cross-border circumvention;
- Kill-switch and rollback success;
- Policy-engine availability and reconciliation failures.

No experiment launches without commercial, player-health, competitive, compliance, and privacy guardrails, predefined stopping rules, and a post-experiment decision record.

## 15. Implementation roadmap

### Phase 1: First 30 days — establish control

- Create the country requirements register;
- Classify requirements as legal, platform, internal, risk default, cultural, or open issue;
- Obtain current written local advice;
- Inventory all monetization mechanics;
- Freeze new high-risk features;
- Define jurisdiction and age rules;
- Define competitive-integrity standards;
- Assign accountable owners and decision rights;
- Specify policy-engine, entitlement, and audit architecture;
- Identify legacy-entitlement and technical gaps;
- Establish baseline commercial, player-health, and fairness metrics.

### Phase 2: Days 31–90 — build the safe default

- Build deterministic Belgium and Netherlands catalogs;
- Implement global disclosure and probability history;
- Add age, country, payment, platform, and entitlement controls;
- Add purchase history, parental controls, spending limits, cooling-off, and self-exclusion;
- Implement fixed-value gifting restrictions;
- Create refund matrices;
- Establish the Review Board;
- Complete cultural research for upcoming events;
- Red-team travel, VPN, shared-account, cached-offer, offline-payment, gifting, refund, and cross-platform scenarios.

### Phase 3: Months 4–6 — validate the model

- Pilot direct cosmetics, passes, subscriptions, and fixed-value gifting;
- Validate Korean disclosure workflows;
- Complete China operating and data readiness;
- Complete country-level Gulf reviews;
- Test Brazil pricing and payment methods;
- Commission independent assurance;
- Establish competitive-parity and player-health baselines;
- Complete legacy migration testing;
- Complete conservative, base, and upside financial scenarios.

### Phase 4: Months 7–12 — scale selectively

- Expand products that pass legal, technical, cultural, commercial, and player-impact gates;
- Publish the Player Monetization Charter;
- Introduce approved adult randomized products only where justified;
- Conduct regional parity and affordability reviews;
- Retire unnecessary variants;
- Remove products with disproportionate harm, complaints, or legal exposure;
- Renew high-risk products only after documented review.

No phase proceeds without a readiness gate covering local legal approval, cultural review, technical eligibility, support capacity, refund handling, auditability, rollback testing, legacy migration, and financial viability.

## 16. Incident and advocacy response

### Triggers

- Probability or disclosure error;
- Minor exposure or unauthorized spending;
- Evidence of pay-to-win impact;
- Regulatory or platform inquiry;
- Material complaint spike;
- Cross-border circumvention;
- Credible advocacy allegation of harm;
- Data or payment incident.

### Response

1. Suspend the affected offer when harm or accuracy is uncertain;
2. Preserve configurations, disclosures, advertisements, communications, and player records;
3. Assign an incident commander;
4. Notify legal, trust, privacy, communications, support, regional operations, and executives;
5. Identify affected players and assess refunds or remediation;
6. Notify regulators, platforms, or players where required;
7. Communicate factually without attacking advocates;
8. Correct the product, not merely the explanation;
9. Commission independent review when defined thresholds are met;
10. Update policy and publish lessons learned where appropriate.

Maintain structured dialogue with consumer organizations, researchers, player-protection groups, and community representatives. Protect internal whistleblowers and prohibit retaliation.

Publish a Player Monetization Charter and material change log covering:

- What can be purchased;
- What cannot be purchased;
- How randomness works;
- Minor protections;
- Regional differences;
- Material price, probability, and event changes;
- Complaint and support routes.

Transparency supports trust but does not substitute for safe design.

## 17. Explicit decisions requiring local legal validation

Before launch or material change, local counsel must provide written advice on the following:

### Belgium and the Netherlands

- Whether the exact paid randomized mechanic is permissible;
- Whether cosmetic, free, earned, or premium-currency-mediated randomness changes the analysis;
- Whether rewards have economic or transferable value;
- Whether trading, gifting, account migration, or marketplace features create additional exposure;
- Which jurisdiction-of-service and payment signals control.

### Korea

- Which randomized products and reward classes are covered;
- Required probability granularity, language, location, timing, and change notices;
- Treatment of paid currency, free currency, bundles, pity, guarantees, and duplicate conversion;
- Record-retention and audit requirements.

### China

- Publishing and operating structure;
- Local operator responsibilities;
- Content and event approvals;
- Probability-disclosure requirements;
- Real-name, minor, playtime, and spending controls;
- Virtual-currency and payment requirements;
- Data localization and cross-border transfer;
- Identity, analytics, support, moderation, and entitlement boundaries between global and local systems.

### Japan

- Exact treatment of collection, exchange, pity, guarantee, rerun, and completion mechanics;
- Advertising, limited-time, prize, and premium-representation requirements;
- Refund and customer-support obligations.

### Gulf countries

For each country in scope:

- Gambling or prize-related treatment of the proposed mechanic;
- Advertising and platform requirements;
- Age assurance and parental controls;
- Gifting and transfer rules;
- Payment, tax, data, and consumer obligations;
- Content, imagery, language, and event restrictions.

### Brazil

- Consumer, youth, privacy, advertising, payment, refund, subscription, and virtual-currency requirements;
- Treatment of randomized and earned rewards;
- Local disclosure and support obligations.

### United States

- Relevant state consumer-protection, gambling, youth-privacy, advertising, subscription, payment, and platform exposure;
- Required or advisable probability disclosures;
- Treatment of minors, family accounts, gifting, and behavioral safeguards;
- State or platform differences in refunds and digital-content rights.

### Cross-market decisions

Counsel must also validate:

- The jurisdiction-of-service hierarchy;
- Age-assurance methods and unknown-age fallback;
- Treatment of travel, relocation, shared accounts, and foreign payment cards;
- Restrictions attaching to purchasers, recipients, accounts, transactions, items, transfers, or competitive use;
- Legacy currency, box, pity, entitlement, and refund migration;
- Data-protection and cross-border analytics arrangements;
- Advertising and influencer disclosures;
- Platform rules in force at deployment.

## Final recommendation

Proceed with one global game and product family built on a deterministic monetization foundation and controlled regional adapters.

The publisher should accept differences in acquisition method where law, culture, or player protection require them. It should not accept differences in competitive integrity, core participation, social belonging, safety, or product quality.

The durable model is:

- Deterministic monetization globally;
- Paid randomness only for approved adults in approved markets;
- Direct and selectable alternatives in Belgium and the Netherlands;
- Full, machine-verified probability and content disclosure;
- No pay-to-win progression;
- Fixed-value, tightly controlled gifting;
- Privacy-proportionate age and spending protections;
- Country-specific legal and cultural review;
- Clear decision rights and measurable controls;
- Independent assurance and evidence-ready logs;
- Risk-adjusted financial planning;
- Structured stakeholder engagement;
- Fast rollback and deterministic fallback capability.

This strategy does not eliminate uncertainty. It makes uncertainty manageable by isolating risky features, distinguishing legal requirements from internal standards, measuring parity and player impact, and preserving one coherent global game without forcing every market into the same monetization model.
