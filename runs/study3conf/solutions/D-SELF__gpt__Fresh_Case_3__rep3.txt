# Supplier Onboarding and Integration Plan  
## Mexico, Morocco, and Vietnam

## 1. Objective and operating principle

Manage the shift from the historical East Asian supply base as a controlled **industrialization, qualification, and continuity program**, not merely a purchasing transfer.

The governing principle is:

> **Standardize the required manufacturing outcome; adapt communication, training, relationship-building, and implementation methods to each supplier and region.**

Country assumptions must not determine supplier risk ratings. Regional controls should reflect supplier-specific evidence, workforce capability, legal requirements, logistics conditions, and observed communication patterns.

The program must protect:

- Product quality, reliability, and safety
- Customer availability and delivery performance
- Regulatory, customs, origin, and export-control compliance
- Traceability, warranty, and recall readiness
- Intellectual property, firmware, data, and cybersecurity
- Labor, environmental, and ethical standards
- Total cost and business continuity

---

## 2. Governance and responsibilities

### Supplier Integration Office

Create an executive-sponsored office led by one accountable program director. Members should include:

- Procurement
- Supplier quality
- Product and manufacturing engineering
- Operations planning
- Logistics and customs
- Finance
- Legal, regulatory, and export-control specialists
- Cybersecurity and intellectual-property specialists
- Regional supplier-development teams
- Receiving-factory operations and quality

The office owns:

- Qualification and volume-allocation decisions
- Program budget, staffing, and priorities
- Global standards, templates, and scorecards
- Waivers and risk acceptance
- Safety-stock and backup-capacity decisions
- Customer and regulatory notification
- Allocation freezes, requalification, and de-sourcing
- Incumbent-supplier exit

### Regional teams

Each country or operating cluster should have:

- Regional supplier manager
- Supplier-quality engineer
- Manufacturing/process engineer
- Bilingual or multilingual coordinator
- Planner/logistics representative
- Commercial or finance representative
- Local legal or compliance support as needed

Each supplier must appoint primary and backup contacts for executive sponsorship, program management, engineering, quality, production, planning, maintenance/testing, compliance, and cybersecurity.

### Required decision matrix

A signed RACI must define authority for:

- Specification and manufacturing-package release
- Tooling, fixture, and test-asset release
- Pilot and process-validation approval
- Deviations, concessions, and material substitutions
- Delivery-date changes and partial shipments
- Shipment holds and production stoppage
- Customer and regulatory notification
- Volume increases, allocation freezes, and alternate-source activation
- De-sourcing and incumbent exit

Every action requires one accountable company owner, one supplier owner, an approver, due date, evidence requirement, and escalation route.

---

## 3. Global supplier standard

The following requirements apply to every supplier and cannot be weakened for schedule or cost reasons:

- Approved drawings, models, BOMs, materials, and revisions
- Critical-to-quality and critical-to-function characteristics
- Defined measurement methods, equipment, uncertainty, and calibration
- Functional, reliability, environmental, and regulatory testing
- ESD, contamination, handling, packaging, and storage controls
- Lot, serial-number, firmware, and configuration traceability
- Nonconforming-material containment and disposition
- Corrective-action effectiveness
- Engineering, material, tooling, software, and location change control
- Approved sub-tier suppliers and subcontractors
- Cybersecurity and intellectual-property protection
- Export-control, sanctions, customs, origin, and labeling compliance
- Delivery definitions and escalation thresholds
- Labor, health, safety, environmental, and ethical compliance

Certifications support qualification but do not prove process effectiveness. Audits must distinguish whether controls are **documented, implemented, effective, and sustained**.

---

## 4. Supplier and product risk segmentation

Rate risk by product, process, supplier, logistics lane, and business impact—not country or spend alone.

### Higher-risk examples

- PCB assemblies
- Batteries and battery-management systems
- Displays, cameras, sensors, RF modules, chargers, and power supplies
- Safety- or regulatory-controlled parts
- Sole-source or custom-tooled components
- High-cosmetic-sensitivity products
- Firmware-, software-, or serialization-intensive products
- Long or fragile import routes
- Suppliers dependent on unqualified subcontractors or unsustainable overtime

Supplier assessments should cover:

- Financial stability, ownership, and legal entity
- Contracting and tax structure
- Manufacturing and engineering capability
- Electronics-process experience
- Quality-system effectiveness
- Workforce, technician, and supervisor availability
- Capacity and bottleneck evidence
- Sub-tier and subcontractor controls
- Utility, network, and site resilience
- Cybersecurity and IP controls
- Customs, origin, export-control, and sanctions exposure
- Labor, environmental, and safety compliance
- Business continuity and disaster recovery
- Dependence on imported components, single sites, or key personnel
- Customer qualification, warranty, service, and recall implications

Record evidence, uncertainty, mitigations, residual risk, and review date for each supplier.

## 5. Qualification phases and gates

### Phase 0: Business, legal, and compliance approval  
**Weeks 0–4**

Complete:

- Financial, ownership, and legal-entity review
- Contract and quality-agreement review
- Customs, origin, export-control, and sanctions review
- Cybersecurity and IP assessment
- Labor, safety, environmental, and ethical review
- Preliminary capacity and logistics model
- Product and supplier risk classification
- Incumbent knowledge-transfer plan
- Continuity and recovery assessment
- Customer-qualification requirements
- Insurance review

**Gate decision:** approve, conditionally approve with dated gap closure, or reject.

No production volume may be released based solely on commercial selection.

---

### Phase 1: Requirements and commercial alignment  
**Weeks 2–8**

Issue a controlled manufacturing package containing, as applicable:

- Approved drawings and 3D models
- BOM and approved substitutions
- Material and process specifications
- Critical characteristics and acceptance limits
- Measurement methods and equipment
- Functional, reliability, environmental, and regulatory requirements
- Cosmetic standards, golden samples, and boundary samples
- Torque, adhesive, soldering, crimping, and fastening requirements
- Firmware, software, labeling, and serialization requirements
- Packaging and shipping specifications
- Process flow, PFMEA, control plan, and inspection plan
- Change and deviation procedures
- Safety, EMC, RoHS/REACH, battery, origin, and export-control requirements

Use a signed requirements matrix:

| Requirement | Source/revision | Supplier interpretation | Verification method | Acceptance limit | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The supplier must explain critical requirements in its own words and identify ambiguity, conflicts, feasibility concerns, measurement limitations, and capacity or material dependencies.

Define these terms formally:

- Forecast
- Requested date
- Committed date
- Material-ready date
- Production-complete date
- Ship date
- Arrival date
- Partial shipment
- Tooling complete
- Sample approved
- Process ready
- Production authorized
- Temporary deviation
- Final approval

Silence, attendance, or purchase-order acknowledgment does not constitute approval.

---

### Phase 2: Tooling, equipment, and process readiness  
**Weeks 6–16**

Require evidence of:

- Tooling dimensional approval and successful trials
- Equipment qualification
- Measurement-system analysis and calibration
- Controlled work instructions
- Operator and technician qualification
- Test-equipment capability
- Maintenance and spare-parts plans
- Traceability and serialization design
- Packaging validation
- Approved sub-tier suppliers
- Change-control readiness
- Programming-station and production-system cybersecurity
- Business-continuity and recovery plans

“Tooling complete” requires dimensional approval, trial evidence, maintenance records, spare parts, and process authorization.

Define ownership, location, insurance, maintenance, calibration, software version, and retrieval rights for company-funded tools, fixtures, testers, and programming assets.

---

### Phase 3: Pilot and process validation

Use the sequence:

1. Engineering sample build  
2. Pilot build  
3. Process-validation build  
4. Controlled production  
5. Full production authorization  

Review:

- First-pass yield
- Defect Pareto and repeat defects
- Scrap and rework
- Cycle time and labor content
- Capability on critical characteristics
- Measurement-system adequacy
- Functional-test coverage and false-fail rate
- Traceability retrieval
- Actual capacity by shift
- Material consumption
- Reliability results
- Corrective-action status
- Operator and technician competence
- Firmware and cybersecurity evidence

Product-family checklists should define mandatory controls for PCBAs, batteries, displays, chargers, cables, cosmetic parts, final assemblies, and firmware-programmed products.

Cpk may be used for suitable, stable characteristics but is not a universal release criterion.

---

### Phase 4: Controlled ramp

Use allocation increases only when evidence supports them. Illustrative ranges are:

- 10–20% after initial production approval
- 25–40% after stable pilot performance
- 50–70% after demonstrated quality, capacity, and delivery
- Full allocation after sustained performance

Allocation also requires:

- Normal staffing capability
- Sustainable overtime assumptions
- Stable traceability
- Effective corrective actions
- Accurate commitments
- Backup capacity or approved safety stock
- No unresolved critical quality, compliance, cybersecurity, or IP issues
- Required customer and regulatory approvals

Maintain incumbent or alternate capacity until the new source meets defined exit criteria.

---

### Phase 5: Steady state

A supplier enters normal governance only after meeting predefined risk-tier requirements, including:

- No unresolved critical specification ambiguity
- Stable performance across relevant shifts and staffing conditions
- Demonstrated capacity at planned demand
- Accurate commitments across multiple order cycles
- Effective traceability and change control
- Effective sub-tier controls
- Successful mock escalation, traceability, and recall exercises
- Major corrective actions closed and verified effective
- Required customer, regulatory, origin, labeling, warranty, and service approvals
- Sustained performance across defined lots, order lines, production weeks, and demand conditions

The required qualification period must be defined before launch.

---

## 6. Schedule, resources, and accelerated transition

Maintain a master schedule for every supplier-product combination, including:

- Contract completion
- Requirements baseline
- Tooling release
- First samples
- Pilot and validation builds
- Reliability testing
- Customer qualification
- Backup-capacity readiness
- First controlled shipment
- Allocation increases
- Incumbent exit

Track baseline date, current forecast, committed date, variance, dependency, owner, recovery action, criticality, and escalation date.

Preliminary feasibility work may begin before requirements freeze. Tool fabrication requires an approved baseline. Pilot production requires process-readiness approval. Production authorization requires validation approval.

Budget for:

- Regional engineering and quality staffing
- Translation and interpretation
- Site visits and audits
- Testing and metrology
- Training
- Dual running
- Safety stock
- Source inspection
- Backup capacity
- Tooling and supplier development
- Contingency logistics

### Accelerated-transition rules

If tariff, export-control, customer-demand, or continuity pressure requires acceleration:

- Safety, regulatory, traceability, cybersecurity, critical-characteristic, and legally required controls may not be waived.
- Parallel activities require written technical approval.
- Conditional shipments require containment, a maximum duration, customer notification where necessary, and executive risk acceptance.
- Waivers must state the requirement, risk, affected quantity and period, compensating controls, owner, expiration date, closure evidence, and approver.
- Waivers may not be open-ended or automatically renewed.

## 7. Communication and document control

Maintain:

1. Controlled English master documents  
2. Approved local-language translations  
3. Visual work instructions  
4. Golden and boundary samples  
5. Technical terminology glossary  
6. Requirements and assumptions log  
7. Decision and action register  
8. Revision acknowledgments  
9. Training and competency records  

For critical documents:

- Use technical translators familiar with electronics manufacturing.
- Perform back-translation or independent technical review.
- Validate instructions with engineers, supervisors, and operators.
- Define which language controls if versions conflict.
- Withdraw obsolete documents and uncontrolled screenshots.
- Retrain affected personnel after changes.

Interpreters must translate completely, preserve disagreement, identify uncertainty, and avoid negotiating technical content. Critical decisions must be confirmed in writing by the accountable technical owner.

Meetings must identify whether each statement is a request, forecast, decision, approval, conditional commitment, or firm commitment. End each meeting with owners, dates, assumptions, and escalation requirements.

Ask suppliers:

- What is least feasible in this plan?
- What could prevent the committed date?
- Which requirement is difficult to measure?
- What alternative would reduce risk?
- What information is still missing?

## 8. Quality, reliability, and cybersecurity controls

### Quality

Require:

- Process-flow, PFMEA, and control-plan alignment
- Error-proofing
- Calibrated measurement systems
- Operator and technician qualification
- Lot and serial-number traceability
- Incoming-material and counterfeit-prevention controls
- Nonconforming-material segregation
- Sub-tier controls
- Layered process audits
- Change control
- Corrective-action effectiveness verification

### Sampling and release

Define approved methods for:

- Incoming inspection
- In-process inspection
- Final inspection
- First-article inspection
- Lot release
- Tightened inspection after failure
- Return to normal inspection
- 100% inspection and exit criteria

Sampling must reflect product risk, characteristic type, measurement uncertainty, process stability, and customer requirements.

### Reliability

For each product family, define:

- Applicable standards
- Sample sizes
- Test duration
- Acceptance criteria
- Failure-analysis method
- Retest rules
- Production-monitoring requirements
- Failure-release authority
- Customer and regulatory notification requirements

### Firmware and cybersecurity

Control:

- Secure firmware release and signing
- Programming-station identity and access
- Key custody and rotation
- Version and configuration management
- Debug-port restriction
- Software-bill-of-materials ownership where applicable
- Factory-data deletion
- Factory-network segmentation
- Vulnerability reporting deadlines
- Incident severity levels
- Recovery testing
- Log retention and audit evidence

## 9. Delivery and capacity controls

Every supplier response must state:

| Field | Required response |
|---|---|
| Quantity | Accepted, constrained, or rejected |
| Requested date | Accepted or revised |
| Production-complete date | Specific committed date |
| Ship date | Specific committed date |
| Arrival date | Date and lane assumptions |
| Material status | Available, ordered, or at risk |
| Capacity status | Validated, constrained, or overtime-dependent |
| Tool/test readiness | Status and evidence |
| Partial shipment | Quantity and timing |
| Constraints | Named and dated |
| Recovery plan | Required if at risk |

Planning must include:

- Rolling 12–16-week forecast
- Frozen and flexible windows
- Material lead times
- Weekly order confirmation
- Shipment-booking cutoffs
- Arrival confirmation
- Engineering-change calendar
- Cancellation and rescheduling rules
- Capacity reservations
- Material-liability rules

Capacity must be demonstrated through observed production, controlled trials, or historical evidence. Validate staffing, bottlenecks, test capacity, maintenance downtime, overtime assumptions, material constraints, and backup equipment.

Each lane must define:

- Incoterms
- Approved carriers
- Transit assumptions
- Customs documents
- Packaging and pallet requirements
- Shipment milestones
- Contingency routes
- Airfreight authorization
- Inventory buffers
- Return, repair, warranty, and recall procedures

## 10. Regional adaptations

Regional guidance is an operating hypothesis to validate at each supplier, not a national stereotype.

### Mexico

- Assess Spanish and English requirements by role.
- Use bilingual operational instructions where necessary.
- Maintain local escalation capability and frequent line presence.
- Validate turnover, staffing, supervisor coverage, and second- or third-shift capability.
- Confirm that automotive-derived systems address consumer-electronics cosmetics, firmware, high mix, and rapid engineering changes.
- Review IMMEX or other applicable structures, VAT, temporary imports, rules of origin, customs valuation, and imported-component dependence.
- Plan border, customs, returnable-packaging, repair, warranty, and recall flows.
- Assess domestic transport security, utilities, water availability, industrial-park resilience, and environmental permits.
- Control tooling and test-asset movement, ownership, maintenance, and retrieval.
- Do not assume geographic proximity eliminates customs, security, or safety-stock risk.

### Morocco

- Assess language requirements by workforce level; use French, Arabic, English, or combinations as appropriate.
- Provide controlled local-language safety and operator materials.
- Use private technical confirmation when group meetings do not surface disagreement.
- Verify electronics-process capability rather than relying on certification or automotive experience.
- Review free-zone or export-processing status, import and VAT treatment, customs responsibility, labor obligations, utilities, port concentration, and re-export requirements.
- Include port, customs, transshipment, and re-export assumptions in delivery plans.
- Validate technician and test-engineer availability.
- Plan repair and warranty-return customs procedures.
- Maintain backup technical contacts and qualified interpreters.
- Assess alternative routes, industrial-zone emergency response, and dependence on European or Asian sub-tier suppliers.
- Apply anti-bribery and hospitality controls.

### Vietnam

- Assess English and Vietnamese needs by management, engineering, supervisor, and operator level.
- Provide Vietnamese work instructions, visual standards, and defect examples.
- Use small technical reviews and written requirements playback.
- Require accountable engineers and planners to state constraints and alternatives.
- Test whether schedules depend on unsustainable overtime.
- Assess technician, supervisor, and skilled-operator retention.
- Audit subcontracting, sister-site production, and unauthorized location changes.
- Review legal entity, export-processing or industrial-zone status, import requirements, power continuity, port routing, imported-component dependence, data transfer, labor, and cybersecurity obligations.
- Control factory access, programming stations, data flows, and sister-site authorization.
- Validate backup-power tests, repair flows, and warranty-return procedures.
- Provide private or anonymous escalation channels where appropriate.

An affirmative response must be verified as both understood and feasible.

## 11. Trust, workforce, and commercial fairness

Trust should be built through predictable performance and fair behavior, not informal assurances alone.

The company will:

- Pay according to agreed terms.
- Avoid contradictory instructions.
- Provide timely deviation and engineering-change decisions.
- Share realistic forecasts.
- Address buyer-driven changes through agreed cost and schedule mechanisms.
- Use capacity reservations, tooling advances, training support, or minimum-volume commitments where suppliers must invest.
- Define ownership of obsolete inventory, safety stock, tooling, test assets, and premium freight.
- Conduct reciprocal site visits and joint problem-solving reviews.
- Protect good-faith escalation from retaliation.
- Apply rules for gifts, hospitality, travel, conflicts of interest, and government interaction.
- Conduct confidential supplier feedback on specification clarity, forecast credibility, decision speed, payment performance, and escalation fairness.

### Training and competence

Training should cover:

- Product and customer requirements
- Document and revision control
- Critical characteristics and acceptance criteria
- ESD and contamination control
- Assembly, soldering, torque, adhesive, and handling requirements
- Functional and reliability testing
- Traceability and serialization
- Nonconformance containment
- Root-cause and corrective-action methods
- Engineering-change control
- Delivery commitments and escalation
- Labor, safety, ethics, export controls, cybersecurity, and IP

Competence must be demonstrated through task observation, inspection tests, defect-identification exercises, escalation exercises, and skills matrices by process and shift. Attendance alone is not evidence of competence.

## 12. Escalation and failure management

### Response targets

| Issue | Acknowledgment | Containment or plan | Executive notification |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Safety or regulatory risk | 2 hours | 24 hours | Immediate |
| Production stoppage | 4 hours | 24 hours | Same day |
| Critical quality escape | 4 hours | 24 hours | Same day |
| Customer-demand delivery risk | 1 business day | 2 business days | Based on impact |
| Routine corrective action | 2 business days | 10 business days | If overdue or systemic |
| Critical cybersecurity incident | Per severity matrix | Immediate containment | Immediate |

### Escalation levels

- **Level 1—Operating issue:** isolated defect, minor documentation error, small delivery variance, or material discrepancy.
- **Level 2—Program issue:** repeated defects, capacity risk, missed milestones, unresolved technical interpretation, significant yield loss, or repeated late commitments.
- **Level 3—Executive issue:** safety or regulatory risk, customer shortage, major interruption, uncontrolled change, traceability failure, concealment, export-control or cybersecurity risk, insolvency, or severe continuity threat.

### Hard-stop conditions

A weighted score cannot override:

- Safety or regulatory noncompliance
- Uncontrolled material, process, software, or location change
- Traceability failure
- Concealed defect or inaccurate reporting
- Critical cybersecurity breach
- Unauthorized subcontracting
- Repeated shipment of nonconforming material

These conditions may require shipment hold, containment, customer notification, requalification, or alternate-source activation.

## 13. Performance scorecard

During ramp, use indicative weighting:

- Quality: 35%
- Delivery: 30%
- Responsiveness: 15%
- Capability and resilience: 15%
- Cost and productivity: 5%

Quality, safety, compliance, and delivery remain threshold requirements regardless of total score.

Each metric requires a formula, data source, owner, baseline, target, reporting frequency, exclusions, dispute process, and consequence.

### Quality

- Incoming defects per million
- First-pass yield
- Defect escape rate
- Customer returns
- Cost of poor quality
- Repeat-defect rate
- Critical-characteristic capability
- Corrective-action effectiveness
- Audit findings
- Traceability completeness
- Temporary-control duration

### Delivery

- Customer-request-date performance
- Original-date performance
- Supplier-committed-date performance
- OTIF
- Quantity completeness
- Partial shipments
- Lead-time variance
- Premium freight
- Expedite cause
- Capacity-plan accuracy
- Material-shortage incidents

### Responsiveness and resilience

- Time to acknowledge and contain issues
- Time to credible root cause
- Engineering-change response time
- Early-risk disclosures
- Training effectiveness
- Technician and supervisor coverage
- Sub-tier visibility
- Business-continuity readiness
- Equipment and utility uptime
- Cybersecurity performance

### Performance bands

- **Green:** normal governance and eligibility for planned allocation increase
- **Yellow:** corrective action and enhanced weekly review
- **Red:** allocation hold, executive recovery plan, or increased containment
- **Critical:** shipment hold, customer or regulatory notification, requalification, or alternate-source activation

The scorecard must not reward artificial dates, incomplete shipments, excessive final inspection, permanent temporary controls, unsustainable overtime, premium freight used to conceal planning weakness, or immaterial risk reporting.

## 14. Knowledge transfer and incumbent exit

Before transfer, capture and validate:

- Setup parameters
- Fixture and tooling practices
- Visual judgments
- Test-failure interpretation
- Repair and rework rules
- Packaging and handling
- Maintenance routines
- Common failure modes
- Escalation practices
- Sub-tier dependencies
- Product-specific workarounds

Use approved records, samples, videos, parameter logs, and side-by-side builds where contractually and ethically permitted. Do not transfer confidential incumbent or third-party IP without authorization.

Do not exit incumbent or alternate capacity until:

- New-source qualification is complete
- Customer and regulatory approvals are complete
- Origin and labeling requirements are confirmed
- Warranty and service implications are addressed
- New-source steady-state criteria are met
- Inventory, tooling, returns, repairs, and field issues are covered
- Operations, quality, engineering, planning, finance, and customer-service leadership accept residual risk

## 15. First 120 days

### Days 0–10

- Appoint the program director and regional leads.
- Establish the RACI, decision rights, system of record, budget, and escalation contacts.
- Classify suppliers and products by risk.
- Identify customer, regulatory, and continuity critical paths.
- Begin incumbent knowledge capture.
- Freeze the minimum necessary product baseline.
- Confirm internal staffing and external support requirements.

### Days 10–30

- Complete requirements playback for high-risk products.
- Establish language hierarchy and document controls.
- Complete legal, customs, export-control, cybersecurity, and IP reviews.
- Conduct capability, traceability, ESD, test, labor, and sub-tier audits.
- Build capacity, material, logistics, safety-stock, and backup-source models.
- Establish scorecard baselines.
- Run simulated quality, shortage, cyber, recall, and late-delivery escalations.

### Days 30–60

- Close critical requirements gaps.
- Approve tooling and process-readiness plans.
- Correlate measurement and test methods.
- Complete training and workforce plans.
- Confirm contracts, tooling ownership, payment, warranty, recall, and continuity provisions.
- Validate schedules without indefinite overtime.
- Complete product-family qualification matrices.
- Approve country-specific customs, labor, logistics, utility, and data-control plans.

### Days 60–120

- Conduct pilot and validation builds.
- Review yield, capability, traceability, test coverage, reliability, cybersecurity, and capacity.
- Authorize only limited controlled production where gates are met.
- Maintain incumbent or alternate capacity.
- Begin allocation decisions using scorecard results.
- Reassess staffing, cost, customer impact, and residual risk.
- Complete customer and regulatory qualification activities.

---

## 16. Success measures

The transition is stable when each critical supplier demonstrates:

- No unresolved critical specification ambiguity
- Effective process flow, PFMEA, control plan, and work instructions
- Adequate measurement and calibration systems
- Proven capability on critical characteristics
- Reliable functional and reliability testing
- Complete lot, serial, firmware, and configuration traceability
- Effective material, process, tooling, software, and location change control
- Qualified personnel across relevant shifts
- Stable yields under normal staffing conditions
- Accurate delivery commitments across multiple order cycles
- Capacity at planned demand without indefinite extraordinary overtime
- Timely escalation of quality, material, delivery, compliance, and cyber risks
- Controlled and visible sub-tier suppliers
- Documented continuity and recovery plans
- Acceptable cybersecurity, IP, export-control, labor, and environmental controls
- Successful mock escalation, traceability, and recall exercises
- Required customer, regulatory, origin, labeling, warranty, and service approvals
- Sustained performance for the predefined risk-tier qualification period

This approach preserves a common global standard while adapting execution to each supplier’s language, workforce, operating environment, and demonstrated capability.
