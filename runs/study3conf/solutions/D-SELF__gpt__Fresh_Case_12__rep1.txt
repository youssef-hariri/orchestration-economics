# 2026 Localized Subscription Strategy  
## Expanding Growth Markets While Protecting Consolidated USD Economics

---

## Executive recommendation

Adopt a **global platform with locally configured monetization**—not uniform USD pricing and not fully bespoke country products.

Localize six connected decisions:

1. **Product rights:** device eligibility, resolution, downloads, streams, profiles.
2. **Local price:** rounded price points within affordability and contribution corridors.
3. **Billing cadence:** recurring monthly, weekly, 30-day, 90-day, or annual access.
4. **Payment rails:** cards, wallets, mobile money, QR, instant payments, banks, carrier billing, and vouchers.
5. **Household access:** transparent Family products with progressive, privacy-conscious enforcement.
6. **Content and distribution:** local merchandising, regional content, and economically disciplined partnerships.

The governing objective is:

> Maximize retained, legitimately collected household revenue while maintaining positive contribution under realistic tax, payment, FX, churn, sharing, discount, content, and operating-cost stresses.

Growth should be scaled on **retained household contribution and cash realization**, not gross subscriber additions, local-currency revenue, or nominal ARPU alone.

### Decision hierarchy

1. Legal, privacy, consumer-protection, and customer-trust compliance.
2. Positive contribution before CAC under the approved downside case.
3. Positive 12-month—or equivalent prepaid—cohort contribution after CAC.
4. Incremental paid households and retention.
5. Strategic content, distribution, or learning value.

Strategic exceptions may support limited pilots below normal floors only with a fixed budget, accountable owner, measurable learning objective, sunset date, and stop/go rule.

# 1. Market archetypes

Geography is a starting lens, not a pricing methodology. A country may contain multiple archetypes by income, city, device, household, or payment behavior.

| Archetype | Customer and market pattern | Recommended starting model | Primary risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wallet-led, mobile-first mass market** | Smartphone-led viewing; mobile money, wallets, agents, vouchers, or carrier billing important; irregular income | Mobile/Essential; 7- and 30-day access; low-data playback; wallets, mobile money, vouchers | Low prices consumed by payment, distribution, fraud, and support costs |
| **Large-household, mixed-payment market** | Multiple viewers and devices; cards, banks, wallets, QR, and prepaid coexist; sharing is material | Mobile, Standard, Family; monthly and 30-/90-day prepaid; progressive sharing controls | Family underpricing replaces multiple individual accounts |
| **Higher-income, connected-TV market** | Greater formal-payment and TV access; willingness to pay for quality and exclusives; competitive switching | Standard and Family; recurring monthly; selective annual and installment tests | Promotions and content costs erode high nominal ARPU |
| **Cash-heavy, recurring-billing-resistant market** | Irregular cash flow; preference for customer-initiated payment; retail and vouchers important | 7- and 30-day prepaid; retail distribution; simple reactivation | Small transactions become uneconomic after commissions and support |
| **FX-volatile or repatriation-constrained market** | Rapid currency movement; short commitments preferred; settlement or transfer constraints | Short prepaid; local settlement; repricing bands; limited fixed-price commitments | Local growth fails to translate into USD contribution or headquarters cash |

### Regional starting hypotheses

These are **illustrative hypotheses, not country facts**:

- **Selected African segments:** prioritize mobile money, wallets, vouchers, agents, and carrier billing where available. Validate recurring authorization, balance failures, data costs, and cash settlement.
- **Selected South Asian segments:** test instant-payment systems, bank rails, wallets, QR, and prepaid. Validate recurring-mandate capability, manual renewal behavior, and household economics.
- **Selected Latin American segments:** test cards, wallets, instant payments, QR, bank transfers, retail cash networks, and selective installments. Validate tax, promotion, chargeback, and connected-TV economics.

Named payment systems should be treated as country-specific options requiring validation of reach, success, cost, settlement, regulation, and reconciliation.

# 2. Localized offer architecture

Keep the core customer-facing portfolio simple: **three products, with prepaid as a billing format**.

## Mobile / Essential

**Target:** smartphone-first, price-sensitive, or payment-constrained customers.

Potential rights:

- One mobile device
- One concurrent stream
- Mobile-optimized resolution
- Core catalog
- Limited downloads
- Restricted casting or TV access

The Mobile tier must be differentiated from Standard. An identical product at a lower price creates broad cannibalization.

## Standard

**Target:** individuals and small households.

Potential rights:

- Mobile and TV access
- One or two concurrent streams
- HD where appropriate
- Downloads
- Core catalog

Standard is the reference price and value anchor.

## Family / Household

**Target:** larger households and high-sharing accounts.

Potential rights:

- Three or four concurrent streams
- Profiles and member controls
- Device management
- Reasonable travel permissions
- Household verification
- Higher quality or download limits

The Family price should be below the expected cost of multiple individual accounts but sufficiently above Standard to reflect additional household value.

## Prepaid formats

Offer selectively:

- 7-day
- 30-day
- 90-day
- Annual, only in stable-currency and lower-refund-risk segments

Every prepaid offer must specify its entitlement—for example, Mobile prepaid versus Standard prepaid. A “30-day prepaid” label without defined rights is not sufficient for pricing or reporting.

### Price fences

Use a limited number of understandable fences:

- Mobile versus TV access
- Resolution
- Concurrent streams
- Downloads
- Profiles and member controls
- Catalog or release-window differences where legally and commercially appropriate
- Advertising load, if an ad-supported offer is separately tested

Do not create a separate product for every market. Localize configuration, not core architecture.

# 3. Localized pricing architecture

## Price-setting formula

For each country, segment, product, and channel:

\[
P_{\text{local}}
=
\text{rounded local price satisfying}
\begin{cases}
\text{affordability and willingness-to-pay}\\
\text{credible local value position}\\
\text{minimum stressed contribution}
\end{cases}
\]

The model must include:

- Tax treatment
- Payment, app-store, carrier, wallet, and reseller fees
- Fixed and percentage transaction costs
- Refunds and chargebacks
- Discounts
- Fraud and sharing leakage
- Variable content and delivery costs
- Customer support
- FX and settlement exposure
- Local operating overhead
- Data-cost context for mobile plans

Use a reference FX band based on recent averages and stress cases, not a single spot exchange rate.

## Illustrative price relationships

These are **test hypotheses, not recommendations or market facts**:

- Weekly access: initially test approximately 25–35% of monthly pricing, subject to transaction costs and incrementality.
- 30-day prepaid: price near monthly recurring, with a premium or modest discount based on payment economics.
- 90-day prepaid: modest discount to three monthly payments.
- Family: clear premium over Standard.
- Annual: selective use in stable currencies and formal-payment segments.

Weekly plans should generally be Mobile-only or otherwise rights-fenced. A Standard-equivalent weekly plan risks excessive payment cost and cannibalization.

## Country-level price-book template

Every pilot market should have a completed table:

| Market / segment | Product and rights | Local price | Tax basis | Payment rail | Base contribution | Downside contribution | Key hypothesis |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Country A / mobile wallet | Mobile prepaid; one phone; one stream | **To validate** | Inclusive or exclusive, documented | Wallet / voucher | **To model** | **To model** | Incremental conversion |
| Country B / household | Family; three streams; profiles | **To validate** | Documented | Card / QR / bank | **To model** | **To model** | Household revenue uplift |
| Country C / FX-sensitive | Mobile or Standard prepaid | **To validate** | Documented | Local wallet / retail | **To model** | **To model** | Stressed USD contribution |

No price should be approved without competitor, affordability, data-cost, tax, payment, FX, churn, and cannibalization inputs.

## Price governance

Define in advance:

- Inflation and FX trigger thresholds
- Approval authority
- Customer notice period
- Grandfathering duration
- Treatment of prepaid vouchers already sold
- Direct, app-store, partner, and retail synchronization
- Tax and invoice updates
- Customer-support procedures
- Separation of test prices from permanent price changes

# 4. Payment and billing model

## Billing cadence

| Archetype | Primary cadence | Secondary cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Wallet-led mobile | 7-day or 30-day prepaid | Monthly wallet authorization |
| Large-household mixed-payment | Monthly recurring | 90-day prepaid |
| Higher-income connected-TV | Monthly recurring | Annual or installments |
| Cash-heavy | 7-day and 30-day prepaid | Retail voucher bundles |
| FX-volatile | Short prepaid | Monthly with repricing controls |

Recurring billing should be optional where mandates are unreliable, legally constrained, or distrusted.

## Payment-rail selection

Prioritize the two to four rails with the best combination of:

1. Target-customer reach
2. Initial authorization rate
3. Renewal or refill success
4. All-in cost
5. Settlement timing
6. Refund capability
7. Chargebacks
8. Fraud exposure
9. Tax and regulatory burden
10. Reconciliation cost
11. Incremental—not transferred—conversion

### Wallets and mobile money

Best suited to:

- Mobile plans
- Small-value monthly access
- Prepaid purchases
- Reactivation
- Customers without cards

Required controls:

- Explicit recurring authorization
- Balance reminders
- Retry windows
- Easy cancellation
- Clear failure codes
- Refund and dispute handling
- Phone-number and SIM-change controls
- Transaction-level reconciliation

One-time wallet payment capability does not establish recurring-billing viability.

### QR and bank transfer

Useful for:

- Prepaid purchases
- Standard and Family plans
- Retail and telecom campaigns
- Customers unwilling to store credentials

Controls should include unique payment references, automated matching, limited provisional access, and clear refund handling.

### Carrier billing

Carrier billing can improve reach but may carry significant revenue share and support obligations. Use it only where incremental contribution exceeds:

- Operator commission
- Refund and chargeback losses
- Settlement costs
- Support costs
- Customer-ownership and dependency risks

### Vouchers and retail

Use in cash-linked markets where digital access is limited.

Track:

- Retailer and agent commission
- Unredeemed inventory
- Voucher leakage
- Fraud and code resale
- Reconciliation breaks
- Expiry and refund treatment
- Incremental versus shifted purchases

### Cards

Retain cards for recurring and higher-value customers, but do not assume cards alone support growth across all segments.

## Prepaid economics

Prepaid should be evaluated on:

- Contribution per pass
- Contribution per active day
- Refill rate within defined windows
- Reactivation interval
- Retail and payment costs
- Voucher leakage
- Messaging cost
- Displacement of recurring subscriptions

Prepaid is valuable when it reaches customers who would not otherwise subscribe, reduces involuntary churn, or improves reactivation. Upfront cash collection alone is insufficient.

## Installments

Use only through regulated or reliable partners where:

- Total price and fees are disclosed
- Collection responsibility is clear
- Missed-payment consequences are clear
- Refund and cancellation processes work
- Incremental conversion exceeds fees and losses

Do not use direct consumer lending as a subscription-growth tactic.

# 5. Household-sharing approach

Move from unmanaged sharing to **priced, transparent household access**.

## Recommended policy

- Define a primary household.
- Permit specified profiles, members, and concurrent streams.
- Allow reasonable temporary travel.
- Use device, stream, account-history, and approximate-location signals together.
- Avoid reliance on a single IP address, particularly on mobile networks.
- Offer Family or extra-member upgrades where payment and identity controls support them.
- Use progressive rather than immediate hard blocking.

## Enforcement sequence

1. Education and warning
2. Low-friction verification
3. Family or extra-member offer
4. Temporary restriction
5. Persistent-abuse block

## Sharing taxonomy

Measure separately:

- Same-residence household use
- Extended-family use across residences
- Friend sharing
- Temporary travel
- Commercial or resale abuse

## Economic test

\[
\text{Net recovery}
=
\text{upgrade revenue}
+\text{legitimate additional accounts}
-\text{incremental churn}
-\text{support}
-\text{enforcement cost}
-\text{cannibalization}
\]

A reduction in shared viewing is not revenue recovery unless it produces a paid outcome.

## Privacy guardrails

- Data minimization
- Purpose limitation
- Retention limits
- Clear customer notice
- Human review for consequential blocks
- Appeals and account recovery
- Approximate rather than precise location where sufficient
- Restricted partner data sharing
- Monitoring for false positives and disparate impact

# 6. Unit-economics framework

## Revenue waterfall

First determine whether the consumer-facing price is tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive.

If the displayed price \(P\) is tax-inclusive and the applicable tax rate is \(t\):

\[
\text{Pre-tax revenue}=\frac{P}{1+t}
\]

\[
\text{Indirect tax}=P-\frac{P}{1+t}
\]

The exact treatment must follow local law and channel rules.

Then:

\[
\text{Consumer price}
-\text{indirect tax}
-\text{discounts}
-\text{refunds}
-\text{chargebacks}
-\text{payment and partner deductions}
-\text{withholding}
=
\text{Net collected revenue}
\]

\[
\text{Net collected revenue}
-\text{variable content}
-\text{delivery}
-\text{support}
-\text{fraud and sharing leakage}
-\text{payment recovery}
=
\text{Contribution before CAC}
\]

\[
\text{Contribution before CAC}
-\text{CAC}
-\text{incremental market operating cost}
=
\text{Cohort contribution after CAC}
\]

Report corporate income tax and fixed corporate overhead separately unless a fully loaded market-profit view is explicitly used.

## Illustrative recurring example

**Illustrative assumptions only—not a forecast, benchmark, or target:**

- Tax-inclusive consumer price: **$4.00**
- Indirect tax: **15%**
- Payment/distribution cost: **8% of gross billings**
- Variable content: **$0.85**
- Delivery and support: **$0.20**
- Fraud and sharing leakage: **$0.10**
- CAC: **$12**
- Monthly churn: **5%**

\[
\text{Pre-tax revenue}=4.00/1.15=\$3.478
\]

| Item | Illustrative amount |
|---|---:|
| Consumer price | $4.000 |
| Indirect tax | (0.522) |
| Payment/distribution | (0.320) |
| Net collected revenue | **$3.158** |
| Variable content | (0.850) |
| Delivery/support | (0.200) |
| Fraud/sharing leakage | (0.100) |
| Contribution before CAC | **$2.008** |

A simple perpetuity indicator would be:

\[
2.008/5\%=\$40.16
\]

This is not investor-grade LTV. Reported LTV should come from a discounted cohort model including churn curves, upgrades, downgrades, reactivation, discounts, price changes, FX, and content exposure.

## Illustrative prepaid example

**Illustrative assumptions only:**

- 30-day Mobile prepaid price: **$2.50 tax-inclusive**
- Tax: **15%**
- Payment and retail distribution: **15%**
- Content and delivery: **$0.55**
- Voucher leakage and support: **$0.12**

| Item | Illustrative amount |
|---|---:|
| Consumer price | $2.500 |
| Indirect tax | (0.326) |
| Payment/distribution | (0.375) |
| Content/delivery | (0.550) |
| Leakage/support | (0.120) |
| Contribution per pass | **$1.129** |

This is not prepaid LTV. Model pass activity, gaps, refill, reactivation CAC, breakage, retail acquisition, and migration from recurring products.

## Content-cost views

Report at least three layers:

1. Contribution before content investment
2. Contribution after incremental content cost
3. Fully loaded contribution after allocated content cost

Use incremental content cost for pricing and marginal launch decisions. Use fully loaded allocation for portfolio and investor reporting, with the methodology disclosed.

# 7. FX and risk guardrails

## FX exposure model

Classify every line item as:

- Local-currency variable
- Local-currency fixed
- USD-denominated
- Other foreign currency
- Hedged or unhedged
- Collection-date exposure
- Settlement-date exposure
- Repatriation exposure

Then:

\[
\text{Contribution}_{USD}
=
\frac{\text{local-currency revenue}-\text{local-currency costs}}{\text{settlement FX}}
-\text{foreign-currency costs}
\]

Do not apply an arbitrary haircut to contribution. Model:

- Local price response
- Inflation-driven cost increases
- Foreign-currency content and platform costs
- Payment fees with fixed and percentage components
- Settlement delays
- Blocked or unrepatriated cash
- Withholding and conversion losses

## Required stress cases

Each market should model at least:

- Base FX
- 15% depreciation
- 30% depreciation
- 50% depreciation
- No repricing
- Partial repricing
- Full repricing, with elasticity assumptions

## Guardrails

Set product- and market-specific limits for:

- Positive contribution before CAC
- Positive contribution under approved FX stress
- 90-day and 180-day retained contribution
- CAC payback
- Payment cost as a percentage of billings
- Fraud and sharing leakage
- Refunds and complaints
- Family cannibalization
- Promotion and discount share
- Reconciliation accuracy
- Settlement delay
- Repatriation loss
- Local operating cost

A market should not scale if nominal local growth masks negative constant-currency cohort economics.

# 8. Content, data, and partnerships

## Content investment

Prioritize:

- Global tentpole content
- Regional content reusable across countries
- Local-language content with measurable conversion or retention impact
- Licensed catalog depth at controlled risk
- Dubbing, subtitles, artwork, and merchandising
- Downloads and low-data playback

Approve local content only when:

\[
\text{Incremental contribution}
+\text{cross-market value}
>
\text{production or licensing}
+\text{marketing}
+\text{localization}
+\text{delivery}
+\text{rights and tax costs}
\]

Measure retained paid households and contribution—not starts or viewing hours alone.

## Data and connectivity

For mobile-oriented plans, track:

- Viewing hours per paid customer
- Data consumption per active day
- Customer-paid data cost
- Company delivery cost
- Zero-rating or bundle subsidy
- Telecom revenue share
- Conversion and retention impact of downloads and low-data modes

\[
\text{Total customer cost}
=
\text{subscription price}
+\text{expected data cost}
\]

A nominally affordable subscription may not be practically affordable if data costs are high.

## Partnerships

Potential partners include telecom operators, wallets, banks, QR systems, retailers, device manufacturers, broadband providers, local producers, app stores, and aggregators.

Every partnership business case must identify:

- Incremental versus transferred customers
- Revenue share and payment costs
- Tax treatment
- Refund and chargeback responsibility
- Settlement timing
- Customer ownership and data access
- Fraud and outage exposure
- Exclusivity and termination terms
- Reconciliation obligations
- Ongoing integration and account-management costs

# 9. Rollout roadmap

## Phase 0: Readiness — 8–12 weeks

Complete:

- Country-by-segment archetyping
- Willingness-to-pay and competitor research
- Local-currency price hypotheses
- Payment and renewal audit
- Tax and settlement memorandum
- FX exposure schedule
- Sharing measurement
- Content business cases
- Privacy and consumer-protection review
- Billing, refund, and reconciliation readiness
- Support, fraud, engineering, and partner-capacity plan
- Minimum market-size and integration-payback analysis

## Phase 1: Controlled pilots — 3–6 months

Prioritize markets with:

- At least two viable payment rails
- Manageable legal and tax requirements
- Local-language support capability
- Meaningful learning potential
- Regional content reuse
- Sufficient measurement quality
- Credible path to amortizing fixed operating costs

Launch:

- Mobile
- Standard
- Selected prepaid formats
- One controlled Family test

## Phase 2: Scale proven combinations

Scale only when the market meets approved thresholds for:

- Net paid additions
- 90-day retained contribution
- CAC payback
- Renewal or refill
- Fraud and leakage
- Complaints and refunds
- Reconciliation
- Constant-currency economics
- Cash settlement and repatriation

## Phase 3: Complex or FX-constrained markets

Use:

- Shorter prepaid terms
- Local settlement
- Limited fixed-price commitments
- Higher approval hurdles
- Explicit blocked-funds and devaluation scenarios

Delay markets with unresolved tax, privacy, payment, settlement, or repatriation exposure.

- Randomization unit
- Eligible population

- Sample-size method

- Contamination risk
- 30-, 90-, and 180-day readouts

- Mobile prepaid versus recurring
- 30-day versus 90-day prepaid

### Payment

- Wallet versus QR versus card routing

- Carrier billing versus direct payment
- Voucher denominations

### Sharing

Compare:

- Warning-only
- Verification
- Family offer
- Temporary restriction
- Progressive enforcement

Measure:

- Upgrade revenue
- Household revenue

- Support cost
- False positives
- Piracy indicators

### Content

Test:

- Local-language artwork
- Dubbing and subtitles
- Regional merchandising
- Release timing
- Content-led prepaid campaigns

Avoid overlapping price, payment, content, and sharing changes unless using a deliberate factorial design.

# Prioritized decisions and next steps

## Decisions required

1. Approve the archetype-based model instead of continent-wide pricing.
2. Approve the three-product architecture: Mobile, Standard, and Family.
3. Approve prepaid as a controlled billing format with explicit product entitlements.
4. Approve payment localization as a core product priority.
5. Approve priced household access with progressive enforcement.
6. Approve country-level price books governed by stressed USD contribution floors.
7. Require separate reporting of gross billings, net collections, contribution, constant-currency results, reported USD, and repatriated cash.
8. Require country business cases to include tax, FX, payment, content, fraud, data, CAC, operating, and cannibalization economics.
9. Approve pilot exceptions only with fixed budgets, owners, sunset dates, and stop/go rules.

## Next steps

1. Select pilot markets across the major archetypes.
2. Build country-by-segment price and entitlement hypotheses in local currency.
3. Complete payment, tax, privacy, settlement, and regulatory memoranda.
4. Build the recurring and prepaid state-transition cohort models.
5. Quantify Mobile, Family, prepaid, promotion, and partner cannibalization at household level.
6. Establish product- and market-specific launch and scale thresholds.
7. Launch controlled pricing, payment, sharing, and content experiments.
8. Publish the first monthly local-to-USD revenue and cash bridge.
9. Review pilot results at 30, 90, and 180 days.
10. Scale only where retained contribution, cash realization, customer trust, and operating readiness are demonstrated.

**Bottom line:** the winning model is not the lowest local price. It is the most accessible product that can be collected reliably, retained efficiently, delivered at acceptable cost, and translated into durable consolidated-USD value.
