# Global Framework for Geopolitically Driven Consumer Activism

## Executive purpose

Geopolitically motivated boycotts and consumer activism can create opposing pressures across markets. A corporate action may be praised in one region and condemned in another; franchisees may demand contradictory statements; employees may ask the company to take sides; and social media can turn a local incident into a global narrative within hours.

The company’s objective is not to satisfy every political demand or to achieve absolute neutrality. It is to:

- Protect people and business continuity.
- Uphold consistent global standards.
- Address company-linked harm responsibly.
- Respect lawful employee and consumer expression.
- Communicate accurately and proportionately.
- Permit legitimate local adaptation without creating contradictory facts or commitments.
- Make decisions through a repeatable, evidence-based process.

## Governing rule

> **Apply one global standard to safety, dignity, legality, nondiscrimination, factual accuracy, and responsible business conduct. Allow local variation in implementation, engagement, timing, and communication. Escalate actions with material cross-market, legal, contractual, human-rights, operational, or political consequences.**

Local adaptation must not:

- Produce contradictory material facts.
- Apply global standards selectively.
- Conceal company conduct or relationships.
- Present a local political preference as the company’s global position.
- Create commitments the company cannot lawfully or operationally fulfill.

---

# 1. Decision priorities

When objectives conflict, apply the following hierarchy:

1. **Protect life and physical safety.**
2. **Comply with applicable law and binding regulatory requirements.**
3. **Prevent, cease, and remedy company-linked human-rights harm.**
4. **Prevent discrimination, retaliation, harassment, and unlawful exclusion.**
5. **Protect employees, customers, franchisees, suppliers, communities, and continuity.**
6. **Optimize brand trust, financial performance, and reputation.**

Commercial and reputational considerations remain important, but they do not override safety, legality, nondiscrimination, or serious company-linked harm.

---

# 2. Corporate posture

## 2.1 Political restraint, not indifference

The company does not endorse political parties, governments, factions, military positions, or contested geopolitical narratives as a matter of corporate policy.

It will nevertheless speak and act where necessary to:

- Protect people.
- Comply with law.
- Address its own conduct.
- Prevent or remedy substantiated harm.
- Protect employees from discrimination, retaliation, or violence.
- Correct material inaccuracies.
- Maintain safe and responsible operations.

Political restraint does not require silence where the company has a material connection to an issue or where silence would create a materially misleading impression.

## 2.2 Universal commitments

The following standards apply across the company, subject to local law:

- Safety and protection from violence.
- Nondiscrimination and anti-harassment.
- Respect for human dignity.
- Lawful and ethical conduct.
- Product and food safety.
- Accurate corporate facts and disclosures.
- Non-retaliation for protected activity.
- Responsible business relationships.
- Appropriate governance of political activity and charitable giving.
- Privacy-respecting monitoring and stakeholder engagement.

## 2.3 Local discretion

Regions may adapt:

- Language, tone, examples, and channels.
- Timing and level of public detail.
- Customer-service scripts.
- Employee listening formats.
- Security arrangements.
- Community partnerships.
- Local humanitarian or community programs.
- Temporary operating measures.
- Franchisee support.

Local teams may provide less public detail where disclosure could endanger employees, franchisees, aid recipients, suppliers, or communities.

## 2.4 Decisions requiring enhanced approval

Global or executive approval is normally required for:

- Public statements about a geopolitical conflict or government.
- Market entry, exit, suspension, or major withdrawal.
- Material donations or humanitarian commitments.
- Changes to strategic suppliers or partnerships.
- Major franchise enforcement or termination.
- Restrictions on employee expression beyond existing policy.
- Actions affecting sanctions, export controls, anti-boycott rules, licensing, or diplomatic relationships.
- Any action likely to be interpreted as a global political position.

# 3. Issue classification

Before deciding whether to act or communicate, classify the issue.

## 3.1 Perceived or misattributed association

Examples include alleged funding, ownership, sponsorship, licensing, or political affiliation that cannot be verified.

**Response:** Establish the facts, identify the relevant entity, issue a concise correction where material, and avoid debating the underlying geopolitical dispute.

## 3.2 Actual company conduct

Examples include sourcing, donations, partnerships, executive conduct, workplace decisions, or supplier relationships that may conflict with company standards.

**Response:** Investigate, prevent or cease harmful conduct, provide remedy where appropriate, assign accountability, and communicate verified facts and corrective action.

## 3.3 Humanitarian concern

Examples include civilian suffering, displacement, emergency food needs, or threats to vulnerable communities.

**Response:** Consider targeted assistance based on documented need, using appropriately screened partners and applicable legal controls. Humanitarian assistance is not a substitute for remedying company-linked harm.

## 3.4 Legal or regulatory issue

Examples include sanctions, anti-boycott, labor, advertising, privacy, charitable-giving, licensing, or government-enforcement concerns.

**Response:** Obtain jurisdiction-specific legal advice, document the analysis, preserve relevant records, and avoid premature legal conclusions in public statements.

## 3.5 Operational or commercial disruption

Examples include store closures, threats, supply interruption, staffing impacts, franchisee distress, or sustained sales changes.

**Response:** Prioritize safety and continuity measures. Analyze causation carefully; do not attribute sales or operational effects to activism without considering seasonality, pricing, competition, availability, currency, and macroeconomic conditions.

## 3.6 Political demand

Examples include requests to endorse a government, faction, slogan, boycott, divestment, or contested narrative.

**Response:** Explain the company’s role, standards, facts, and limits. Consider a political position only where the issue directly concerns company conduct, legal obligations, safety, or a substantiated human-rights responsibility.

# 4. Decision process

## 4.1 Initial questions

For every material incident, record:

1. What precisely is alleged or demanded?
2. Which entity is involved: parent company, subsidiary, franchisee, licensee, supplier, employee, executive, or third party?
3. What is verified, disputed, unknown, or false?
4. What is the company’s actual connection?
5. Who is affected?
6. Is the issue humanitarian, legal, operational, political, or multi-category?
7. Is there a safety, human-rights, discrimination, or retaliation risk?
8. What authority does the company have to act?
9. Could the response create cross-market spillover?
10. What is the minimum effective response?
11. What evidence would change the decision?
12. When will the decision be reviewed?

Each material claim should have a source, date, owner, confidence level, and materiality assessment.

## 4.2 Evidence categories

Classify information as:

- Verified fact.
- Material factual error.
- Unverified allegation.
- Opinion or advocacy.
- Satire or parody.
- Threat or unlawful content.
- Authentic but materially misleading edit.
- Unknown or unresolved.

Do not describe activism as coordinated, inauthentic, malicious, or foreign-influenced without reliable evidence and appropriate legal review.

## 4.3 Response options

Select the least expansive effective response:

1. Monitor and prepare.
2. Correct a material factual error.
3. Engage privately with affected stakeholders.
4. Adapt local operations.
5. Provide targeted support.
6. Investigate.
7. Issue a regional statement.
8. Issue a global statement.
9. Suspend, change, or terminate an activity.
10. Activate crisis management.

---

# 5. Communication decision tree

1. **Is there an immediate safety threat?**  
   Issue safety guidance and activate security procedures first.

2. **Is a material factual error spreading?**  
   Correct the precise claim using verified facts.

3. **Is there a verified company-linked issue?**  
   State the issue, action, remedy, owner, and timeline.

4. **Is the issue political but not company-linked?**  
   Explain the company’s role and limits without endorsing a side.

5. **Is the issue local and unlikely to spread?**  
   Use a regional response.

6. **Is the issue legally material, global, or likely to create cross-market contagion?**  
   Coordinate global and regional communications.

Every public statement should answer, where possible:

- What happened?
- What is verified?
- What is the company doing?
- What is the company not claiming?
- Who can provide further information?
- When will the company update stakeholders?

## 5.1 Message architecture

A credible statement should include:

1. **Acknowledgment:** Recognize the seriousness of the issue.
2. **Corporate role:** Explain what the company is and is not positioned to do.
3. **Relevant commitment:** State the applicable standard.
4. **Specific action:** Describe verified steps.
5. **Limits and next steps:** Explain uncertainty, review scope, and timing.

### Global holding statement

> We recognize that people in the communities where we operate are affected by deeply serious events and hold strongly differing views. Our responsibility as a company is to operate lawfully, protect the safety and dignity of our people and customers, treat people fairly, and address the impacts of our own operations and business relationships.  
>
> We do not endorse political parties, governments, factions, military positions, or contested geopolitical narratives as a matter of corporate policy. We will speak and act where necessary to protect people, comply with law, correct material inaccuracies, or address substantiated concerns connected to our business. Implementation may differ by market because legal requirements, operating conditions, and stakeholder needs differ; our corporate standards and material facts do not.

### Regional statement

> We understand the concerns being raised in [market]. The issue currently affecting our local operations is [verified fact]. Our local team is taking [specific action], consistent with our company-wide commitments to safety, nondiscrimination, lawful conduct, and responsible operations. This communication addresses local operations and does not represent a political endorsement by [Company]. We will not speculate about disputed claims. We will provide a further update by [date] or sooner if material facts change.

### Factual correction

> We reviewed the claim that [precise claim]. Based on our records as of [date], [verified fact]. We have not verified [remaining allegation]. We will update this information if relevant evidence changes.

### Employee message

> We recognize that colleagues hold strong and differing views about current events. The company is not asking employees to support or reject a political position. We are committed to a safe, respectful, and nondiscriminatory workplace. Employees may raise concerns through [channels]. Threats, harassment, retaliation, and discriminatory conduct are not acceptable regardless of viewpoint, subject to applicable law and required consultation procedures.

### Humanitarian support message

> We are providing [amount or type of assistance] through [recipient], which has been screened under applicable legal and compliance procedures. The assistance is intended to address [specific humanitarian need] and does not constitute endorsement of any government, political faction, or contested narrative.

# 6. Regional application guidance

Each region should maintain a playbook covering:

- Local legal, labor, privacy, sanctions, charity, advertising, and political-activity requirements.
- Security conditions and emergency authority.
- Government and regulator contacts.
- Local employee, franchisee, community, and civil-society stakeholders.
- Approved spokespeople and deputies.
- Local-language templates.
- Translation and back-translation controls.
- Known misinformation themes.
- Franchise and ownership structures.
- Safe reporting channels.
- Supply-chain and business-continuity options.
- Cross-border digital audiences.
- Circumstances where public disclosure may create retaliation or security risks.
- Escalation triggers to global governance.

### Regional decision rules

Regions may change execution, but may not independently change:

- Global safety, nondiscrimination, and anti-retaliation commitments.
- Material facts about ownership, funding, relationships, or conduct.
- Sanctions, anti-corruption, or product-safety standards.
- Global political-activity rules.
- Human-rights expectations.
- The company’s approved global position.

A local communication must be reviewed centrally when it could be translated, reposted, or interpreted as a global statement.

# 7. Stakeholder playbooks

## 7.1 Consumers and boycott organizers

- Distinguish criticism, lawful activism, misinformation, threats, and criminal conduct.
- Identify whether the demand concerns company conduct, perceived association, or political solidarity.
- Engage where the company can provide facts, remedy, or practical action.
- Do not attack organizers or debate geopolitical issues on individual posts.
- Do not treat online volume as proof of representativeness or causation.

## 7.2 Franchisees and licensees

The company should:

- Provide a common fact base and rapid operational guidance.
- Clarify who may speak for the brand.
- Support safety and business continuity.
- Avoid forcing owners or franchisees to express personal political views.
- Apply brand and contractual rules consistently.
- Provide a route to challenge guidance that creates local legal or safety risks.
- Define cost allocation for closures, security, repairs, and emergency communications.
- Use cure periods and proportionate remedies before termination where appropriate.

A franchisee’s legal independence does not by itself determine brand attribution. Assess actual control, branding, communication channels, procurement, training, technology, data, marketing, and operating approvals.

Recommended guidance:

> Franchisees may communicate about local operations within their contractual authority and approved brand guidelines. A personal or local political statement must not be presented as the position of [Company]. The company may require clarification, correction, removal, retraining, or other contractual remedies where branded channels create a materially misleading impression.

## 7.3 Employees and contractors

The company should:

- Respect lawful expression, lawful collective activity, and legally required accommodations.
- Apply workplace rules viewpoint-neutrally.
- Protect employees from retaliation, harassment, discrimination, and targeted online abuse.
- Consult unions and works councils where required.
- Establish procedures for protected work actions, attendance, pay, and scheduling.
- Avoid collecting or inferring political, religious, ethnic, or union affiliation except where strictly necessary and lawful.
- Provide independent review of alleged viewpoint discrimination.
- Apply equivalent safeguards to contractor and franchisee personnel where the company has influence or responsibility.

Employee demands should be classified as symbolic solidarity, humanitarian concern, safety or discrimination concern, company-linked conduct, workplace accommodation, or unauthorized corporate endorsement.

## 7.4 Suppliers and partners

Use risk-based due diligence covering:

- Ownership and beneficial control.
- Sanctions and restricted-party exposure.
- Human-rights risks.
- Anti-bribery and corruption.
- Security and labor practices.
- Public statements and brand attribution.
- Remediation and termination rights.

A serious substantiated concern requires prevention, cessation, remedy, accountability, and follow-up—not merely a communications response.

## 7.5 Regulators and governments

- Maintain jurisdiction-specific legal and government-affairs advice.
- Document requests and responses.
- Review sanctions, export controls, anti-boycott rules, licensing, labor law, and public-company disclosure obligations.
- Do not make public commitments before legal approval.
- Escalate conflicting government requirements through the conflict-of-laws process.

# 8. Digital and social-media protocol

## 8.1 Monitoring principles

Social listening is for early warning and question generation, not as the sole basis for policy.

Separate:

- Reach from engagement.
- Narrative from verified impact.
- Criticism from threats.
- Organic activity from uncertain or coordinated activity.
- Local activity from cross-market contagion.
- Correlation from causation.

## 8.2 Response tiers

### Monitor

Use for low-reach, non-threatening, unverified content with no material company connection.

### Correct

Use when a material factual error is spreading and correction is likely to reduce harm.

### Engage

Use when customers, employees, franchisees, or credible media require practical information.

### Crisis response

Use for threats, violence, serious allegations, regulatory inquiries, account compromise, or material multi-market disruption.

## 8.3 Authenticity and privacy controls

The company should maintain procedures for:

- Deepfakes and synthetic media.
- Impersonation and hijacked accounts.
- Edited or context-stripped content.
- Original URLs, timestamps, screenshots, and metadata.
- Platform reporting and account security.
- Employee-generated content using uniforms or workplaces.
- Influencer disclosures.
- Human review of automated classifications.
- Data minimization, retention, access, and cross-border transfer.

Monitoring must not create ideological profiles of employees or customers. Automated classifications must not be the sole basis for discipline, customer restrictions, partner termination, or geopolitical decisions.

# 9. Crisis escalation and governance

## 9.1 Escalation tracks

Escalate through the highest applicable track:

- **Safety:** Threats, doxxing, vandalism, violence, targeted harassment.
- **Legal and regulatory:** Sanctions, labor, political activity, advertising, privacy, charity, licensing, or government inquiries.
- **Operational:** Closures, supply interruption, staffing impacts, franchisee noncompliance.
- **Reputation:** Rapid narrative spread, media attention, perceived hypocrisy, cross-market contagion.
- **People and culture:** Employee activism, retaliation, discrimination, polarization, retention concerns.

## 9.2 Escalation levels

### Level 1: Emerging concern

Online criticism or limited local activism with no credible safety or legal risk.

**Actions:** Monitor, verify, prepare FAQs, identify spokespeople, and contact affected operators.

### Level 2: Material regional pressure

Sustained activism, local disruption, credible allegations, or significant media attention.

**Actions:** Activate the response group, conduct legal and human-rights review, engage stakeholders, and issue regional guidance if useful.

### Level 3: Major multi-market crisis

Coordinated pressure, serious threats, regulatory inquiry, material operational impact, or substantial internal division.

**Actions:** Establish a crisis command center, create one global fact base, coordinate regional messaging, consider independent review, and brief the board.

### Level 4: Safety-critical or existential event

Serious injury or loss of life, imminent threats, legal prohibition, sanctions exposure, or evidence of serious misconduct requiring immediate action.

**Actions:** Protect people and facilities, coordinate with authorities, suspend relevant activity if necessary, preserve evidence, communicate verified facts, and initiate a formal investigation.

## 9.3 Emergency and strategic approval lanes

### Emergency lane

Pre-authorized local action may include:

- Employee protection.
- Temporary closure.
- Security activation.
- Law-enforcement contact.
- Account protection.
- Urgent operating changes.

Actions should be documented retrospectively if prior approval would delay protection.

### Strategic lane

Enhanced approval is required for:

- Political statements.
- Material donations.
- Market exit or suspension.
- Supplier or partnership changes.
- Major franchise enforcement.
- Global policy changes.
- Sanctions-sensitive actions.

## 9.4 Service levels

Set operating targets appropriate to the business, including:

- Immediate safety threat: security activation within minutes.
- Credible legal or regulatory issue: legal owner assigned within one hour.
- Material factual claim: initial verification within a defined response period.
- Regional disruption: employee and operator guidance within a defined period.
- Global response: approval deadline based on severity, not executive availability alone.

Every market must have a minimum response package:

1. Safety contact.
2. Fact-verification owner.
3. Approved holding statement.
4. Employee and franchisee escalation route.
5. Local legal support.
6. Translation support.
7. Incident log.
8. Review date.

# 10. Governance and decision rights

Maintain a standing response group comprising:

- Global and regional communications.
- Legal and compliance.
- Human resources and employee relations.
- Security and enterprise risk.
- Operations and supply chain.
- Franchise management.
- Government affairs.
- Corporate responsibility.
- Data and insights.
- Finance.
- Relevant country leadership.

Each function should have a primary owner, deputy, after-hours contact, and delegated authority.

| Decision | Primary owner | Required consultation |
|---|---|---|
| Local holding statement | Regional communications | Legal and country leadership |
| Global public position | CEO or delegate | Legal, communications, executive committee |
| Employee safety response | Regional HR/security | Legal and communications |
| Franchisee operating guidance | Franchise leadership | Legal, operations, regional leadership |
| Humanitarian support | Corporate responsibility/finance | Legal, compliance, regional leadership |
| Supplier or partner review | Procurement/compliance | Legal, human rights, operations |
| Market closure or major change | Executive committee | Board as appropriate |
| Political or government-related position | Government affairs/legal | CEO and relevant board committee |

The board or appropriate committee should receive reporting on material safety incidents, regulatory exposure, major employee or franchise disputes, significant commitments, sustained multi-market boycotts, and decisions affecting long-term market access.

# 11. Mandatory pre-action review

For every material statement, donation, supplier change, franchise action, employee-policy restriction, or operating change, record:

- Issue classification.
- Entity involved.
- Verified facts and sources.
- Known uncertainties.
- Applicable jurisdictions.
- Safety assessment.
- Human-rights assessment.
- Employment and discrimination assessment.
- Sanctions, export-control, and anti-boycott assessment.
- Political-activity assessment.
- Privacy and monitoring assessment.
- Advertising and consumer-law assessment.
- Franchise and contractual assessment.
- Public-company disclosure assessment, where relevant.
- Cross-market spillover assessment.
- Stakeholder fairness assessment.
- Alternatives considered.
- Proposed action.
- Authorized spokesperson.
- Required qualifications.
- Owner, budget, and resources.
- Review and expiry date.
- Rollback criteria.
- Follow-up reporting obligations.

The review should be proportionate to urgency. Emergency action may be documented after the immediate risk is controlled.

# 12. Fairness and ethics test

Before implementing a significant measure, document:

- Who benefits?
- Who bears costs or risks?
- Are vulnerable or protected groups disproportionately affected?
- Are comparable cases treated consistently?
- Is the distinction based on legitimate evidence rather than identity or viewpoint?
- Were less discriminatory alternatives considered?
- Were affected stakeholders able to raise concerns?
- Is support allocated according to need and risk rather than online visibility?

No operational, employment, customer-service, or partner decision may be based solely on nationality, ethnicity, religion, perceived political affiliation, or participation in lawful consumer activism.

Legal compliance does not, by itself, establish ethical legitimacy. High-impact humanitarian, security, market-exit, and supplier decisions should receive an independent ethics review.

---

# 13. Reversal and changing conditions

Every major action should include:

- Trigger conditions.
- Reassessment date.
- Reversibility.
- Rollback criteria.
- Exit plan.
- Communication plan if circumstances change.

A decision may be revised when evidence, law, safety risks, or operating conditions materially change. The company should explain:

- What changed.
- Why the previous approach is no longer adequate.
- What the revised action will be.
- How implementation and accountability will occur.

# 14. Metrics and accountability

Each metric should have a baseline, target, owner, reporting frequency, escalation threshold, and corrective action.

## Safety and people

- Number and severity of threats and harassment incidents.
- Time to activate security support.
- Unequal access to security or employee support.
- Employee reports of retaliation or discrimination.
- Time to resolve employee concerns.
- Confidence in reporting channels.
- Training completion.

## Operations

- Closures and service interruptions.
- Staffing and absenteeism trends.
- Franchisee retention and satisfaction.
- Supply-chain disruption.
- Time to provide guidance.
- Time to implement approved mitigations.

## Communications

- Time from verified issue to response.
- Accuracy of public statements.
- Number of contradictory or unauthorized statements.
- Material corrections, including corrections to company-generated errors.
- Percentage of regional statements receiving spillover review.
- Stakeholder comprehension and credibility by region.

## Governance and ethics

- Percentage of material incidents handled through the approved process.
- Completion of legal, compliance, and ethics reviews.
- Humanitarian due-diligence failures.
- Commitments lacking owners or funding.
- Framework adherence during high-pressure events.
- Decisions changed as evidence evolved.
- Timeliness of executive and board reporting.

Sales and online sentiment are inputs, not standalone measures of whether a decision was responsible or correct.

# 15. Implementation roadmap

## First 30 days

- Establish the response group and deputies.
- Approve the decision hierarchy and approval lanes.
- Create the global fact-base and incident-record templates.
- Map high-risk markets and stakeholders.
- Approve global, regional, employee, franchisee, and social-media templates.
- Confirm emergency contacts and service levels.
- Review relevant legal, employment, franchise, supplier, charity, and political-activity controls.

## Days 31–60

- Complete regional and franchise playbooks.
- Train leaders, managers, spokespeople, social teams, and franchisees.
- Establish employee listening and franchisee advisory channels.
- Build multilingual FAQs and correction protocols.
- Implement privacy-compliant monitoring.
- Confirm screened humanitarian partners.
- Conduct legal, security, and communications exercises.

## Days 61–90

- Run a cross-market simulation involving contradictory regional demands.
- Test deepfake, account-compromise, translation, and reposting scenarios.
- Audit branded social accounts and franchisee communications.
- Establish board reporting.
- Confirm staffing, budget, vendor, and surge-capacity requirements.
- Define rollback, correction, and independent-review procedures.

## Ongoing

- Conduct geopolitical horizon scanning.
- Refresh facts and regional playbooks.
- Review material incidents at set intervals.
- Track commitments to completion.
- Conduct after-action reviews.
- Update training and controls based on evidence and stakeholder experience.

---

## Final operating standard

The company should be:

- **Principled:** consistent on safety, dignity, legality, nondiscrimination, and responsible conduct.
- **Fact-based:** clear about what is known, unknown, disputed, and changing.
- **Locally responsive:** attentive to legal, cultural, security, and operating realities.
- **Politically disciplined:** not a substitute for governments or political movements.
- **Operationally credible:** matching public commitments with funded, measurable action.
- **Fair to employees and franchisees:** protecting lawful expression without requiring political alignment.
- **Prepared to act:** willing to investigate, correct, adapt, remedy, or suspend activity when evidence and responsibilities warrant it.

> **Verify the facts. Protect people. Classify the issue. Apply global standards. Adapt execution locally. Communicate only what the company can support. Correct material errors. Remedy substantiated harm. Escalate decisions with material safety, legal, human-rights, operational, contractual, or cross-market consequences.**
