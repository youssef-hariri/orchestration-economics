# International Adaptive-Learning Product Strategy  
## Vietnam, Saudi Arabia, France, and Kenya

## 1. Executive summary

The company should expand through a **shared adaptive-learning platform with country-governed educational, commercial, and regulatory layers**.

The US product should not be exported through translation alone. Curriculum sequence, language of instruction, assessment practices, teacher authority, parent expectations, connectivity, data governance, procurement, and pricing differ materially across the four markets.

### Strategic recommendation

- **Standardize globally:** adaptive-learning infrastructure, competency modeling, content tooling, assessment primitives, offline delivery, security, accessibility, analytics, and experimentation.
- **Localize by country:** curriculum and textbook mappings, language, examples, pedagogy, assessments, teacher and parent workflows, privacy configuration, pricing, support, partnerships, and procurement claims.
- **Launch narrowly:** begin with foundational mathematics and literacy, limited grades, and defined customer segments.
- **Use readiness gates:** do not commit to broad commercialization until learning value, teacher adoption, technical reliability, compliance, and unit economics are demonstrated.
- **Position for trust:** the product should augment teachers, provide transparent recommendations, minimize unnecessary data collection, and avoid manipulative engagement or unsupported high-stakes automation.

### Portfolio decision

| Country | Near-term decision | Initial route | Conditions for progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vietnam** | Proceed to bounded paid pilot | Selected private schools and tutoring providers | Validate curriculum/textbook fit, Vietnamese quality, payment economics, data requirements, and public-sector route |
| **Kenya** | Proceed to bounded funded pilot | One defined NGO/donor, private-school, or county deployment | Prove offline/shared-device reliability, learning impact, language fit, and full delivery economics |
| **Saudi Arabia** | Discovery and readiness only | Private and bilingual schools first | Complete Arabic/RTL, cultural, safeguarding, data, support, partner, and procurement gates |
| **France** | Discovery and compliance-led preparation | Private schools, tutoring, research, and selected local authorities | Complete accessibility, privacy, interoperability, teacher, and procurement readiness |

Only Vietnam and Kenya should enter initial execution pilots. Saudi Arabia and France may conduct research, content validation, partner development, and compliance preparation. No more than two costly execution pilots should run concurrently without explicit capacity approval.

---

## 2. Strategic principles

1. **Standardize infrastructure, not the educational contract.**  
   Reuse technology primitives; localize what is taught, how it is expressed, how it is assessed, and how it is bought.

2. **Configure before customizing.**  
   Curriculum, language, reporting, privacy, assessment, and pricing differences should be handled through country configuration wherever possible. Country-specific code requires formal review.

3. **Treat curriculum alignment as an operating capability.**  
   Alignments must be versioned, sourced, reviewed locally, and maintained as curricula and examinations change.

4. **Make teacher control non-negotiable.**  
   Recommendations must be explainable, editable, and optional. Teachers retain authority over assignments, sequencing, grouping, and interpretation.

5. **Treat offline, print, group, and teacher-led modes as core capabilities.**  
   These are essential for Kenya and valuable for Vietnam, Saudi Arabia, and France.

6. **Use evidence discipline.**  
   Separate verified requirements, market evidence, management hypotheses, and open questions. Do not generalize evidence from one country, segment, or channel to another.

7. **Do not equate engagement with learning.**  
   Optimize for learning gain, teacher usefulness, appropriate use, trust, and retention—not downloads or time online.

8. **Protect children by design.**  
   No child-targeted advertising, sale of learner profiles, unrelated commercial profiling, or unvalidated high-stakes automated decisions.

9. **Fund local capability.**  
   Curriculum experts, language editors, teacher trainers, legal advisors, implementation staff, and support teams are part of the product investment.

10. **Scale only when economics work after delivery costs.**  
    Pricing must account for localization, training, support, hosting, connectivity, partners, taxes, compliance, and evaluation.

## 3. Shared technology core

### 3.1 Capabilities to standardize globally

| Capability | Global standard |
|---|---|
| Learner model | Competency and skill graph independent of a single national curriculum |
| Adaptation | Practice selection, remediation, spaced review, difficulty adjustment, language support, and exam mode |
| Explainability | Evidence-based recommendations with confidence and uncertainty |
| Teacher control | Manual assignment, grouping, sequencing, suppression, and override |
| Curriculum tooling | Versioned standards, textbook, pacing, and examination mappings |
| Content management | Local authoring, review, licensing, metadata, versioning, and rollback |
| Assessment | Diagnostic, formative, classroom, exam-preparation, rubric, and teacher-observation modes |
| Delivery | Web, Android, low-bandwidth, offline synchronization, print, projector, and group modes |
| Administration | Rosters, identity, permissions, audit logs, exports, and integrations |
| Trust and safety | Privacy-by-design, consent, retention, deletion, encryption, child safety, and AI governance |
| Accessibility | Shared engineering standards plus country-specific validation |
| Analytics | Common KPI definitions, experimentation, model monitoring, and subgroup analysis |
| Commercial infrastructure | Billing, entitlements, CRM, partner management, and reporting |

The learner model must separate:

1. Underlying competency.
2. Country curriculum objective.
3. Assessment evidence.
4. Instructional resource.

This allows reuse without assuming that the same competency has identical sequencing, depth, terminology, or assessment meaning across countries.

### 3.2 Capabilities to localize

- Curriculum and textbook sequence.
- Language of instruction and support languages.
- Subject terminology, reading level, audio, and pronunciation.
- Examples, names, imagery, history, geography, and civic references.
- Assessment formats, rubrics, and reporting conventions.
- Teacher and administrator workflows.
- Parent permissions, reports, communications, and screen-time defaults.
- Data-controller, consent, retention, hosting, and transfer settings.
- Pricing, taxes, payment methods, contract structures, and support hours.
- Procurement claims, local documentation, training, and implementation.

## 4. Country-by-country localization plan

### 4.1 Vietnam

#### Initial proposition

- **Target:** selected private schools and tutoring providers.
- **Initial subjects:** mathematics and English.
- **Initial value:** textbook- and curriculum-linked practice, differentiated support, and a separate exam-preparation mode.
- **Delivery:** Android-first, with offline homework, shared-device support, and printable extensions.

#### Product requirements

- Vietnamese-first learner, teacher, and parent experiences.
- Vietnamese explanations supporting English learning.
- Assignment by textbook chapter, curriculum objective, class, group, or examination goal.
- Distinct mastery and exam-preparation modes.
- Validated Vietnamese educational terminology, reading levels, diacritics, and regional language variation.
- Speech features initially formative and advisory only.

#### Content and cultural adaptation

Review names, locations, currency, units, transport, food, geography, daily life, historical and civic references, classroom imagery, and sensitive topics through a local editorial board with authority to revise or withdraw content.

#### Channel strategy

Prioritize private schools, tutoring providers, publishers, teacher-training organizations, and selected telecom/device partnerships. Test D2C only in selected urban segments.

Tutoring contracts should include guardrails against child ranking, excessive drilling, inappropriate profiling, and opaque reporting.

#### Required validation

- First grades, strands, textbooks, cities, and curriculum version.
- Buyer priority: examination performance, English proficiency, remediation, or convenience.
- Payment, tax, app-store, and partner-margin economics.
- Local entity, content approval, procurement, privacy, and data-transfer requirements.
- Necessity and feasibility of local messaging integrations.

---

### 4.2 Saudi Arabia

#### Initial proposition

- **Target:** selected private and bilingual schools.
- **Initial subjects:** Arabic mathematics or literacy.
- **Initial value:** Arabic-first, teacher-controlled, culturally reviewed, supervised learning.
- **International schools:** separate configuration and discovery path.

#### Product requirements

- Native Arabic across learner, teacher, parent, and administrator experiences.
- Complete right-to-left support across interfaces, formulas, charts, mixed-script content, and accessibility tools.
- Arabic numeracy and mathematical terminology.
- Arabic reading-level controls and age-appropriate formal language.
- Arabic audio and assistive-technology testing.
- Configurable public, Saudi private, bilingual, and international-school pathways.
- Arabic training and customer support.
- Family and school session controls.

#### Content and cultural adaptation

Mandatory review should cover religious and historical material, gender representation, family structures, clothing and classroom imagery, music and visual content, biological and health topics, political references, and safeguarding.

The review board needs documented blocking authority, escalation rules, release records, and rollback procedures.

#### Channel strategy

Begin with private and bilingual schools through qualified local implementation capacity. Consider publishers, telecoms, device providers, and premium D2C only after economic and operational validation. Government tenders require separate readiness work.

Specific hosting, transfer, security, and public-sector requirements must be confirmed for each deployment. Local hosting should not be treated as universally mandatory without evidence.

#### Required validation

- Initial curriculum pathways, grades, and assessment requirements.
- Language standards for formal instruction, children, parents, and audio.
- Entity, vendor-registration, cloud, cybersecurity, integration, and support obligations.
- Reporting permissions for families, teachers, schools, and authorities.
- Premium D2C demand after acquisition, payment, support, and partner costs.

### 4.3 France

#### Initial proposition

- **Target:** private schools, tutoring providers, research or teacher-training partners, and selected local authorities.
- **Initial subjects:** mathematics or literacy in limited curriculum cycles.
- **Initial value:** teacher-controlled, privacy-safe, accessible support requiring minimal setup.

#### Product requirements

- Professional French editorial production.
- Alignment to selected curriculum cycles and competencies.
- French institutional, civic, geographic, and measurement contexts.
- Manual assignment and teacher-controlled sequencing.
- Explainable recommendations and full override.
- No child ranking or attention-maximizing mechanics.
- Print, projection, and teacher-led extensions.
- Strong desktop, browser, keyboard, screen-reader, caption, and managed-device support.

#### Compliance and institutional fit

Complete a deployment-specific French legal, accessibility, privacy, and interoperability assessment. Potential requirements include:

- Data minimization and purpose limitation.
- Controller/processor agreements.
- Retention and deletion controls.
- Transfer documentation.
- Impact assessment where appropriate.
- Algorithmic transparency.
- Accessibility evidence and remediation.
- Identity, roster, learning-management, and data-export compatibility.

“WCAG 2.2 AA where feasible” is not sufficient as a public-sector readiness claim.

#### Channel strategy

Use private, research, tutoring, and local-authority pilots to establish teacher and institutional fit. D2C may test demand but does not establish public-school readiness.

#### Required validation

- First curriculum cycle, subject, buyer, and deployment environment.
- Required identity, roster, learning-management, and data-export integrations.
- Applicable public-sector accessibility evidence.
- Reports that support teachers without appearing to replace official judgment.
- Consumer, subscription, refund, and educational-claim requirements for D2C.

### 4.4 Kenya

#### Initial proposition

- **Target:** one defined NGO/donor, private-school, or county deployment archetype.
- **Initial subjects:** foundational literacy and mathematics.
- **Initial value:** offline, shared-device, teacher-led learning with hybrid competency evidence.

#### Product requirements

- Offline-first Android application.
- Shared-device and group-learning modes.
- Downloadable content packs and delayed synchronization.
- Printable learner and teacher materials.
- Teacher observation and rubric workflows.
- Large-class grouping and remediation.
- Low-data dashboards.
- Projector and teacher-led modes.
- English and Kiswahili configured by grade, subject, and school.
- SMS, voice, or messaging only after consent, security, carrier, cost, and safeguarding validation.

#### Pedagogy and assessment

A quiz engine alone must not be marketed as competency-based. Support:

- Teacher observation.
- Projects.
- Oral performance.
- Practical application.
- Collaboration and communication evidence where relevant.
- Rubric-based assessment.
- Offline evidence capture.

#### Infrastructure and economics

The deployment budget must explicitly assign responsibility for devices, charging, connectivity, content transfer, maintenance, replacement, teacher training, coaching, monitoring, evaluation, and local support.

Testing must occur in genuinely low-connectivity and shared-device environments.

#### Channel strategy

Prioritize NGOs, donors, private schools, county programs, telecoms, device providers, teacher-training organizations, and foundations. A donor-funded pilot may demonstrate field impact but does not automatically establish national procurement eligibility or recurring commercial viability.

#### Required validation

- First grades, counties, school types, and learning areas.
- Language-of-instruction requirements by grade and context.
- Device, power, content-pack, synchronization, and recovery requirements.
- Funding responsibility for devices, power, training, connectivity, and support.
- Evidence needed for donor renewal, county procurement, or commercial expansion.

## 5. Curriculum, language, and content governance

### 5.1 Curriculum alignment levels

All claims must identify the level of alignment:

1. **Reference alignment:** mapped to an official objective.
2. **Instructional alignment:** sequence and terminology reviewed against local teaching materials and pacing.
3. **Assessment alignment:** item format and cognitive demand reviewed against local assessment practice.
4. **Official approval or endorsement:** used only with written evidence.

Each alignment record must include:

- Country and education sector.
- Grade and subject.
- Curriculum version and effective date.
- Source documents.
- Textbook and pacing dependencies.
- Mapping methodology.
- Coverage and limitations.
- Local reviewer.
- Approval status.

### 5.2 Content production

Each country requires:

- Curriculum lead.
- Language/editorial lead.
- Practicing teacher reviewers.
- Accessibility reviewer.
- Cultural and safeguarding panel.
- Legal/privacy advisor.
- Implementation lead.

Workflow:

1. Align to official objectives and local pacing.
2. Adapt language, examples, pedagogy, assessments, and visuals.
3. Test with teachers and learners.
4. Review accessibility, bias, and safeguarding.
5. Approve, version, and release.
6. Monitor complaints and withdraw or revise when required.

### 5.3 Evidence register

Each country must maintain a live register identifying whether each material assumption is:

- Verified requirement.
- Market evidence.
- Management hypothesis.
- Open question.

The register must record source, confidence, owner, validation method, deadline, and consequence if false.

## 6. Channel and procurement strategy

### 6.1 Channel roles

| Channel | Strategic role | Principal risk |
|---|---|---|
| D2C | Demand testing, brand, and payment validation | CAC, churn, payment friction, limited institutional proof |
| Private schools | Controlled pilots and references | May not predict public-sector requirements |
| Tutoring providers | Reach and exam demand | Pressure toward ranking, drilling, and customization |
| NGOs/donors | Funded access and impact evidence | Reporting burden and uncertain recurring revenue |
| Telecom/device partners | Distribution and affordability | Margin pressure, lock-in, and governance complexity |
| Local authorities | Public credibility and scale | Procurement and implementation complexity |
| National government | Potential scale and legitimacy | Eligibility, long cycles, strict compliance, low pricing |

### 6.2 Procurement readiness package

Before institutional or government sales, prepare:

- Curriculum and textbook alignment matrix.
- Claim register with sources and limitations.
- Security and privacy dossier.
- Deployment data-flow and hosting documentation.
- Data-processing agreement.
- Accessibility evidence.
- Interoperability and data-export specifications.
- Local-language contracts and support materials.
- Service-level agreement.
- Implementation and teacher-training plan.
- Safeguarding procedures.
- Evaluation protocol.
- Total-cost-of-ownership model.
- Tax, invoicing, entity, and vendor-registration documentation.
- References relevant to the target channel.

For each country and target segment, create one primary and one fallback procurement route specifying:

- Buyer and payer.
- Procurement authority.
- Eligibility and registration.
- Local entity or partner requirement.
- Expected process and timing.
- Required evidence.
- Payment terms.
- Implementation obligations.
- Accountable owner.

### 6.3 Partner governance

Assess partners for market access, implementation capability, language support, safeguarding, data governance, technical capacity, financial stability, economics, and exclusivity risk.

Contracts must define:

- Customer ownership.
- Data roles and restrictions.
- Content ownership and licensing.
- Training and support responsibilities.
- Device and connectivity responsibilities.
- Security and safeguarding duties.
- Revenue share.
- Exclusivity limits.
- Audit rights.
- Exit, continuity, and data-return provisions.

## 7. Rollout roadmap

### Phase 0: Evidence and architecture — months 0–3

Complete:

- Country evidence registers.
- Legal, privacy, and procurement assessments.
- Curriculum and textbook inventories.
- Buyer, teacher, parent, and learner research.
- Device and connectivity audits.
- Willingness-to-pay research.
- Language-quality benchmarks.
- Data, consent, and safeguarding models.
- Minimum global core.
- Local advisory boards.
- Pilot budgets, staffing, RACI, and evaluation protocols.

### Phase 1: Bounded execution pilots — approximately months 4–9

- **Vietnam:** mathematics and English in selected private schools and tutoring providers.
- **Kenya:** foundational mathematics and literacy in a genuinely offline or shared-device deployment.
- **Saudi Arabia:** discovery and content validation only.
- **France:** discovery and institutional preparation only.

### Phase 2: Conversion and evidence — months 10–18

- Convert successful pilots into multi-school or cohort contracts.
- Conduct independent or credible evaluations.
- Add grades and subjects selectively.
- Establish local customer-success capacity.
- Prepare targeted public-sector bids.
- Publish evidence without overstating causal impact.

### Phase 3: Selective scale — months 19–36

Scale only segments with:

- Demonstrated learning value.
- Sustainable teacher use.
- Proven technical reliability.
- Resolved privacy and safeguarding issues.
- Positive contribution margin after full delivery costs.
- Renewal or expansion intent.
- Funded content maintenance and local support.

## 9. KPIs and scale gates

### Learning and assessment

- Pre/post learning gain against a defined baseline or comparison.
- Mastery growth by skill, grade, language, and segment.
- Time to mastery.
- 30-, 90-, and 180-day retention.
- Assessment reliability.
- Recommendation usefulness.
- Differential performance across gender, language, disability, region, device, and school type.
- Completion of required teacher- or rubric-based competency evidence.

### Teacher and institutional adoption

- Weekly active teachers.
- Active classes with assignments.
- Class setup and assignment-creation time.
- Recommendation acceptance, modification, and override.
- Training completion.
- Support tickets per active school.
- Pilot-to-paid conversion.
- Renewal and expansion.

### Parent and learner

- Activation and conversion.
- Retention.
- Parent report access.
- Parent trust and usefulness.
- Learner confidence and usefulness.
- Learning gain per minute.
- Refund, complaint, and safeguarding rates.

### Commercial

- Net revenue per learner.
- Gross and contribution margin by channel.
- CAC and payback period.
- Partner-sourced revenue.
- Sales-cycle length.
- Localization cost per active learner.
- Training and support cost per deployment.
- Government pipeline coverage.

### Reliability and trust

- Offline synchronization success.
- Data-loss incidents.
- Uptime and incident resolution.
- Consent and deletion completion.
- Privacy and safeguarding incidents.
- Accessibility defects and remediation time.
- Content complaint resolution time.
- Model error and subgroup disparity.

### Scale gate

A country or segment may scale only when it demonstrates:

1. Verified curriculum and procurement assumptions.
2. Human-reviewed and child-tested content.
3. Reliable language and accessibility performance.
4. No unresolved critical privacy or safeguarding issues.
5. Reliable operation on target devices and connectivity.
6. Meaningful learning improvement against a defined baseline or comparison.
7. Teacher usage appropriate to the deployment.
8. Acceptable assessment and recommendation quality.
9. Sustainable contribution margin after localization, training, support, infrastructure, partner, tax, compliance, and evaluation costs.
10. Renewal or expansion intent.
11. Funded content maintenance and local support.
12. Procurement readiness for the intended channel.

Targets must be set before each pilot. A 60–70% teacher-use level may be an internal hypothesis, not a universal standard.

## 10. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Unsupported curriculum or approval claim | Versioned claim register with source, scope, reviewer, and limitations |
| Curriculum or examination change | Monitoring, impact analysis, revalidation, maintenance budget |
| Poor translation or altered item difficulty | Local educators, controlled glossary, child testing, psychometric review |
| Arabic RTL or mixed-script failure | Dedicated RTL engineering and assistive-technology testing |
| Teacher rejection | Co-design, minimal setup, explanation, override, and training |
| Invalid adaptive recommendations | Local calibration, confidence reporting, subgroup monitoring, rollback |
| Safeguarding incident | Country playbooks, moderation, escalation, and partner obligations |
| Privacy or data-transfer breach | Deployment data map, legal review, minimization, access controls |
| Cybersecurity incident | Threat modeling, penetration testing, offline-device protection, disaster recovery |
| Offline/shared-device failure | Field testing, delayed synchronization, print, group, and teacher-led modes |
| Partner dependency | Multiple channels, nonexclusive contracts, exit and data-return rights |
| Unsustainable pricing | Full contribution-margin and affordability model |
| Government payment delays | Cash-flow planning and contractual protections |
| Cultural controversy | Local release authority, complaint process, rapid rollback |
| Accessibility failure | Country-specific testing and procurement evidence |
| Political or regulatory change | Scenario planning, pause triggers, exit options, and content rollback |
| Model drift | Monitoring, recalibration, release controls, and local validation |
| Content licensing dispute | Rights inventory and local legal review |
| Donor or subsidy discontinuity | Multiple funding routes and transition economics |
| Foreign-exchange or tax exposure | Currency scenarios and contract protections |
| Vendor lock-in | Data portability, alternate providers, and continuity planning |

## 11. Explicit assumptions

The strategy assumes:

1. The company can fund a configurable global core and local country capabilities concurrently.
2. Vietnam and Kenya offer suitable environments for bounded product-learning pilots; this must be validated.
3. Saudi Arabia and France require additional readiness work before execution pilots.
4. Government procurement may be slower and more documentation-intensive than private or partner channels; timing must be verified by country.
5. Curriculum and language alignment will require recurring local editorial and psychometric work.
6. Teacher adoption will be more predictive of institutional retention than learner engagement alone.
7. D2C can validate demand and payment behavior but will not substitute for institutional evidence.
8. Offline, print, and teacher-led delivery will improve both access and acceptance.
9. High-stakes language automation should remain deferred until local validity and fairness evidence is available.
10. Pricing must cover training, implementation, support, localization, compliance, and infrastructure—not software alone.
11. Local legal, procurement, accessibility, and data requirements will vary by customer and deployment, even within a country.
12. The company will stop or redesign a country initiative if learning value, trust, compliance, adoption, or economics fail agreed thresholds.

## 12. Key decisions required

Leadership should approve:

1. A configurable shared platform rather than country-specific forks.
2. A narrow initial scope focused on mathematics and literacy.
3. Bounded execution pilots in Vietnam and Kenya.
4. Discovery and compliance work for Saudi Arabia and France.
5. A maximum of two concurrent execution pilots without additional capacity approval.
6. Local country leads and editorial decision rights.
7. No unsupported curriculum, approval, endorsement, or regulatory claims.
8. No advertising or unrelated profiling using child data.
9. No high-stakes automated language scoring before local validity evidence.
10. Full cost-to-serve modeling before pricing or partner commitments.
11. Formal privacy, safeguarding, accessibility, cybersecurity, and assessment governance.
12. Standard pilot protocols with predefined stop and scale criteria.
13. A three-year investment plan with country budgets, owners, milestones, and stop-loss limits.
14. Completion of the evidence registers, procurement maps, RACI, staffing plan, and pilot protocols before execution.

The company should proceed with **bounded validation, not broad commercialization**. The winning model is shared adaptive-learning infrastructure with locally governed curriculum, language, assessment, cultural content, teacher and parent workflows, data stewardship, implementation, procurement, and pricing.
