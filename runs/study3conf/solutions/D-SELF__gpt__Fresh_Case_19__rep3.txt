# Smallholder Precision-Agriculture Product Strategy — 2026

## 1. Strategic recommendation

Do not export the existing large-farm sensor bundle into India, Kenya, and Vietnam. Launch a **managed, cluster-based advisory service** that combines limited local sensing, external weather and satellite data, farmer observations, and local field support.

The initial product should deliver **one validated, actionable decision per country**, rather than a broad precision-agriculture suite.

| Market | Initial beachhead | Primary payer | Primary farmer decision | Primary outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **India** | One irrigated, high-value horticulture crop in one state | FPO, aggregator, processor, or irrigation organization | Irrigate now, wait, or adjust only after validation | Pumping cost, water productivity, or quality |
| **Kenya** | Organized irrigated horticulture | Exporter, aggregator, cooperative, or service provider | Irrigate now/wait | Water productivity, quality, or avoided loss |
| **Vietnam** | One defined Mekong Delta water, flood, or salinity use case | Cooperative, processor, or exporter | Irrigation, flood, or salinity response | Yield stability, quality, or avoided risk |

A separate, lower-hardware weather-risk experiment may be tested in Kenya’s rainfed systems.

The exact state, crop, partner, payer, and price must be finalized before deployment. No country should begin with a national or multi-crop rollout.

---

# 2. Product proposition and target users

## Core proposition

> Convert sparse measurements and local context into one clear, explainable action delivered through trusted organizations and field agents.

Smallholders are unlikely to pay for sensors, dashboards, or raw data. The value proposition must instead be tied to:

- Lower irrigation or pumping costs
- Reduced weather, disease, flood, or salinity risk
- Better quality and fewer rejected deliveries
- More reliable supply planning
- Buyer compliance or traceability
- Timely agronomic support

## Users

### Farmers

Need:

- A specific action for a specific plot
- Local language and units
- Advice compatible with available equipment
- SMS, voice, WhatsApp, agent, or simple Android delivery
- A brief reason and confidence level
- Ability to accept, reject, or override advice

### Field agents and agronomists

Need:

- Offline plot and farmer registration
- Installation and calibration tools
- Sensor-health diagnostics
- Manual observation capture
- Recommendation explanation and escalation workflows

### Cooperatives and buyers

Need:

- Cluster and plot management
- Device and alert monitoring
- Farmer communication
- Crop-progress, quality, or irrigation reports
- Data exports and integrations

---

# 3. Focused MVP

Each pilot should include:

1. **One primary agronomic recommendation**
2. **One supporting weather or risk alert**
3. Sensor and service-health monitoring
4. Human technical or agronomic escalation
5. One primary and one fallback communication channel

Examples:

- “Irrigate Plot 2 today or wait” plus a heavy-rain alert
- “Spraying conditions are suitable or unsuitable” plus a wind/rain warning
- “Plant now or wait” plus a dry-spell alert

Traceability, quality monitoring, and supply forecasting should be separate buyer-facing modules, with separate pricing and data permissions.

---

# 4. Hardware simplification

## MVP hardware

### Cluster weather station

Deploy only where local data materially improves the recommendation:

- Air temperature
- Relative humidity
- Rainfall, where external data is inadequate
- Solar and battery monitoring
- Connectivity status
- Local storage for several weeks
- Weatherproof enclosure
- Remote diagnostics and configuration

### Representative soil-moisture station

Deploy only in irrigated blocks where farmers can change irrigation:

- One or two probes at crop-relevant root-zone depths
- Soil type, probe depth, crop, planting date, irrigation method, and GPS
- Installation photographs and calibration records
- Manual-reading fallback
- Replaceable modules

The cooperative, aggregator, or service provider should generally own or lease equipment during early pilots. This reduces farmer capital burden and simplifies maintenance and redeployment.

## Exclude from the MVP

- Generic real-time NPK sensors
- Dense multi-depth arrays
- Continuous imaging or cameras
- Autonomous pesticide recommendations
- Broad AI disease diagnosis
- Generic fertilizer-reduction recommendations
- Variable-rate prescriptions
- Machinery integration
- Unsupported high-resolution maps
- Complex farmer optimization dashboards
- Generic salinity or EC modules outside a validated Vietnamese pilot

## Required hardware specifications

Before procurement, define:

- Environmental protection rating
- Operating temperature and humidity
- Soil-moisture accuracy by soil class
- Calibration procedure and frequency
- Sampling and transmission intervals
- Battery reserve and solar sizing
- Offline storage capacity
- Expected operating life and failure-rate target
- Maximum replacement time
- Replacement-part cost
- Communications technology by geography
- Theft and tamper assumptions
- Firmware update and rollback process
- End-of-season recovery and redeployment

Compare three designs:

1. External weather data only  
2. Local weather station plus representative soil probes  
3. Higher-density sensing for premium blocks  

Choose according to incremental agronomic value versus installation and service cost.

# 5. Connectivity and user experience

Connectivity should be treated as intermittent rather than continuously available.

## Must-have capabilities

- Store-and-forward synchronization
- Local data logging for several weeks
- Low-power communication modes
- Bluetooth or short-range field synchronization
- Offline installation and diagnostics
- Manual observations
- Battery and signal-health alerts
- Data-freshness indicators
- Recommendation expiry
- Firmware updates that tolerate poor connectivity

A delayed “spray now” message must expire or be marked stale rather than delivered as current.

## Communication channels

Select one primary channel per pilot:

1. Lightweight Android app or WhatsApp  
2. SMS  
3. IVR or voice  
4. Cooperative, lead-farmer, or agent relay  

Budget for delivery failures, inactive phone numbers, SIM changes, shared devices, translations, voice support, and data costs. WhatsApp should not be assumed to be universally available.

## Alert design

Every alert should state:

- Affected plot and crop
- Recommended action
- Timing
- Brief reason
- Confidence or reliability
- What to do if action is not possible
- Expiry, where relevant

Avoid raw readings, unexplained color codes, repeated alerts, and advice requiring unavailable equipment.

Test comprehension by gender, age, literacy, phone access, and language. Users should be able to identify the plot, action, timing, reason, confidence, and fallback.

# 6. Agronomic and data-quality design

## Station representativeness

Define empirically:

- Maximum station-to-plot distance
- Acceptable soil-type similarity
- Acceptable crop and planting-date similarity
- Acceptable irrigation-method similarity
- Maximum plots or hectares per station
- Conditions requiring another sensor
- Conditions requiring advice to be withheld

A shared station must not be presented as precise for every plot in a cluster.

## Data-quality states

Use:

- **Good:** recent, complete, locally relevant data
- **Usable with caution:** partial, modeled, or cluster-level data
- **Insufficient:** withhold prescriptive advice or request review

Confidence should consider:

- Data freshness
- Sensor uptime
- Missing-data duration
- Implausible readings
- Calibration status
- Probe depth
- Sensor-to-plot distance
- Crop and growth stage
- Soil water-holding characteristics
- Recent rainfall and irrigation
- Forecast uncertainty
- Irrigation method

The system must distinguish measured data, modeled estimates, external weather, satellite data, farmer observations, and defaults.

## Initial analytics model

Use rule-based or hybrid models initially. For irrigation, combine:

- Soil moisture
- Root-zone depth
- Crop and growth stage
- Soil characteristics
- Recent irrigation
- Rainfall and forecast rain
- Heat and evapotranspiration conditions
- Irrigation method
- Data quality

Do not recommend specific irrigation volumes or durations until validated against local practice and crop outcomes.

## Safety classes

| Class | Examples | Control |
|---|---|---|
| **Low risk** | Weather observation, device-health alert | Automate when data is fresh |
| **Moderate risk** | Irrigate now/wait, spray-window advice | Require confidence, freshness, and applicability thresholds |
| **High risk** | Pesticide choice, fertilizer reduction, major irrigation cuts, consequential disease diagnosis | Require trained agronomist review |

Automatically downgrade or suspend recommendations after repeated sensor anomalies, excessive missing data, multiple error-indicating overrides, agronomist-reported conflicts, or a harmful incident.

# 7. Analytics priorities

## Must have now

- One primary crop-management recommendation
- One supporting weather or risk alert
- Sensor and service-health monitoring
- Confidence and data-quality indicators
- Delivery and expiry tracking
- Human technical or agronomic escalation

## Experiments

Only test where a customer funds the work or has a clear operational reason:

- Narrow disease-risk models
- Leaf-wetness sensing
- EC/salinity monitoring in defined zones
- Satellite crop-stage estimation
- Buyer crop-progress reporting
- Traceability
- Quality-risk prediction
- Supply-volume forecasting
- Voice-first advisory
- Shared irrigation coordination
- Higher-density sensing in premium blocks

## Defer or drop

- Generic real-time NPK
- Broad autonomous disease diagnosis
- Generic fertilizer-reduction advice
- High-resolution maps unsupported by measurements
- Continuous imaging
- Variable-rate prescriptions without compatible equipment
- Complex farmer dashboards
- Unvalidated yield or harvest forecasts
- Cross-farm benchmarking
- Carbon, credit, or insurance scoring
- Autonomous pesticide recommendations

# 8. Delivery partnerships

## B2B2F operating model

| Party | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Company** | Platform, analytics, hardware standards, model governance, security, training, second-line support |
| **Institutional buyer** | Farmer aggregation, payment or harvest deduction, crop-program coordination, farmer communication |
| **Local service partner** | Installation, calibration, field checks, first-line support, observations, replacement, escalation |
| **Farmer** | Provides plot and crop information, receives advice, gives feedback, accepts or overrides recommendations |

## Cooperatives and FPOs

Use where they have:

- Active members
- Geographic concentration
- Field staff
- Payment capability
- A clear member, water, buyer, or compliance benefit
- Basic governance and data discipline

## Input dealers

They can provide frequent contact, installation, referrals, and payment facilitation, but their input-sales incentives create risk.

Controls:

- No commission based on fertilizer or pesticide volume
- Pay for installation accuracy, data completeness, comprehension, service resolution, adoption, and renewal
- Independent agronomic escalation
- Audits of dealer communications
- No autonomous chemical advice
- Contractual remedies for poor installation or misrepresentation

## Required partner SLAs

Contracts must define:

- Hardware ownership
- Installation and calibration standards
- Replacement and theft liability
- Technician certification
- Critical-device response time
- Agronomic escalation response time
- Spare-parts requirements
- Complaint handling
- Data and consent duties
- Recommendation override procedures
- Renewal ownership
- Data access after termination

Also set maximum service radius, clusters per technician, farmer-to-agronomist ratios, and unresolved-alert limits.

# 9. Pricing and economics

## Commercial packages

### Managed cluster service

For cooperatives, aggregators, irrigation organizations, processors, and buyers:

- Shared hardware
- Installation and maintenance
- Connectivity
- Organization dashboard
- Farmer alerts
- Field-agent support
- Seasonal reporting

### Farmer advisory add-on

- Localized weather and crop alerts
- Basic irrigation or crop-calendar advice
- Optional soil-moisture recommendations

Charge per season or crop cycle, not monthly.

### Buyer value-chain module

- Traceability
- Quality-risk monitoring
- Crop progress
- Supply forecasting
- Compliance reporting

Price separately and obtain separate data permissions.

## Payment options

Test:

- Seasonal billing
- Per-hectare or crop-cycle pricing
- Harvest deductions
- Cooperative collection
- Mobile money in Kenya
- Buyer-funded service
- Farmer co-pay
- Hardware lease
- Bundling with agronomy, inputs, credit, or contracts

Free pilots, surveys, verbal interest, and app downloads do not establish demand. Require deposits, paid pilots, signed commitments, seasonal prepayments, or renewals.

## Required economic gates

Model by country and deployment configuration:

- Landed hardware cost
- Installation and calibration
- Connectivity
- Field-agent travel and compensation
- Agronomist support
- Partner commissions
- Spare parts
- Theft and replacement
- Payment collection
- Customer acquisition
- Revenue per cluster and hectare
- Gross margin after service
- Payback period
- Customer ROI
- Minimum cluster density
- Maximum service radius
- Company capital exposure

Set explicit thresholds for:

- Maximum installation cost per active hectare
- Maximum support cost per plot
- Minimum customer ROI
- Minimum paid renewal rate
- Maximum replacement rate
- Minimum hectares per cluster
- Maximum company capital exposure

# 10. Country-specific execution

## India

Begin with one state, one irrigated high-value crop, and one active FPO, aggregator, processor, or irrigation organization.

- Primary decision: irrigation scheduling
- Supporting alert: heavy rain or spray window
- Delivery: SMS and agent-mediated communication initially
- Payment: seasonal or harvest-linked
- Localization: state-specific language, units, crop calendar, and terminology

Do not assume national cooperative coverage or government procurement. Verify wireless, import, battery, e-waste, data, measurement, and agricultural-advice requirements before deployment.

## Kenya

### Irrigated horticulture

- Primary decision: irrigation scheduling
- Supporting alert: spray window or disease-favorable weather
- Payer: exporter, aggregator, cooperative, or service provider
- Channels: SMS, voice, or WhatsApp based on evidence
- Payment: mobile money or seasonal deduction

### Rainfed experiment

- Primary decision: planting-window or dry-spell alert
- Supporting alert: heavy-rain warning
- Start with external weather data
- Avoid soil sensors unless farmers can act on the information

Validate network coverage, technician travel, theft, replacement, and partner capacity by county. Mobile money improves collection but does not prove affordability.

## Vietnam

Choose one initial path:

### Mekong Delta

- Flood, irrigation, or salinity response
- Supporting rainfall or water-level alert
- Payer: cooperative, processor, or exporter

### Central Highlands

- Water management for one perennial crop
- Supporting narrow disease-risk or spray-window experiment

Do not launch both as one product. Use Vietnamese terminology, regional crop calendars, and local units. Deploy EC/salinity sensing only where it changes a validated decision. Treat traceability as a separately funded buyer module.

# 11. Phased execution

## Phase 0: Qualification — months 0–3

- Select crop, geography, payer, decision, and partner.
- Capture baseline irrigation, inputs, yields, prices, labor, and practices.
- Test connectivity, power, language, comprehension, and payment.
- Confirm farmers can act on the advice.
- Complete regulatory, privacy, and liability reviews.
- Agree renewal pricing before deployment.
- Set staffing, inventory, service-radius, and spare-parts budgets.

**Go/no-go:** concentrated farmer base, qualified partner, clear ROI hypothesis, feasible service radius, paid commitment or deposit, farmer ability to act, and acceptable regulatory pathway.

## Phase 1: Technical validation — months 3–6

- Deploy a small number of clusters.
- Compare weather-only and representative-sensor configurations.
- Validate station representativeness.
- Audit installation and calibration.
- Test offline synchronization and alert latency.
- Review recommendations with local agronomists.
- Test comprehension and fallback channels.
- Establish failure, replacement, and support-cost baselines.

## Phase 2: Full-season paid pilot — months 6–15

Use a predefined evaluation design covering:

- Sample size and statistical power
- Randomized, matched-cluster, or phased rollout methodology
- Treatment and comparison groups
- Baseline and endline measurement
- Spillover and weather-shock handling
- Missing-data rules
- Primary and secondary outcomes
- Independent analysis responsibility
- Safety thresholds and stopping rules

## Phase 3: Controlled replication — months 15–24

Expand only within validated crop-and-channel combinations. Add one major experiment at a time.

## Phase 4: Selective scale — month 24 onward

Scale only after paid renewal, reliable decision-period data, timely delivery, acceptable hardware and service economics, farmer comprehension, measurable value, partner execution, and compliance are demonstrated.

# 12. Success criteria

## Commercial

- Paid conversion
- Renewal rate
- Revenue per active hectare
- Gross margin after support
- Support cost per active plot
- Customer ROI
- Partner profitability
- Company capital exposure

## Reliability

- Usable data availability during decision periods
- Installation accuracy
- Device uptime
- Recommendation delivery latency
- Sensor failure and replacement rate
- Mean time to resolution
- Stale-message rate
- Offline synchronization success

“Usable data” means fresh, valid, decision-relevant data—not merely a connected device.

## Adoption and inclusion

- Recommendation comprehension
- Adoption rate
- Override rate and reasons
- Agent active use
- Repeat use
- Complaint rate and resolution time
- Farmer trust
- Intended decision-makers reached
- Comprehension by gender, age, literacy, and phone access
- Shared-device privacy incidents

## Outcomes

- Water use
- Pumping cost
- Yield-adjusted water productivity
- Yield
- Spray timing
- Disease incidence
- Quality grade
- Rejection rate
- Harvest timing
- Supply-forecast error
- Net farmer income
- Buyer operating savings

## Guardrails

- Harmful or false recommendations
- Crop-loss incidents plausibly linked to advice
- Unsafe pesticide-related events
- Stale advice delivered as current
- Privacy or consent violations
- Unauthorized data access
- Partner incentive violations
- Unresolved high-risk alerts

# 13. Principal risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Sparse sensors create false precision | Empirical representativeness rules, management zones, coverage display, confidence gating, advice withholding |
| Sensor drift or poor installation | Soil-specific calibration, installation photos, audits, plausibility checks, field replacement |
| Connectivity delays advice | Local storage, store-and-forward, SMS/voice fallback, expiry timestamps |
| Hardware failure, theft, or damage | Modular replacement, telemetry, partner asset ownership, spares, tamper controls |
| Advice reduces yield | Conservative thresholds, crop-stage rules, agronomist review, comparison plots, stopping rules |
| Disease advice drives unnecessary spraying | Narrow validation, spray-window guidance, no autonomous chemical advice |
| Input-dealer conflicts | Outcome-based commissions, independent review, communication audits |
| Cooperative cannot execute | Partner scorecards, certification, SLAs, phased rollout |
| Free pilots overstate demand | Paid pilots, deposits, signed commitments, pre-agreed renewal |
| Service costs exceed revenue | Cluster-density thresholds, service-radius limits, local technicians, cost tracking |
| Farmer data is misused | Local-language consent, minimization, role-based access, audit logs, secondary-use restrictions |
| Shared phones or gender exclusion | Multi-channel delivery, household workflows, gender testing, agent relay |
| Country variation overwhelms platform | Common hardware and data model; localized rules, language, units, and workflows |
| Regulatory delays | Complete telecom, import, environmental, data, cybersecurity, and advisory reviews before deployment |
| Harmful advice creates liability | Risk classes, escalation, incident response, logs, insurance review, contractual boundaries |
| Buyer interests conflict with farmers | Separate buyer and farmer permissions, transparent data use, farmer override, aggregated reporting |

---

# Final recommendation

Approve partner qualification, hardware prototyping, regulatory review, and preparation of narrow paid pilots.

Do not approve broad commercial launch until each country independently demonstrates that the company can:

1. Deliver usable data during critical decision periods.  
2. Provide timely, understandable, and safe recommendations.  
3. Maintain and replace hardware at acceptable cost.  
4. Operate through partners with enforceable service standards.  
5. Produce measurable farmer or buyer value.  
6. Convert that value into repeat payment.
