# Global Flexible-Work Policy

**Policy owner:** Global People/HR  
**Required partners:** Employment Legal, Country HR, Employee Relations, Privacy, Information Security, IT, Finance/Tax, Payroll, Operations, Client Services, and DEI/Accessibility  
**Applies to:** Employees of the company and its participating entities in twelve countries, subject to the limitations below  
**Effective date:** Only after country readiness approval and completion of required consultation  
**Review cycle:** At least annually, or sooner following a material legal, operational, client, or workforce change

> **Important limitation:** This is a global framework, not legal advice and not a substitute for country-specific requirements. The twelve countries, employing entities, worker categories, contracts, collective arrangements, and applicable client obligations must be confirmed before implementation.

---

## Executive summary

The company supports flexible work where it can be implemented lawfully, fairly, securely, and without compromising employee well-being, performance, client service, or business continuity.

Work models may differ by country, role, team, client, or individual circumstance. Differences must be based on documented, role-related, operational, legal, safety, security, or accommodation considerations—not physical visibility, cultural stereotypes, perceived loyalty, informal relationships, or managerial preference.

This policy establishes:

- Global minimum standards for fairness, sustainable working time, inclusion, privacy, performance, and non-retaliation;
- A role-based eligibility framework;
- Standard request, approval, documentation, review, and appeal processes;
- Safeguards for hybrid, remote, flexible-hours, compressed-week, reduced-hours, and temporary arrangements;
- Client-coverage and collaboration requirements;
- Global right-to-disconnect expectations;
- Controlled four-day-week pilots;
- Twelve country addenda and readiness gates; and
- Governance, metrics, audit, and remediation requirements.

**Mandatory local rule:** Applicable mandatory law and binding collective or employee-representation arrangements prevail. Contractual terms and local policies apply only to the extent lawful. Client or operational requirements may not reduce statutory, collective, contractual, privacy, safety, accommodation, or disconnect rights.

This policy does not create an automatic entitlement to remote work, hybrid work, compressed hours, reduced hours, a four-day week, a particular work location, or work outside the employing country.

# 1. Global principles

## 1.1 Fairness without requiring identical arrangements

Fairness does not require every employee or country to use the same work model. Comparable roles must be assessed against comparable criteria.

Different outcomes must be supported by one or more of the following:

- Physical, equipment, site, or role requirements;
- Client, service, or coverage obligations;
- Security, confidentiality, export-control, or regulatory requirements;
- Team collaboration or handoff needs;
- Health and safety considerations;
- Applicable law or binding employee-representation arrangements;
- A documented, current, role-related performance, conduct, or security issue; or
- A protected or reasonable accommodation.

“Visibility,” “loyalty,” “seriousness,” “seniority,” cultural preference, or a manager’s preference for office work are not sufficient reasons by themselves to deny flexibility or disadvantage an employee.

## 1.2 Outcome-based performance and career progression

Performance, compensation, promotion, and development decisions must be based on documented outcomes and behaviors, including:

- Delivery against agreed objectives;
- Quality, reliability, and customer outcomes;
- Technical or functional capability;
- Collaboration, communication, and knowledge sharing;
- Risk, security, and compliance;
- Judgment and problem-solving; and
- Leadership and development of others, where relevant.

The following must not be used as stand-alone evidence of performance or commitment:

- Physical presence;
- Online or “active” status;
- Camera use;
- Building-access data;
- Keystrokes or screen activity;
- Late-night messages;
- Immediate responses outside scheduled hours;
- Attendance at optional social events; or
- Informal access to senior leaders.

Managers must provide employees using approved remote, hybrid, compressed, reduced-hours, or non-standard schedules with comparable access to important projects, mentoring, sponsorship, training, feedback, recognition, and promotion information.

## 1.3 Sustainable working time

The company will not reward excessive hours, routine after-hours responsiveness, skipped breaks, or reduced rest.

Managers must address persistent overtime, fatigue, or out-of-hours work as a staffing, workload, planning, or coverage issue. Flexible arrangements must not be used to conceal unpaid overtime or assign a full-time workload to a reduced-hours employee.

## 1.4 Inclusion and accommodation

Flexible-work practices must not disadvantage employees because of disability, health condition, pregnancy, caregiving, religion, gender, age, race, nationality, socioeconomic status, or another protected characteristic.

Requests connected to disability, health, pregnancy, caregiving, religious observance, domestic safety, or another protected basis must also be assessed through the applicable accommodation or statutory process. Medical and other sensitive information must be handled confidentially; managers should receive only information necessary to implement the arrangement.

## 1.5 Privacy and proportionate monitoring

The company will not use intrusive productivity surveillance as a substitute for effective management.

Any monitoring must have:

- A defined and legitimate purpose;
- An appropriate legal basis;
- Transparency and required notices;
- Proportionate scope;
- Access controls;
- Retention limits; and
- Required privacy, employment-law, impact-assessment, and employee-representation reviews.

New monitoring, attendance, location, productivity, or communications tools require Privacy and Legal approval before deployment.

## 1.6 Non-retaliation

Retaliation is prohibited against anyone who:

- Requests or uses approved flexibility;
- Exercises a right to disconnect;
- Requests an accommodation;
- Raises concerns about bias, excessive hours, or unfair treatment;
- Reports a safety, security, privacy, or coverage issue; or
- Participates in a pilot, audit, investigation, or survey.

# 2. Work-pattern options

Availability depends on local law, role, team, client, security, and operational requirements.

## 2.1 Primarily office- or site-based

Work is normally performed at a designated office, site, laboratory, secure facility, or customer location.

This may be appropriate where work requires:

- Specialized equipment;
- Secure or regulated facilities;
- Physical infrastructure;
- Frequent on-site customer interaction;
- Physical operations; or
- Location-specific security or regulatory controls.

Office- or site-based employees must receive predictable schedules and reasonable flexibility in start, finish, or shift times where operationally feasible.

## 2.2 Hybrid

Work is performed through a planned combination of office and remote days.

Teams may establish minimum in-person expectations only where they can explain the specific purpose, such as collaboration, onboarding, training, client work, security, equipment access, or defined relationship-building.

Recurring office requirements must be documented, proportionate, reviewed by HR, and not broader than necessary. Optional social attendance must not affect performance, promotion, sponsorship, or recognition.

## 2.3 Primarily remote

Work is performed remotely for most scheduled days from an approved location in the employing country.

Periodic office attendance may be required for defined activities, provided the expectation is reasonable, communicated in advance, and consistent with the employee’s approved arrangement and local requirements.

## 2.4 Flexible hours

The employee works their ordinary contractual hours but may vary start, finish, or break times within agreed limits.

The arrangement must specify:

- Permitted working windows;
- Core collaboration hours;
- Coverage requirements;
- Meeting expectations;
- Rest and overtime controls;
- Time-recording requirements, where applicable; and
- The process for urgent matters.

## 2.5 Compressed full-time schedule

The employee works the same contractual hours over fewer days, such as four longer days or a nine-day fortnight.

This model may be approved only after legal, safety, workload, and operational review. It must not:

- Breach maximum daily or weekly hours;
- Remove legally required breaks or rest;
- Create unsafe fatigue levels;
- Shift unreasonable work to colleagues;
- Reduce required client or operational coverage; or
- Conceal unpaid overtime.

## 2.6 Reduced-hours or part-time schedule

The employee works fewer contractual hours. Compensation, benefits, leave, workload, bonus treatment, and promotion implications must be documented in accordance with applicable requirements.

Workload must be proportionate to contracted hours. Employees must not be assigned full-time deliverables within part-time hours.

## 2.7 Temporary flexibility

Temporary flexibility may address health, caregiving, family emergencies, severe weather, transport disruption, religious observance, study, business continuity, or another approved need.

The arrangement must state its duration or review date unless a separate accommodation or statutory process applies. Accommodation or statutory arrangements must not be automatically terminated merely because a general flexibility review date has passed.

# 3. Eligibility and approval levels

## Level 1: Presumptively available

Subject to local law, role suitability, team coverage, and documented objective requirements:

- Flexible start and finish times;
- Occasional remote work;
- Hybrid work for remote-capable roles;
- Meeting-free focus periods;
- Predictable scheduling;
- Temporary flexibility; and
- Shift choice or shift-swapping for suitable office- or site-based roles.

A Level 1 arrangement may be declined only for a documented, current, role-related reason. A general or subjective concern about visibility is not sufficient.

## Level 2: Controlled approval

Requires documented assessment and may require review by HR, Legal, Tax, Security, Finance, Payroll, or employee representatives:

- Compressed workweeks;
- Reduced-hours arrangements;
- Regular work from a location different from the designated worksite;
- Non-standard client-facing schedules;
- Changes affecting shifts, on-call, or critical coverage; and
- Temporary work outside the employing country.

## Level 3: Restricted or unavailable for defined roles

A work model may be restricted where objectively required by:

- Secure or regulated work;
- Physical infrastructure or equipment;
- Customer-site obligations;
- Production, support, or incident-response coverage;
- Data, export-control, or location restrictions;
- Health and safety requirements; or
- Applicable law or binding collective arrangements.

Restrictions must be recorded in the role eligibility framework, applied consistently, reviewed at least annually, and limited to the affected role or activity.

# 4. Employee request and decision process

## 4.1 Request

Employees must use the applicable local process. A request should identify:

- Proposed work model;
- Proposed days, hours, and location;
- Duration and review date;
- Expected collaboration and client impact;
- Coverage and handoff arrangements;
- Equipment, accessibility, or safety needs; and
- Proposed handling of urgent work.

Employees must not be required to provide unnecessary medical or personal information. Accommodation requests follow the confidential accommodation process.

## 4.2 Assessment

Managers must assess requests against the same criteria:

1. Legal and contractual feasibility;
2. Role, equipment, and site requirements;
3. Client and service impact;
4. Team coverage and collaboration;
5. Security and confidentiality;
6. Health and safety;
7. Workload and handoffs;
8. Equality and potential disparate impact;
9. Cost or operational impact; and
10. Less restrictive alternatives.

A performance-based restriction is permitted only where:

- The concern is current and documented against established objectives;
- The employee has been informed and given an opportunity to respond;
- The concern is unrelated to the request or a protected characteristic; and
- There is a demonstrated connection between the proposed arrangement and the identified risk.

HR must review such decisions.

## 4.3 Decision

The decision must be written and specify:

- What is approved, modified, or declined;
- Days, hours, location, and coverage;
- Performance and communication expectations;
- Duration and review date;
- Required legal, tax, security, payroll, or employee-representation approvals; and
- The reason for the decision.

A refusal must identify a concrete legal, role-related, safety, security, client, coverage, or evidence-based performance reason. Alternatives considered and, where relevant, why they were not feasible must be documented.

Unless local requirements provide a different timeframe:

- Urgent temporary requests should be handled promptly;
- Ordinary domestic requests should normally be decided within 20 business days after the minimum required information is received;
- Accommodation requests follow the applicable accommodation timeline; and
- Cross-border, contractual, or collective arrangements require a risk-based timeline communicated to the employee.

Employees must not experience unreasonable delay through repeated requests for unnecessary information.

## 4.4 Review, amendment, and appeal

Arrangements should be reviewed after three months and at least annually, or earlier if circumstances materially change.

An arrangement may be amended or withdrawn only for a documented reason, with required notice, consultation, and employee-representation involvement. It may not be changed as retaliation or solely because of a manager’s preference.

Employees may appeal through HR, Employee Relations, Ethics/Compliance, a works council, union, employee representative, or applicable local process.

# 5. Employee and manager standards

## 5.1 Manager standards

Managers must:

- Apply the eligibility framework consistently;
- Explain the operational purpose of attendance or coverage requirements;
- Set measurable objectives and proportionate workloads;
- Maintain a written team working agreement;
- Plan coverage, backup ownership, and handoffs;
- Avoid proximity, affinity, cultural, caregiver, and disability bias;
- Protect rest and disconnect time;
- Provide comparable information and development opportunities;
- Document decisions and performance evidence;
- Complete required training;
- Consult HR for Level 2 and Level 3 arrangements; and
- Escalate legal, safety, security, client, workload, or fairness concerns.

Manager objectives must include effective management of distributed and flexible teams.

## 5.2 Employee standards

Employees must:

- Meet agreed objectives and quality standards;
- Work during agreed hours and maintain required coverage;
- Keep calendars and availability information reasonably current;
- Attend required meetings or provide timely input;
- Complete handoffs and communicate risks or delays;
- Follow security, privacy, health, and safety requirements;
- Complete required time records;
- Respect colleagues’ working hours and disconnect periods;
- Maintain an approved work location; and
- Obtain approval before materially changing the arrangement or location.

Flexible work does not permit outside employment during scheduled company hours, undisclosed conflicts of interest, or unapproved work from another country.

---

# 6. Visibility, performance, and career progression

Teams must use remote-accessible systems for substantive work, information, and decisions. Hybrid meetings must provide equivalent participation through shared documents, accessible collaboration tools, remote-accessible whiteboards, and documented decisions.

Substantive decisions must not be made solely through informal office conversations when affected employees are excluded.

Performance reviews must use behaviorally defined criteria, multiple sources of evidence where appropriate, calibration, and documented outcomes. Communication and leadership criteria must not be proxies for physical presence or cultural conformity.

Promotion and talent-review panels must review, where lawfully possible:

- Performance ratings;
- Promotion and time-to-promotion;
- Access to high-value assignments;
- Sponsorship and mentoring;
- Leadership exposure;
- Recognition; and
- Work-model and location patterns.

Career conversations and important development opportunities should be scheduled during working time and must not systematically exclude employees because of an approved non-working day or schedule.

---

# 7. Team collaboration and visibility standards

Each team must maintain a written working agreement covering:

- Normal working windows by location;
- Core collaboration hours;
- Office-presence expectations and purpose;
- Meeting-free periods;
- Time-zone boundaries;
- Response-time standards;
- Handoffs and backup ownership;
- Communication channels;
- Documentation requirements;
- Incident and urgent-contact procedures;
- Client coverage;
- Public-holiday coverage; and
- Review date.

Core hours must be limited to the period genuinely needed for collaboration and must respect local working-time, rest, religious, accessibility, caregiving, and disconnect requirements.

Teams must:

- Use agendas and defined outcomes;
- Prefer asynchronous work where practical;
- Record decisions, owners, and deadlines;
- Rotate inconvenient meeting times;
- Avoid recurring meetings outside reasonable local hours;
- Provide accessible materials and alternatives; and
- Protect focus time.

Absence outside core hours must not be treated as a lack of commitment.

---

# 8. Client and collaboration requirements

Each client-facing or critical-service team must maintain a coverage plan stating:

- Required coverage hours;
- Service-level commitments;
- Primary and backup owners;
- Time-zone handoffs;
- On-call and incident procedures;
- Public-holiday coverage;
- Escalation contacts;
- Minimum staffing levels; and
- Client communication responsibilities.

Client preference for office attendance is not, by itself, sufficient to deny flexibility. The client owner must document the precise contractual, service, security, regulatory, or relationship requirement and assess whether rotation, handoff, or regional coverage can meet it.

Client communications must describe role coverage only and must not disclose an employee’s medical, caregiving, or other personal information without appropriate authority and consent.

Critical roles may require shifts, on-call duties, office or site presence, or restrictions on compressed schedules. Such requirements must be stated in role materials, team agreements, and approval decisions.

Client coverage must be achieved through planned, lawful, recorded, and appropriately compensated staffing. It must not depend on unrecorded or unpaid availability.

---

# 9. Right to disconnect

The company supports employees’ right to disconnect from routine work communications outside scheduled working time.

Employees are generally not required to:

- Read or respond to routine messages outside scheduled hours;
- Remain continuously online;
- Keep work devices active overnight;
- Attend non-essential meetings outside normal hours; or
- Demonstrate commitment through after-hours activity.

Managers should use delayed delivery, quiet-hour, and scheduled-send tools where available. Response standards apply within agreed working windows unless the employee is assigned to formally approved on-call or emergency coverage.

## 9.1 Exceptions

Exceptions may include:

- Critical incidents;
- Security or safety events;
- Business-continuity events;
- Approved on-call duties; or
- Contractually defined client coverage.

Exceptions must be:

- Necessary and limited;
- Planned where possible;
- Assigned through lawful rotations;
- Recorded;
- Compensated or balanced with time off where required;
- Subject to required consent or consultation; and
- Reviewed after recurring or material events.

“Emergency” must be defined in the applicable country or team procedure. Client pressure must not automatically override disconnect rights. No employee may be disadvantaged for declining voluntary out-of-hours coverage where refusal is legally protected or permitted.

# 10. Four-day-week and compressed-work pilots

## 10.1 No global entitlement

A four-day schedule is not automatically available in every country, role, or team. It may be introduced only after country readiness approval and operational design.

## 10.2 Pilot formats

Pilots may include:

- Compressed full-time hours;
- Reduced-hours work over four days;
- A nine-day fortnight;
- Team-based pilots; or
- Staggered non-working days.

The company must clearly distinguish compressed full-time hours from reduced hours because pay, benefits, leave, overtime, and legal treatment may differ.

## 10.3 Required safeguards

Every pilot must define:

- Eligible roles and employees;
- Working hours, breaks, rest, and maximum daily duration;
- Fatigue and health-and-safety controls;
- Workload redesign;
- Coverage, backup, and handoffs;
- Client and incident arrangements;
- Overtime and time recording;
- Leave, public-holiday, pay, and benefit treatment;
- Training and employee communications;
- Duration, normally three to six months;
- Baseline or comparison measures;
- Success and failure criteria; and
- Modification, pause, withdrawal, or conclusion procedures.

A pilot must not compress five days of deliverables into four days.

Employees excluded from a pilot should receive an equivalence assessment and, where feasible, alternative flexibility such as predictable schedules, shift choice, shift-swapping, flexible start and finish times, or equivalent time-off opportunities. Equivalence must consider predictability, control over time, total time off, compensation, workload, and career impact.

Pilot withdrawal or modification must follow applicable notice, consultation, contractual, and employee-representation requirements.

# 11. Fairness and inclusion safeguards

HR must monitor whether flexible-work practices create disproportionate disadvantage for:

- Women and caregivers;
- Employees with disabilities or chronic health conditions;
- Early-career employees;
- Lower-paid employees lacking suitable home-work resources;
- Employees in office- or site-based roles;
- Employees working compressed or non-standard schedules;
- Employees in countries with different holidays or cultural expectations; and
- Underrepresented groups.

Controls include:

- Published role-based eligibility criteria;
- HR review of recurring denial patterns;
- Objective reasons for office requirements;
- Equal access to information, development, sponsorship, and promotion;
- Proportionate workloads;
- Accessible tools, documents, meetings, offices, and equipment;
- An office or other suitable alternative where home work is impractical;
- Confidential accommodation procedures; and
- Regular audits of informal practices, including meetings, assignments, sponsorship, office conversations, and exceptions.

Data must be collected only where lawful, voluntarily where required, privacy-protected, aggregated with appropriate minimum group sizes, and supplemented with qualitative methods where demographic analysis is restricted.

---

# 12. Remote work, safety, technology, and security

Employees must use approved devices, identity controls, networks, applications, and data-handling practices. They must protect confidential information from household members, public spaces, unauthorized parties, and insecure networks.

The company will provide or reimburse equipment, connectivity, ergonomic support, or other costs where required by local law or approved country policy.

Employees must report safety, security, privacy, or work-related injury incidents promptly.

Each country addendum must address:

- Home-work risk assessments;
- Employer and employee health-and-safety responsibilities;
- Equipment and expense reimbursement;
- Insurance and work-related injury;
- Privacy of the home;
- Any inspection or certification requirements; and
- Accessible alternatives for employees unable to work safely from home.

---

# 13. Working from another country or location

Employees may not work from another country without prior written approval from HR, Employment Legal, Tax, Payroll, Security, and the relevant manager.

Approval must separately assess:

- Immigration and work authorization;
- Payroll withholding and social security;
- Tax and establishment risk;
- Employment-law exposure;
- Insurance and workers’ compensation;
- Data protection and localization;
- Export controls;
- Client restrictions;
- Security requirements; and
- Duration and permitted location.

Business travel, temporary work while traveling, relocation, and work from a third country are separate categories and must not be approved through a generic remote-work request.

# 14. Country adaptation process

Each country must have an approved addendum before any work model is activated.

## 14.1 Required country addendum content

Each addendum must state:

1. Employing entity and covered worker categories;
2. Contractual and applicable working time;
3. Maximum hours, breaks, rest, overtime, and time recording;
4. Available, restricted, or unavailable work models;
5. Four-day and compressed-schedule requirements;
6. Reduced-hours and part-time treatment;
7. Right-to-disconnect requirements;
8. Remote-work health, safety, equipment, and expense rules;
9. Privacy and monitoring requirements;
10. Public-holiday and leave treatment;
11. On-call, night, weekend, and client-coverage rules;
12. Cross-border work restrictions;
13. Accommodation and protected-leave procedures;
14. Contract, payroll, pay, and benefits implications;
15. Union, works-council, or employee-representative requirements;
16. Local-language notice requirements;
17. Appeal and complaint channels; and
18. Effective date, owner, version, legal review date, and next review date.

## 14.2 Country readiness gate

Before implementation, the country must confirm:

- Legal review by appropriately qualified counsel;
- Working-time, overtime, rest, and time-recording compliance;
- Right-to-disconnect assessment;
- Privacy and monitoring assessment;
- Health-and-safety process;
- Tax, payroll, immigration, and establishment review;
- Contract and benefits review;
- Equality, accommodation, and leave review;
- Required consultation, negotiation, notification, or approval;
- Client and operational coverage;
- Local-language and accessible communications;
- Manager training;
- Employee complaint and appeal routes; and
- Baseline metrics and reporting capability.

The central register must maintain current and archived versions of every country addendum. Material changes require renewed review where applicable.

# 15. Implementation roadmap

## Phase 1: Discovery and validation

- Confirm the twelve countries, entities, worker categories, and work locations;
- Map roles by physical, client, security, equipment, and coverage requirements;
- Inventory contracts and collective arrangements;
- Complete country readiness reviews;
- Identify cultural and employee-experience risks through consultation and listening;
- Establish baseline operational, workload, career, and equity data; and
- Classify roles under Levels 1–3.

## Phase 2: Design

Prepare:

- Country addenda;
- Role eligibility matrices;
- Request and decision forms;
- Responsibility and approval matrix;
- Team working-agreement templates;
- Client coverage templates;
- Four-day pilot protocols;
- Accommodation referral procedures;
- Security, accessibility, and home-work guidance;
- Appeal and escalation routes; and
- Local-language communications.

## Phase 3: Training and communication

Managers must complete training before approving Level 2 arrangements or participating in pilots. Training covers:

- Local working-time and disconnect requirements;
- Outcome-based performance;
- Proximity, cultural, caregiver, and disability bias;
- Inclusive scheduling;
- Workload and fatigue;
- Accommodation confidentiality;
- Client coverage;
- Privacy and monitoring;
- Documentation; and
- Escalation.

Employees must receive clear information about available models, eligibility, performance expectations, disconnect rights, security duties, appeals, and local requirements.

## Phase 4: Controlled pilots

Pilots must have:

- Legal and employee-representation approval;
- Defined eligibility;
- Workload redesign;
- Operational and client safeguards;
- Employee and manager training;
- Baseline or comparison measures;
- Equity monitoring; and
- A defined continuation, modification, or conclusion decision.

## Phase 5: Evaluation and scale

At the end of each pilot and at least annually, the company will review legal, employee-relations, operational, workload, client, career, and equity outcomes. The Council may continue, modify, expand, pause, or end an arrangement, subject to required notice and consultation.

## Crisis flexibility

The company must maintain a separate crisis-flexibility procedure for severe weather, office closure, public-health events, cyber incidents, or other emergencies. It must define activation authority, duration, communications, security controls, working-time safeguards, staffing, and post-event review.

# 16. Governance, exceptions, and accountability

## 16.1 Global Flexible Work Council

The Council includes representatives from:

- Global and country HR;
- Employment Legal and Employee Relations;
- Privacy and Information Security;
- IT;
- Finance, Tax, and Payroll;
- Operations and Client Services;
- DEI and Accessibility; and
- Employee representatives where appropriate.

The Council will:

- Oversee this framework and country readiness;
- Review legal and regulatory changes;
- Approve or oversee pilots;
- Review equity, performance, workload, and service metrics;
- Monitor exceptions, appeals, and reversals;
- Set mandatory manager-training standards;
- Resolve cross-country inconsistencies; and
- Publish material policy changes.

## 16.2 Responsibility and approval matrix

The company must maintain a current matrix identifying:

- Who may approve each work model;
- Mandatory reviewers;
- Decision timelines;
- Who may amend or withdraw an arrangement;
- Escalation points; and
- Required consultation or employee consent.

Routine domestic Level 1 requests should not require unnecessary Tax, Legal, Security, or Finance review. Additional review is required only where defined risk triggers apply.

## 16.3 Exceptions

Exceptions must be:

- Legally reviewed where required;
- Approved at the appropriate level;
- Written and time-limited where practicable;
- Based on a documented reason;
- Reviewed for consistency and disparate impact; and
- Recorded in the central exception register.

Managers may not create informal, undocumented, or relationship-based exceptions.

---

# 17. Metrics and reporting

Global HR will maintain a balanced scorecard, subject to local privacy requirements.

## Access and fairness

- Approval, modification, and denial rates;
- Reasons for denial;
- Access by country, role, level, work model, and pay band;
- Access to equivalent flexibility for office- or site-based roles;
- Appeals, reversals, and exceptions; and
- Informal office-presence patterns.

## Career equity

- Performance ratings;
- Promotion and time-to-promotion;
- Compensation outcomes;
- High-value assignments;
- Leadership exposure;
- Mentoring and sponsorship;
- Recognition; and
- Attrition by work model and legally permitted demographic categories.

## Workload and well-being

- Overtime and time-recording issues;
- After-hours communications;
- Rest-period concerns;
- Workload and burnout measures;
- Absence and health-related trends;
- Schedule predictability; and
- Ability to disconnect.

## Business and client outcomes

- Delivery against commitments;
- Quality and defect rates;
- Customer satisfaction;
- Response and resolution times;
- Incident response and recovery;
- Coverage failures;
- Appropriate productivity or cycle-time measures; and
- Office, travel, and remote-work costs.

## Legal, safety, privacy, and security

- Working-time violations;
- Disconnect complaints;
- Safety incidents;
- Security and privacy incidents;
- Unapproved cross-border work;
- Client-service failures;
- Consultation completion;
- Addendum status; and
- Exception and remediation status.

Each metric must have an owner, reporting frequency, privacy controls, and defined escalation or remediation criteria. Unexplained disparities, repeated complaints, or material service or safety failures require investigation and corrective action.

Aggregated global and relevant local findings should be shared with employees and representatives where lawful and appropriate. Low complaint volumes must not automatically be interpreted as low risk.

# 18. Local legal-validation checklist

Before issuing or implementing this policy or a country addendum, confirm:

### Jurisdiction and workforce
- [ ] All twelve countries and employing entities are identified.
- [ ] Covered employee, agency, intern, secondee, contractor, and subsidiary populations are defined.
- [ ] Contracts, handbooks, collective agreements, and existing local practices are reviewed.
- [ ] Applicable work-location rules are identified.

### Working time
- [ ] Ordinary contractual and applicable statutory working time is confirmed.
- [ ] Maximum daily and weekly hours are confirmed.
- [ ] Breaks, daily and weekly rest, overtime, night work, weekend work, and public-holiday rules are confirmed.
- [ ] Time-recording requirements are confirmed.
- [ ] On-call and standby requirements are confirmed.

### Flexible patterns
- [ ] Flexible-hours arrangements are legally and operationally validated.
- [ ] Hybrid and remote-work requirements are confirmed.
- [ ] Compressed and four-day schedules are reviewed for hours, breaks, fatigue, consent, contract changes, consultation, leave, pay, and benefits.
- [ ] Reduced-hours and part-time treatment is confirmed.
- [ ] Pilot withdrawal, notice, and return-to-standard arrangements are documented.

### Right to disconnect
- [ ] Applicable statutory or contractual disconnect requirements are confirmed.
- [ ] Emergency and on-call exceptions are defined.
- [ ] Compensation, time off, recording, consent, and consultation requirements are confirmed.
- [ ] Technical quiet-hour and escalation controls are available.

### Employee representation
- [ ] Unions, works councils, and employee representatives are identified.
- [ ] Required information, consultation, negotiation, approval, or notification is completed.
- [ ] Existing collective or representative arrangements are not overridden improperly.

### Privacy and technology
- [ ] Employee-data and monitoring requirements are confirmed.
- [ ] Required privacy notices, impact assessments, and consultations are completed.
- [ ] Data transfer, retention, access, and security controls are approved.
- [ ] Accessibility of digital tools and communications is validated.

### Health, safety, and expenses
