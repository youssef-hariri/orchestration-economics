# 2026 African Vaccine Cold-Chain Distribution Strategy

## 1. Executive summary

The firm should operate a **lane-qualified, quality-led hub-and-corridor network** rather than a single continental distribution model.

The strategy differentiates routes by customs performance, road access, electricity reliability, security, seasonal disruption, storage readiness, and ministry capacity.

### Strategic decisions

1. **Use validated passive packaging by default** for cross-border, long-haul, uncertain, security-sensitive, and remote last-mile movements.
2. **Concentrate powered refrigeration** at qualified regional hubs, national stores, and selected domestic trunk routes with reliable fuel, maintenance, backup power, telemetry, and recovery capacity.
3. **Use airfreight selectively** for urgent, high-value, short-shelf-life, insecure, or seasonally inaccessible shipments.
4. **Pre-position inventory before predictable access deterioration**, while controlling expiry, theft, power, and capital exposure.
5. **Authorize routine distribution lane by lane**, only after regulatory, engineering, pilot, commercial, security, ministry, and seasonal gates are passed.
6. **Keep quality authority independent of logistics operations.** A temperature alarm triggers protection, quarantine, and investigation—not automatic release or destruction.
7. Treat customs, ministry receiving, storage, security, monitoring, and recovery as integral parts of the cold chain.

The primary network should use:

- **Accra and Dakar** as initial West African control-hub candidates.
- **Nairobi** as the principal Northern Corridor control and consolidation hub.
- **Dar es Salaam** as the Central Corridor import and staging hub.
- **Kigali, Entebbe/Kampala, Addis Ababa, Mombasa, Abidjan, Lagos, Lomé, Cotonou, Bamako, and Ouagadougou** as conditional country-serving or forward nodes, subject to qualification.

# 2. Scope and operating assumptions

The product label, approved stability data, and national regulatory requirements govern every shipment.

## Product categories

Separate procedures are required for:

| Product type | Primary logistics implication |
|---|---|
| +2°C to +8°C vaccines | Passive packaging for most road and last-mile movements; powered storage at qualified hubs |
| Frozen vaccines | Dedicated frozen packaging, dry ice or active refrigeration, and specialized airport/storage controls |
| Ultra-cold products | Specialist active or passive systems, redundant power, limited routes, and high-control airfreight |
| Controlled-room-temperature products | Permitted only where explicitly authorized by product stability data |

No product may be transported, refrozen, relabeled, or released under a different condition merely for operational convenience.

## Product master

Every SKU must have a controlled product record containing:

- Approved temperature range.
- Freeze sensitivity and freeze-event rules.
- Authorized heat-excursion limits.
- Light, vibration, and handling restrictions.
- Shelf life and minimum remaining shelf life by destination.
- Approved pack-out and payload.
- Dry-ice and dangerous-goods requirements.
- Manufacturer-approved excursion decision rules.
- Batch-release and import requirements.
- Ownership and regulatory status during transit.

## 3.3 Node definitions

| Node | Function | Minimum requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Regional control hub | Visibility, routing, escalation, governance | 24/7 or campaign-period control capability |
| Regional inventory hub | Qualified stockholding and consolidation | Mapped storage, backup power, monitoring, quality oversight |
| National store | Authorized country-level storage | Regulatory approval and ministry acceptance |
| Forward store | Planned stock near difficult destinations | Qualified storage, security, power, FEFO controls |
| Cross-dock | Temporary controlled transfer | Approved handling process; not automatically a storage site |
| Recovery site | Emergency storage during disruption | Qualified capacity and rapid access |

Regional control or quality coordination does not replace destination-country import, batch-release, or regulatory authority.

# 4. Corridor sequencing and operating models

## 4.1 West Africa

### W1: Coastal and urban cross-border corridors

Examples:

- Accra–Lomé–Cotonou–Lagos.
- Abidjan–Accra.
- Dakar–Banjul.
- Other coastal country-to-country movements.

#### Risk profile

- Urban and port congestion.
- Border queues and inspections.
- Heat exposure during stationary periods.
- Weekend and holiday closures.
- Importer, permit, or consignee errors.
- Multiple handoffs between brokers, ministries, and facilities.

#### Operating model

- Use validated passive packaging for cross-border shipments.
- Use active refrigerated transport only on qualified domestic, high-volume lanes.
- Schedule border arrival during staffed operating hours.
- Obtain broker confirmation before dispatch.
- Use sealed and tracked vehicles.
- Keep qualified bonded cold storage near major ports and borders.
- Avoid unrelated mixed cargo where it may trigger inspection.

#### Preferred contingency

If dwell threatens thermal duration, remaining shelf life, or campaign timing:

1. Move to qualified bonded storage.
2. Replace packaging if authorized.
3. Reroute through an approved border.
4. Switch to airfreight where justified.
5. Quarantine if product conditions or evidence are compromised.

---

### W2: Coastal-to-Sahel corridors

Examples:

- Tema/Accra–Ouagadougou.
- Abidjan–Bamako.
- Dakar–Bamako.
- Lomé–Ouagadougou.
- Cotonou–Niamey.

#### Risk profile

- Long road legs and high ambient temperatures.
- Border and customs variability.
- Security-related delays.
- Weak inland cold storage and electricity.
- Rain-related road deterioration.
- Sparse breakdown and recovery services.

#### Operating model

- Make route-qualified passive packaging the default.
- Calculate duration using P95 transit, P95 customs dwell, handling, recovery, and contingency time.
- Use smaller, more frequent consignments where large loads increase dwell or expiry exposure.
- Pre-position stock at qualified inland facilities before access deterioration.
- Use active reefers only on proven domestic lanes with fuel, maintenance, secure parking, and recovery support.
- Prohibit unqualified transloading.
- Use airfreight for urgent, high-value, short-shelf-life, or security-constrained shipments.

#### Key tradeoff

Forward stock reduces access and stockout risk but increases expiry, theft, power, and financing exposure. Airfreight reduces road and border risk but increases cost, airport handling, dangerous-goods, and flight-disruption dependencies.

---

### W3: Fragile or high-security Sahel corridors

Examples include selected destinations in:

- Niger.
- Northern Mali.
- Burkina Faso.
- Chad.
- Remote border or campaign areas.

#### Operating model

- Make current security approval a hard dispatch gate.
- Pre-position stock before access deteriorates.
- Use airfreight to the nearest functional airport for priority movements.
- Use long-duration validated passive systems for final road legs.
- Use ministry-approved forward caches.
- Require secure parking, driver check-ins, and communications redundancy.
- Audit local delivery partners before use.
- Define approved stop, hold, and recovery points.

If the route cannot reliably reach a qualified recovery site, the shipment should not be dispatched regardless of packaging duration.

---

## 4.2 East Africa

### E1: Northern Corridor

Examples:

- Mombasa–Nairobi–Kampala.
- Mombasa–Nairobi–Kigali.
- Uganda-linked Great Lakes routes.
- Kenya–Uganda–South Sudan routes where authorized.

#### Risk profile

- Strong commercial freight capacity.
- Border variability at Malaba, Busia, and other crossings.
- Road works, truck congestion, and breakdown risk.
- Different import, batch-release, and ministry procedures.
- Additional handling risk if shipments are consolidated in Nairobi.

#### Operating model

- Use Nairobi as the qualified control and consolidation hub.
- Use active reefers only on proven domestic trunk lanes.
- Use passive packaging for border and final-mile movements.
- Use electronic seals, GPS, and temperature monitoring.
- Validate each border pair separately.
- Maintain qualified recovery sites near major borders and cities.
- Assign a named custodian at every handoff.

Consolidation should be used only where the control benefit exceeds the added handling and dwell risk.

---

### E2: Central Corridor

Examples:

- Dar es Salaam–Dodoma.
- Dar es Salaam–Kigoma.
- Western Tanzania.
- Routes toward Rwanda, Burundi, Zambia, and the Great Lakes.

#### Risk profile

- Port and customs dwell.
- Long inland distances.
- Sparse service and maintenance points.
- Ferry and road dependencies.
- Rain-related deterioration.
- Limited recovery storage in western areas.

#### Operating model

- Use Dar es Salaam as a controlled import and staging hub.
- Do not dispatch inland until release, documents, receiving, and storage are confirmed.
- Use passive packaging for cross-border and district legs.
- Use active reefers only on lanes with proven service, fuel autonomy, secure parking, and recovery storage.
- Establish emergency cold storage near Dodoma and a western forward point.
- Use port, ferry, rainfall, road, and security triggers.

Direct airfreight may be preferable for urgent or short-shelf-life product where port and inland dwell threaten product integrity.

---

### E3: Ethiopia, Djibouti, and Horn routes

#### Risk profile

- Variable security and internal access.
- Airport and road alternatives with different reliability.
- Heat, altitude, and handling effects.
- Stronger power in some urban locations but limited peripheral resilience.
- Potential need for emergency air transport.

#### Operating model

- Use airfreight for priority, high-value, or access-constrained movements.
- Use validated passive systems for final road legs.
- Maintain country-specific release, customs, security, and transport procedures.
- Position safety stock in Addis Ababa or Nairobi only where release, shelf life, storage, and demand justify it.
- Maintain alternate routes and communications redundancy.

A shorter route is not automatically safer if its customs, airport, security, or recovery performance is weaker.

---

### E4: Great Lakes and remote last mile

Examples:

- Eastern DRC.
- Burundi.
- South Sudan.
- Remote Uganda.
- Western Tanzania.

#### Risk profile

- Poor feeder roads and mountainous terrain.
- Seasonal isolation.
- Ferry, boat, and bridge dependencies.
- Weak electricity and refrigeration maintenance.
- Reliance on motorcycles, boats, small aircraft, or ministry vehicles.
- Rapidly changing security conditions.

#### Operating model

- Use small, appointment-based deliveries.
- Use qualified vaccine carriers and cold boxes.
- Use ministry-approved transport where it is better integrated with immunization sessions.
- Qualify motorcycles, boats, aircraft, and ministry vehicles separately.
- Use solar refrigeration only at secure, supportable, high-demand sites.
- Pre-position before each route’s local access deterioration.
- Maintain alternatives for road closure, ferry disruption, flood, and security events.

Each last-mile mode must have an approved container, payload, maximum journey time, monitoring method, custody procedure, and recovery point.

# 5. Equipment and energy choices

## 5.1 Passive packaging

Passive packaging is the default where powered equipment, fuel, maintenance, or recovery capacity is uncertain.

### Required duration

\[
T_{\text{required}} =
P95(\text{door-to-door transit})
+ P95(\text{customs dwell})
+ T_{\text{handling}}
+ T_{\text{recovery}}
+ T_{\text{contingency}}
\]

Where weather, customs, security, and road delays are correlated, use a joint severe-event scenario rather than independently adding averages.

### Qualification requirements

Validate pack-outs under:

- Maximum representative ambient temperatures.
- Solar and stationary-vehicle exposure.
- Day/night cycles.
- Repeated opening and inspection.
- Minimum and maximum payload.
- Freeze-protection conditions.
- Road and air handling.
- Humidity and altitude where relevant.
- Planned recovery and shipper-replacement procedures.

Nominal 96-hour or 168-hour ratings are not sufficient evidence of suitability.

### Mandatory controls

Each high-risk shipment must have:

- Product-specific pack-out.
- Correctly conditioned coolant.
- Freeze protection.
- Trained packing personnel.
- Pack-out checklist.
- Unique shipment ID.
- Tamper-evident seal.
- Calibrated logger.
- Defined logger placement and alarm limits.
- Customs and receiving instructions.
- Replacement or recovery procedure.

Individual logging is mandatory for first-time, high-risk, long-duration, frozen, ultra-cold, high-value, or materially changed shipments. Sampling on established low-risk lanes requires written quality approval and objective risk criteria.

## 5.2 Powered transport

Powered refrigeration is appropriate for:

- Regional and national stores.
- High-volume domestic trunk lanes.
- Predictable routes with service coverage.
- Frozen and ultra-cold products where required.
- Suitable fixed remote facilities.

Each active unit requires:

- Independent temperature sensors.
- Local data storage.
- High and low alarms.
- Fuel or battery autonomy exceeding the approved severe-delay scenario.
- Secure overnight parking.
- Driver alarm-response training.
- Breakdown and replacement-vehicle arrangements.
- Qualified recovery storage.
- Preventive maintenance and calibration.
- Controlled loading, transfer, and unloading procedures.

Active reefers may be approved only after lane qualification demonstrates acceptable product integrity and total cost compared with qualified passive transport.

## 5.3 Fixed storage and power

Each hub, national store, and forward store must have:

- Temperature-mapped refrigerators or cold rooms.
- Generator sized for refrigeration startup and full critical load.
- Tested automatic or manual transfer.
- Fuel-security and replenishment controls.
- Battery backup for monitoring and alarms.
- Preventive maintenance and critical spares.
- Defined maximum outage and transfer time.
- Emergency transfer procedures.
- Alternate qualified storage.

Backup capacity must be based on actual outage history, fuel availability, generator reliability, security, and recovery options.

## 5.4 Solar refrigeration

Solar direct-drive or solar-battery systems may be used at remote fixed facilities where:

- Demand is sufficient.
- The facility is secure.
- Users are trained.
- Local maintenance and spare parts are available.
- Theft and vandalism are controlled.
- Lifecycle economics are favorable.

## 5.5 Frozen and ultra-cold products

Dedicated procedures must cover:

- Dry-ice sourcing and replenishment.
- Sublimation calculations.
- Airline and airport acceptance.
- Airport cold storage.
- Dry-ice ventilation.
- PPE.
- Lithium-battery and dangerous-goods requirements.
- Ultra-cold backup storage.
- Power redundancy.
- Thawing and refreezing.
- Flight cancellation and rebooking.
- Emergency transfer and disposition.

# 7. Customs and border controls

## Pre-dispatch clearance gate

Confirm:

- Importer of record.
- Product authorization.
- Import permits.
- Batch-release documents.
- Consignee.
- Transit documents.
- Ministry authorization.
- Customs broker and backup broker.
- Border operating hours.
- Qualified bonded storage.
- Alternate border where feasible.

Broker confirmation must establish that documentation has been accepted or that unresolved issues have an approved resolution. Document submission alone is not sufficient.

## During customs processing

- Keep shipments sealed, shaded, and monitored.
- Prohibit uncontrolled storage and direct sun exposure.
- Record border arrival.
- Record inspection start and end.
- Record release time.
- Escalate before remaining thermal duration or shelf life is threatened.

Measure median, P90, and P95 transit and customs dwell by lane, direction, season, day of week, and holiday period.

# 8. Seasonal windows and sequencing

Use corridor-specific operating states rather than a universal continental calendar.

Each lane must specify:

- Expected access deterioration date.
- Latest safe dispatch date.
- Minimum forward-stock coverage.
- Heat-risk period.
- Flood, rainfall, ferry, bridge, or road-closure trigger.
- Holiday and customs closures.
- Security reassessment date.
- Suspension authority.
- Reopening criteria.

## Operating states

### State 1: Readiness and stable-lane launch

- Qualify hubs, facilities, carriers, brokers, packaging, and monitoring.
- Launch controllable coastal, urban, and domestic lanes.
- Position stock for destinations expected to deteriorate.
- Confirm ministry receiving and recovery capacity.

### State 2: Inland and remote pre-positioning

Before each lane’s access deterioration:

- Move forward stock.
- Confirm district storage, power, staff, and session dates.
- Dispatch campaign stock early.
- Increase validated time and thermal margins.
- Secure alternative modes and recovery capacity.

### State 3: Seasonal disruption

During heavy rainfall, flooding, cyclone risk, severe heat, or access deterioration:

- Reduce long cross-border road movements.
- Replenish from forward stock.
- Use air, boat, motorcycle, or other local modes only under qualified procedures.
- Review access daily.
- Avoid dispatch before weekends or known customs closures.
- Maintain emergency reserves.

### State 4: Recovery and rebalancing

When access improves:

- Replenish depleted districts gradually.
- Rebalance inventory using consumption, expiry, and wastage data.
- Requalify changed routes.
- Complete maintenance.
- Consume or transfer forward stock before expiry.

Dry season is not automatically safer; heat, dust, security, vehicle wear, and congestion must be included in route decisions.

# 9. Contingency playbooks

## 9.1 Customs delay

1. Notify the control tower, broker, ministry, and quality.
2. Keep the shipment sealed, shaded, and monitored.
3. Move to qualified bonded cold storage if available.
4. Recalculate thermal duration and remaining shelf life.
5. Replace packaging, reroute, or switch to air before limits are exceeded.
6. Quarantine if product conditions or evidence are compromised.

## 9.2 Vehicle or reefer breakdown

1. Keep doors closed and stop unnecessary handling.
2. Notify operations and quality.
3. Dispatch a qualified backup vehicle, passive shipper, or recovery team.
4. Transfer only under an approved procedure.
5. Record temperature, time, custody, seals, and vehicle details.
6. Quarantine if limits are exceeded or evidence is incomplete.

## 9.3 Power failure

1. Activate generator, battery, or solar backup.
2. Minimize door opening.
3. Verify actual product temperature.
4. Call maintenance and fuel support.
5. Transfer to alternate qualified storage if restoration is uncertain.
6. Quarantine if product limits or evidence requirements are not met.

## 9.4 Flood, road closure, ferry disruption, or weather event

1. Stop at the nearest approved recovery site.
2. Do not leave product in an unmonitored vehicle.
3. Notify ministry, control tower, security, and quality.
4. Recalculate remaining thermal duration.
5. Use an approved alternate route, mode, or forward stock.
6. Reconfirm the receiving appointment.

## 9.5 Security deterioration

Triggers include:

- Route incident.
- Curfew.
- Convoy requirement.
- Communications loss.
- Changed security intelligence.
- Loss of secure parking.
- Seal or custody anomaly.

Actions:

- Hold at secure qualified storage.
- Do not proceed without current security approval.
- Switch to an approved alternate route or air mode.
- Maintain driver check-ins and communications redundancy.
- Recover product if the vehicle remains in an insecure location beyond the approved limit.
- Investigate unexplained delays, seal breaches, or custody gaps.

## 9.6 Packaging damage or tampering

1. Secure and photograph the shipment.
2. Inspect inner packaging, product, and logger data.
3. Quarantine where integrity is uncertain.
4. Do not re-pack or release without quality approval.
5. Investigate handling, custody, and security records.

## 9.7 Temperature excursion

1. Stop use and segregate the product.
2. Apply physical and electronic quarantine.
3. Preserve logger, pack-out, seal, custody, ambient, and timing records.
4. Notify quality, manufacturer, ministry, and regulator where required.
5. Assess against product-specific stability data.
6. Document release, restricted use, return, or destruction.
7. Open deviation and CAPA where required.
8. Reconcile inventory and notify downstream recipients.

Separate decision trees are required for:

- High-temperature excursions.
- Suspected freezing.
- Missing or corrupted logger data.
- Physical, light, or vibration damage.
- Seal breach or tampering.
- Insufficient shelf life.
- Cumulative or repeated excursions.

Any expiry change or stability-based disposition requires explicit manufacturer and/or regulatory authorization.

## 9.8 Flight cancellation or airport disruption

1. Keep product in qualified airport storage.
2. Confirm packaging duration, dry-ice status, and remaining shelf life.
3. Rebook through an approved airline and route.
4. Use an alternate airport or mode where authorized.
5. Maintain qualified airport-to-store transfer.
6. Quarantine if handling or temperature evidence is inadequate.

## 9.9 Campaign cancellation or receiving failure

- Do not leave product at an unprepared destination.
- Hold at qualified storage.
- Recalculate shelf life and revised campaign requirements.
- Reallocate or return under approved procedures.
- Update ministry allocation and FEFO records.
- Use emergency transport only where necessary and approved.

## 9.10 Spoilage or unusable stock

- Quantify affected doses and locations.
- Stop further distribution as directed by quality.
- Activate emergency replacement stock.
- Notify the ministry of service impact.
- Dispose through an authorized contractor.
- Retain destruction certificates.
- Complete root-cause analysis.
- Requalify or suspend the affected lane before restoring volume.

# 10. Validation and qualification requirements

No lane may enter routine operation until the following evidence is complete.

## 10.1 Regulatory validation

Confirm and document:

- Importer-of-record status.
- Product registration or authorization.
- Import permits.
- Batch-release requirements.
- Local storage and distribution licenses.
- Ministry acceptance requirements.
- Inter-country transfer permissions.
- Recall and destruction responsibilities.
- Insurance and liability arrangements.

## 10.2 Facility validation

Complete:

- Temperature mapping.
- Refrigeration qualification.
- Generator load and transfer testing.
- Fuel-autonomy testing.
- Alarm challenge testing.
- Monitoring-system qualification.
- Backup-storage verification.
- Security and controlled-access assessment.
- Maintenance and spare-parts review.

## 10.3 Packaging validation

Demonstrate performance under:

- Maximum route ambient.
- Stationary vehicle exposure.
- Customs queues.
- Repeated opening and inspection.
- Actual payload range.
- Road and air handling.
- Freeze-protection conditions.
- Recovery and replacement procedures.

## 10.4 Route and carrier validation

Measure:

- Actual door-to-door transit.
- P90 and P95 transit time.
- P90 and P95 customs dwell.
- Inspection and transfer time.
- Security and road interruptions.
- Recovery response time.
- Temperature exposure.
- Data completeness.
- Cost per delivered dose.

## 10.5 Last-mile validation

Qualify each mode separately:

- Ministry vehicles.
- Motorcycles.
- Boats.
- Small aircraft.
- Outreach carriers.
- Campaign cold boxes.

Define for each mode:

- Container.
- Payload.
- Maximum journey time.
- Ambient and exposure limits.
- Secure handling.
- Monitoring.
- Custody documentation.
- Recovery point.
- Delayed-session procedure.

## 10.6 Pilot validation

Pilot at least:

- One coastal West African lane.
- One inland or Sahel lane.
- One Northern Corridor lane.
- One Central Corridor lane.
- One remote or Great Lakes lane.

Pilot results must include:

- Temperature compliance.
- Freeze-event performance.
- Logger and custody completeness.
- Customs and transit performance.
- Receiving success.
- Shelf-life compliance.
- Recovery performance.
- Security events.
- Spoilage and expiry.
- Cost per delivered dose.
- Deviation and CAPA closure.

## 10.7 Requalification triggers

Requalify after:

- Product or payload change.
- Pack-out or coolant change.
- New carrier or vehicle.
- Route, border, or cross-dock change.
- Major repair or facility relocation.
- Seasonal or ambient-profile change.
- Repeated excursions.
- Significant security, road, or customs change.
- Change in regulatory or ministry requirements.

# 11. Implementation roadmap

## Phase 0: Regulatory and commercial readiness

**Outputs**

- Product master.
- Country and border matrix.
- Importer-of-record confirmation.
- Quality agreement.
- RACI and decision-rights matrix.
- Insurance and contract package.
- Demand and campaign forecast.
- Initial corridor risk register.

**Gate:** no shipment until legal, commercial, regulatory, and decision-rights requirements are closed.

## Phase 1: Engineering and partner readiness

**Outputs**

- Qualified hubs and stores.
- Validated packaging.
- Qualified vehicles and last-mile modes.
- Tested power systems.
- Monitoring and data systems.
- Recovery-site agreements.
- Security and waste procedures.
- Trained personnel and ministry receivers.

**Gate:** all critical facilities, equipment, monitoring, recovery, and security controls pass qualification.

## Phase 2: Controlled pilots

Run the five minimum pilot lanes described above.

**Gate:** pilot results meet approved thresholds for temperature, data, customs, receiving, recovery, security, shelf life, cost, spoilage, and expiry.

## Phase 3: Conditional scale-up

- Increase volume only on qualified lanes.
- Apply temporary shipment-volume and inventory caps.
- Review KPIs weekly during campaigns.
- Maintain spare packaging, backup transport, recovery storage, and emergency air capacity.
- Escalate recurring deviations through CAPA.

## Phase 4: Seasonal authorization and continuous improvement

Before each lane’s operating season:

- Reassess weather and access.
- Review security.
- Confirm customs and ministry readiness.
- Verify storage and power.
- Recalculate inventory and shelf life.
- Confirm emergency capacity.
- Reauthorize, modify, or suspend the lane.

For new countries, new regulated roles, or new infrastructure, assume **four to nine months** for readiness. A 60–120-day rollout applies only where licenses, facilities, carriers, brokers, agreements, and emergency capacity already exist.

# 12. KPIs and management controls

Targets should be set by corridor. Product integrity, regulatory compliance, and traceability are non-negotiable everywhere.

## Service KPIs

- On-time delivery to national stores.
- Campaign completion rate.
- Receiving appointment success.
- District stockout days.
- Percentage received with required remaining shelf life.

## Temperature and quality KPIs

- Percentage within product-specific temperature range.
- Excursions per 1,000 shipments.
- Freeze events per 1,000 shipments.
- Temperature-related wastage.
- Expiry-related wastage.
- Time from alarm to quarantine.
- Time from quarantine to disposition.
- CAPA closure time.
- Logger, batch, and custody completeness.

## Transport and customs KPIs

- Median, P90, and P95 transit time.
- Median, P90, and P95 customs dwell.
- Percentage departing with clearance confirmation.
- Vehicle breakdown rate.
- Reefer uptime.
- Recovery response time.
- Alternate-mode readiness.
- Seal breaches and unexplained custody gaps.

## Storage and energy KPIs

- Cold-room uptime.
- Generator transfer success.
- Fuel autonomy achieved.
- Alarm-response compliance.
- Calibration compliance.
- Solar-system uptime.
- Backup-storage availability.

## Inventory and commercial KPIs

- Inventory accuracy.
- Emergency reserve coverage.
- Forward-stock utilization.
- Near-expiry inventory.
- Cost per delivered dose.
- Actual versus expected spoilage.
- Actual versus expected expiry.
- Replacement lead time.

## Management triggers

| Trigger | Required action |
|---|---|
| P95 transit exceeds validated duration | Change pack-out, route, or mode |
| Repeated lane excursions | Suspend scale-up and requalify |
| Missing temperature data | Quarantine pending quality assessment |
| Failed receiving appointments | Pause dispatch until readiness is restored |
| Forward stock approaches expiry | Stop replenishment and rebalance |
| Recovery exceeds product tolerance | Change recovery site, equipment, or route |
| Security approval lapses | Suspend dispatch |
| Qualified storage unavailable | Hold, reroute, or activate approved recovery plan |
| Actual cost or wastage materially exceeds plan | Review lane design and mode selection |

---

# 13. Mandatory lane operating file

No lane may enter routine operation without an approved file containing:

- Exact origin, destination, and border crossings.
- Product SKU and temperature profile.
- Regulatory and importer status.
- Approved pack-out and logger configuration.
- Validated thermal duration.
- P95 transit and customs dwell.
- Joint severe-event scenario.
- Maximum uncontrolled exposure.
- Vehicle and carrier requirements.
- Secure holding points.
- Qualified recovery facilities.
- Customs, broker, ministry, security, and quality contacts.
- Receiving appointment and shelf-life rules.
- Seasonal and security triggers.
- Alternate route and mode.
- Emergency airfreight option.
- Inventory reserve.
- Escalation SLAs.
- Excursion, replacement, recall, and destruction authority.
- Cost per delivered dose.
- Expected spoilage and expiry estimate.
- Pilot acceptance record.
- Signed RACI and quality approval.

---

# Final recommendation

Approve the strategy as the governing framework, with routine distribution authorized only after each lane passes its qualification and pilot gates.

## West Africa

- Accra and Dakar as initial control hubs.
- Passive-dominant coastal and cross-border transport.
- Selective inland forward stock.
- Air support for fragile or high-security Sahel routes.
- Powered refrigeration concentrated at qualified facilities and proven domestic lanes.

## East Africa

- Nairobi-led Northern Corridor.
- Dar es Salaam-led Central Corridor.
- Conditional forward nodes at Kigali, Entebbe, Addis Ababa, and Mombasa.
- Passive-dominant Great Lakes and remote last mile.
- Air support for urgent, insecure, inaccessible, or short-shelf-life movements.

## Universal standard

Vaccines move only when the complete system—product requirements, packaging, storage, power, customs, transport, security, monitoring, maintenance, ministry coordination, inventory control, recovery, and quality governance—demonstrably protects product integrity from release through final receipt and administration.
