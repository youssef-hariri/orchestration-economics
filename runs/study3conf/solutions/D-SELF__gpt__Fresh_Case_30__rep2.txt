# 2026 Vaccine Cold-Chain Distribution Strategy for West and East Africa

## 1. Strategic model

Use a **country-specific, risk-segmented hub-and-spoke network** rather than one continent-wide operating model.

### Core design

- **Powered cold storage** at import gateways, national stores, and selected high-throughput regional hubs.
- **Qualified passive containers** for most border crossings, rural legs, outreach movements, and lower-volume shipments.
- **Powered transport** only on predictable, high-volume routes with validated equipment, reliable power or fuel, maintenance support, and recovery capacity.
- **Pre-positioned inventory** before validated weather, campaign, customs, holiday, and security disruptions.
- **Formal agreements** with ministries of health, regulators, customs, carriers, storage providers, security providers, and waste contractors.
- **Product-specific quality controls** based on the approved label, manufacturer instructions, stability data, vaccine vial monitors (VVMs) where applicable, and freeze controls.
- **A recovery option for every shipment** before validated hold time or product stability limits are reached.

This plan is a design framework. Routine shipments should begin only after the relevant country and lane documentation has been approved.

---

# 2. Planning assumptions and controls

## Product assumptions

The initial network may include vaccines labeled for **+2°C to +8°C**, but that range must not be applied generically. Each product requires confirmation of:

- approved storage and transport conditions;
- manufacturer shipping instructions;
- authorized excursion allowances;
- freeze and light sensitivity;
- VVM and freeze-indicator requirements;
- shelf life and minimum remaining shelf life;
- diluent, reconstitution, and open-vial requirements;
- product-specific requirements for frozen or ultra-cold handling.

Frozen and ultra-cold products require separate packaging, monitoring, dry-ice or active-system procedures, airport handling, occupational-safety controls, replenishment arrangements, and destination storage.

## Local validation

Before approving any route, validate:

- shipment volume, frequency, value, and product mix;
- route-by-segment transit times;
- high-percentile and disruption-scenario delays;
- border dwell and inspection practices;
- road, bridge, ferry, flood, and seasonal conditions;
- ambient and solar-exposure profiles;
- current security restrictions;
- communications coverage;
- customs, port-health, import, and batch-release requirements;
- qualified storage, recovery sites, carriers, brokers, and maintenance providers;
- power, generator, solar, and battery performance;
- ministry transport, staffing, and receiving capacity;
- airfreight and airport cold-chain availability;
- reverse-logistics and waste arrangements.

Port access, capital-city infrastructure, grid connection, ministry involvement, or a nominal container hold time must not be treated as proof of readiness.

# 3. Corridor archetypes and recommended operating models

The examples below are planning archetypes, not verified 2026 classifications.

| Corridor type | Illustrative examples for assessment | Main risks | Recommended model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gateway-to-capital | Mombasa–Nairobi, Dar es Salaam–Dodoma, Djibouti–Addis Ababa, coastal gateway-to-capital routes | Port or airport dwell, urban congestion, power failure at receipt | Powered storage at gateway and national store; passive systems for smaller consignments; powered transport only where volume and recovery are proven |
| Long inland or landlocked trunk | Abidjan–Ouagadougou, Dakar–Bamako, Mombasa–Kampala/Kigali, Dar es Salaam extensions | Border queues, customs inspection, long heat exposure, security and road disruption | Passive-first for small and medium shipments; powered transport for validated high-volume lanes; border treated as a separate custody and temperature-control point |
| Capital-to-regional or district | National capital to provincial, regional, or district stores | Variable roads, local receiving delays, weak regional storage and power | Passive pallet or parcel shippers; quantities matched to demand and storage; qualified regional stores with tested backup power |
| Remote or security-sensitive last mile | Rural districts, flood-prone areas, mountainous routes, mobile or boat deliveries | Seasonal isolation, weak communications, cancelled sessions, limited power, security restrictions | Vaccine carriers and qualified passive containers; small session-based quantities; pre-positioning at qualified hubs; approved alternative communications |

### Regional posture

- **West African inland and Sahel corridors:** prioritize passive systems, smaller consignments, secure national or regional buffers, seasonal dispatch windows, and rapid suspension or rerouting capability.
- **East African trunk corridors:** consider powered transport where volumes and service support justify it, while retaining passive systems for border and rural legs.
- **Remote areas in both regions:** use passive last-mile systems and session-based delivery; add solar or generator-backed storage only where maintenance and monitoring are demonstrated.

Wave sequencing should improve access and controls, not permanently defer remote populations.

# 4. Lane dossier and approval

Every lane should have a controlled dossier containing:

- route, border, and handoff map;
- product and shipment profile;
- route-by-segment transit data;
- seasonal and disruption scenarios;
- customs and port-health procedures;
- security status and stop-movement criteria;
- approved primary and backup carriers;
- qualified storage and recovery sites;
- receiving-site capacity, hours, staff, and authority;
- equipment and pack-out configuration;
- emergency-mode and funding arrangements;
- reverse-logistics and waste procedures;
- owner, approval date, review date, and change history.

## Mandatory approval gates

Do not authorize routine movement if:

- qualified hold time does not cover the maximum planned duration plus approved recovery buffer;
- no qualified receiving site exists;
- customs documentation has not been accepted in practice;
- no authorized excursion-disposition authority exists;
- no recovery site or alternate mode is available;
- security approval is absent or expired;
- ministry handoff responsibilities are unclear;
- shelf life, shipment size, or storage capacity are incompatible;
- monitoring, communications, or custody records cannot be maintained.

A weighted scorecard may prioritize lanes, but it must not override a failed mandatory gate.

## Lane status

- **Green:** routine operation permitted after all gates pass.
- **Amber:** restricted or pilot movements only, with smaller lots and active recovery planning.
- **Red:** routine movement suspended until the critical risk is mitigated.

Review status after a critical deviation, excursion, theft, tampering event, repeated Amber event, material route or equipment change, major weather or security change, or the scheduled review date.

# 5. Sequencing

## Wave 0: Readiness and validation — weeks 1–12

1. Confirm product profiles, stability, freeze, VVM, light, and shelf-life requirements.
2. Confirm importer-of-record and regulatory responsibilities.
3. Validate customs and port-health documentation.
4. Survey routes, facilities, power, communications, weather, and security.
5. Qualify packaging, vehicles, storage, monitoring devices, and backup power.
6. Sign quality, ministry, carrier, customs, security, and waste agreements.
7. Assign RACI responsibilities and escalation authority.
8. Confirm emergency cold stores, recovery carriers, alternate routes, and alternate modes.
9. Train and assess drivers, warehouse teams, customs handlers, and receivers.
10. Complete representative pilots and contingency exercises.

## Wave 1: Anchor corridors

Start with gateway-to-capital and capital-to-regional lanes having:

- accepted customs procedures;
- qualified national-store capacity;
- confirmed receiving arrangements;
- carrier and technical support;
- current security approval;
- recovery capability.

Pilots must represent the intended product, payload, season, carrier, border process, and receiving site. Favorable dry-season movements alone do not establish year-round readiness.

## Wave 2: Cross-border and landlocked extensions

Proceed after border handoffs, hold-time margin, storage, security, ministry responsibilities, and recovery arrangements have been demonstrated.

## Wave 3: Remote and high-variability routes

Use smaller shipments, more frequent replenishment, pre-positioning, qualified passive systems, mobile monitoring, and enhanced security and communications controls. Each deferred route should have a mitigation plan and review date.

# 6. Equipment selection

## Passive systems

Use qualified passive equipment when:

- volumes are low or irregular;
- routes include multiple handoffs;
- power is unreliable;
- the last mile uses motorcycles, boats, small vehicles, or manual carrying;
- active transport cannot be maintained locally;
- the shipper provides adequate product and recovery margin.

Qualification must cover:

- actual product and payload;
- minimum and maximum load;
- seasonal ambient and solar exposure;
- loading, unloading, inspection, and opening exposure;
- coolant-pack conditioning;
- freeze prevention;
- maximum approved duration;
- airfreight or altitude effects where relevant;
- reuse condition and pack-out consistency.

\[
\text{Qualified hold time}
>
\text{maximum planned transit}
+
\text{handling time}
+
\text{approved recovery buffer}
\]

The buffer must be justified by lane evidence and quality approval. A universal percentage margin must not be assumed.

## Powered transport

Use reefers, active containers, or powered vehicles when:

- shipment volume and frequency are high and predictable;
- duration makes passive transport inefficient;
- loading and unloading can be controlled;
- power, fuel, batteries, maintenance, and technical support are validated;
- the unit can remain powered during border and overnight stops;
- a replacement vehicle or passive fallback is available;
- the destination can receive promptly.

Require:

- pre-trip inspection;
- payload-zone monitoring;
- verified set point and alarms;
- fuel or battery-autonomy checks;
- safe parking;
- door-opening controls;
- breakdown escalation;
- cleaning and maintenance records;
- qualified recovery arrangements.

Powered transport is not automatically safer than a qualified passive shipper.

## Storage and power

Approved storage sites must demonstrate:

- temperature mapping;
- adequate capacity;
- alarm functionality;
- preventive maintenance;
- trained staff;
- backup-power testing under actual load;
- documented fuel or battery autonomy;
- spare parts and technical support;
- emergency transfer procedures;
- physical security.

Use WHO/PQS-qualified equipment where applicable and comply with national requirements. Unqualified household or commercial refrigerators must not be used for vaccine storage.

# 7. Customs and ministry coordination

## Customs

Before loading, confirm:

- importer of record;
- permits and product authorizations;
- duty and tax exemptions;
- batch-release documentation;
- ministry allocation or consignee authorization;
- broker authority;
- port-health requirements;
- border hours and closures;
- demurrage, storage, emergency freight, and replacement-cost responsibility;
- legality of temporary storage;
- return requirements for reusable packaging and coolant packs.

If lawful inspection requires opening:

- notify authorities of the temperature sensitivity;
- use shade or controlled space where possible;
- minimize exposure;
- document opening, temperature, custody, and resealing;
- supervise repacking under an approved procedure;
- replace and reconcile seals;
- perform post-inspection temperature and packaging checks.

Each lane needs Amber and Red customs-delay thresholds based on current product temperature, remaining validated hold time, estimated time to an approved facility, inspection exposure, and product stability.

## Ministry handoffs

Each country requires signed agreements and a RACI covering:

- allocation and release;
- importer-of-record activities;
- central-store acceptance;
- regional and district transport;
- receiving hours and alternate consignee;
- storage and backup power;
- routine replenishment;
- campaign and outbreak delivery;
- session confirmation;
- security approval;
- excursion quarantine and disposition;
- emergency redistribution;
- dose reconciliation;
- return and waste;
- emergency expenditure.

A ministry agreement does not prove that vehicles, fuel, staff, storage, or local-government support are available. Verify these before high-risk dispatches.

# 8. Monitoring and quality control

Each shipment must use a qualified, traceable monitoring method appropriate to the product and risk, such as:

- calibrated or verified electronic data logger;
- approved indicator system for defined small outreach movements;
- VVM;
- freeze indicator;
- seal and packaging-integrity checks.

The monitoring standard must specify:

- accuracy and tolerance;
- calibration or verification interval;
- recording interval;
- alarm thresholds and delay;
- placement and number of devices;
- time synchronization;
- data retention;
- missing-data treatment;
- device-failure contingency;
- access controls, audit trails, backup, and cybersecurity.

Assess temperature and product condition at dispatch, major handoffs, after border inspection or transloading, at receipt, at outreach level where applicable, and after returns.

## Excursion procedure

A temperature alarm, suspected freezing, data gap, damaged shipper, broken seal, or uncertain custody requires:

1. Immediate quarantine.
2. Continued storage under the appropriate condition.
3. Preservation of logger, VVM, seal, and custody records.
4. Lot-level identification.
5. Review of temperature, duration, and exposure history.
6. Inspection of VVMs, freeze indicators, vials, labels, and packaging.
7. Notification of quality, ministry, and product owner representatives.
8. Authorized stability assessment.
9. Written disposition.
10. Root-cause investigation and CAPA.

An alarm must not cause automatic destruction, but absence of an alarm must not be treated as proof of suitability.

For remote sites without immediate logger review, the approved SOP must clearly state whether administration is permitted and what evidence is required. Unresolved quality concerns must not be bypassed.

# 9. Seasonal scheduling and inventory

## Seasonal scheduling

For each lane, define:

- planning periods;
- forecast sources and review frequency;
- dispatch cutoffs;
- flood, bridge, heat, security, and border triggers;
- pre-positioning deadlines and limits;
- alternate-route, airfreight, postponement, and suspension criteria.

### Rain and access disruption

- Complete bulk replenishment before expected disruption.
- Pre-position only in qualified storage.
- Use smaller loads and more frequent deliveries.
- Confirm alternate roads, bridges, ferries, and recovery sites.
- Suspend movement when road or security conditions exceed approved limits.

### Heat

- Validate packaging against actual ambient and solar exposure.
- Minimize customs-yard and loading-apron dwell.
- Use shade and insulated staging.
- Do not park active units without cooling.
- Schedule movement during cooler periods where safe.

### Holidays and campaigns

- Review border and ministry calendars regularly.
- Build receiving and customs buffers.
- Do not dispatch to a closed or unstaffed consignee.
- Pre-position only within storage, demand, and shelf-life limits.

## Inventory buffers

\[
\text{Reorder point}
=
\text{expected demand during lead time}
+
\text{safety stock for lead-time variability}
\]

Increase buffers for unpredictable customs, seasonal access, security restrictions, fixed campaign dates, weak alternatives, and limited redistribution.

Reduce buffers for short shelf life, uncertain demand, weak storage, high expiry risk, or reliable emergency replenishment.

Illustrative planning ranges—not universal requirements—are:

- stable trunk lanes: 7–14 days;
- variable cross-border lanes: 14–28 days;
- remote or security-sensitive lanes: 21–42 days only where storage, demand, shelf life, and rotation support them.

Balance stockout, expiry, temperature-loss, missed-session, and emergency-replacement risks. Use FEFO and track doses dispatched, received, administered, returned, wasted, expired, destroyed, or unexplained.

# 10. Contingency planning

Every lane needs a three-level recovery plan.

## Hold

- Keep product closed, shaded, secure, and monitored.
- Confirm current temperature, seal status, and remaining hold time.
- Notify the responsible operations, quality, ministry, carrier, and security contacts.

## Transfer

- Move to a qualified cold store or controlled cross-dock.
- Replace the vehicle or active unit.
- Preserve chain of custody and temperature records.
- Use only approved coolant and pack-out procedures.

## Reroute, change mode, or replace

- Use the approved alternate route or air-road combination.
- Redistribute from another national or regional store.
- Replace product if quality disposition requires it.
- Do not rely on airfreight unless capacity, permits, airport handling, destination storage, security, and funding have been verified.

### Incident triggers

Define:

- Amber and Red thresholds;
- response deadlines;
- remaining-hold-time calculation;
- transfer-site capacity;
- stop-movement authority;
- emergency-spend authority;
- quality-disposition authority;
- restart criteria.

### Key scenarios

- **Vehicle breakdown:** dispatch qualified recovery equipment or vehicle before hold-time expiry.
- **Power or cold-room failure:** activate backup power; transfer if restoration is not achieved within the site threshold.
- **Road closure or security event:** stop movement, move to a safe qualified holding point, and activate the approved alternate plan.
- **Theft or tampering:** prioritize personal safety, freeze inventory records, reconcile seals and lots, and quarantine suspect product.
- **Major spoilage or missed campaign:** quarantine, notify required authorities, replace or redistribute stock, reschedule sessions, and complete CAPA before routine restart.

---

# 11. Reverse logistics, waste, and anti-diversion

Returned, expired, damaged, suspected-frozen, or excursion-affected product must be:

- segregated and labeled;
- reconciled by lot and quantity;
- stored and transported appropriately;
- quarantined where required;
- disposed of under national pharmaceutical and healthcare-waste rules;
- destroyed with authorized records and witnessing.

Unused campaign vaccine must not automatically be returned, reused, or destroyed. Reintroduction requires authorized assessment of identity, custody, temperature history, expiry, VVM or freeze evidence, and vial condition.

Track and control:

- reusable shippers;
- coolant packs;
- vials, syringes, sharps, and diluents;
- dry ice;
- contaminated packaging;
- cross-border returns;
- seal and batch reconciliation;
- suspected diversion or counterfeit product.

# 12. Governance and performance management

## Governance

The program should include:

- quality agreements with the product owner, ministry, importer, carriers, storage providers, and subcontractors;
- applicable GDP/GSP and national requirements;
- supplier and carrier qualification;
- training and competency records;
- calibration, maintenance, audits, complaints, recalls, and mock recalls;
- deviation, CAPA, and change control;
- document retention and data integrity;
- insurance and liability allocation;
- business continuity for warehouses, IT, communications, staffing, and power.

Use normal-hours control for stable routine lanes and extended-hours or 24/7 coverage for campaigns, outbreaks, and high-risk movements. Name the authorities who can stop, transfer, reroute, fund, dispose, and restart shipments.

## Performance measures

These are proposed management or contractual objectives, not universal regulatory requirements:

| Measure | Initial objective |
|---|---:|
| Complete pre-alert documentation | 100% |
| Qualified monitoring method | 100% |
| Lot, shipment, seal, and monitoring traceability | 100% |
| Documented receipt and condition confirmation | ≥98% |
| OTIF delivery on stable lanes | ≥95% |
| Excursions formally dispositioned | 100% |
| Receiving sites passing qualification | 100% |
| Preventable temperature loss | Target below 1%, subject to baseline |
| FEFO compliance | ≥98% |
| Critical deviations past closure deadline | 0 |
| Contingency exercises on critical lanes | At least quarterly |

Also track customs dwell, logger-data completeness, recovery before hold-time expiry, time to quarantine and disposition, power-outage response, expiry, open-vial wastage, emergency freight, unexplained loss, missed sessions, and CAPA closure.

Segment results by country, lane, product, season, shipment type, and routine, campaign, outbreak, or emergency status.

# 13. 2026 implementation priorities

## First 30 days

- Confirm product and regulatory requirements.
- Identify ministry, regulator, customs, QA, carrier, security, and waste authorities.
- Select candidate corridors.
- Begin route, facility, power, weather, customs, and security surveys.
- Inventory storage, vehicles, passive shippers, monitoring devices, generators, and solar systems.
- Draft lane dossiers, quality agreements, RACI matrices, and contingency plans.

## Days 31–90

- Complete equipment, packaging, vehicle, storage, monitoring, and power qualification.
- Confirm importer-of-record and customs procedures.
- Sign ministry and quality agreements.
- Contract primary and backup carriers and brokers.
- Establish emergency cold stores and alternate modes.
- Train and assess personnel.
- Conduct representative pilots and contingency exercises.
- Establish KPI baselines.

## Months 4–6

- Scale validated anchor lanes.
- Launch cross-border extensions only after formal approval.
- Repair or install backup power.
- Implement shipment, inventory, temperature, deviation, reconciliation, and security dashboards.
- Test border, breakdown, power, refrigeration, theft, and route-closure playbooks.

## Months 7–12

- Add remote routes selectively and equitably.
- Recalculate buffers using observed demand and lead times.
- Review passive-versus-powered economics by lane.
- Audit customs, temperature records, handoffs, FEFO, waste, and reconciliation.
- Requalify after material changes.
- Use performance evidence for the 2027 network plan.

## Final operating principle

For every movement, match:

\[
\text{Product profile}
+
\text{qualified equipment}
+
\text{validated lane duration}
+
\text{authorized receiving site}
+
\text{feasible recovery option}
\]

Routine distribution should begin only when current local evidence confirms that the product can be protected, legally cleared, safely transported, accepted by the responsible health authority, reconciled through use or disposal, and recovered or quarantined if the primary plan fails.
