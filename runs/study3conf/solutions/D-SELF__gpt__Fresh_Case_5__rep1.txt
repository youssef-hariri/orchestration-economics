# Market-Entry and Product-Adaptation Strategy  
## Gulf States, Malaysia and Indonesia | 2026–2028

## 1. Executive decision

Enter the region as a **locally governed savings and financial-wellness platform**, not as a translated European robo-adviser.

### Customer proposition

> **Save step by step, plan for the people and goals that matter to you, and invest only when the product, risk and values fit your needs.**

### Recommended decisions

1. **Launch no more than one live market in 2026.**
2. Treat **Malaysia as the provisional first-market candidate**, subject to the scorecard and no-go criteria below.
3. Prepare the UAE in parallel, but launch it only after the first pilot passes an independent review.
4. Enter **only one** of Saudi Arabia or Indonesia in 2027.
5. Defer Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar and Oman until a partner-specific business case and operating capability are proven.
6. Launch a narrow MVP comprising:
   - Financial wellness and goals;
   - One regulated savings product;
   - One conservative diversified investment pathway, if the partner and regulatory structure are ready.
7. Keep regulated advice, portfolio management, execution, custody, payment services and customer-money handling with appropriately licensed local entities.
8. Treat Shariah governance, suitability, privacy, reconciliation, fraud prevention, complaints and AI controls as core product requirements.

**Operating principle:**  
> **Automate convenience, preserve customer control, and localize accountability.**

---

# 2. Assumptions and matters requiring validation

The following are hypotheses, not established market facts:

- Customers will generally accept automated savings before fully automated investing.
- Shariah considerations will influence product choice, but customers will differ in the level of religious labeling and governance they expect.
- Banks, employers, Islamic-finance institutions and trusted educators may build trust more efficiently than direct advertising alone.
- Family goals will be relevant to many customers, while individual privacy remains important.
- A European brand may signal technical capability but may also raise concerns about local accountability, foreign ownership and data transfer.
- Indonesia may require low minimums, wallet connectivity and high-volume economics to support small-balance customers.
- A global securities screen will not, by itself, establish that a product is Shariah-compliant in every jurisdiction.

### Mandatory validation before any launch

Obtain current official-source confirmation, local counsel advice or licensed-partner confirmation covering:

- Corporate structure and foreign ownership;
- Financial advice, portfolio management and investment distribution;
- Payments, e-money, wallets and account aggregation;
- Customer-money safeguarding and custody;
- Product registration or approval;
- Shariah governance and product review;
- Data protection, cloud, outsourcing and cross-border transfers;
- AML/CFT, sanctions, PEP and suspicious-activity responsibilities;
- Consumer protection, compensation and complaints;
- Tax, CRS/FATCA, withholding and customer reporting;
- FX and remittance restrictions;
- AI and automated-decision requirements;
- Marketing, influencer and affiliate restrictions.

Regulatory, Shariah and market statements in this strategy are directional until verified for the specific product, entity, partner and jurisdiction.

# 3. Market selection and sequencing

## 3.1 First-market decision

A selection committee should make the binding first-market decision by **30 April 2026**.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Lawful pilot route and regulatory clarity | 20% |
| Quality and availability of licensed partners | 15% |
| Product and Shariah-governance complexity | 10% |
| Integration and operational readiness | 10% |
| Distribution and acquisition potential | 10% |
| Average balance and contribution economics | 10% |
| Data, cloud and outsourcing feasibility | 10% |
| Strategic learning value | 10% |
| Fraud and support burden | 5% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

The highest score is not sufficient if any no-go condition remains:

- No credible authorization or licensed-partner route;
- Unresolved custody, safeguarding or withdrawal arrangements;
- Unresolved launch-critical Shariah or product-structure issue;
- No acceptable data-transfer or outsourcing structure;
- Negative downside economics without a credible subsidy or partner model;
- Insufficient compliance, operations or local support capacity.

If neither Malaysia nor the UAE passes, defer public launch.

## 3.2 Geographic capacity constraint

- **2026:** one live market only.
- **2027:** one additional market only, unless regional compliance, operations, fraud, support and partner-management functions are fully staffed.
- **2028 onward:** additional markets only after partner portability, operational resilience and country-level economics are demonstrated.

Detailed implementation work should initially focus on the top two candidates. The other six markets should receive desk-level screening only.

# 5. MVP product scope

## Product A: Financial wellness and goals

The startup-controlled layer should initially include:

- User-entered goals;
- Spending categorization;
- Cash-flow visualization;
- Emergency-fund planning;
- General education;
- Non-personalized reminders;
- Shared goals and contribution invitations.

Unless specifically approved, exclude:

- Named investment recommendations;
- Product rankings;
- Personalized product comparisons that direct a transaction;
- Startup-controlled payment initiation;
- Unlicensed account aggregation;
- High-risk investment prompts.

### Account-data decision

For the first pilot, use **user-entered data or partner-provided data only** unless a licensed aggregation provider and local counsel confirm the relevant permissions, responsibilities and data-transfer structure.

## Product B: One regulated savings pathway

Offer one clearly defined partner product per pilot market.

The interface must state:

- Deposit or investment status;
- Whether principal is protected;
- Whether returns are guaranteed;
- Who holds the money;
- Whether assets or funds are segregated;
- Applicable protection or compensation arrangements;
- Withdrawal timing and conditions;
- Fees;
- Conventional or Islamic structure.

## Product C: One conservative investment pathway

Offer only if the partner, regulatory and governance structure is ready:

- One low- or moderate-risk diversified portfolio;
- One Shariah-screened or locally approved option where supported;
- A conventional option only if justified;
- Manual customer approval before first investment;
- Partner-owned or partner-supervised advice and execution.

The product fact sheet must define:

- Asset allocation bands;
- Permitted instruments;
- Risk indicator;
- Liquidity and settlement;
- Currency exposure;
- Loss scenarios;
- Fees;
- Rebalancing rules;
- Custody and ownership;
- Shariah governance, where relevant.

### Launch exclusions

Do not offer initially:

- Leverage;
- Derivatives;
- Speculative digital assets;
- Concentrated single-stock trading;
- Gold;
- Takaful bundles;
- Complex sukuk structures;
- Informal pooled family investments;
- Automated zakat payments;
- Customer-level purification features unless separately approved;
- Unapproved cross-border products.

# 6. Shariah product governance

## 6.1 Product-level review

A securities screen alone is insufficient. Review each Islamic product for:

### Assets

- Prohibited sectors;
- Financial-ratio methodology;
- Debt, cash and non-permissible income;
- Fund and sukuk structures;
- Derivatives, short selling and securities lending;
- Corporate actions;
- Data quality and review frequency.

### Structure

- Customer relationship with the provider;
- Agency, partnership, fund or management structure;
- Fees and performance compensation;
- Loss treatment;
- Redemption and liquidity;
- Custody and ownership.

### Cash

The first Islamic product specification must explicitly state whether idle funds are:

- Held in an Islamic account;
- Held as safeguarded uninvested cash;
- Invested in an approved cash-management product;
- Temporarily held by the regulated partner under a defined arrangement.

Do not use “cash,” “savings” or “Islamic” as shorthand for “risk-free” or “universally compliant.”

## 6.2 Customer-facing labels

| Label | Use |
|---|---|
| Conventional | Not represented as Shariah-compliant |
| Shariah-conscious | Values-oriented design preference; not certification |
| Shariah-screened | Assets reviewed against a stated methodology |
| Shariah-compliant | Use only after qualified review of structure and ongoing operation |
| Islamic product | Use only where applicable local legal and institutional requirements are met |

Every claim must identify the product, reviewing body, methodology, jurisdiction, review date and limitations.

A regulatory license does not establish religious compliance. A Shariah opinion does not authorize a regulated financial service.

## 6.3 Governance and disagreement

Establish:

- An independent qualified Shariah adviser or panel for each product family;
- Conflict-of-interest rules;
- Local scholar or partner involvement where required;
- Pre-launch and event-driven review;
- Screening and purification records;
- Non-compliance procedures;
- Local-language summaries;
- A defined governance hierarchy.

For local customer-facing claims, the legally responsible local product provider and its applicable approved governance process should control. The startup’s central adviser may set internal policy but should not override local requirements.

Define procedures for:

- Conflicting scholarly opinions;
- Changed screening thresholds;
- Expired approvals;
- Product relabeling;
- Freezing new purchases;
- Existing-holding treatment;
- Customer notification;
- Regulatory or partner notification;
- Remediation costs.

## 6.4 Purification, zakat and rewards

### Purification

If applicable, document:

- Methodology;
- Responsible party;
- Review frequency;
- Treatment of non-permissible income;
- Charitable destination;
- Customer reporting;
- Withdrawal and exit treatment.

Do not describe purification as zakat or as satisfying the customer’s personal religious obligation.

### Zakat

Exclude from the MVP. If introduced later, provide only an optional estimator that is:

- Based on disclosed assumptions;
- Separated by asset type;
- Exportable;
- Clearly not a religious ruling or tax opinion;
- Subject to explicit confirmation before any payment or donation.

### Rewards and referrals

At launch:

- No randomized rewards;
- No rewards linked to higher risk or holding periods without review;
- No hidden interest-derived rewards in Islamic pathways;
- No unlicensed investment referrals;
- Fixed fee waivers or disclosed partner-funded benefits only, subject to local legal and Shariah review.

# 7. Family functionality and privacy

## Launch features

- Shared savings goals;
- Contribution invitations;
- View-only access;
- Contribute and approve permissions;
- Separate personal and shared balances;
- Activity logs;
- Immediate revocation;
- Re-authentication for sensitive actions;
- Permission-change alerts.

## Deferred features

- Pooled family investment accounts;
- Trading authority without formal authorization;
- Child investment accounts without country-specific review;
- Automatic balance disclosure;
- Family performance rankings;
- Features that pressure one household member to invest.

The permission model must specify who may:

- View targets, balances and transactions;
- Contribute;
- Change goals;
- Request or approve withdrawals;
- Approve investments;
- Invite other users.

It must also address:

- Permission expiry;
- Contributor ownership;
- Reclaim rights;
- Disputed contributions;
- Relationship breakdown;
- Death or incapacity;
- Liability for unauthorized actions;
- Sensitive notifications.

Provide a discreet support route for suspected financial coercion or abuse, with trained staff, safe notifications and permission-revocation procedures.

# 8. AI, data and consumer protection

## 8.1 Permitted initial AI uses

AI may assist with:

- Transaction categorization;
- Cash-flow forecasting;
- Savings reminders;
- Goal progress;
- Scenario explanations;
- General education;
- Missing-information prompts;
- Support routing.

AI must not independently:

- Transfer funds;
- Change beneficiaries;
- Increase risk;
- Rebalance into new assets;
- Override suitability rules;
- Issue religious rulings;
- Generate purification or zakat calculations;
- Produce unsupported legal, regulatory or Shariah claims;
- Make irreversible investment decisions without required approval.

## 8.2 Deterministic systems

Use governed rules and calculation engines for:

- Eligibility;
- Risk scoring;
- Affordability;
- Suitability;
- Fees;
- Purification;
- Zakat estimates;
- Trading permissions;
- Withdrawal restrictions;
- Disclosure triggers.

The language model may explain results but must not be the source of truth.

## 8.3 Recommendation transparency

For each personalized recommendation, retain and disclose:

- Information used;
- Missing information;
- Goal horizon;
- Liquidity assumptions;
- Risk level;
- Fees;
- Shariah pathway or screening basis;
- Alternatives considered;
- Conditions that would change the recommendation;
- Model or rules version;
- Recommendation date.

Clearly label:

- General education;
- Personalized guidance;
- Regulated advice;
- Human advice;
- Portfolio management.

Customer approval is a conduct control, not a substitute for authorization.

## 8.4 AI governance

Create an AI governance committee with authority to approve, restrict or suspend models. Assign owners for:

- Each model;
- Compliance;
- Shariah content;
- Data protection;
- Human review;
- Vendor management;
- Incident response;
- Model suspension.

Maintain:

- Model inventory;
- Model-risk tiers;
- Model cards;
- Validation records;
- Bias tests;
- Change control;
- Vendor assessments;
- Incident severity levels;
- Suspension criteria;
- Senior-management reporting.

Minimum standards:

- Zero unauthorized AI transactions;
- Zero fabricated licenses, approvals, scholars or Shariah claims;
- 100% accuracy for approved fee, custody and principal-protection facts;
- Defined maximum error rates for risk and liquidity explanations;
- Defined maximum disparity between language groups;
- Maximum age for unreviewed legal or religious content;
- Defined human-escalation response time.

External AI vendors must be assessed for data training, storage location, cross-border transfers, subprocessors, deletion, audit, update notification, outages and replacement.

## 8.5 Suitability and vulnerable customers

Separate risk tolerance from risk capacity. Assess:

- Income stability;
- Emergency savings;
- Debt;
- Dependants;
- Family support;
- Goal horizon;
- Liquidity needs;
- Existing assets;
- Currency exposure;
- Investment experience.

Require human review for:

- High-risk or concentrated recommendations;
- Conflicting goals and risk capacity;
- Significant losses;
- Vulnerable customers;
- Religious-compliance complaints;
- Purification or zakat questions;
- Minors, estates or deceased customers;
- Suspected coercion;
- Material product or screening changes;
- Contradictory data or model uncertainty.

# 14. Launch gates, stop criteria and KPIs

## Mandatory launch gates

### Regulatory and legal

- Current country memorandum;
- Feature-permission matrix;
- Licensed partner or authorization;
- Approved terms, disclosures and marketing;
- Defined AML, complaints and redress responsibilities;
- Data, cloud and outsourcing assessment;
- Tax and customer-reporting plan.

### Shariah

- Product-level review;
- Contract, cash and fee analysis;
- Screening methodology;
- Approved claims;
- Purification policy if applicable;
- Review and breach-remediation calendar;
- Disagreement hierarchy.

### Product and conduct

- Fee, risk and liquidity comprehension testing;
- Suitability and affordability validation;
- Family-permission testing;
- Vulnerable-customer testing;
- Accessibility and low-literacy testing;
- Working human escalation;
- No unauthorized AI actions in red-team testing.

### Operations and economics

- Tested payment, custody and reconciliation;
- Customer-support capacity;
- Fraud controls;
- Disaster-recovery testing;
- Partner-exit plan;
- Successful failure tabletop;
- Viable base and downside economics.

## Product-specific primary metrics

### Savings

- Six-month sustained contribution or emergency-fund progress.

### Investment

- Customers remaining in suitable portfolios with appropriate liquidity, periodic suitability review and no material conduct incident.

### Financial wellness

- Repeat goal engagement combined with demonstrated fee, risk and liquidity comprehension.

## Guardrails

Set country-specific numerical thresholds before launch for:

- KYC completion;
- First-funding rate;
- Three- and six-month retention;
- Fee, risk and liquidity comprehension;
- Complaints per 1,000 funded users;
- Fraud loss per funded user;
- Suitability exceptions;
- Reconciliation-break duration;
- Human-escalation response time;
- Contribution margin;
- Partner uptime;
- AI explanation error;
- Language-group disparity;
- Unresolved complaint age.

Non-negotiable standards:

- Zero unauthorized AI transactions;
- Zero fabricated licenses, approvals, scholars or Shariah claims;
- No material unresolved customer-money reconciliation break beyond the approved threshold;
- No investment launch without documented custody and withdrawal arrangements.

## Pause or stop criteria

Pause acquisition or product activity if:

- A material regulatory uncertainty emerges;
- A partner loses required authorization;
- Customer funds or assets cannot be reconciled;
- An unauthorized AI action occurs;
- A material Shariah or product-governance breach occurs;
- Fraud exceeds the approved threshold;
- Complaint or misunderstanding rates exceed the threshold;
- Withdrawals cannot be processed within disclosed terms;
- The downside economic case becomes unsustainable.

Complete an independent review after six months and before any second-market launch.

# 15. Principal risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Unlicensed personalized advice | Feature register, local counsel and partner supervision |
| Ambiguous startup/partner accountability | Contractual RACI and customer-facing entity disclosure |
| Overbroad Shariah claims | Product-level review, local governance and claims library |
| Scholarly or regulatory disagreement | Defined hierarchy, disclosure, relabeling and remediation |
| Mischaracterized cash, fees or rewards | Separate product labels, worked examples and governance review |
| Customer misunderstanding | Comprehension testing, conservative defaults and human review |
| Family coercion or unauthorized access | Consent, role-based permissions, safe support and revocation |
| Data-transfer breach | Data minimization, local assessment and partner controls |
| Fraud and account takeover | Strong authentication, transaction signing and monitoring |
| AI hallucinations | Approved knowledge base, deterministic calculations and escalation |
| Third-party AI vendor risk | Contractual data, security, audit and exit controls |
| Weak Indonesia economics | Wallet/employer partnerships, aggregation and cost controls |
| Partner failure | Portability, alternative providers, migration support and tabletop testing |
| Operational errors | Reconciliation, incident response and business continuity |
| Tax or reporting failures | Country-specific tax memo and RACI allocation |
| Market abuse or poor execution | Partner controls for order handling, allocation, best execution and surveillance |
| Regulatory change | Continuous monitoring and staged capital commitments |
| Excessive complexity | Narrow MVP and binding launch exclusions |

## Final recommendation

Proceed with a **single-market, evidence-gated pilot in 2026**, provisionally Malaysia, subject to the scorecard and no-go criteria.

After the first pilot:

1. Launch the UAE only after an independent readiness review.
2. Select either Saudi Arabia or Indonesia for 2027 based on regulatory readiness, partner capability, customer evidence, fraud performance and economics.
3. Defer Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar and Oman until a proven operating and partner model exists.
4. Offer one clearly governed savings product and one conservative diversified investment pathway.
5. Keep gold, takaful bundles, complex sukuk, automated zakat, customer-level purification and pooled family investing outside the MVP.
6. Use AI for explanation and routine support, not autonomous regulated decisions or religious determinations.
7. Scale only after demonstrating lawful authorization, customer comprehension, Shariah governance, reliable custody and reconciliation, operational resilience, responsible AI performance and sustainable country-level economics.
