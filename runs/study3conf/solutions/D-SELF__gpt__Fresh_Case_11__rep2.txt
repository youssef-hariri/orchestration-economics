# Cross-Border Payments Financial-Operations Strategy — 2026

## Executive summary

The firm should manage every **country pair, product, currency, payout method, customer segment, legal entity, and partner arrangement** as a separate regulated business. The EU, Gulf, Southeast Asia, and Latin America are useful planning groupings, but they are not legally or economically uniform.

The objective is not maximum geographic coverage. It is:

> **Lawful, recoverable, auditable, resilient, and profitable settlement capacity.**

### Portfolio recommendation

| Posture | Apply when | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| **Defend and expand** | Legal authority is confirmed; customer funds are protected; controls, settlement, liquidity, staffing, and economics are proven | Increase volume gradually, automate, diversify infrastructure |
| **Redesign** | Corridor is strategically valuable but constrained by licensing, currency, capital controls, payout method, partner, or liquidity | Narrow the product, change the funds flow or partner, reprice, add controls |
| **Digitally upgrade** | Legal and risk model are sound but manual processing, weak reconciliation, or poor status data limits scale | Invest in APIs, reconciliation, monitoring, data quality, and resilience |
| **Controlled growth** | Corridor is lawful but evidence, partner maturity, capacity, or economics are incomplete | Pilot with explicit caps, milestones, and expiry |
| **Constrain or suspend** | Legal, sanctions, safeguarding, settlement, liquidity, fraud, data, or partner risk is unresolved | Stop expansion or affected activity; protect existing customers |
| **Exit** | Activity cannot be conducted lawfully, safely, recoverably, or profitably after remediation | Execute an orderly wind-down |

No corridor should be approved solely because of volume, customer demand, quoted FX spread, or partner assurances. No alternative routing, currency substitution, netting, intermediary structure, or technology arrangement may be used to evade licensing, sanctions, reporting, foreign-exchange, AML, or capital-control requirements.

### Mandatory investment test

\[
\text{Launch or expansion}
=
\text{Legal permission}
\land
\text{Adequate controls}
\land
\text{Protected customer funds}
\land
\text{Resilient settlement}
\land
\text{Positive stressed economics}
\]

A strategic subsidy may relax ordinary economic requirements only. It cannot override legal, sanctions, safeguarding, customer-funds, data, consumer-protection, or minimum-liquidity requirements. Any subsidy requires board approval, a capped budget, named sponsor, milestones, monthly burn-rate reporting, expiry, and an exit decision.

# 1. Current-state baseline and decision discipline

Before approving specific expansion, management must produce a corridor dashboard covering every active route.

| Required field | Minimum information |
|---|---|
| Corridor identity | Origin, destination, product, currency, funding method, payout method, customer segment |
| Legal structure | Entity, license, regulator, partner authorization, license expiry and conditions |
| Funds flow | Customer receipt, FX, safeguarding, prefunding, settlement, payout, refund |
| Infrastructure | Bank, correspondent, clearing system, cloud, screening, fraud, and data dependencies |
| Economics | Volume, revenue, realized FX, fully loaded contribution, break-even volume |
| Liquidity | Average and peak prefunding, restricted balances, currency mismatch, counterparty exposure |
| Operations | Settlement distribution, finality, failure rate, reconciliation breaks, manual intervention |
| Risk | Fraud, AML, sanctions, customer-funds, data, consumer, and regulatory findings |
| Capacity | Compliance, treasury, operations, engineering, customer-support, and partner capacity |
| Decision | Defend, expand, redesign, upgrade, controlled growth, constrain, suspend, or exit |

The dashboard must also identify:

- Current control gaps and overdue remediation
- Partner, bank, correspondent, cloud, and vendor concentration
- Regulatory-capital and corporate-liquidity headroom
- Customer complaints, refunds, abandonment, and access effects
- Assumptions, evidence sources, owners, validation dates, and consequences if assumptions fail

Without this baseline, regional priorities are hypotheses rather than investment decisions.

# 2. Mandatory country-pair corridor dossier

No corridor may launch, materially expand, or continue unchanged after a material regulatory event without a current dossier.

## 2.1 Legal and regulatory analysis

The dossier must identify:

- Origin and destination jurisdictions and regulators
- Legal entities involved
- License, registration, passporting, branch, subsidiary, agent, or outsourcing basis
- Product classification, including remittance, payment service, e-money, wallet funding, FX, or merchant settlement
- Permitted customer, beneficiary, transaction-purpose, currency, payout, and value limits
- AML, sanctions, reporting, recordkeeping, safeguarding, and consumer-protection duties
- Capital-control and foreign-exchange requirements
- Data-localization and cross-border transfer requirements
- Tax and withholding treatment
- Customer cancellation, refund, complaint, and error-resolution obligations
- License expiry, renewal, conditions, and supervisory correspondence

Qualified local counsel must provide a written memorandum addressing the actual customer journey, marketing, onboarding, funds flow, FX, settlement currency, payout method, data architecture, reporting, refunds, and wind-down.

The memorandum must be refreshed after a material change in law, product, partner, currency, ownership, customer flow, or funds flow. Where reliable legal confirmation cannot be obtained, the default is non-launch or suspension. A pilot is permitted only if counsel confirms that the pilot itself is lawful.

# 3. Regional and corridor recommendations

These are **planning hypotheses**, not factual rankings. Final posture must be assigned from country-pair evidence.

## 3.1 European Union corridors

**Recommended posture:** Defend existing lawful corridors; expand only after entity, passporting, agent, safeguarding, consumer, AML, data, and banking analysis is complete.

Priorities:

- Confirm the relevant licensed entity and supervisory authority.
- Validate cross-border service or passporting arrangements.
- Maintain group minimum standards with country-specific annexes.
- Review local agent, distributor, safeguarding, complaints, refund, and reporting requirements.
- Avoid treating a group authorization or policy as automatically sufficient for every activity or destination.
- Use the EU as a potential control hub, but retain local accountability and local legal analysis.

**Expand:** Established digital or bank-account routes with proven settlement, complete data, reliable partners, and positive stressed economics.  
**Redesign:** Routes relying on unclear agent structures, weak local reporting, or centralized data processing not yet approved.  
**Constrain:** Cash-heavy, high-risk, or high-fraud products until customer, partner, and conduct impacts are measured.

## 3.2 Gulf corridors

**Recommended posture:** Country-by-country, partner-led expansion with strong attention to licensing classification, ownership or establishment rules, cash channels, currency, and correspondent dependencies.

Priorities:

- Determine whether the activity is remittance, payment services, e-money, money changing, wallet funding, cash payout, or another regulated activity.
- Confirm local establishment, ownership, agent, and outsourcing requirements.
- Use regulated banks or payment institutions for local receipt, conversion, and payout where required.
- Apply enhanced controls to cash-intensive transactions, complex beneficiaries, high-risk geographies, and unusual activity.
- Do not use informal settlement networks or unlicensed intermediaries.
- Map whether multiple partners actually share the same correspondent or clearing infrastructure.

**Expand:** Account-based or otherwise well-controlled routes with complete originator and beneficiary data and tested partner capacity.  
**Redesign:** Cash-heavy routes through narrower customer segments, lower limits, enhanced review, and improved reconciliation.  
**Constrain or exit:** Routes dependent on informal settlement, unclear licensing, or unavailable repatriation.

## 3.3 Southeast Asian corridors

**Recommended posture:** Selective digital expansion, but only after separate analysis of bank, wallet, cash, merchant, and FX products.

Priorities:

- Confirm whether each product is remittance, payment facilitation, e-money, wallet funding, FX, or merchant settlement.
- Validate where customer funds must be held and whether cross-border data processing is permitted.
- Confirm local transaction-purpose, beneficiary, documentation, agent, and reporting requirements.
- Do not assume that instant-payment connectivity establishes authorization, irrevocability, or finality.
- Require local-language customer support where applicable.
- Restrict high-value or instant products where partners cannot provide timely status and duplicate-payment controls.

**Expand:** Digital bank-account or wallet corridors with complete data, real-time risk controls, and stable settlement.  
**Upgrade:** File-based or manual routes where automation can reduce breaks and cost.  
**Constrain:** Cash payout, wallet funding, or routes with unresolved data-localization or customer-funds requirements.

## 3.4 Latin American corridors

**Recommended posture:** Prefer stable, bank-account corridors initially; treat volatile-currency, cash-out, and capital-controlled products as enhanced-control or pilot activities.

Priorities:

- Model inflation, devaluation, convertibility, local liquidity, repatriation, tax, and central-bank reporting.
- Treat local currency as restricted capital where conversion or repatriation is delayed or conditional.
- Validate refund obligations in the original currency.
- Apply strong beneficiary, account-ownership, mule-account, and rapid-cash-out controls.
- Price on realized FX after execution, hedging, devaluation, tax, and repatriation costs.

**Expand:** Stable bank-account routes with realizable FX economics and tested repatriation.  
**Redesign:** Volatile-currency routes through limited currencies, scheduled settlement, lower limits, and higher liquidity charges.  
**Constrain or exit:** Routes where funds may become trapped, refunds cannot be made, or capital controls make the proposed flow impracticable or unlawful.

## 3.5 Cross-region prioritization

In the absence of completed corridor data, the firm should prioritize investment in this order:

1. Remediation of existing corridors with confirmed legal permission and durable demand  
2. Automation and reconciliation upgrades where manual processing is the binding constraint  
3. Lower-risk products and account-based payout where customer-access effects are acceptable  
4. Additional payout rails in already approved jurisdictions  
5. Adjacent corridors using validated partners and infrastructure  
6. New jurisdictions through licensed partners  
7. Direct licensing or subsidiary investment only after durable demand and positive stressed economics  

No corridor should be expanded based solely on a regional label.

# 4. Compliance and control operating model

## 4.1 KYC and customer risk

The customer-risk methodology must document:

- Approved risk factors and weights
- Scoring and override rules
- Review intervals
- Event-driven refresh triggers
- Approval rights
- QA sampling
- Exit and restriction criteria
- Treatment of low-data or documentation-constrained customers

Relevant factors include residence, nationality, location, occupation, income, source of funds, source of wealth where appropriate, transaction purpose, frequency, value, velocity, beneficiary relationship, third-party funding, cash use, PEP status, adverse media, high-risk sectors and jurisdictions, prior alerts, fraud, and account compromise.

Track:

- Identity-verification defects
- Duplicate identities
- Stale records
- Beneficial-owner resolution
- EDD rejection
- Unresolved risk overrides
- Sample-testing defects
- Customer abandonment after KYC

## 4.2 AML monitoring

Monitor for:

- Structuring and aggregation
- Unusual frequency, velocity, or value
- Rapid movement and circular flows
- Unexpected corridor or beneficiary changes
- Third-party funding
- Common devices, accounts, beneficiaries, or addresses
- Inconsistent customer profiles
- High-risk purposes, sectors, or geographies
- Capital-control circumvention

The case process must document triage, investigation, evidence, decision, restriction, rejection, freezing, MLRO escalation, reporting, tipping-off controls, retention, and QA. Backlogs must be risk-weighted.

## 4.3 Sanctions

Maintain a sanctions applicability matrix by entity, employee, partner, bank, currency, correspondent, and payment route. Address:

- Applicable regimes
- Ownership and control
- Geographic and sectoral restrictions
- Blocking versus rejection
- General or specific license reliance
- Reporting obligations
- Blocked-funds handling
- Re-screening triggers

Screen customers, beneficial owners, originators, beneficiaries, banks, agents, counterparties, vendors, payment messages, and relevant geographic, sectoral, goods, vessel, and ownership risks.

The firm must have independent authority to stop or hold transactions. Missing mandatory data must be a processing gate, not an informal exception.

## 4.4 Fraud

Maintain a separate fraud risk appetite and loss limit. Use:

- Account-takeover detection
- Device, IP, geolocation, and behavioral analytics
- Step-up authentication
- Beneficiary confirmation
- Cooling-off periods
- Velocity and transaction-risk scoring
- Mule-account and network analysis
- Risk-based payout holds where lawful and disclosed
- Recovery, reimbursement, dispute, and partner-liability procedures

Fraud, AML, sanctions, and operational losses must be reported separately.

## 4.5 Customer restrictions and exits

Procedures must define:

- Permitted triggers
- Approval rights
- Funds return and refund treatment
- Customer communications
- Non-tipping-off requirements
- Complaints and appeals where applicable
- Re-onboarding rules
- Consistency and disparate-impact testing

Controls must not operate as unsupported proxies for nationality, ethnicity, residence, or socioeconomic status.

# 6. Settlement, safeguarding, treasury, and FX

## 6.1 Customer-funds protection

For each customer-funds or prefunding arrangement, confirm:

- Account title and beneficial ownership
- Legal segregation or safeguarding basis
- Permitted institution
- Insolvency treatment
- Setoff, lien, and security rights
- Reserve and collateral terms
- Withdrawal controls
- Refund and recovery process
- Regulatory reporting
- Access to funds after partner failure

A corridor cannot launch where customer funds cannot be traced, reconciled, refunded, and recovered with an acceptable insolvency and setoff profile.

Report separately:

- Safeguarded customer funds
- Corporate cash
- Partner prefunding
- Restricted local currency
- Regulatory capital
- Committed credit
- Unsecured partner exposure

Safeguarded funds and uncommitted partner balances are not corporate liquidity.

## 6.2 Settlement states

Distinguish:

1. Payment initiated  
2. Accepted by partner or rail  
3. Posted  
4. Funds available to beneficiary  
5. Irrevocable or final  
6. Return or recall period expired  
7. Customer obligation discharged  

Map each state to customer liability, refund eligibility, revenue recognition, liquidity release, fraud attribution, partner exposure, and reconciliation treatment. Technical acceptance is not necessarily final settlement.

## 6.3 Reconciliation

Material corridors should reconcile at least daily, and more frequently where exposure or settlement speed warrants. Reconcile:

- Customer ledger
- Bank or safeguarded account
- Partner ledger
- Payment-rail status
- FX and treasury ledger
- Returns, cancellations, chargebacks, and recalls
- Fees and reserves
- Refund obligations
- Unidentified receipts

Every break requires an owner, age, materiality, resolution target, root-cause analysis, and escalation. Finance, Treasury, Compliance, and Risk must approve maximum break age and value.

## 6.4 Currency and repatriation

Classify each currency as:

1. Freely convertible and repatriable  
2. Convertible subject to documentation or timing  
3. Convertible only through approved institutions  
4. Functionally trapped or highly restricted  
5. Not permitted for the proposed service  

Categories 2–4 are restricted capital.

Before relying on a currency, confirm permission to hold, convert, transfer, and repatriate it; approved institutions; documentation; refund currency; correspondent acceptance; hedging; tax; accounting; and settlement finality.

## 6.5 Liquidity methodology

The following is a preliminary planning approximation only:

\[
L_{\text{required}}
=
V_{\text{daily}}
\times D_{95}
\times (1+\text{stress buffer})
+
R_{\text{partner}}
+
E_{\text{unreconciled}}
+
H_{\text{FX}}
\]

Treasury must replace this with an approved methodology covering:

- Time-of-day cash-flow modeling
- Gross and net settlement
- Intraday, overnight, and peak-day exposure
- Weekends and holidays
- Volume and settlement-delay correlation
- Refunds, returns, recalls, and chargebacks
- Bank withdrawal limits
- Haircuts on restricted assets
- Partner default probability
- Reserve recoverability
- Repatriation delays
- Multi-corridor correlated stress
- Reverse stress testing
- Required confidence levels and stress horizons

For every corridor, Treasury must set:

- Maximum prefunding
- Maximum intraday and overnight exposure
- Maximum trapped-currency balance
- Maximum unsecured partner exposure
- Minimum stressed liquidity coverage
- Maximum reconciliation exposure
- Emergency funding source
- Escalation timing

## 6.6 FX

Use:

- Quote-validity periods
- Automatic repricing thresholds
- Currency and position limits
- Approved hedging counterparties
- Daily independent valuation
- Separate customer pricing and treasury execution
- Controls for thinly traded or restricted currencies
- Reconciliation of quoted, executed, and realized FX

Finance must define accounting, impairment, and management-reporting treatment for restricted balances, devaluation, repatriation delays, reserves, and potentially unrecoverable funds.

# 7. Partner and technology strategy

## 7.1 Partner due diligence

Assess:

- License scope and regulatory history
- Ownership and control
- AML and sanctions framework
- Agent and subcontractor controls
- Data and audit access
- Audited financial statements
- Capital, liquidity, and settlement capacity
- Reserves, collateral, liens, setoff, and insolvency exposure
- Settlement finality and reconciliation
- Data completeness
- Incident history and continuity
- API or file resilience
- Surge capacity
- Wind-down cooperation
- History of de-risking or account closures

Contracts must address regulated roles, customer-funds treatment, missing-data rejection, sanctions and AML obligations, fraud and operational-loss allocation, duplicate payments, settlement delays, customer remediation, reserves, recovery, subcontracting, change in law, termination, migration, and wind-down.

## 7.2 Partner economics waterfall

For every material provider, calculate:

1. Customer fees  
2. Realized FX revenue  
3. Partner and rail fees  
4. Fixed minimums  
5. Return, recall, failure, and chargeback fees  
6. Reserve and collateral opportunity cost  
7. Compliance and support costs  
8. Liquidity cost  
9. Fraud, default, and operational losses  
10. Allocated fixed costs  
11. Exit and migration costs  
12. Net contribution  

Stress-test partner repricing, reserve increases, service degradation, and termination.

## 7.3 Technical maturity

| Level | Capability | Permitted posture |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Manual or email processing | Pilot only; very low limits and dual control |
| 1 | Secure batch files and basic status | Low-to-moderate volume; no high-risk instant products |
| 2 | API or structured files with reconciliation | Standard products and controlled growth |
| 3 | Real-time screening, fraud, pricing, liquidity, and status | High-volume or faster payout products |
| 4 | Tested multi-rail failover and continuous reconciliation | Strategic core corridors |

At lower maturity levels, restrict instant payout, high-value payments, new beneficiaries, high-risk customers, and products requiring real-time sanctions or liquidity decisions.

Before production, require pass/fail testing for functional and negative cases, duplicate and retry scenarios, reconciliation, capacity, security, incident response, disaster recovery, regulatory reporting, rollback, and data recovery.

Define authoritative systems of record for customer balances, transaction status, screening, fraud, treasury, partner settlement, reporting, complaints, and refunds.

Expansion requires workload models for KYC, EDD, AML, sanctions, fraud, reconciliation, support, reporting, and incident response. Staffing, outsourcing, or throttling must occur before service levels fail.

Emergency manual processing must have lower limits, approved customer and beneficiary types, no new high-risk onboarding, dual approval, duplicate-payment prevention, a maximum duration, and return-to-normal criteria.

Pricing should reflect:

- Origin and destination
- Currency and volatility
- Funding and payout method
- Customer-risk tier
- Speed and service level
- Partner and banking cost
- Liquidity use
- Fraud and dispute exposure

# 9. Risk controls and scenario responses

## 9.1 Escalation triggers

### Immediate red triggers

- Legal authorization is unclear, lost, or exceeded
- Sanctions screening fails or mandatory transaction data is unavailable
- Customer funds cannot be safeguarded, traced, or refunded
- Settlement access is unlawful or materially unreliable
- Required liquidity or customer-funds coverage is unavailable
- Material capital-control prohibition takes effect
- Critical cyber, data, screening, or ledger failure occurs without safe fallback

### Amber triggers

- Contribution falls below floor
- Stress-case contribution deteriorates
- Prefunding, currency, or partner exposure approaches limit
- Reconciliation breaks age or increase
- Fraud, AML, sanctions, complaint, or manual-review metrics deteriorate
- Partner SLA or data-completeness breaches recur
- Staffing or technical capacity approaches threshold

Amber status requires an owner, deadline, remediation plan, and possible volume cap. Red status requires suspension or executive escalation.

## 9.2 Scenario playbooks

Each playbook must specify the trigger, owner, first-hour actions, same-day actions, customer treatment, regulatory notifications, liquidity response, restrictions, recovery criteria, and post-incident reviewer.

### Capital controls tighten

- Stop affected products or transactions pending legal validation.
- Reduce limits and customer eligibility where lawful.
- Use only confirmed currencies and channels.
- Require purpose documentation and system-enforced evidence checks.
- Recalculate liquidity, repatriation, FX, accounting, and refund exposure.
- Do not use alternative routing or informal settlement to avoid restrictions.

### Correspondent or payout bank exits

- Freeze new volume if settlement certainty is impaired.
- Activate only a legally approved and tested independent route.
- Prioritize customer-fund reconciliation and refunds.
- Notify customers and regulators where required.
- Assess reserve recovery, migration cost, and replacement pricing.
- Wind down if no lawful route remains.

### Fraud increases sharply

- Apply dynamic limits and step-up authentication.
- Introduce beneficiary cooling-off periods.
- Restrict vulnerable payout methods or customer cohorts where lawful.
- Review partner data, liability, and control performance.
- Measure fraud reduction against conversion, access, and complaint impact.

### Volume exceeds forecast

- Use preapproved caps and surge procedures.
- Increase liquidity only after Treasury approval.
- Route to tested secondary capacity.
- Prioritize lower-risk transactions.
- Add temporary compliance, operations, and support capacity.
- Suspend marketing or throttle volume if controls or settlement cannot scale.

### Common infrastructure failure

For cloud, screening-vendor, or shared correspondent failure:

- Stop affected products if screening, ledger, or settlement integrity cannot be assured.
- Use only approved emergency procedures with lower limits.
- Maintain independent data copies and manual reconciliation.
- Reconcile all transactions before returning to normal.
- Conduct a group-wide concentration and resilience review.

# 10. Governance and KPIs

## 10.1 Governance

### Corridor Approval Committee

Legal, Compliance/MLRO, Treasury, Risk, Operations, Finance, Product, Privacy, Information Security, and regional management. Approves launch, expansion, redesign, restriction, suspension, and exit.

### Treasury and Liquidity Committee

Approves currency, prefunding, counterparty, hedging, restricted-capital, stress, and emergency-funding limits.

### Partner Risk Committee

Approves banks, correspondents, payout providers, FX providers, agents, material subcontractors, and technology providers.

### Three lines of defense

1. Business and operations execute controls.  
2. Legal, compliance, risk, treasury, privacy, and security challenge and approve.  
3. Internal audit independently tests design and operation.  

Commercial targets cannot override legal, compliance, safeguarding, or customer-funds requirements.

## 10.2 KPI dictionary

Every KPI must have a definition, formula, scope, data source, owner, frequency, baseline, threshold, required action, and data-quality validation.

Board reporting should include:

### Financial

- Fully loaded and stressed corridor contribution

- Return on restricted capital
- Break-even volume
- Aggregate strategic subsidy
- Regulatory-capital utilization

### Customer funds and treasury

- Customer-funds coverage ratio, including legally recoverable coverage
- Safeguarding reconciliation completion
- Unreconciled balance and aging
- Settlement finality and beneficiary availability
- Repatriation delay by percentile
- Currency mismatch
- Prefunding utilization

# 11. Implementation roadmap

- Complete current-state corridor dashboard and inventory.
- Map legal entities, licenses, products, currencies, partners, correspondent dependencies, and customer-funds flows.
- Apply temporary hard stops to unresolved legal, sanctions, safeguarding, data, or settlement issues.
- Define dossier, KPI dictionary, risk appetite, and interim operating statuses.
- Produce preliminary fully loaded economics.
- Identify control, staffing, capital, technology, and concentration gaps.

## 30–90 days: validate and quantify

- Obtain written legal memoranda for priority corridors.
- Complete regulated-role matrices with accountable owners.
- Validate safeguarding, insolvency, setoff, reserve, refund, recovery, and repatriation treatment.
- Approve the liquidity and capital-allocation methodology.
- Complete partner economics waterfalls, concentration maps, and exit-cost models.
- Complete staffing, target-architecture, implementation-budget, and customer-impact plans.
- Test sanctions, reconciliation, data, incident, manual-fallback, and wind-down controls.
- Establish proposed numerical limits and KPI baselines.

## 90–180 days: remediate and pilot

- Replace or constrain deficient partners.
- Automate reconciliation and exception management.
- Implement capital-control, AML, sanctions, fraud, and pricing rules.
- Establish currency, liquidity, counterparty, prefunding, and reconciliation limits.
- Run controlled pilots with explicit customer, transaction, currency, and payout caps.
- Measure control effectiveness, staffing load, customer outcomes, and stressed economics.
- Certify production readiness for any upgraded integration.

## 6–24 months: selective scale

- Expand only after evidence supports the intended scale.
- Prioritize products and payout methods with reliable controls and settlement.
- Add cash, wallet, merchant, or high-volatility products only after separate approval.
- Build genuine route diversity where legally and economically feasible.
- Consider direct licensing or subsidiary investment only after durable demand, positive stressed economics, adequate corporate capital, and local capability are demonstrated.

# 12. Explicit assumptions requiring local validation

The following must not be treated as facts until validated:

- Existing licenses cover all products, currencies, customer journeys, and payout methods.
- Partner licenses cover assigned activity and customer-facing responsibilities.
- Local regulators accept the proposed agent, outsourcing, technology, or referral model.
- Customer funds are legally segregated and recoverable after partner insolvency.
- Reserves are accessible and not subject to adverse setoff or lien rights.
- Local currencies can be held, converted, refunded, and repatriated as modeled.
- A backup route is legally permitted, independently available, funded, and operationally tested.
- Centralized cloud, screening, data, and analytics arrangements are lawful.
- Instant-payment rails provide the assumed finality and beneficiary availability.
- Local banks will provide required accounts, correspondent access, and transaction data.
- Customer demand will withstand enhanced KYC, documentation, limits, delays, or payout restrictions.
- Staffing and partner capacity will scale without nonlinear increases in alerts, fraud, exceptions, or complaints.
- Realized FX margins will remain positive after execution, hedging, devaluation, tax, and repatriation.
- Regulatory-capital, safeguarding, tax, accounting, and customer-remediation requirements are affordable.
- Laws and supervisory expectations will remain stable throughout implementation.

Each assumption requires an owner, evidence source, expiry date, validation status, and contingency.

# 13. Board approval and decision rules

Before approving corridor expansion, the board should receive:

1. Current-state corridor dashboard  
2. Country-pair legal memorandum and license inventory  
3. Regulated-role allocation with accountable owners  
4. Fully loaded economics and expected-loss methodology  
5. Approved liquidity methodology and numerical limits  
6. Safeguarding, insolvency, setoff, reserve, refund, and recovery analysis  
7. Partner economics, concentration, contingency, and exit-cost analysis  
8. Staffing, technology, capital, liquidity, and implementation budget  
9. Customer-access and conduct-impact assessment  
10. Tested resilience, manual-fallback, suspension, and wind-down playbooks  
11. KPI dictionary, baselines, thresholds, and escalation actions  
12. Assumptions register and unresolved-issues log  
13. Decision record with conditions, expiry date, review date, and accountable executive  

### Decision rule

- **Expand** only when all mandatory gates pass and stressed economics are positive.
- **Redesign** when the corridor is lawful and valuable but the current product, partner, currency, or funds flow is not sustainable.
- **Constrain** when the corridor remains lawful but risk, capacity, or customer-impact concerns require caps.
- **Suspend** when legal permission, sanctions, safeguarding, settlement, data, liquidity, or control effectiveness is impaired.
- **Exit** when the activity cannot be made lawful, recoverable, resilient, or economically viable within an approved remediation period.

An orderly exit must reconcile balances, complete or refund permissible transactions, protect customer funds, communicate with customers and regulators where required, resolve complaints, preserve records, terminate contracts correctly, and complete a post-exit review.

## Final recommendation

The firm should pursue a deliberately uneven portfolio:

- **Defend and invest** in evidenced core corridors.
- **Redesign** strategically valuable but constrained routes.
- **Upgrade** technically immature operations where automation has a measurable business case.
- **Control or suspend** corridors with unresolved legal, capital-control, safeguarding, settlement, liquidity, sanctions, fraud, data, or partner exposure.
- **Exit** routes that cannot meet legal, customer-funds, resilience, or stressed-economic requirements.

This framework is suitable for executive and board review, but it does not itself approve corridor expansion. Specific growth decisions should wait until the required country-pair legal conclusions, current-state evidence, quantitative limits, resource plans, partner analysis, customer-impact assessment, and tested controls are complete.
