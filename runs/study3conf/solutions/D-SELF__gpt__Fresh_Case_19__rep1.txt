# Executive recommendation

Approve a **bounded, partner-led validation program—not a full three-country rollout**.

Enter India, Kenya, and Vietnam with an **advisory-first, cluster-based farm decision service**. Do not sell the existing enterprise sensor bundle directly to individual farmers. Reach farmers through cooperatives, FPOs, aggregators, irrigation groups, processors, and selected dealers; deploy sensors only where they improve a measurable decision.

Recommended sequencing:

1. **2026:** Validate one or two crop-region combinations in each country, with Vietnam as the leading candidate for first scale.
2. **2027:** Scale only the country-region-crop-partner combinations that achieve paid renewal, measurable farmer value, and sustainable servicing costs.
3. **2027–2028:** Expand Kenya through cooperative and climate-advisory playbooks; expand India selectively by state and crop, not nationally.
4. **Exit or pause** any country, partner, or use case that cannot demonstrate a credible payer and viable economics without permanent subsidy.

The winning proposition is not “more farm data.” It is:

> **A trusted local service that tells a farmer or field agent what to do next, through the channel they can use, at a cost justified by the outcome.**

# 1. Market assumptions and strategic implications

| Assumption | Product implication |
|---|---|
| Farms are small, fragmented, and heterogeneous | Sell cluster or block services; do not install a full kit on every plot |
| Seasonal income and liquidity are constrained | Use seasonal pricing, harvest deduction, or partner funding |
| Smartphone access varies by geography, gender, and household | Support agent, SMS, voice, USSD, WhatsApp, and app workflows |
| Farmers value outcomes, not raw data | Deliver one clear action per alert |
| Local agronomy is essential | Build country-region-crop playbooks with local validation |
| Hardware may be damaged, moved, or poorly installed | Invest in local agents, spare stock, diagnostics, and replacement |
| Buyers often capture as much value as farmers | Make aggregators, processors, cooperatives, and irrigation schemes explicit payers |
| Shared sensors are not automatically representative | Label recommendations as zone-, block-, reference-field-, or farm-specific |
| Forecast and sensor data will sometimes be missing | Provide conservative fallback advice and disclose data freshness |
| Partner capacity is uneven | Qualify partners on field staff, governance, payments, and maintenance—not farmer count alone |

Initial target users should be farmers producing irrigated, high-value, quality-sensitive, or contract crops through geographically concentrated organizations. Rainfed staple farmers remain an important reach segment, but should initially receive partner-funded advisory rather than sensor-heavy services.

# 2. Product strategy and strict MVP scope

## Launch MVP

The MVP should contain **three farmer-facing decisions** and a small partner operations layer.

### Farmer-facing priorities

1. **Weather and spray-timing alerts**
   - Delay spraying before rain
   - Avoid spraying in high winds
   - Prepare for heat, frost, flood, or heavy rainfall
   - Identify planting or harvest windows

2. **Crop-stage and task reminders**
   - Scout for pests
   - Check irrigation lines
   - Apply inputs at an appropriate stage
   - Prepare for harvest
   - Record a crop event

3. **Irrigation guidance in selected crops only**
   - Offered only where irrigation costs or crop value justify sensing
   - Advice expressed as an action window, not false precision  
   - Example: “Irrigate Plot 2 within 24 hours if no rain occurs.”

### Partner operations core

- Farmer, plot, crop, and season registration
- Local-language and channel preferences
- Consent capture
- Sensor and gateway assignment
- Device-health monitoring
- Missing or implausible data alerts
- Field-agent task lists
- Alert delivery and action tracking
- Basic cooperative, block, or buyer risk summaries

## Conditional add-ons

These should be introduced only when a paying partner identifies a measurable use case:

- Human-assisted pest and disease scouting
- Water-level or flow monitoring
- Satellite stress prioritization
- Traceability and quality reporting
- Salinity monitoring
- Procurement forecasts

## Explicit decisions

| Decision | Action |
|---|---|
| Weather and spray timing | **Prioritize** |
| Crop-stage reminders | **Prioritize** |
| Irrigation scheduling | **Prioritize selectively** |
| Human-assisted scouting | **Pilot as a partner service; do not automate fully** |
| Satellite imagery | **Adapt** for boundaries, flood, drought, and field-visit prioritization |
| Yield prediction | **Adapt** to cooperative or buyer planning with ranges |
| Soil analytics | **Adapt** to moisture trends and laboratory-test integration |
| Traceability | **Bundle through buyers/cooperatives where funded** |
| Raw farmer dashboards | **Drop** |
| Automated fertilizer prescriptions | **Defer** |
| Fully automated disease diagnosis | **Defer** |
| Dense sensor networks | **Drop from initial offer** |
| Machinery telemetry and optimization | **Drop** |
| Autonomous valves and pump control | **Defer** |
| Individual carbon accounting and digital twins | **Defer** |
| Field-level yield guarantees | **Drop** |

A feature should not enter the product unless it changes a decision within approximately 1–7 days, has a measurable baseline, and fits the farmer’s available labor, cash, and equipment.

# 3. Hardware simplification

## Recommended architecture

### Base shared-sensing kit

1. **Low-power gateway**
   - Short-range field connectivity such as LoRaWAN or an equivalent
   - Cellular backhaul selected by pilot geography
   - At least 30 days of local storage
   - Store-and-forward synchronization
   - Solar power or replaceable battery
   - Remote diagnostics
   - Water- and dust-resistant enclosure
   - Simple status indicators

2. **Reference weather sensing**
   - Temperature and humidity
   - Rainfall where local weather data is insufficient
   - Wind data sourced from a validated station or network where possible rather than installing costly stations everywhere

3. **One or two soil-moisture probes**
   - Only for irrigated or high-value pilot crops
   - Installed in representative fields or blocks
   - Thresholds calibrated by crop, soil, and region

4. **Installation and replacement kit**
   - Durable labels
   - Diagram-based instructions
   - Spare connectors and probes
   - GPS and installation-photo capture
   - Basic tamper and theft deterrence

## Deployment tiers

| Tier | Product | Default ownership | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Advisory | Weather, crop-stage, and task service with little or no field hardware | Company or partner service | Rainfed staples and broad cooperative reach |
| Shared sensing | Gateway and reference sensors serving a block or cluster | Cooperative, aggregator, or company lease | Irrigation groups and organized production |
| Premium precision | Additional soil, flow, EC, or fertigation sensing | Lease or financed purchase; avoid mandatory outright sale | High-value crops, greenhouses, larger smallholders |

A sensor must be identified in the system as **zone-level, block-level, reference-field, or farm-specific**. Advice based on a reference field must not be presented as precise farm-level measurement for every member.

## What to exclude initially

- Dense multi-depth soil networks
- Full weather stations at every cluster
- Cameras and autonomous pest traps
- Machinery telemetry
- Automated valves
- High-frequency sampling without a defined action
- Specialist-calibration hardware
- Proprietary components without local replacement options

### Hardware investment rule

No new sensor module should be built or purchased unless a paying partner can identify:

1. The decision it enables;
2. The user who will act;
3. The baseline outcome;
4. The expected economic benefit;
5. The installation and maintenance owner;
6. A payback period of no more than one or two crop seasons.

The company should field-test cellular versus gateway architectures, radio coverage, network availability, battery life, and installation time before committing to a single design.

# 4. Delivery partnerships and operating model

## Partner priorities

### 1. Cooperatives, FPOs, and irrigation groups

Use for:

- Cluster formation
- Shared hardware custody
- Farmer training
- Seasonal fee collection
- Irrigation coordination
- Aggregated crop and risk reporting

Require:

- Geographically concentrated members
- Active field staff
- A named hardware custodian
- At least two trained operators
- Payment or funding capability
- Data-consent and governance processes
- A post-pilot funding commitment

### 2. Aggregators, processors, and exporters

Prioritize where they can capture value from:

- Better quality
- Lower rejection
- Traceability
- More reliable supply
- Compliance
- Lower field-agent costs
- Improved procurement planning

These organizations may be stronger payers than farmers, particularly in export-linked crops.

### 3. Input and irrigation dealers

Use selectively for recruitment, installation, and local support. Do not allow dealer incentives to determine agronomic recommendations.

Controls:

- Separate advice logic from product catalogs
- Label sponsored offers
- Pay dealers for activation, maintenance, and retention—not only input sales
- Audit advice quality and complaints
- Require independent agronomic review
- Restrict product recommendations in early dealer-led pilots if conflicts cannot be controlled

### 4. Extension and development organizations

Use for demonstrations, trust, and reach, but do not make public funding essential to the long-term model.

## Operating responsibilities

| Activity | Company | Local partner |
|---|---|---|
| Core platform and analytics | Own | Provide requirements and feedback |
| Hardware specification and QA | Own | Install and custody |
| Farmer recruitment | Support | Own |
| Installation and first-line support | Train and monitor | Own |
| Local agronomic validation | Lead framework | Provide local experts |
| Payments | Enable systems | Collect or facilitate |
| Consent and data access | Define controls and audit | Explain and obtain consent |
| Device replacement | Maintain supply and policy | Execute locally |
| Model monitoring | Own | Report field anomalies |

Local service capacity is part of the product. Budget for installation, seasonal reactivation, agronomy, spare parts, travel, and replacement from the beginning.

# 5. Connectivity, literacy, and alert design

## Channel architecture

| Need | India | Kenya | Vietnam |
|---|---|---|---|
| Routine advisory | WhatsApp/app plus agent | SMS/WhatsApp plus agent | App/Zalo plus agent |
| Urgent alerts | SMS and voice | SMS, USSD, and voice | SMS and app/Zalo |
| Registration | Agent app, assisted | Agent app or USSD | Agent app |
| Scouting | Voice/photo through agent or WhatsApp | Voice/photo through agent or WhatsApp | App/Zalo/photo |
| Partner operations | Offline mobile app | Offline mobile app | Offline mobile app |

Do not build every channel for every geography in the first release. Validate channel use, cost, phone control, and literacy in the selected pilot locations.

## Offline requirements

- Sensor data cached locally
- Store-and-forward synchronization
- Offline agent registration and field actions
- Manual data entry and event capture
- Embedded language packs
- Low-bandwidth payloads
- SMS/IVR fallback for critical alerts
- Conservative advice when data is stale or missing

## Message requirements

Each message should answer:

1. What is happening?
2. Which crop or plot is affected?
3. What should the farmer do?
4. When should they do it?
5. Why does it matter?
6. What should they do if conditions differ?

Use one action per message, local units, familiar terminology, icons where tested, and voice for low-literacy users. Track:

- Delivery
- Comprehension
- Ability to act
- Actual action

“Unable to act” must be distinguished from “ignored” and “did not understand.”

# 6. Country-specific adaptations

## India: state-and-crop playbooks

**Decision:** Enter selectively; do not launch nationally in the first phase.

### Initial targets

- Irrigated horticulture
- Protected cultivation
- FPO-led crops
- Sugarcane or organized procurement
- Export-linked grapes, spices, and vegetables
- Micro-irrigation and fertigation users

### Priority features

- Monsoon and local rainfall alerts
- Spray timing
- Irrigation guidance
- Pest and disease scouting
- Crop-stage reminders
- FPO and field-agent dashboards
- Regional-language voice and messaging
- Agent-assisted registration and local payment methods

### Delivery

Use FPOs, cooperatives, capable input dealers, irrigation-equipment providers, and aggregators. Select two or three state-crop combinations with concentrated plots and a credible payer.

### Key adaptation

India offers the greatest long-term scale but the highest localization burden. Product, language, agronomy, and partner operations must be configured by state and crop.

---

## Kenya: cooperative-led climate and value-chain service

**Decision:** Advisory-first for rainfed staples; sensing concentrated in organized and higher-value production.

### Initial targets

- Horticulture
- Irrigation schemes
- Tea and coffee cooperatives
- Contract farming
- Climate-risk services for maize and other staples, funded by partners

### Priority features

- Rainfall and planting-window guidance
- Drought and heat alerts
- Spray timing
- Pest warnings
- Shared weather stations
- Cooperative dashboards
- Mobile-money collection
- Traceability and quality reporting where a buyer pays

### Delivery

Use cooperatives, agro-dealers, aggregators, NGOs, and county-level agricultural programs. Support English and Swahili initially, with SMS, USSD, IVR, WhatsApp, and agent workflows.

### Key adaptation

Kenya has strong mobile-money potential but more demanding service logistics. Do not deploy individual sensors broadly in low-margin rainfed systems.

---

## Vietnam: cooperative, irrigation-block, and buyer-funded precision

**Decision:** Make Vietnam the leading candidate for first scale, subject to pilot proof.

### Initial targets

- Rice irrigation and water management
- Mekong Delta flood and salinity risk
- Central Highlands coffee
- Fruit, pepper, and export-linked crops
- Traceability and residue/compliance programs

### Priority features

- Irrigation and water-level management
- Flood, drought, and salinity alerts
- Disease and spray timing
- Fertilizer-efficiency guidance
- Cooperative and irrigation-block dashboards
- Traceability and quality reporting
- Smartphone and Zalo integration

### Delivery

Use cooperatives, irrigation managers, processors, and export aggregators. Retain offline field workflows despite stronger smartphone and connectivity conditions.

### Key adaptation

Vietnam may offer the fastest route to buyer- or cooperative-funded services, but only where the product improves quality, compliance, procurement, or coordinated irrigation.

# 7. Economics and pricing logic

## Commercial principle

The product should be priced against the **decision and value captured**, not the number of sensors.

Possible units:

- Farmer per crop season
- Irrigated hectare or block
- Cooperative or service territory
- Contracted farmer
- Greenhouse or production block
- Quality or traceability program

Use seasonal payment, cooperative collection, mobile money, harvest deduction, or partner-funded access where appropriate.

## Illustrative economic model

Every pilot should calculate:

**Revenue per cluster**  
= number of paying farmers or hectares × seasonal price  
+ partner-funded value-chain fees  
+ eligible implementation or integration fees

**Contribution margin**  
= revenue  
− connectivity  
− installation and travel  
− agent compensation  
− agronomist support  
− cloud and messaging  
− replacement reserve  
− partner margin  
− collection losses

Model three scenarios:

| Scenario | Hardware intensity | Likely payer | Economic test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Advisory-only | Minimal | Cooperative, buyer, NGO, public program | Low cost per active farmer and high reach |
| Shared sensing | One gateway/reference fields | Cooperative, aggregator, irrigation group | Cluster utilization and measurable water/input/quality value |
| Premium precision | Field-specific sensors and support | Larger farmer, greenhouse, buyer | Customer payback within one or two seasons |

### Required commercial gates

Before scale, each offer must demonstrate:

- A named payer with renewal intent
- Payment completion, not only signed interest
- Positive farmer or buyer value after service cost
- Sustainable partner compensation
- A funded replacement reserve
- A clear hardware-custody model
- Declining subsidy dependence

Do not commit to universal impact targets such as 10–20% water reduction until validated by crop and region. Treat them as hypotheses.

# 8. Phased rollout and investment gates

## Phase 0: Discovery and design — 0–3 months

For each country:

- Select no more than two initial crop-region playbooks
- Identify at least two credible partners per playbook
- Validate payer and payment timing
- Test phone access, literacy, language, and channel use
- Field-test hardware and network architecture
- Define local agronomic thresholds
- Complete data, telecom, hardware, payment, and liability reviews
- Establish baseline comparison groups
- Build a full cost-per-active-cluster model

**Proceed only if:**

- A credible payer exists
- Farmers are geographically concentrated
- Partner service capacity is verified
- The use case has a measurable baseline
- Hardware and service costs fit the target economics

## Phase 1: Controlled pilots — 4–9 months

Run one or two playbooks per country, using a mix of:

- Advisory-only users
- Shared-sensing clusters
- Premium users where relevant
- Comparison groups

Test:

- Recommendation comprehension and action
- Installation and maintenance burden
- Data quality
- Agronomic accuracy
- Partner workload
- Payment behavior
- Farmer trust and consent
- Actual gross-margin or quality impact

## Phase 2: Paid validation — 10–18 months

Introduce:

- Seasonal contracts
- Full payment collection
- Certified partner training
- Replacement and refurbishment operations
- Limited scouting or traceability add-ons
- Premium sensing tiers

**Scale a playbook only if it meets all of the following:**

- At least 60% seasonal retention or paid renewal intent
- At least 70% comprehension of tested alerts
- At least 50% action rate on relevant recommendations
- Positive or clearly improving farmer gross margin or buyer economics
- Partner contribution margin improves toward sustainability
- Sensor and gateway reliability is acceptable for the decision window
- No material unresolved data or agronomic liability issue

Targets should be refined after the first season, but gates must be agreed before results are known.

## Phase 3: Selective scale — 19–36 months

Scale only proven combinations of:

> Country + region + crop + partner + decision + price + service model

Add buyer, input, irrigation, or financing integrations only when they improve farmer value and do not create undisclosed data or advice conflicts.

# 9. Data governance and responsible use

Implement a country-specific framework covering:

- Farmer and partner consent
- Data controller and processor roles
- Farm-level access permissions
- Data retention and deletion limits
- Cross-border transfers and localization
- Government or buyer access
- Security incident response
- Data export and correction
- Use in credit, insurance, procurement, or input sales

Separate consent for:

1. Service delivery;
2. Agronomic improvement;
3. Aggregated benchmarking;
4. Commercial or research use.

Do not promise unrestricted deletion or ownership rights that cannot be honored technically or legally. Explain plainly what is collected, who can see it, whether participation affects other services, and what happens when a partnership ends.

# 10. Principal risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Hardware cost exceeds value | Advisory-only tier, shared sensing, selective premium kits |
| Sensor does not represent surrounding plots | Reference-field rules, granularity labels, conservative recommendations |
| Poor installation invalidates data | Agent certification, installation photos, plausibility checks |
| Connectivity or telecom changes | Store-and-forward, multiple network options, SMS/voice fallback |
| Battery, theft, flooding, or vandalism | Replacement reserve, asset custody, local spares, tamper evidence |
| Forecast or model error | Multiple data sources, local validation, uncertainty-aware advice, rollback |
| Advice causes agronomic or financial harm | Local agronomists, conservative thresholds, escalation and liability review |
| Alert fatigue | Severity ranking, message consolidation, frequency limits |
| Dealer bias | Independent agronomy, disclosure, service-based incentives, audits |
| Farmers cannot act despite understanding | Track labor, cash, equipment, and access constraints; offer alternatives |
| Weak partner capability or insolvency | Due diligence, minimum staffing, scorecards, backup partners |
| Data is perceived as surveillance | Plain-language consent, role-based access, no undisclosed monetization |
| Regulatory or import barriers | Country legal review before hardware commitment |
| FX, inflation, or supply-chain volatility | Local sourcing options, modular replacement, scenario pricing |
| Seasonal churn | Crop-cycle reactivation, partner contracts, reminders before next season |
| Product becomes too customized | Common platform core and limited crop playbooks |
| Subsidy ends | Scale only after paid renewal and declining subsidy share |

# 11. Success metrics

## Farmer and agronomic outcomes

- Recommendation comprehension
- Recommendation action rate
- Percentage unable to act because of cash, labor, equipment, or access
- Irrigation events or pumping cost in suitable crops
- Avoided or better-timed applications
- Pest or weather loss reduction
- Yield, quality, rejection, or harvest-timing improvement
- Incremental farmer gross margin after service fees
- Seasonal retention and paid renewal

## Commercial

- Paid activation rate
- Collection rate
- Revenue and gross margin by package
- Cost per active farmer and cluster
- Partner renewal rate
- Hardware payback period
- Replacement reserve adequacy
- Subsidy share of revenue
- Share of value captured by farmer versus buyer

## Operational and technical

- Gateway and sensor reporting reliability
- Data completeness and freshness
- Installation time and error rate
- Sensor displacement or misinstallation rate
- Replacement rate and resolution time
- Agent workload and travel cost
- Support tickets per 100 active farmers
- Percentage of recommendations based on current measured data versus modeled data

## Trust and governance

- Consent comprehension
- Unauthorized-sharing complaints
- Data-access requests resolved
- Partner audit completion
- Farmer trust score
- Security incidents
- Opt-out rates by data-use category

# Explicit leadership decisions

## Prioritize

- Advisory-first product
- Weather and spray timing
- Crop-stage reminders
- Selective irrigation guidance
- Shared gateways and reference sensors
- Offline operation
- Local agents and agronomic validation
- Partner-funded seasonal packages
- Vietnam as likely first scale candidate, subject to evidence
- Country-region-crop playbooks

## Adapt

- Satellite analytics
- Yield prediction
- Soil analytics
- Traceability
- Mobile payments
- Existing enterprise APIs and data models
- Hardware architecture after field testing

## Defer

- Automated fertilizer recommendations
- Fully automated disease diagnosis
- Autonomous irrigation
- Complex carbon accounting
- Digital twins
- Dense sensing
- Broader country expansion

## Drop

- Direct sale of the enterprise bundle to average smallholders
- Raw farmer sensor dashboards
- Large-farm machinery workflows
- High-frequency data with no decision use
- Precision maps requiring unavailable equipment
- Field-level yield guarantees
- Any feature or sensor lacking a defined payer, action, and measurable outcome

## Final recommendation

Fund a tightly scoped validation program with **limited country and crop exposure, explicit economic gates, and an option to pause or exit**. Build the shared platform and service operating model first; expand hardware and advanced analytics only after farmers act on recommendations, partners renew paid contracts, and the resulting economics remain viable after installation, support, replacement, and localization costs.
