## PART 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Strategic Context & Urgency

An industrial-equipment manufacturer currently consolidated in a single East Asian hub faces a critical 2026 tariff realignment that will impose **€4.2–6.8M annual tariff exposure** under current MFN rates. Simultaneous rules-of-origin (RoO) tightening in CPTPP, RCEP, and bilateral trade agreements creates an **18-month window (Q4 2024 – Q4 2025)** to establish a diversified ASEAN manufacturing footprint that achieves **70%+ ASEAN-origin content eligibility**, unlocking **€4.8–6.6M in annual tariff savings by 2027+**.

## PART 4: INTEGRATED TARIFF, SUPPLY CHAIN & ORGANIZATIONAL FEASIBILITY ANALYSIS

### 4.1 Rules-of-Origin (RoO) Compliance Pathway

#### The RoO Challenge

**Rule:** CPTPP requires 70%+ ASEAN-origin content for preferential tariff rates.

**Current state (Thailand hub alone):** Only 40–50% ASEAN content (rest Japan, Korea, EU) → **Ineligible for CPTPP preferential rates** → Pays full MFN (12–15% to EU; 12–18% to US)

**Target state (3-country model):** 75–80% ASEAN content → **CPTPP-eligible** → Qualifies for preferential rates (0–3% to EU; 5–8% to US via RCEP re-export)

#### Content Sourcing by Site

| BOM Category | % of Cost | Thailand (Current) | Vietnam (Target) | Malaysia (Target) | ASEAN % (Target) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Motors/Drives (28%)** | 28% | Japan 70% | Mitsubishi Vietnam 60% | — | 60% ASEAN |
| **Hydraulics (18%)** | 18% | Japan 60%, Thailand 25% | Thailand/Malaysia 80% | Malaysia 95% | 80% ASEAN |
| **Electronics (15%)** | 15% | Japan/Korea 50%, Thailand 40% | Vietnam 90% | Malaysia 80% | 85% ASEAN |
| **Structural/Castings (16%)** | 16% | Thailand 50%, Japan 40% | Vietnam 80% | Malaysia 70% | 75% ASEAN |
| **Fasteners/Consumables (8%)** | 8% | Thailand/Indonesia 85% | Vietnam 90% | Malaysia 85% | 87% ASEAN |
| **Assembly Labor & Overhead (15%)** | 15% | Thailand 100% | Vietnam 100% | Malaysia 100% | 100% ASEAN |
| **BLENDED ASEAN CONTENT** | **100%** | **51%** | **85%** | **88%** | **76% (CPTPP-Eligible)** |

**Origin certification mechanism:**
- Each source supplier issues Customs Form D (CPTPP origin certificate) documenting ASEAN origin
- Thailand hub aggregates inputs; documents value-add (assembly, testing, packaging)
- Final product certified as CPTPP-eligible origin if 70%+ ASEAN content threshold met
- Transfer pricing: Hub procures inputs at cost + 5% markup; charges satellites at arm's-length equivalent to prevent customs disputes (contingency: third-party customs broker reviews quarterly; €50K annual cost)

#### Phase-In Timeline (Risk of Tariff Cliff)

| Phase | Timeline | RoO Compliance % | Tariff Status | Action Items |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase A: Transition (Q4 2024 – Q3 2025)** | Q4 2024 – Q3 2025 | 40–50% (current state) | MFN rates (12–15% EU, 12–18% US) | Supplier qualification; facility prep; no tariff relief yet |
| **Phase B: Interim (Q4 2025 – Q2 2026)** | Q4 2025 – Q2 2026 | 70–75% (interim suppliers from Mitsubishi, Thailand transship) | CPTPP preferential rates IF documentation approved by customs | Pilot production; parallel validation; origin certification setup |
| **Phase C: Mature (Q3 2026+)** | Q3 2026+ | 80–85% (Vietnam local + Malaysia suppliers mature) | Full CPTPP preferential rates + cost optimization | Scale Vietnam to 25–30%; Malaysia to 12–15%; realize full tariff savings + cost arbitrage |

**Risk if Phase B completion delayed >6 weeks:** Miss Q4 2025 RoO compliance window → Continue paying MFN rates through early 2026 → Lose €1.6–2.4M in annual tariff savings for 12 months.

**Mitigation:** Negotiate Mitsubishi Vietnam motor assembly MOU by Q3 2024 (6-month lead time to supply readiness); parallel qualify 2+ alternate motor suppliers (Vietnam local) by Q2 2025; dual-source during transition to de-risk supplier capability gaps.

### 4.2 Supply-Chain Resilience & Inventory Optimization

#### The Inventory Trade-Off

**Challenge:** Geographically distributed manufacturing creates inventory complexity:
- **Centralized supply model** (all suppliers serve all sites) → Lower total inventory; higher logistics cost
- **Decentralized model** (each site sources locally) → Faster replenishment; higher inventory safety stock per site

**Strategy resolution:** **Hybrid approach** with centralized demand planning + localized execution

| Function | Model | Inventory Impact | Logistics Cost | Decision Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **High-cost commodities (motors, hydraulics, controls; 45% of BOM cost)** | Centralized VMI (vendor-managed inventory); suppliers stock at regional hubs (Thailand primary, Malaysia secondary) | –5–8% total inventory (hub consolidation benefit) | +2–3% (longer transit times, but consolidated shipments) | Hub allocates demand across satellites; suppliers replenish centralized pools |
| **Mid-cost parts (fasteners, seals, consumables; 32% of BOM cost)** | Local sourcing (Vietnam/Malaysia source locally; reduce inbound lead times) | +3–5% (higher safety stock per site; no consolidation) | –8–10% (shorter distances) | Satellites manage local suppliers; hub coordinates commodity pricing |
| **Direct labor + assembly overhead (23% of BOM cost)** | Satellite-managed | 0 (labor cost, not inventory) | — | Sites optimize local labor utilization |
| **BLENDED IMPACT** | Hybrid | **±0 to –3% net** (modest optimization vs. status quo) | **–2–4% net** (logistics savings exceed local inventory premium) | **Net working-capital benefit: €400K–1.2M** (1–2% of €20–60M manufacturing pool) |

**ERP synchronization (critical enabler):**
- Centralized MRP system (single instance) calculates global demand forecast
- Hub allocates demand to satellites per capacity plan (Thailand 50%, Vietnam 25%, Malaysia 12%, remaining inventory buffer)
- Satellites execute production; inventory management tied to actual shipment schedule
- Bi-weekly reconciliation (demand vs. actual orders; adjust allocation if variance >10%)

**Capex requirement:** €1.2–1.8M (ERP system; phased rollout Q4 2024 – Q2 2026)

### 4.3 Quality Continuity & Risk Mitigation

#### Quality Assurance Model (Multi-Site Defect Control)

**Baseline assumption:** Industrial-equipment manufacturing requires <2% defect rate; <1% critical defects (safety-related, field failures).

**Challenges in distributed model:**
1. **Hub quality staff dilution:** Embedded engineers at satellites reduce hub QA capacity by 15–25% (extended audit cycles)
2. **Local team capability:** Vietnam/Malaysia teams less experienced in hub company's standards; rework rates +3–5% initially
3. **Supply-chain variability:** New suppliers (Vietnam/Malaysia) have inconsistent quality; acceptance testing cycles extend 1–2 weeks per supplier

**Mitigation architecture:**

| Quality Checkpoint | Responsibility | Frequency | Success Metric | Contingency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Incoming Material Inspection** | Satellite QA + centralized sampling (hub spot-checks 5–10% of received batches) | Continuous; 100% for new suppliers (first 10 shipments) | ≤0.5% defects on received materials | Reject lot; source alternate supplier or import from Japan (premium cost) |
| **In-Process Quality** | Satellite QA + real-time IoT defect tracking (SPC charts uploaded to central dashboard) | Shift-basis (8-hour intervals) | Process capability index (Cpk) ≥1.33 maintained | Halt line; escalate to hub process engineering; parallel run with hub until resolution |
| **Final Assembly Inspection** | Satellite QA (100% inspection for pilot SKUs; 10% statistical sampling for mature runs) | Per-unit (pilot); shift-basis (mature) | ≤2% defects on final assembly | 100% rework; delay shipment if defect >3% on lot |
| **Customer-Return Analysis** | Hub Quality (centralized repository of all field returns) | Monthly review (hub + satellite quality leads) | ≤1% field return rate; zero critical safety failures | Root-cause investigation; design/supplier corrective actions; escalate to VP Quality + COO if trending |

**Hub QA resource model (revised):**
- **Phase 1–2 (2024–2025):** 20 FTE (full staff; support facility setup & supplier qualification)
- **Phase 3 Pilot (Q4 2025–Q1 2026):** 19 FTE hub + 1 rotating audit engineer + 2–3 temporary contract QA resources (bridge for capacity gap) = **Net 5 FTE deployed but no bottleneck**
- **Mature state (Q4 2026+):** 6 FTE hub (centralized audits) + 3 Vietnam QA + 4 Malaysia QA = **13 FTE total** (vs. 20 baseline; net 7 FTE reduction = €700K annual savings)

**Capex investment:** €400K–600K (QA infrastructure: defect tracking system, SPC software, audit protocols, training)

**Temporary staffing contingency:** €300K–500K (6-month contract QA resources during Phase 3 pilot; reduces quality risk to acceptable <1% field return threshold)
