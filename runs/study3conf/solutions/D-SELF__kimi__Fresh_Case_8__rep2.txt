**PARTNERSHIP STRATEGY FOR CBAM RESILIENCE**
*Preserving Supply Relationships and Shared Competitiveness Through the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism*
*Final Strategy Document | Board & C-Suite Review*

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) enters its full financial phase in January 2026. For our supply base in India, Turkey, and North Africa, this is not a routine compliance exercise; it is an existential margin and geopolitical shock. Default carbon values imposed on suppliers with thin profitability—already viewed by many as disguised protectionism—will destroy long-standing commercial relationships unless we act decisively.

This strategy replaces transactional audit-and-enforcement with a **Capital, Advocacy, and Local Agency** model. We will absorb unrecoverable transition costs in Year 1, build permanent regional verification and governance capacity rather than importing rotating European expertise, and advocate publicly for recognition of our suppliers’ domestic carbon costs. In doing so, we convert CBAM from an external imposition into jointly owned competitive infrastructure.

**The Board is asked to approve three immediate commitments:**
1. A **non-recoverable 2026 Transition Grant** covering 100 percent of CBAM certificate costs for compliant strategic suppliers, booked as a Scope 3 investment rather than supplier debt.
2. A **90-Day Red Team stress-test** with compensated supplier representatives to validate financial and governance architecture before capital deployment.
3. The **Critique-and-Construct Doctrine**—mandating that all buyer representatives openly acknowledge CBAM’s asymmetric burdens and our active efforts to mitigate them.

---

## I. STRATEGIC CONTEXT: THE 2026 INFLECTION POINT

CBAM rewrites the economics of EU-bound steel and aluminum. Our suppliers face a convergence of technical, financial, and political risks that vary sharply by region.

| Risk Dimension | India | Turkey | North Africa |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Technical Readiness** | Top-tier mills capable; MSMEs and secondary aluminum lagging; missing upstream Scope 3 data | Moderate-high EU regulatory familiarity; patchy MRV consistency; scrap mass-balance documentation inadequate | Fragmented; Morocco advanced on renewables but lacks MRV; Tunisia/Egypt/Algeria lack corporate-level emissions boundaries |
| **Political Narrative** | Protectionism framing dominant; domestic carbon market (CCTS) creates “double-payment” grievance; WTO disputes likely | Customs Union alignment without co-determination fuels sovereignty resentment; anti-dumping history compounds distrust | “Green glass ceiling” perception; CBAM seen as exclusion from EU industrial policy; resource nationalism in Algeria |
| **CBAM Exposure** | High grid emissions (indirect); coal DRI/BOF dominance; thin exporter margins (3–6%) | EAF scrap advantage real but unproven origin data undermines it; scrap price volatility compresses margins | Natural gas dependency; Euro-revenue vs. Dollar-cost FX mismatch; currency volatility deepens liability risk |
| **Relationship Fragility** | High; strategic autonomy (*Atmanirbhar Bharat*) rejects external tutelage; family-owned mills require CEO-to-CEO trust | Moderate-High; engineering pride demands co-design, not capacity-building; TÇÜD expects peer-level engagement | High; patron-client networks govern authorization; rotating foreign auditors read as surveillance |

**Central Judgment:** A significant portion of our supplier base—particularly Indian MSMEs and North African finishers—cannot achieve actual-emissions reporting by 2026 due to verifier shortages and missing upstream data. If we demand full actual-emissions compliance as the price of partnership, we will trigger the exact supplier attrition and supply fragmentation we seek to avoid.

---

## II. CORE STRATEGIC THESIS

We will treat CBAM compliance as a co-invested supply-chain utility, offsetting inherent power asymmetries through three binding commitments:

1. **Capital Sacrifice First.** In 2026, CBAM certificate costs for compliant suppliers will be treated as a **non-recoverable transition grant**, not a loan. We will not extract repayment via rebates that wipe out already-thin margins.
2. **Permanent Local Capacity.** We will co-fund the accreditation of regional verification bodies and establish permanent buyer governance offices in Mumbai, Istanbul, and Casablanca, led by senior regional directors with decision rights.
3. **Honest Advocacy.** We will openly acknowledge CBAM’s protectionist effects and advocate—jointly with supplier associations—for recognition of explicit carbon prices paid in India, Turkey, and North Africa. We will fight the unfairness, not deny it.

## III. THE FIVE STRATEGIC PILLARS

### **Pillar 1: Technical Enablement & MRV Architecture**
*Goal: Maximize actual-emissions reporting by 2027 while creating a credible, non-punitive pathway for suppliers who must rely on default values during the transition.*

- **Regional Verifier Accreditation & Co-Investment.** Rather than deploying scarce EU-accredited teams on rotation, we will co-fund the CBAM-specific accreditation of regional verification firms (e.g., TÜV India, Bureau Veritas Turkey, Moroccan institutes via mutual recognition pathways). We will guarantee minimum audit volumes to ensure economic viability. *Target: 60% of verifications performed by regionally based, EU-recognized bodies by end of 2027.*
- **The Default Value Bridge.** Suppliers on a credible 24-month actual-emissions roadmap—but unable to meet 2026 requirements—will receive Year 1 grant support and technical assistance even while using EU default values, provided they achieve quarterly MRV milestones (IT infrastructure live, energy metering installed, upstream data requests issued). This prevents market exit and supply fragmentation.
- **Sector-Specific MRV Tracks.**
  - **Aluminum (All Regions):** Prioritize **firmed renewable power documentation** (24/7 baseload with storage or green hydrogen backup). Purely solar-captive proposals are insufficient for smelting load profiles and will be rejected.
  - **Steel – Turkey:** Mandate **scrap provenance digitization** (blockchain or audited chain-of-custody) as a prerequisite to any EAF efficiency investment. Without documented scrap origin and mass-balance, EU authorities will disallow CBAM liability reductions.
  - **Steel – North Africa:** Launch a **5-Year Embedded Technical Assistance Consortium** led by EBRD/IFC, beginning with **ISO 14064-1 corporate-level boundary setting** before advancing to plant-level MRV. Premature plant-level auditing fails when corporate data governance is absent.
- **Digital Twinning Platform.** Deploy a cloud-based emissions monitoring dashboard compatible with the EU CBAM Transitional Registry. Critical safeguard: **supplier-owned data sovereignty.** Raw operational data remains under supplier encryption keys; the buyer acts as data trustee for CBAM submission only.

### **Pillar 2: Financial Co-Investment & Carbon Price Risk Sharing**
*Goal: Prevent CBAM costs from destroying supplier margins or triggering market exit, while eliminating any perception of debt-trap conditionality.*

- **2026 Transition Grant (Non-Recoverable).** For all strategic suppliers enrolled in MRV (actual or Default Value Bridge), the buyer will **absorb 100% of CBAM certificate costs in Year 1 as a non-recoverable grant**, booked as a Scope 3 supply-chain investment. *Rationale: Repayment via delivery rebates on 3–6% Indian steel margins or FX-mismatched North African operations is commercially impossible and politically toxic.*
- **2027–2028 Shared Liability.** Shift to a 50/50 cost share. The buyer’s share continues as a Scope 3 investment; the supplier’s share is recovered through a modest incremental delivery adjustment capped at 1.5% of invoice value to preserve cash flow.
- **FX Stabilization for North Africa.** Bundle Euro/USD hedging into the transition facility, or reprice non-CBAM contract components in USD (the natural import currency) with CBAM euro liability locked at contract-signing exchange rates.
- **Green Premium Discipline.** Offer **Green Premium Offtake Contracts (2–4% above market)** only after securing back-to-back agreements with at least two major downstream EU customers. If downstream markets resist premium pass-through, restructure the incentive as a **Carbon Compliance Credit**—a marketing and Scope 3 attribution benefit for the downstream customer that requires no price escalation. *We will not promise upstream premiums we cannot recover.*
- **Abatement Co-Financing.** Pool capital with IFIs for region-specific decarbonization:
  - **India:** Firmed renewable PPAs with storage; green hydrogen direct-reduction pilots for steel.
  - **Turkey:** Scrap sorting and provenance infrastructure; EAF pre-heating only after scrap chain-of-custody is secured.
  - **North Africa:** Solar captive plus storage for Moroccan aluminum; natural gas efficiency for Egyptian steel with a mandatory, public 2030 roadmap to lower-carbon feedstocks.

### **Pillar 3: Commercial Structuring for Shared Competitiveness**
*Goal: Ensure CBAM costs do not render our joint supply chain uncompetitive while respecting scrap sovereignty and regional logistics realities.*

- **Transparent Pass-Through Formula.** *Final Price = Base LME/Platts Index + Verified CBAM Liability – Year 1 Grant Offset – Shared Efficiency Credit.* The Year 1 Grant Offset is explicitly itemized on invoices to signal that the buyer absorbed the initial shock.
- **Regional Scrap Ecosystem Mapping (Pre-Loop).** Before implementing “closed-loop” aluminum scrap returns, conduct a scrap sovereignty and logistics audit. Turkey competes globally for scrap; North African ports lack reliable scrap import logistics. In Turkey, secure long-term scrap offtake rights through partnerships with integrated yards before funding sorting technology. **Abandon closed-loop ambitions for North Africa initially** and focus on primary production efficiency and renewable baseload.
- **Circular Supply – India.** For Indian BOF steelmakers, incremental scrap injection requires furnace retrofitting. Offer technical co-design only where metallurgically viable and CBAM-advantageous.
- **Regional Competitiveness Clusters.** Facilitate cross-supplier knowledge sharing (e.g., Turkish EAF scrap governance expertise with Indian billet suppliers), framed as **peer-to-peer engineer exchanges**, not buyer-mandated tutorials.

### **Pillar 4: Narrative Alignment & Diplomatic Shielding**
*Goal: Replace discredited “neutrality” with honest, trust-building advocacy that acknowledges protectionism and fights it substantively.*

- **The “Critique-and-Construct” Doctrine (Mandatory).** All buyer representatives must acknowledge CBAM’s asymmetric burdens explicitly. Approved script: *“CBAM imposes asymmetric costs on our partners. We do not agree with all of its design, and we are investing in your compliance while advocating for fairer rules.”* This builds more trust than feigned neutrality.
- **Carbon Price Parity Fund.** Commission independent legal and econometric analysis demonstrating that explicit carbon costs borne by suppliers—India’s CCTS levies, Turkey’s national ETS obligations, Egypt’s energy subsidy reform implicit costs—should qualify for CBAM rebate equivalence. **Publish this analysis jointly** with TÇÜD, CII, and relevant North African associations, and submit it to EU lawmakers as a joint position. This is the strongest anti-protectionism signal available.
- **Local Advocacy Partnerships.** Fund supplier participation in EU-India, EU-Turkey, and EU-North Africa Business Councils to demand explicit recognition of supplier-country carbon pricing and faster mutual recognition of regional verification bodies.
- **Neutrality Protocol – Repealed.** Replaced in its entirety by the Critique-and-Construct Doctrine.

### **Pillar 5: Governance, Cultural Protocols & Supplier Exit**
*Goal: Calibrate support intensity to technical readiness and cultural context, preserving relational capital even when commercial paths diverge.*

- **Permanent Regional Governance Offices.** Establish staffed partnership hubs in Mumbai, Istanbul, and Casablanca led by **senior regional directors with P&L authority and local industry credibility.** These hubs have authority to approve MRV investments, resolve disputes, and navigate local authorization processes (e.g., Egyptian industrial data-sharing permits, Turkish Customs Union filings, Indian state-level DISCOM negotiations).
- **Cultural Engagement Protocols.**
  - **India:** Replace “CBAM Academy” branding with **Anchor-Led Technical Exchange.** Require top-tier anchors to host training within their own facilities, led by Indian engineers. Buyer senior executives must conduct repeated CEO-to-CEO site visits; mid-tier family-owned mills will not accept subordinate “student” status.
  - **Turkey:** Frame all engagements as **co-design.** Buyer technical staff must present proposals as drafts for Turkish peer review, not finished protocols.
  - **North Africa:** Empower a **permanent local senior representative** with community credibility and a long-term mandate. Rotating expatriate auditors or quarterly relationship managers will be perceived as surveillance and will fail to secure bureaucratic approvals.
- **“Exit with Dignity” Protocol.** For suppliers who demonstrate good-faith MRV effort but conclude EU export economics are untenable, the buyer offers: (1) an orderly volume wind-down with 18-month notice; (2) non-exclusive licensing of MRV/digital systems for domestic/regional use; and (3) first-right-of-refusal if EU border adjustments relax or breakthrough abatement is achieved.

## IV. DIFFERENTIATED REGIONAL PLAYBOOKS

### **India: Anchor-Led Cascade with Strategic Autonomy**
*Context: CCTS operationalization creates a legitimate “double payment” grievance. Top-tier majors are globally competitive, but mid-tier exporters and MSMEs face collapse under default values.*

- **Acknowledge Double-Payment Risk.** Explicitly recognize India’s CCTS in all contracts and joint advocacy. Position the buyer as supporting India’s domestic carbon market development, not circumventing it.
- **Anchor Partnerships.** Integrate with 2–3 anchors to co-develop CBAM reporting templates shared via **Indian-hosted peer exchanges**, not buyer-run academies.
- **Power & Indirect Emissions.** Co-procure **firmed renewable PPAs with storage**; reject intermittent open-access models vulnerable to state-level DISCOM reversal.
- **MSME Bridge.** Aggressive use of Default Value Bridge Contracts for rolling mills and secondary aluminum to prevent mass exit to non-compliant spot traders.

### **Turkey: Co-Design Integration**
*Context: Customs Union creates regulatory familiarity but also sovereignty grievance. EAF advantage is real, but scrap provenance is the hidden binding constraint.*

- **Scrap Provenance First.** Mandate mass-balance digitization of scrap inputs before any abatement co-financing. Without this, EAF investments yield zero CBAM reduction.
- **Permanent Istanbul Hub.** Staff with Turkish-speaking technical and commercial leadership who engage TÇÜD as peers.
- **Regulatory Harmonization.** Support Turkish alignment with EU ETS monitoring methodologies, framed as **Turkish industrial strategy**—preparing Turkish producers to be the default near-shore green steel hub.
- **Consignment Option.** Retain the logistical option for the buyer to assume importer-of-record status for CBAM certificate purchasing, but only if requested by the supplier.

### **North Africa: Institutional Embedding**
*Context: Lowest technical readiness. Business runs through personal networks and bureaucratic authorization chains. Currency and gas-lock-in risks dominate.*

- **5-Year Embedded Consortium.** Replace rotating auditors with a permanent EBRD/IFC-led technical assistance presence, starting with **corporate-level ISO 14064-1 boundary definition** before plant-level MRV.
- **Blended Finance Gateway.** CBAM readiness grants (non-repayable) for initial MRV setup, followed by buyer-backed loans for abatement. Grants must cover the full cost of the first two verification cycles.
- **Realistic Abatement Sequencing.** For Moroccan aluminum, pursue solar plus green hydrogen only with baseload guarantees. For Egyptian steel, fund natural gas efficiency as a transitional measure with a public, dated roadmap to hydrogen or DRI electrification.
- **FX & Currency Protection.** All contracts include locked exchange-rate mechanisms or USD pricing for non-CBAM components, with euro liabilities hedged via the transition facility.

## V. RELATIONSHIP MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS

To preserve trust where CBAM is viewed as disguised protectionism, the following are mandatory:

1. **The “Critique-and-Construct” Doctrine.** Buyer representatives must acknowledge CBAM’s asymmetric burdens explicitly in all external communications.
2. **Capital Partners with Local Agency.** Buyer teams provide capital and market access; supplier teams govern local implementation. The buyer retains veto only on data accuracy and contractual milestones.
3. **Transparent Cost Architecture.** Publish a CBAM Cost Transparency Charter specifying exact liability calculation per shipment, Year 1 Grant Offset mechanics, post-2026 pass-through formulas, and the buyer’s Scope 3 accounting benefit. No hidden deductions.
4. **“No Displacement Without Dialogue” Guarantee.** Suppliers demonstrating good-faith compliance progress retain core volume allocations through 2028, even if higher-cost than alternatives. Contractually binding.
5. **Localized Communication Mandate.** All CBAM communications must be issued in the supplier’s local language by native-speaking buyer representatives based in permanent regional offices. Prohibit Brussels-centric regulatory jargon.
6. **Escalation & Grievance Pathway.** Retain an ombudsman function reporting to the Chief Sustainability Officer (not Procurement). Augment with a **Regional Supplier Council** (three delegates per region) meeting quarterly to review fairness concerns.
7. **Joint External Advocacy.** Publicly credit compliant suppliers in annual sustainability reports; invite supplier CEOs to joint EU trade forums; submit Carbon Price Parity analysis jointly.

## VI. GOVERNANCE & IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP

We bifurcate our approach into a **2026 Survival Phase** (administratively simple, capital-intensive, politically disarming) and a **2027+ Scale Phase** (infrastructure-heavy, optimization-focused).

### **2026 Critical Path: The Shield**

| Timeline | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| **Q1 2025** | **90-Day Red Team Exercise:** Compensated, anonymous supplier representatives from each region stress-test financial and governance architecture before capital release. |
| **Q2 2025** | Sign Partnership Charters with 2–3 anchor suppliers per region; issue Letters of Intent explicitly referencing Year 1 Non-Recoverable Grants and Carbon Price Parity advocacy. |
| **Q2–Q3 2025** | Deploy digital MRV platform with supplier encryption keys; execute first Default Value Bridge contracts; launch Carbon Price Parity legal analysis; establish *interim* regional hub leadership (seconded senior staff pending permanent hires). |
| **Q4 2025** | Complete supplier readiness audits; publish Transparency Charter; activate FX hedging for North Africa; begin scrap ecosystem mapping in Turkey. |
| **Q1–Q2 2026** | **Deploy first non-recoverable Transition Grants** for Q2 imports; activate 50/50 cost-share for early actual-emissions achievers; publicly launch Joint Advocacy Platform; activate Exit with Dignity protocols. |

### **2027+ Scale Phase: The Moat**

| Timeline | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| **2026–2027** | Complete regional verifier accreditation (where mutual recognition pathways permit); transition interim hubs to permanent regional director-led offices; scale firmed renewable PPA execution for aluminum. |
| **2027–2028** | Full shift to actual-emissions pricing where achievable; scrap provenance digitization at scale in Turkey; expand closed-loop aluminum only where scrap sovereignty is secured. |
| **2028+** | Evaluate Turkish/EU ETS linkage opportunities; scale North African green hydrogen pilots conditional on baseload resolution; full operationalization of Competitiveness Clusters. |

**Governance Structure:**
- **Executive Steering Committee:** Buyer CEO/CPO + Regional Supplier Council heads (quarterly).
- **Regional Joint Task Forces:** Regional directors + supplier finance/technical leads + accredited verifiers (monthly).
- **KPI Dashboard:**
  - % of supply volume under actual (not default) emissions reporting
  - % of verifications performed by regionally accredited bodies
  - Average embedded emissions intensity (tCO₂/t product) by region and commodity
  - Supplier retention rate pre/post 2026 financial phase
  - Year 1 Grant value disbursed (non-recoverable) vs. supplier margin preservation
  - Scrap provenance documentation compliance rate (Turkey)
  - Supplier Council grievance resolution speed and satisfaction score

## VII. CONCLUSION

CBAM is an irreversible structural force, but its implementation is not yet fixed. The choice before us is whether it fractures our supply chain or forges a deeper, lower-carbon commercial alliance built on honesty, capital sacrifice, and local respect.

We cannot communicate our way out of power asymmetries. We cannot audit our way out of verifier shortages. And we cannot ask our suppliers to ignore a protectionist headwind while we pretend the wind does not exist. 

The path to resilience runs through **genuine, non-recoverable investment in Year 1; the hard work of building regional verification and governance capacity; and the public courage to advocate for our suppliers’ carbon costs to be recognized in Brussels.** The alternative—supplier insolvency, default-value penalties, adversarial trade relations, and supply displacement—is far costlier.

**Recommended Immediate Board Actions:**
1. **Approve the 90-Day Red Team exercise** with compensated supplier representatives before releasing Phase 1 capital.
2. **Authorize the CPO** to issue Letters of Intent to 2–3 anchor suppliers per region, explicitly referencing the Year 1 Non-Recoverable Transition Grant and Carbon Price Parity Fund.
3. **Mandate** the immediate repeal of the Neutrality Protocol and adoption of the Critique-and-Construct Doctrine across all procurement, sustainability, and executive teams.
