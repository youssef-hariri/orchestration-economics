# Global Monetization and Product-Governance Strategy, 2026

## Executive decision

Operate **one global game with a controlled regional monetization layer**.

The global title will share:

- gameplay, balance, characters, story, modes, item IDs, rarity taxonomy, and content roadmap;
- a global non-pay-to-win progression model;
- common privacy, accessibility, marketing, and player-protection standards;
- one authoritative catalog, entitlement, offer, and probability infrastructure.

Regions may vary in:

- acquisition and reward-fulfillment methods;
- prices, currencies, taxes, and payment methods;
- age eligibility and spending controls;
- gifting;
- event timing and presentation;
- legally required disclosures;
- culturally appropriate content and marketing.

Direct-value monetization—cosmetics, battle passes, subscriptions, premium content, choice-based collection, and bounded convenience—should be the commercial foundation. Randomized rewards should be optional, legally reviewed, transparent, capped, auditable, and replaceable through direct or earnable routes.

This model may reduce short-term revenue from highly concentrated spenders and increase engineering, legal, support, audit, and localization costs. It is intended to improve payer breadth, retention, trust, regulatory resilience, and long-term contribution margin.

Local counsel and regulatory specialists must validate all market-specific conclusions. This strategy is not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal advice or ongoing regulatory monitoring.

# 1. Global principles

## 1.1 Law and platform policy are launch gates

Maintain a dated jurisdiction-and-platform register covering:

- gambling and loot-box rules;
- probability-disclosure and reporting obligations;
- consumer protection and advertising;
- age assurance and parental controls;
- minor spending and play restrictions;
- subscriptions and automatic renewals;
- refunds and chargebacks;
- virtual-currency and transferability rules;
- privacy, data localization, and cross-border transfers;
- publishing and local-partner requirements;
- platform rules and enforcement posture.

Each monetization mechanic must receive separate assessments for:

- chance and gambling risk;
- consumer-protection risk;
- advertising risk;
- minor-protection risk;
- platform risk;
- transferability and market-value risk;
- pay-to-win risk;
- privacy risk;
- cultural and reputational risk.

| Tier | Definition | Launch rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Red** | Prohibited, unlicensed, materially misleading, or globally disallowed | Cannot launch |
| **Amber** | Legally arguable but high regulatory, platform, ethical, or reputational risk | Requires local counsel, Council approval, residual-risk acceptance, sunset date, enhanced monitoring, and rollback plan |
| **Yellow** | Permissible only with specified safeguards | Compliance and product approval required |
| **Green** | Direct, fixed-value, earnable, or otherwise low-risk | Standard approval process |

Each assessment must identify the precise user journey, payment flow, reward characteristics, age eligibility, competitive effects, operating entity, platform, legal sources, responsible counsel, business owner, review date, and re-review triggers.

A legal or compliance veto cannot be overridden while a prohibited condition or missing mandatory safeguard remains.

## 1.2 Global ethical floor

Regardless of local law, prohibit:

- paid-exclusive competitive power;
- undisclosed dynamic or account-specific odds;
- fake near-miss effects;
- misleading scarcity or repeatedly resetting countdowns;
- hidden currency conversions;
- repeated prompts after refusal;
- child-directed purchase pressure;
- targeting inferred financial, emotional, or behavioral vulnerability;
- public spending rankings that shame non-spenders;
- coercive or unbounded gifting;
- misleading influencer or advertising claims;
- gambling-like terminology or presentation where inappropriate.

## 1.3 One game, different fulfillment paths

Regional adaptations may change **how players acquire value**, but not the core identity or competitive balance of the game.

For each material reward, establish approved tolerances for:

- time to acquisition;
- minimum and maximum monetary cost;
- earnable availability;
- competitive access;
- cosmetic prestige;
- event participation;
- gifting functionality;
- value per unit of spending.

The Monetization and Player Protection Council must approve these tolerances before launch. Exceeding them requires remediation, such as additional earnable rewards, direct-purchase windows, price changes, or event extensions.

# 2. Global monetization architecture

## Direct cosmetics

Prioritize:

- skins;
- emotes;
- animations;
- profile customization;
- housing and collection displays;
- visual effects;
- themes and music.

Test cosmetics for effects on visibility, hitboxes, audio cues, readability, timing, information advantage, and competitive recognition. An item may not be marketed as purely cosmetic until this assessment is complete.

## Battle passes

Require:

- complete reward lists;
- meaningful free-track rewards;
- realistic completion requirements;
- transparent expiry and carryover rules;
- no paid competitive power;
- no requirement to buy future passes to complete an earlier one;
- clear treatment of incomplete progress.

## Subscriptions

Provide fixed benefits with:

- explicit recurring pricing;
- affirmative renewal consent;
- simple cancellation;
- clear trial conversion;
- renewal notices where required;
- no competitive exclusion.

## Choice-based collection

Use:

- selectors;
- crafting;
- exchange tokens;
- duplicate conversion;
- direct unlocks after rotation;
- earnable collection currency.

This is the default replacement for paid randomness in Belgium and the Netherlands and should become an increasingly important global revenue stream.

## Premium content

Monetize story chapters, modes, housing, creator tools, and cosmetic expansions without making ordinary competitive participation dependent on repeated spending.

## Convenience

Permit convenience features only where competitive effects are limited and measurable. Inventory, loadout, and cosmetic slots are generally lower risk. Combat power, matchmaking priority, energy restrictions, and severe time gates require heightened review.

## Optional randomization

Where legally cleared:

- randomization must add variety rather than determine competitive viability;
- every material reward must have a direct or earnable route;
- pity, ceilings, duplicate handling, and event duration must be disclosed;
- paid rerolls and paid odds increases require separate approval;
- random rewards must not be necessary to complete core content.

# 3. Regional monetization strategy

## Belgium

Use direct purchases, fixed bundles, selectors, crafting, passes, subscriptions, and earnable rewards by default.

Disable or redesign:

- paid loot boxes and gacha;
- paid rerolls;
- paid odds increases;
- randomized competitive power;
- transferable, cash-like, or marketplace-linked rewards;
- casino-style presentation.

Any paid acceleration of an otherwise earnable random system requires separate Belgian legal review. Regional events should retain the same content and participation goals while using a choice-based reward path of equivalent value.

## Netherlands

Use the same conservative commercial default while maintaining a current, fact-specific legal assessment rather than assuming a permanent blanket prohibition.

Globally prohibit:

- tradable or cash-like randomized rewards;
- marketplace-linked value;
- paid competitive randomization;
- gambling-style terminology and presentation.

Monitor Dutch legislation, regulator guidance, court decisions, and platform policy at least quarterly. Any exception requires written local counsel, an expiry date, enhanced monitoring, and Council approval.

## Korea

Treat probability disclosure as a **transaction-integrity system**.

Before purchase, provide in Korean:

- all material rewards;
- legally required probabilities, preferably at item level;
- cost per attempt;
- pity, ceiling, guarantee, and carryover rules;
- duplicate treatment;
- event dates;
- rate and pool-change conditions;
- direct or earnable alternatives.

Use one authoritative, version-controlled source for reward logic and disclosure. Maintain tamper-evident records of advertised rates, deployed rates, changes, approvals, purchases, outcomes, refunds, and remediation.

Block deployment if storefront, client, server, and disclosure versions differ. Appoint a Korean compliance owner independent of revenue teams, with authority to pause a banner.

Use a severity-based remedy table covering display errors, short-lived mismatches, material probability errors, undisclosed pool changes, and systemic reward failures. The table must define investigation deadlines, player notice, refund or compensation, regulator notification, public correction, and independent-review requirements.

## China

Treat China as a distinct operating and approval environment. Before launch, document whether operation uses:

- a licensed local publisher;
- a platform-led model;
- a joint or outsourced operator;
- direct operation where legally available.

A **China readiness certificate** is required before monetization launch. It must evidence:

- publishing and content approvals;
- real-name registration;
- server-side minor controls;
- spending limits and reminders;
- approved payment and refund flows;
- local-language customer service;
- data localization and cybersecurity review;
- partner audit rights;
- incident escalation;
- business continuity and partner-exit plans.

Do not run monetization experiments on minor accounts. Use fixed-value and earnable systems for minors and consider using them for all players.

Partner agreements must allocate responsibility for disclosures, payments, refunds, data, support, audits, incidents, and regulator communications. Global live-ops teams may not alter China offers without local approval.

## Japan

Emphasize character attachment, craftsmanship, narrative, seasonal collections, and social identity.

Where randomization is used:

- provide meaningful pity or ceilings;
- make duplicates useful;
- publish banner histories and rate changes;
- avoid complete-gacha-like combination requirements;
- offer direct character and cosmetic purchases;
- avoid excessive currency complexity and overlapping banners.

Require Japanese review of set-completion mechanics, collection advertising, duplicate economics, limited-time claims, and event-completion costs.

## Gulf markets

Do not use one Gulf configuration. Create separate launch dossiers at minimum for Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, and Oman covering:

- chance and gambling restrictions;
- content and censorship;
- payments and taxes;
- age and parental controls;
- advertising and influencer rules;
- gifting;
- religious and seasonal events;
- local-language support.

Prioritize direct cosmetics, status customization, subscriptions, social experiences, and controlled gifting. Avoid gambling terminology and imagery where inappropriate. Do not use Ramadan, Eid, family obligations, or religious identity to intensify purchase pressure.

## Brazil

Optimize for broad conversion and value:

- local-currency pricing;
- small denominations;
- transparent currency conversion;
- PIX and other relevant payment methods;
- low-cost starter offers;
- fixed-value passes;
- community and social rewards;
- Portuguese customer service.

Display total prices and applicable charges clearly. Monitor payment reconciliation, fraud, refunds, and chargebacks. Measure payer conversion, median payer value, retention, and lifetime value—not only top-spender revenue.

## United States

Use a strong national baseline despite state-level variation:

- transparent odds;
- parental and purchase controls;
- clear subscription enrollment and cancellation;
- receipts and transaction history;
- accessible refunds;
- truthful influencer and advertising disclosures;
- no deceptive scarcity or child-directed pressure.

Maintain a state-law register covering consumer protection, subscriptions, children’s privacy, advertising, gambling, and platform risks. Create state-specific variants only where legally necessary.

# 4. Player protection and competitive fairness

## Age and spending controls

Provide:

- age assurance;
- server-side minor restrictions;
- parental controls;
- spending dashboards;
- transaction and reward history;
- spending limits;
- self-imposed purchase blocking;
- local-currency display;
- clear premium-currency conversion;
- high-value confirmations;
- cooling-off or delayed confirmation for unusually large purchases;
- straightforward refund and complaint channels.

Use protective defaults for minors, new accounts, unusually large purchases, rapid repeated purchases, sudden spending spikes, suspicious gifting, and account or payment anomalies.

For minors, require:

- no paid randomized mechanics;
- default purchase friction;
- spending notifications;
- account-holder receipts;
- no monetization experiments;
- server-side enforcement.

## Vulnerability and data governance

Do not use inferred:

- financial distress;
- emotional distress;
- compulsive-spending likelihood;
- loneliness or grief;
- sensitive health information;
- socioeconomic status;
- refund behavior;
- minor status to intensify purchase pressure.

Permitted protective uses are limited to reducing harm, such as detecting unusual spending spikes, offering a spending pause, enforcing limits, detecting account takeover, and applying minor restrictions.

Require:

- data minimization;
- retention limits;
- access controls;
- model documentation;
- privacy-impact review;
- bias and false-positive testing;
- human review for significant restrictions;
- separation of global configuration data from regionally restricted personal data.

Protective systems must reduce pressure, pause purchasing, or block suspicious activity—not generate more aggressive offers.

## Pay-to-win controls

Adopt these global rules:

- no combat power sold only for money;
- no paid item may exceed the power of an earnable item in the same competitive tier;
- new competitive content must become obtainable through play within a defined period;
- non-payer time-to-competitive-viability must remain within a Council-approved range of payer time;
- payer/non-payer win rates and progression must be monitored by skill tier, mode, region, platform, and season;
- competitive convenience features require a documented cap, owner, and sunset review.

Before launch, approve game-specific numerical thresholds for:

- maximum time to competitive viability;
- maximum payer/non-payer progression gap;
- maximum spending-attributable win-rate difference;
- maximum period before competitive content becomes earnable;
- permitted competitive impact of convenience features;
- permitted visual, audio, or informational advantages from cosmetics.

Any breach triggers review and may require suspension, balance changes, refunds, or a new earnable path.

## Refunds and cooling-off

Adopt a written global standard covering:

- high-value and repeated-purchase thresholds;
- accidental purchases;
- minor purchases;
- gifting disputes;
- response times;
- evidence requirements;
- non-retaliation for legitimate refunds;
- chargeback treatment;
- account-sanction rules.

Thresholds should reflect local purchasing power and be reviewed annually.

# 5. Gifting, marketing, and social events

## Gifting

Prefer direct-item gifting over transferable premium currency.

Require:

- recipient confirmation;
- spending and frequency caps;
- age restrictions;
- cooldowns for new relationships;
- fraud and account-takeover detection;
- dispute and refund procedures;
- anti-coercion reporting;
- limits on public gifting pressure;
- controls preventing gifting from bypassing regional restrictions.

## Marketing

Prohibit:

- implying non-guaranteed rewards are guaranteed;
- showing unavailable regional items;
- hiding material conditions;
- endlessly resetting scarcity;
- pressure tied to friendship, family, status, or religion;
- child-directed calls to purchase;
- undisclosed influencer relationships;
- advertising practically unattainable rewards;
- claims inconsistent with in-game rates or prices.

Marketing claims should be generated from the same catalog and offer data used in the game.

## Cultural and religious events

For religious, national, family, or seasonal events:

- core participation must be free;
- no public spender rankings;
- spending must not be framed as proof of devotion, generosity, loyalty, or friendship;
- countdowns must be genuine;
- reward return dates must be clear;
- local cultural review is mandatory.

# 6. Governance and technical controls

## Monetization and Player Protection Council

Create a Council including:

- product and game design;
- legal and regulatory affairs;
- compliance;
- regional leadership;
- trust and safety;
- data science;
- security and fraud;
- finance;
- customer support;
- accessibility;
- user research and player advocacy.

Its charter must define:

- an independent legal or compliance co-chair;
- appointing authority and member tenure;
- quorum and voting rules;
- conflict-of-interest recusal;
- emergency suspension authority;
- appeal procedures;
- decision deadlines;
- board or risk-committee reporting;
- documented minutes;
- audit and information rights;
- remediation ownership.

Distinguish:

- **legal veto:** cannot be overridden while a prohibited condition remains;
- **ethical objection:** appealable through documented review;
- **commercial disagreement:** may be escalated without changing the legal classification;
- **emergency pause:** remains effective until the triggering risk is resolved.

Revenue leaders may participate but may not override a legal or compliance veto.

## Stage-gated approval

Every material feature must pass:

1. mechanic-level legal and platform classification;
2. local legal and cultural review;
3. ethical and vulnerability assessment;
4. economic, inflation, and competition review;
5. privacy and data review;
6. accessibility and comprehension testing;
7. competitive fairness testing;
8. controlled launch;
9. post-launch review.

## Central infrastructure

Build an authoritative catalog, offer, entitlement, and rate service that:

- stores item IDs, prices, taxes, currencies, pools, probabilities, eligibility, and regional rules;
- generates disclosures from production data;
- versions every change;
- records approvals;
- enforces age and jurisdiction rules server-side;
- detects client, server, storefront, payment, and disclosure inconsistencies;
- supports rollback and kill switches;
- produces audit exports;
- prevents unauthorized live-ops changes.

Separate global configuration data from regionally restricted personal data. Define data residency, access, retention, deletion, transfer, and breach-notification requirements by market.

# 7. Circumvention, migration, and continuity

## Circumvention and arbitrage risks

Monitor for:

- VPN access to cheaper or more permissive stores;
- payment-region switching;
- account-region changes;
- gifting used to bypass restrictions;
- unofficial marketplaces and account trading;
- modified clients and proxy services;
- adults purchasing for minors;
- chargebacks after gifts are received;
- exploitation of different event schedules;
- partner changes without approval;
- rate-table tampering;
- misleading regional marketing.

Use multiple signals, subject to privacy law:

- payment country;
- account region;
- platform region;
- identity and age status;
- device and fraud signals;
- gifting patterns;
- rapid region changes;
- chargeback behavior.

Use graduated enforcement. Do not permanently suspend accounts solely because of IP inconsistency. Publish an account-region policy covering travel, relocation, entitlements, currencies, event eligibility, age status, gifting, refunds, taxes, and payment treatment.

## Migration

When replacing a random system or changing a regional monetization model:

1. announce the change in advance;
2. freeze the old system;
3. preserve or fairly convert pity, tokens, currencies, and event progress;
4. disclose duplicate treatment;
5. provide refunds or exceptions where appropriate;
6. reconcile all entitlements;
7. test rollback and support flows;
8. conduct a post-migration audit;
9. provide local-language transition support.

Regional configuration services must support effective dates, dependency checks, staged rollout, rollback, and region- and age-specific rules.

# 8. Complaints, incidents, and transparency

Create local-language complaint channels covering:

- probability errors;
- accidental purchases;
- minor purchases;
- gifting coercion;
- deceptive promotion;
- pay-to-win;
- refunds and chargebacks;
- account takeover;
- regional inequality.

For a probability error, unauthorized offer, minor-spending failure, data issue, account compromise, or harmful promotion:

1. pause the affected offer or flow;
2. preserve rates, logs, approvals, transactions, and marketing;
3. assess affected regions, ages, platforms, and cohorts;
4. notify legal, compliance, security, support, and local partners;
5. notify platforms or regulators where required;
6. inform players promptly in relevant languages;
7. refund, compensate, or restore value under the remedy policy;
8. correct disclosures and promotions;
9. publish a material correction where appropriate;
10. complete an independent post-incident review;
11. implement recurrence controls.

Maintain a 24/7 incident commander, severity levels, contact trees, local-language templates, refund authority, executive communications procedures, and social-media monitoring. Conduct annual tabletop exercises covering probability errors, minor spending, partner failure, data incidents, and platform takedowns.

Publish a Global Monetization and Player Protection Standard covering:

- no pay-to-win;
- probability integrity;
- minor protection;
- gifting safeguards;
- transparent pricing;
- vulnerability protections;
- complaint and remediation procedures;
- audit commitments.

Audit summaries should identify auditor independence, scope, period, systems tested, material findings, remediation deadlines, and unresolved issues. Mandatory public-reporting criteria should include material probability errors, significant minor-protection failures, material data incidents, misleading promotions, platform enforcement, and unresolved audit findings.

# 9. Accessibility and inclusion

All monetization and disclosure interfaces must support:

- screen readers;
- scalable text;
- adequate contrast;
- non-color-only rarity indicators;
- plain-language explanations;
- accessible parental controls;
- accessible refunds and complaint forms;
- alternatives to rapid-tap purchasing;
- high-quality localization.

Accessibility is a launch gate. Acceptance testing must cover screen readers, scalable text, contrast, alternative input, probability and price comprehension, parental controls, refunds, complaint forms, and multiple language groups.

# 10. Metrics and intervention

## Commercial metrics

- payer conversion;
- median and average payer spend;
- top-decile revenue concentration;
- revenue by monetization category;
- direct-purchase substitution;
- 30-, 90-, and 180-day payer retention;
- subscription cancellation;
- contribution margin;
- refunds and chargebacks.

## Player-protection metrics

- complaints per thousand purchases;
- accidental-purchase reports;
- refund rates;
- spending-limit use;
- high-frequency spending indicators;
- minor purchase attempts;
- disclosure comprehension;
- perceived fairness;
- payer/non-payer competitive gap;
- gifting abuse and coercion reports.

## Compliance and operations

- rate or catalog mismatches;
- unauthorized offer changes;
- time to correct errors;
- audit exceptions;
- regulatory and platform inquiries;
- age-verification failures;
- complaint-resolution time;
- regional arbitrage incidents;
- partner-control failures.

Before launch, create a metric register specifying for each measure:

- baseline period;
- target;
- warning threshold;
- critical threshold;
- measurement method;
- data owner;
- review frequency;
- mandatory action;
- escalation owner;
- remediation deadline.

Minimum automatic actions include:

- any advertised/deployed probability mismatch: immediate offer pause;
- material unauthorized offer change: revoke access and begin forensic review;
- increased accidental-purchase complaints: pause the flow and conduct usability testing;
- minor purchase attempts above the approved threshold: review age and payment controls;
- competitive disparity above tolerance: balance and monetization review;
- regional acquisition disparity above tolerance: equivalence remediation;
- material increase in refunds, chargebacks, or regret complaints: suspend pressure-oriented campaigns.

Segment metrics, where lawful and privacy-preserving, by age category, region, spending percentile, payer status, skill tier, platform, account maturity, and offer type. Use minimum cohort sizes and retention limits.

# 11. Financial sustainability

Model three scenarios:

1. **Conservative:** substantial random-reward removal and weak substitution.
2. **Base:** moderate conversion to cosmetics, passes, subscriptions, and selectors.
3. **Upside:** broader payer conversion, improved retention, and lower refund and enforcement costs.

Include:

- revenue by category;
- payer conversion;
- median and high-spender value;
- retention and lifetime value;
- refunds and chargebacks;
- payment and partner fees;
- engineering, support, legal, audit, and localization costs;
- contribution margin;
- break-even period.

Set:

- category-level revenue targets;
- a board-approved tolerance for top-spender concentration;
- maximum acceptable refund and chargeback rates;
- minimum retention and payer-conversion targets;
- investment, expansion, and rollback rules for replacement systems.

Do not expand a replacement globally based only on first-week revenue. Use 30-, 90-, and 180-day cohorts covering revenue, retention, trust, support volume, refunds, and player-protection outcomes.

Fund legal monitoring, platform compliance, monetization infrastructure, regional QA, support, audits, incident response, migration, accessibility, and partner oversight as core product infrastructure.

# 12. Key risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Revenue decline after randomization removal | Diversify into cosmetics, passes, subscriptions, premium content, selectors, and broad-conversion offers |
| Regulatory change | Maintain dated legal reviews, Amber sunset dates, quarterly monitoring, kill switches, and low-risk fallback systems |
| Platform enforcement | Use platform launch certificates, policy owners, and no-rebuild disablement capability |
| Regional engineering complexity | Shared item IDs, central configuration, automated consistency tests, and regional templates |
| Pay-to-win backlash | Quantified fairness thresholds, earnable competitive paths, and independent testing |
| Minor or vulnerable-player harm | Protective defaults, restricted targeting, server-side controls, and rapid escalation |
| Price and VPN arbitrage | Multi-signal controls, graduated enforcement, and a published travel policy |
| Gifting fraud or coercion | Item-only gifting, caps, cooldowns, recipient confirmation, and anti-coercion reporting |
| Probability or offer errors | Single source of truth, version control, mismatch detection, pause rules, and remedy standards |
| Cultural misalignment | Country-level research, local review, event safeguards, and post-launch monitoring |
| China partner or operating failure | Responsibility matrix, audit rights, business continuity, and exit plan |
| Support overload | Local-language staffing, standardized workflows, funded service levels, and surge capacity |
| Privacy or data-transfer violations | Data maps, minimization, retention controls, regional storage, and privacy-impact reviews |
| Reputational crisis | Crisis playbook, rapid player notice, independent review, public correction, and tabletop exercises |
| Accessibility exclusion | Accessibility launch gates and testing with disabled players and varied literacy levels |

# 13. Implementation roadmap

## First 90 days

- Create the dated legal, platform, cultural, and risk-tier register.
- Inventory all offers, currencies, random systems, targeting rules, and promotions.
- Freeze new high-risk mechanics.
- Define prohibited practices and launch gates.
- Design Belgium and Netherlands direct-value stores.
- Establish Korea and China disclosure, reporting, and remedy procedures.
- Select and document the China operating model.
- Form the Council and approve its charter.
- Define functional-equivalence and anti-pay-to-win thresholds.
- Establish data-governance, accessibility, refund, incident, and account-region standards.
- Approve financial scenarios and revenue-concentration limits.
- Create the metric register and assign owners.
- Establish support-readiness and platform-certification requirements.

## Three to six months

- Launch direct purchases and selectors in Belgium and the Netherlands.
- Deploy the central catalog, offer, entitlement, and rate service.
- Add spending history, limits, parental tools, purchase confirmation, and complaint tracking.
- Implement local payment and price architecture.
- Conduct comprehension, accessibility, fairness, and transaction testing.
- Create migration, rollback, and reconciliation procedures.
- Complete the first external audit.
- Test crisis procedures and platform-takedown controls.
- Establish regional support and partner responsibility matrices.
- Complete the China readiness certification process.

## Six to twelve months

- Shift the global revenue mix toward direct cosmetics, passes, subscriptions, premium content, and selectors.
- Publish the player-protection standard.
- Launch the player advisory panel and advocacy consultation process.
- Implement equivalence scorecards and executive thresholds.
- Complete partner and regional audits.
- Implement incident dashboards and crisis playbooks.
- Reassess every Amber or Yellow mechanic after legal, platform, or material incident changes.
- Review portfolio performance against commercial and player-protection thresholds.

## Ongoing

- quarterly legal and cultural reviews;
- monthly player-health and compliance dashboards;
- annual independent audits;
- annual tabletop exercises;
- continuous red-team testing of arbitrage, gifting, disclosure, age, and account-region controls;
- periodic public reporting and policy updates;
- annual board review of revenue concentration, player-protection outcomes, and regional coherence.

---

## Final recommendation

Approve this strategy only after its controls are converted into:

- binding policies;
- signed legal assessments;
- system requirements;
- numerical thresholds;
- funded budgets;
- launch gates;
- named owners;
- documented escalation and remediation procedures.

The durable model is:

- one global game;
- one item, balance, and entitlement system;
- multiple lawful fulfillment paths;
- direct value as the default;
- randomization optional, transparent, capped, auditable, and replaceable;
- no material regional pay-to-win differences;
- strong age, spending, gifting, marketing, complaint, privacy, audit, accessibility, and data-governance safeguards;
- independent governance with emergency suspension authority;
- measurable regional equivalence;
- active engagement with players, regulators, and advocacy groups.

The publisher should not maximize the most aggressive monetization legally available in each market. It should build a diversified, trusted economy around identity, collection, social participation, subscriptions, premium content, and fair convenience—preserving one coherent global title while adapting responsibly to law, culture, and player welfare.
