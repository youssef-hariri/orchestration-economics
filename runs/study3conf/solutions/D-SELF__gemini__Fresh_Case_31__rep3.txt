# Global Compensation Policy: Our Commitment to Fairness and Transparency

## 1. Introduction
At our company, we believe that talent is global, but economic realities are local. As a distributed workforce operating across 30 countries, our compensation policy is designed to achieve two goals: **Global Internal Equity** and **Local Economic Fairness**. 

This policy ensures that you are compensated based on the value you bring to the company, adjusted fairly for the economic environment where you live.

## 2. Our Core Philosophy: Role-Value + Purchasing Power
We have moved away from "location-based" pay—which assumes an employee's value is determined by their zip code—to a **Role-Value & Purchasing Power model**.

*   **Global Grades (The "What"):** Every role is assigned a "Global Grade" (1–10) based on complexity, scope, and impact. An L5 Engineer has the same grade, the same impact expectations, and the same career trajectory, whether they are in Warsaw, Lisbon, or Toronto.
*   **Purchasing Power Parity (The "Where"):** To ensure that every employee can maintain a consistent standard of living relative to their peers globally, we use **OECD-standardized Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)** data to calibrate your base salary. This ensures that your pay is not "cheaper" because of where you live; it is adjusted so that your ability to thrive is consistent across all our locations.

## 3. Transparency & Disclosures
We are committed to "Explainable Pay." We comply with all global transparency directives (including EU and US regulations) by providing the following to our employees:

*   **Individual Pay Context:** Every employee has access to a "Pay Context Statement" in their HR portal. This document details your Global Grade, the PPP index applied to your location, and the methodology used to calculate your current compensation.
*   **Jurisdiction-Specific Ranges:** When we post open roles, the salary range displayed is specific to the hiring jurisdiction. We no longer use "global tiers" on public job boards, ensuring you have clear, accurate data regarding the market for your role in your region.

## 4. Agility and Economic Protection
Labor markets are dynamic. To prevent "salary drift" and ensure we remain competitive, we utilize two key mechanisms:

*   **The "Market Pulse" Trigger:** We monitor local market data continuously. If market salaries for a specific role in your country shift by more than 15% against our internal bands, our system triggers an automatic, out-of-cycle review. You do not have to wait for an annual cycle to ensure your pay remains competitive.
*   **Economic Adjustment Allowance (EAA):** In cases of sudden, extreme local economic volatility or inflation, we utilize a temporary **Economic Adjustment Allowance**. This is a variable, performance-linked payment that allows us to support you through regional economic shifts without creating the legal or structural complications of permanent base salary inflation.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: Why is there a difference between my salary and a peer’s salary in another country if we have the same Global Grade?**
A: Your Global Grade determines your base compensation for the work performed. The difference in total salary is a result of our PPP adjustment, which is designed to ensure that you and your peer have the same relative "purchasing power." We aim for equal standard of living, not nominal currency equality.

**Q: Is my pay based on "what the market says" or "what the company decides"?**
A: It is based on a transparent, formula-driven approach. We use objective, public data (OECD PPP indices) to calculate the economic adjustment, and your role’s global scope to calculate your baseline. This replaces subjective "market sentiment" with verifiable, objective criteria.

**Q: Can I see how my salary is calculated?**
A: Yes. Your Pay Context Statement is available in your HR portal, and it explicitly links your compensation to your Global Grade and the current PPP data for your region.
