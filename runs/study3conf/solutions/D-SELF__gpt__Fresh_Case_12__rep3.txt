# Localized Subscription Pricing and Business-Model Strategy

## Executive recommendation

Adopt a **controlled, archetype-led localization program**, not a mechanical conversion of USD prices into local currencies.

Retain a common global product and reporting architecture while localizing:

- Price and billing frequency;
- Payment rails and renewal consent;
- Device, stream, download, and catalogue entitlements;
- Household-sharing rules;
- Promotions and distribution;
- Local-language merchandising and customer support;
- FX and price-review mechanisms.

The primary commercial objective should be:

> **Incremental contribution profit per retained paying household**, measured first in local currency and then translated transparently into consolidated USD.

Subscriber growth, ARPU, viewing hours, and piracy indicators are supporting metrics. They should not justify scale unless they improve retained contribution, cash collection, strategic reach, or long-term content economics.

The recommended sequence is:

1. Build a country-level fact base and unit-economic model.
2. Pilot two to four representative markets.
3. Launch a limited tier portfolio and two or three priority payment rails per market.
4. Measure 90- and 180-day cohort economics.
5. Scale only when commercial, customer, operating, and compliance gates are met.

---

## 1. Strategic principles

1. **Localize the business model, not merely the price.**  
   In some markets, the winning proposition will require prepaid access, manual renewal, mobile money, QR activation, low-bandwidth playback, or family access.

2. **Use country and segment evidence rather than regional averages.**  
   Market archetypes are starting hypotheses, not established facts. Each country requires validation of income, payments, devices, household behavior, competition, taxation, regulation, and FX.

3. **Optimize retained households, not gross accounts.**  
   A low-priced account that cancels quickly or causes premium customers to trade down may reduce total value.

4. **Separate operating, cash, accounting, and FX performance.**  
   Local-currency contribution explains execution; constant-currency results explain underlying performance; reported USD results support investor consolidation.

5. **Use graduated sharing controls.**  
   Monetize legitimate family access where possible, while targeting commercial resale, credential farms, and systematic abuse.

6. **Treat affordability as total access cost:**

\[
\text{Total Access Cost}
=
\text{Subscription Price}
+
\text{Data Cost}
+
\text{Payment Cost}
+
\text{Required Device Cost}
\]

7. **No launch without readiness.**  
   Tax, accounting, privacy, payment, consumer-protection, content, fraud, reconciliation, and customer-support requirements must be satisfied before market entry.

# 2. Market archetype framework

Use a two-level framework:

- **Country operating profile:** payment, tax, FX, competition, connectivity, regulation, and cost conditions.
- **Customer segments:** individual mobile viewer, prepaid or liquidity-constrained user, family/shared household, premium connected-TV household, and—if separately approved—ad-tolerant user.

The following archetypes guide initial design only.

| Archetype | Likely opportunity or constraint | Initial model |
|---|---|---|
| **Mobile-first, prepaid-led** | Limited card penetration, mobile-money reach, irregular liquidity, high price sensitivity | Mobile tier, prepaid passes, mobile money, low-bandwidth playback |
| **Large, heterogeneous market** | Wide income dispersion, urban premium pockets, fragmented payment behavior | Limited mobile-to-premium ladder with segment and channel testing |
| **South Asian family-access** | High sharing, price sensitivity, mixed mobile/TV use | Mobile-plus, family access, quarterly/annual prepaid, local-language packaging |
| **Recurring-billing and household-oriented** | Greater TV use, stronger digital payments, family viewing | Standard and Premium Household, annual plans, extra-member testing |
| **Higher-income African market** | Greater broadband and premium willingness to pay | Standard- and premium-led portfolio, annual plans, selected telecom partnerships |

### Country classification scorecard

Before assigning a country to an archetype, validate:

- Income deciles and discretionary entertainment spend;
- Smartphone, broadband, and connected-TV penetration;
- Data cost relative to subscription price;
- Card, bank-debit, wallet, mobile-money, QR, and carrier-billing reach;
- Initial and renewal authorization rates;
- Prepaid and manual-renewal prevalence;
- Household size and sharing patterns;
- Local-language and local-content demand;
- Feature-equivalent competitor prices;
- Tax and regulatory complexity;
- Inflation, currency volatility, and USD-denominated cost exposure;
- Customer-service and partner capacity.

**Required country-level validation:** Every fact should have a source, date, owner, confidence rating, and validation plan. Regional assumptions should not be used as investor forecasts without country evidence.

# 3. Localized tier and entitlement design

Use a common product logic, but do not launch every product in every country.

| Product | Target customer | Illustrative entitlement | Business purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mobile Lite** | Phone-first, price-sensitive user | One mobile stream, SD or bandwidth-efficient quality, downloads where economic | Affordable acquisition and piracy substitution |
| **Mobile Plus** | Higher-value mobile user or small household | Better quality, downloads, possibly one TV device or second stream | Monetize above entry tier |
| **Standard** | Individual or small family | Mobile and TV, HD, one or two streams | Mainstream plan |
| **Premium Household** | Larger or higher-income household | Multiple streams, broad device access, 4K only where justified | Premium monetization and sharing conversion |
| **Prepaid Pass** | Customer reluctant to authorize recurring billing | 7-, 30-, 90-, or 365-day access | Reduce payment friction |
| **Ad-supported** | Price-sensitive but commercially valuable user | Lower subscription price plus advertising | Separate, stage-gated option |

### Recommended launch limits

- **Mobile-first pilot:** one mobile tier and one prepaid duration.
- **Household pilot:** Standard and Premium Household.
- **Prepaid pilot:** no more than two durations initially.
- **Ad-supported access:** separate investment case, excluded from the base rollout until validated.
- Billing frequency should generally be an option attached to an entitlement, rather than a separate product.

The entitlement platform should configure:

- Concurrent streams;
- Registered devices;
- Download devices;
- Video quality;
- TV access;
- Catalogue access;
- Extra members;
- Travel and temporary access.

Monitor product complexity through:

- Active plan/price/channel combinations;
- Billing and entitlement error rates;
- Support contacts per 1,000 subscribers;
- Refund and dispute rates;
- Customer comprehension.

# 4. Price and payment design

## 4.1 Illustrative price corridors

The following are **illustrative planning hypotheses**, not final prices, market facts, or investor commitments.

| Archetype | Entry monthly equivalent | Mainstream monthly equivalent | Premium household monthly equivalent |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low-income mobile-first | US$1–2 | US$2–4 | US$4–7 |
| Mixed-income large market | US$1.50–3 | US$3–7 | US$6–12 |
| Latin American middle-income | US$2–4 | US$5–10 | US$8–16 |
| Higher-income African market | US$2–4 | US$5–10 | US$9–17 |

Final prices must be set in local currency after validating affordability, competitor equivalence, taxes, payment fees, usage, churn, FX, and contribution.

## 4.2 Local price rules

Prices should:

- Be displayed in local currency;
- Include taxes where required;
- Use familiar local denominations;
- Clearly show billing frequency and renewal price;
- Identify promotional versus standard pricing;
- State stream, device, quality, and download limits;
- Provide clear cancellation and expiry terms;
- Remain stable for a defined period except under approved inflation or FX triggers.

Channel-specific prices are acceptable only when payment, distribution, or support costs differ materially and the difference is transparent.

## 4.3 Economic price corridor

For country \(c\), plan \(p\), and payment rail \(r\):

\[
E(C_{c,p,r})
=
E(\text{Collected Billings})
-
E(\text{Taxes and Levies})
-
E(\text{Refunds and Credits})
-
E(\text{Payment and Channel Costs})
-
E(\text{Delivery})
-
E(\text{Support})
-
E(\text{Fraud and Chargebacks})
-
E(\text{Variable Content})
\]

A price is sustainable only if:

\[
E(C_{c,p,r}) \geq C_{\text{approved floor}}
\]

The upper price boundary is determined by measured willingness to pay and churn:

\[
\Delta C
=
\Delta \text{Price Contribution}
-
\Delta \text{Churn Cost}
-
\Delta \text{Payment/Tax Cost}
\]

A price below the economic floor may be approved only as a time-limited, budgeted market-entry investment with an end date, budget cap, and explicit success criteria.

## 5. Payment and billing model

Select payment rails by **net collected value**, not by transaction fee alone:

\[
\text{Net Payment Value}
=
\text{Successful Collections}
-
\text{Transaction Fees}
-
\text{Failed-Payment Leakage}
-
\text{Fraud and Chargebacks}
-
\text{Retry Costs}
-
\text{Support Costs}
-
\text{Settlement and FX Costs}
\]

Track by country, plan, channel, and rail:

- Initial authorization;
- Renewal authorization;
- Failure and recovery;
- Average transaction size;
- Fixed and variable fees;
- Settlement delay;
- Refunds and chargebacks;
- Fraud;
- Support contacts;
- Integration and reconciliation cost;
- Incremental retained contribution.

### Mobile-first markets

Prioritize:

1. Mobile money and wallets;
2. Manual wallet renewal;
3. USSD or app-confirmed payment;
4. Digital and retail vouchers;
5. Carrier billing where net economics support it;
6. Cards as a supplementary rail.

### South Asian markets

Prioritize:

- Wallets;
- QR;
- Bank transfers;
- Vouchers;
- Carrier billing;
- Cards and bank debit where available.

Quarterly and annual prepaid should be tested as core products, not merely promotional offers.

### Latin American and higher-income markets

Prioritize:

- Local cards and bank debit;
- Wallets and QR;
- Direct web checkout;
- Annual plans;
- Telecom bundles;
- Prepaid codes.

Use direct web and local rails where they reduce platform commissions without creating unacceptable fraud or compliance risk.

### QR, carrier billing, and vouchers

QR should support TV activation, web checkout, manual renewal, and wallet or bank payment while preserving account identity, selected plan, currency, tax, and promotion status.

Carrier and wallet agreements must define:

- Revenue share;
- Customer ownership;
- Settlement;
- Refund responsibility;
- Tax treatment;
- Churn and payment reporting;
- Marketing obligations;
- Fraud and reseller controls;
- Customer support;
- Suspension and dispute handling.

Partner registrations should not count as paid subscribers without independently verified payment or entitlement.

Vouchers require geographic controls, fraud-resistant codes, retail reconciliation, expiry disclosures, reseller monitoring, and defined breakage treatment.

### Installments

Use installments selectively for annual, premium, or bundled products. Include financing fees, failed installments, credit losses, refunds, customer-service costs, and local credit-law requirements. Do not absorb financing costs without a demonstrated contribution benefit.

# 6. Recurring billing, prepaid, and payment recovery

Offer a market-specific combination of:

- Monthly auto-renewal;
- Monthly manual renewal;
- Seven-day passes;
- 30- and 90-day prepaid;
- Annual prepaid;
- Partner billing;
- Pause or temporary downgrade;
- One-click reactivation.

Auto-renewal should be optional where local mandate, consent, or consumer-protection rules require it.

## Recovery sequence

1. Notify customers before renewal in local language and currency.
2. Attempt collection at rail-specific funding times.
3. Retry according to payment-method behavior.
4. Offer an alternate payment rail.
5. Provide wallet-funding instructions.
6. Apply a short grace period.
7. Offer temporary downgrade where appropriate.
8. Enable one-click reactivation.
9. Avoid duplicate or excessive retry attempts.

For prepaid products:

\[
\text{30-Day Renewal Rate}
=
\frac{\text{Users purchasing another 30-day period within the defined window}}
{\text{Users whose prior period expired}}
\]

Recurring and prepaid retention must be reported separately.

### Consumer-protection requirements requiring country validation

Before launch, validate:

- Tax-inclusive price display;
- Auto-renewal consent;
- Renewal notices;
- Cancellation parity;
- Digital-content withdrawal or cooling-off rights;
- Refund and proration;
- Installment cancellation;
- Voucher expiry;
- E-invoicing and records;
- Data protection and cross-border transfers;
- Payment-mandate requirements;
- Content licensing and classification;
- Advertising requirements, if applicable.

Checkout should clearly show the final price, currency, billing frequency, renewal date, cancellation method, access limitations, promotional end date, and post-promotion price.

# 7. Sharing and household economics

## 7.1 Sharing design

Do not define a household solely by one residence or IP address. Use:

- Concurrent-stream limits;
- Registered-device limits;
- Download-device limits;
- Known-device management;
- Temporary travel access;
- Risk-based verification;
- Paid extra-member options where appropriate.

Distinguish:

1. Primary household;
2. Temporary traveler;
3. Extended-family user;
4. Paid extra member;
5. Commercial viewer or reseller;
6. Credential farm or automated abuse.

## 7.2 Archetype-specific posture

- **Mobile-first:** flexible device registration, low stream count, minimal punitive enforcement.
- **South Asian family-access:** explicit family plan and reasonable multi-device access; monetize before restricting.
- **Latin American or household-oriented:** strong Premium Household proposition and extra-member testing.
- **Higher-income African:** more stringent controls may be appropriate if premium demand and recovery processes support them.
- **All markets:** target commercial viewing, bulk resale, credential farms, and systematic abuse.

The enforcement business case is:

\[
\text{Incremental Subscription and Add-on Revenue}
-
\text{Enforcement Cost}
-
\text{False-Positive Churn}
-
\text{Support Cost}
>0
\]

Controls should be graduated, explainable, privacy-reviewed, and reversible. A single anomalous location, IP, or device should not trigger automatic closure.

**Required country-level validation:** Conduct qualitative research with multi-residence families, students, migrant workers, shared-device users, retailers, small commercial viewing locations, and customer-service teams.

# 8. Promotions and distribution

Use targeted, time-limited offers:

1. Introductory first-period offer;
2. Quarterly or annual prepaid discount;
3. Payment-partner-funded promotion;
4. Reactivation offer;
5. Referral or extra-member offer;
6. Local-content or event promotion.

Every promotion requires:

- Target segment;
- Standard post-promotion price;
- Discount ceiling;
- Maximum fully loaded CAC;
- Expected payment rail;
- 60-, 90-, and 180-day retention target;
- Holdout group;
- Cannibalization assessment;
- End date and approval owner.

\[
\text{Incremental Promotion Contribution}
=
\text{Contribution from Incremental Retained Users}
-
\text{Discount Cost}
-
\text{Cannibalized Contribution}
-
\text{Incremental Support and Payment Cost}
\]

Promotions should be judged on incremental contribution per eligible household, not gross additions.

---

# 9. Content and catalogue localization

Plans should differ on meaningful value dimensions:

- Device and screen access;
- Video quality;
- Concurrent streams;
- Downloads;
- Local-language content;
- Family and children’s content;
- Sports or event access;
- Regional catalogues;
- Premium franchises.

Entry plans should retain enough useful content to create durable value. Local content should be evaluated on retained households:

\[
\text{Cost per Incremental Retained Household}
=
\frac{\text{Incremental Local Content Investment}}
{\text{Incremental 12- or 24-Month Retained Households}}
\]

Use staggered releases, matched markets, or holdouts where rights permit. Control for seasonality, marketing, competitor launches, and pricing.

Separate:

- Global fixed content;
- Market-specific licensing;
- Revenue-share content;
- Local original investment;
- Variable delivery cost.

### Ad-supported access

Treat ad-supported access as a separate investment case. Validate:

- Fill rate;
- Effective CPM;
- Ad load;
- Sales and ad-tech costs;
- Privacy and consent;
- Content rights;
- Brand safety;
- Viewer retention;
- Paid-tier cannibalization;
- Contribution per user.

It should be excluded from the base forecast until validated.

# 10. Unit economics and consolidated-USD framework

## 10.1 Four financial waterfalls

### Customer price waterfall

Displayed price → taxes and discounts → amount charged.

### Cash waterfall

Amount charged → successful collection → refunds, chargebacks, settlement delays → cash collected.

### Accounting revenue waterfall

Consideration → taxes excluded from revenue → allocation between bundled services → revenue recognized over the entitlement period.

### Contribution waterfall

Recognized or management-defined net revenue → payment/channel costs → variable content → delivery → support → fraud → local contribution.

## 10.2 Local-currency contribution

\[
\text{Net Revenue}_{LC}
=
\text{Billings}_{LC}
-
\text{Taxes Excluded from Revenue}
-
\text{Refunds}
-
\text{Credits}
-
\text{Accounting Deductions}
\]

\[
\text{Contribution Profit}_{LC}
=
\text{Net Revenue}_{LC}
-
\text{Payment and Distribution}
-
\text{Variable Content}
-
\text{Delivery}
-
\text{Support}
-
\text{Fraud and Chargebacks}
\]

Show below contribution:

- Local marketing;
- Local content investment;
- Market overhead;
- Shared global-content allocation;
- Strategic launch investment.

Model delivery and support costs by usage decile, not only average usage. Premium plans should be stress-tested using heavy-viewer cohorts.

## 10.3 Cohort LTV and CAC

\[
\text{Contribution LTV}_{LC}
=
\sum_{m=1}^{M} E(C_{m,LC}) \times S_m
\]

where \(S_m\) is observed survival and \(E(C_m)\) reflects plan, rail, promotion, usage, and support costs.

For prepaid products:

\[
\text{Contribution LTV}_{LC}
=
\text{Initial Contribution}
+
\sum_{n=1}^{N}
(\text{Renewal Contribution}\times r^n)
\]

where \(r\) is the observed renewal probability.

\[
\text{LTV/CAC}
=
\frac{\text{Contribution LTV}}{\text{Fully Loaded CAC}}
\]

\[
\text{Cash CAC Payback}
=
\frac{\text{CAC}}{\text{Monthly Cash Contribution}}
\]

CAC includes media, partner commissions, free access, discounts, voucher distribution, onboarding, and payment setup.

Report three separate measures:

1. Cash contribution LTV;
2. Recognized-revenue contribution LTV;
3. Cash CAC payback.

## 10.4 Illustrative planning gates

These are **internal starting parameters**, not industry benchmarks:

- Positive contribution after variable costs in the base case;
- Base-case LTV/CAC of approximately 1.5x or better at launch, with a path toward 2.0x;
- CAC payback generally under 6–12 months in volatile, high-churn markets;
- CAC payback generally under 12–18 months in more stable recurring-billing markets;
- No permanent negative-margin product without executive investment approval;
- Rail-specific payment and renewal floors;
- No material premium-plan cannibalization;
- Positive contribution under downside FX, payment, tax, and churn scenarios.

Finance should finalize these gates after baseline cohorts are available. No investor forecast should rely on unvalidated churn, payment, content, piracy, or renewal assumptions.

# 11. Consolidated USD reporting and FX governance

## 11.1 Translation

For income-statement flows, use the approved average or transaction FX methodology. Closing rates should generally apply to balance-sheet items under the applicable accounting policy.

\[
\text{Reported USD Revenue}
=
\text{Local-Currency Net Revenue}
\times
\text{Approved Reporting FX Rate}
\]

\[
\text{Constant-Currency Revenue}
=
\text{Current-Period Local Net Revenue}
\times
\text{Prior-Period FX Rate}
\]

\[
\text{FX Translation Impact}
=
\text{Reported USD Revenue}
-
\text{Constant-Currency Revenue}
\]

Apply the same bridge to contribution profit.

## 11.2 Operating versus economic FX effects

Separate:

1. **Operational performance:** local subscribers, price, mix, retention, payment success, local revenue, and local contribution.
2. **Translation effect:** change caused by converting local performance into USD.
3. **Economic FX exposure:** impact when content, technology, debt, or other costs are USD-denominated while revenue is local.

\[
\Delta \text{USD Contribution}
=
\Delta \text{Volume}
+\Delta \text{Local Price}
+\Delta \text{Plan/Channel Mix}
+\Delta \text{Retention}
+\Delta \text{Payment Economics}
+\Delta \text{Local Costs}
+\Delta \text{FX Translation}
+\Delta \text{USD-Cost Exposure}
\]

Where currencies are restricted or multiple rates exist, treasury and accounting must document the applicable rate, conversion timing, repatriation delay, and trapped-cash risk.

## 11.3 FX and price governance

Use the following treasury hierarchy:

1. Match local revenue with local costs;
2. Negotiate local-currency or indexed contracts;
3. Shorten contract durations in volatile markets;
4. Maintain only necessary local balances;
5. Hedge only liquid, forecastable exposures;
6. Report hedge cost and effectiveness separately.

Use quarterly price reviews in stable markets and trigger-based reviews in volatile markets. Triggers may include sustained inflation, devaluation beyond an approved band, tax changes, or material payment-cost changes.

Annual plans require separate modeling of:

- Cash collection;
- Deferred revenue;
- Refunds and chargebacks;
- FX movement during the entitlement period;
- Local cost inflation;
- USD-denominated rights;
- Price-protection obligations;
- Renewal rates.

- Follow-up period;

## Priority experiments

1. Mobile Lite versus Standard entry offer;
2. Mobile Plus feature and price;
3. Monthly recurring versus manual renewal;
4. Prepaid duration;
5. Annual discount;
6. Wallet auto-renewal versus reminder-based renewal;
7. QR versus card-first checkout;
8. Direct versus app-store channel;
9. Telecom or wallet bundle;
10. Family stream and device limits;
11. Extra-member price;
12. Graduated sharing verification;
13. Grace-period and retry timing;
14. Content promotion versus price promotion;
15. Post-promotion price communication.

Test separately among:

- New prospects;
- Existing Standard customers;
- Existing Premium customers;
- Known shared-account users;
- Prepaid lapsed users.

Primary success metric:

\[
\text{Incremental Contribution}
=
\text{Treatment Contribution}
-
\text{Control Contribution}
\]

Measure at least 90 days and preferably 180 days. Annual, content, and sharing tests may require modeled long-term results with confidence intervals.

### Cannibalization test

\[
\text{Incremental Contribution}
=
\text{New-Tier Contribution}
-
\text{Downgrade Loss}
-
\text{Churn Effects}
-
\text{Incremental Cost}
\]

Predefine maximum premium downgrade, blended-contribution decline, and support-cost thresholds before testing.

# 14. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Entry tier is uneconomic | Rail-specific price floors and contribution modeling |
| Entry tier cannibalizes premium | Existing-customer holdouts and blended-contribution gates |
| Prepaid renewal is weak | Renewal cohorts, reminders, reactivation, duration testing |
| Recurring payment failure | Rail-specific retries, grace periods, alternate rails |
| Platform or partner fees erase margin | Direct web and local-rail strategy; transparent channel pricing |
| Sharing controls cause churn | Graduated verification, travel allowances, appeals, extra members |
| FX weakens USD economics | Constant-currency reporting, local-cost matching, trigger-based repricing |
| USD content costs rise | Local/indexed contracts, treasury scenarios, selective hedging |
| Tax treatment is incorrect | Country tax matrix and accounting signoff |
| Annual plans create refund or FX risk | Separate cash, deferred-revenue, refund, and FX model |
| Fraud and geographic arbitrage increase | Risk scoring, velocity controls, geographic voucher rules, human review |
| Local content fails to retain users | Incremental retained-household tests |
| Complexity increases support costs | Launch caps, entitlement flags, complexity KPIs |
| Customer support is overwhelmed | Capacity gates and fully loaded support cost |
| Partner reporting is unreliable | Contractual audit rights and independent entitlement reconciliation |
| Piracy benefit is overstated | Treat piracy reduction as a measured upside hypothesis |
| Data bundles fail to improve economics | Model data subsidy, usage uplift, legal treatment, and contribution |

# 15. Implementation roadmap

## Phase 1: Fact base and economics — 0–90 days

**Owners:** Regional GM, CFO pricing lead, payments lead, tax/legal lead.

- Build country and segment scorecards;
- Validate competitor prices, affordability, data costs, and sharing;
- Confirm tax, payment, privacy, consumer, and content requirements;
- Model each plan and rail in local currency;
- Establish FX, repatriation, and USD-cost scenarios;
- Define the metrics dictionary and event instrumentation;
- Select two to four pilot markets;
- Approve pilot budget and contingency.

**Gate:** validated fact base, legal feasibility, complete unit model, approved budget, and measurement plan.

## Phase 2: Product and operating readiness — 60–150 days

**Owners:** Product, engineering, payments, customer operations.

- Enable local currency and tax-inclusive checkout;
- Integrate two or three priority rails per pilot;
- Build prepaid entitlement and reconciliation;
- Implement retry, grace, refund, and reactivation flows;
- Add device, stream, download, and sharing controls;
- Prepare local-language billing and support;
- Complete fraud, privacy, accounting, and partner tests;
- Validate customer-service capacity.

**Gate:** production billing, collection, refund, support, fraud, reconciliation, and reporting tests pass.

## Phase 3: Controlled pilots — 120–240 days

**Owners:** Growth and regional GM, with finance experiment owner.

- Launch limited products;
- Run pricing, payment, billing-frequency, sharing, and promotion tests;
- Use randomized holdouts;
- Review at 30, 90, and 180 days;
- Track local contribution, cash collection, recognized revenue, and customer outcomes;
- Stop failed variants under predefined criteria.

**Gate:** positive incremental contribution, approved retention and payment performance, no material cannibalization, and no unresolved compliance issue.

## Phase 4: Archetype expansion — 240–365 days

- Extend successful configurations to similar validated markets;
- Renegotiate payment and distribution costs;
- Expand local content only where retention evidence supports it;
- Introduce extra-member monetization where validated;
- Standardize investor reporting;
- Refresh the country classification scorecard.

## Ongoing optimization

- Reforecast monthly;
- Review stable-market prices quarterly;
- Trigger reviews for inflation, FX, tax, or payment-cost changes;
- Reallocate acquisition toward high-LTV cohorts and rails;
- Retire persistently negative or unnecessarily complex plans;
- Refresh legal, tax, privacy, and partner assessments after material changes.

# 16. Governance and decision rights

Create a Localization Pricing Council comprising finance, growth, product, payments, tax, legal, content, fraud, customer operations, treasury, and regional leadership.

| Decision | Primary owner | Required control |
|---|---|---|
| Price within approved corridor | Regional commercial lead | Finance approval |
| Material price change | Pricing Council | CFO, tax/legal, regional GM |
| New payment rail | Payments lead | Finance, legal, operations |
| Promotion | Growth lead | Finance and regional approval |
| Negative-margin investment | Executive investment committee | CFO approval |
| Sharing-enforcement change | Product/fraud | Legal and customer-operations approval |
| Local-content investment | Content and regional GM | Finance/investment approval |
| FX-triggered repricing | Treasury/pricing | CFO, legal, regional approval |
| Accounting and tax treatment | Controller/tax | CFO and local adviser signoff |

Escalation rules:

- Legal or privacy issue: legal may pause activity.
- Accounting or tax issue: controller/CFO may block launch.
- Negative contribution: executive investment approval required.
- Entitlement infeasibility: product may block launch.
- Support or billing overload: operations may pause rollout.
- Material partner breach: commercial leadership may suspend entitlement.

Each approval memo should include:

- Target segment and customer problem;
- Price and entitlement;
- Payment rail and channel;
- Expected acquisition, retention, and mix;
- Cannibalization;
- Local contribution;
- CAC and payback;
- Downside FX, tax, churn, and payment cases;
- Customer communications;
- Legal, tax, privacy, accounting, and support readiness;
- Budget and rollback plan.

---

## Final recommendation

Proceed with a **limited, payment-native, prepaid-compatible, household-aware localization program**.

- Use mobile and prepaid access where income, liquidity, or recurring-billing constraints dominate.
- Use Standard and Premium Household products where TV use, family value, and payment maturity support higher monetization.
- Select payment rails by successful collections and retained contribution.
- Treat sharing as a monetization and customer-value issue before treating it solely as leakage.
- Use targeted promotions with holdouts and post-promotion measurement.
- Fund local content against incremental retained households.
- Manage prices and operating economics in local currency.
- Report consolidated USD using a transparent constant-currency and FX bridge.
- Scale only after commercial, customer, operating, and compliance gates are met.

This approach improves affordability and paid penetration without sacrificing financial discipline. It gives operating leaders a practical market-entry model and gives investors a defensible view of contribution, cash collection, accounting revenue, retention, cannibalization, FX exposure, and long-term profitability.
