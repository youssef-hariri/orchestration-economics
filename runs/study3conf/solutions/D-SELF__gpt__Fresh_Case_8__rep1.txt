# CBAM Supplier Competitiveness Partnership

## 1. Strategic objective

The European industrial buyer should establish a **CBAM Supplier Competitiveness Partnership** for steel and aluminum imports from India, Türkiye, and relevant North African markets.

The objective is to:

- Meet the buyer’s applicable EU customs and CBAM obligations;
- Preserve supply continuity and supplier diversity;
- Reduce avoidable reporting, verification, default-value, and financing costs;
- Help suppliers build reusable emissions-data and control systems;
- Share defined first-cycle readiness costs fairly;
- Finance commercially credible emissions reductions;
- Maintain relationships despite differing views on CBAM’s fairness.

The buyer should not claim that CBAM is inherently fair or beneficial, or promise exemptions or recognition of local carbon charges. The credible message is:

> CBAM creates binding requirements for relevant EU imports. We will meet those requirements while recognizing that the burden differs by country, facility, product, and production route. We will provide practical tools, targeted support, and transparent commercial arrangements. Suppliers must provide traceable data, cooperate with appropriate review, protect data integrity, and pursue credible improvement plans.

---

# 2. Principles

1. **Legal responsibility stays clear.** Supplier data supports the buyer’s process but does not transfer importer or declarant responsibility.
2. **Common minimum standard, differentiated support.** All suppliers meet the same baseline; support reflects capability, financial need, strategic importance, and willingness.
3. **Site-, product-, and route-based treatment.** Country is context, not a proxy for readiness.
4. **No surprises.** Requirements, pricing implications, deadlines, and sourcing consequences are communicated in advance.
5. **Evidence over assumptions.** Use installation- and product-specific data where possible; distinguish technical reliability from regulatory usability.
6. **Fairness through process.** Provide confidentiality, correction rights, consistent scoring, grievance handling, and staged remediation.
7. **Support in exchange for cooperation.** Funding and commercial commitments require agreed data, access, milestones, and improvement plans.
8. **Readiness is not decarbonization.** Reporting support and physical emissions-reduction finance should be budgeted and measured separately.
9. **Remediation before exclusion.** Good-faith suppliers receive a defined improvement path, except where there is fraud, legal prohibition, serious safety or human-rights risk, or unmanaged supply risk.
10. **No unsupported environmental claims.** “Lower-emissions,” “green,” or recycled-content claims require defined boundaries and substantiation.

# 3. Legal and operating model

## 3.1 Import-flow responsibility

For every relevant import flow, the buyer should document:

| Control | Required action |
|---|---|
| Product scope | Confirm the applicable CN code, product form, processing level, and any thresholds or exclusions |
| Installation | Identify the actual producing installation and route |
| Importer | Confirm importer of record and customs structure |
| Declarant | Identify the authorized CBAM declarant or representative, where applicable |
| Data provider | Identify the installation or supplier responsible for emissions data |
| Methodology | Confirm the applicable emissions boundaries and calculation approach |
| Verification | Determine what is legally required versus commercially requested |
| Certificates | Assign procurement, accounting, and surrender responsibilities |
| Carbon price | Review any claimed carbon price paid in the production country |
| Records | Define retention, correction, audit, and historical-restatement procedures |

This must cover direct imports, affiliates, distributors, traders, tolling, consignment, subcontracting, Incoterms, and different customs representatives.

**Legal caveat:** requirements depend on the applicable EU legislation, implementing rules, product classification, transaction structure, reporting period, and official guidance in force. Supplier declarations, corporate sustainability reports, consultant reviews, or domestic carbon payments do not automatically guarantee regulatory acceptance.

## 3.2 2026 control calendar

The legal, customs, sustainability, finance, and treasury teams should jointly maintain a current 2026 calendar covering:

- Scope and CN-code approval;
- Importer or declarant authorization;
- Internal supplier-data deadlines;
- Buyer data review;
- Required verification or assurance;
- Declaration and correction deadlines;
- Certificate-acquisition and surrender requirements;
- Financial provisioning and working-capital needs;
- Regulatory changes and supplier notification.

The buyer should separately model:

- The date of import;
- The reporting period;
- The date of declaration;
- Certificate purchase and surrender timing;
- Supplier invoicing and price adjustment timing;
- FX and certificate-price exposure.

The roadmap below is therefore a management sequence, not a substitute for the current legal timetable.

# 4. Supplier segmentation

Assessment should occur at **facility, product, and production-route level** across two separate dimensions:

- **Capability:** metering, data, engineering, assurance, and financial capacity;
- **Willingness and trust:** transparency, responsiveness, access, and corrective-action behavior.

| Segment | Profile | Buyer response |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Ready and strategic** | Site-specific data, documented controls, strong allocation capability | Integrate data; provide targeted assurance and decarbonization support; consider preferred status |
| **2. Capable but incomplete** | Good operations but gaps in meters, allocation, evidence, or systems | Maintain business subject to milestones; fund targeted improvements |
| **3. Cooperative but financially constrained** | Strategically important and willing to improve but capital-constrained | Provide capped grants, recoverable support, bank finance, and conditional volume commitments |
| **4. Low-readiness or resistant** | Poor traceability, repeated delays, refusal to cooperate, or unsupported data | Set minimum requirements and remediation milestones; restrict discretionary volume if progress fails |
| **5. High-integrity or legal risk** | Deliberate misrepresentation, legal breach, refusal of required access, or unacceptable risk | Stop relying on data, apply remedies, and accelerate alternatives |

A supplier’s disagreement with CBAM policy should not by itself be treated as non-cooperation. The buyer should distinguish political objection, technical inability, financial constraint, refusal, and deliberate misrepresentation.

# 5. Supplier support offer

## 5.1 Common readiness package

Every covered supplier should receive:

- Product and CN-code mapping;
- Installation and production-route identification;
- Required data fields and internal deadlines;
- Steel- and aluminum-specific guidance;
- Precursor, scrap, recycled-content, and captive-power guidance;
- Evidence and record-retention checklist;
- Data-quality and regulatory-usability criteria;
- Glossary distinguishing buyer review, consultant review, assurance, verification, and regulatory acceptance;
- Carbon-price evidence checklist;
- Confidentiality and permitted-use terms;
- Explanation of pricing and sourcing implications;
- Correction, grievance, and escalation routes.

Materials should be available in relevant local languages and in formats suitable for suppliers with limited digital capability.

## 5.2 Measurement and reporting support

For priority suppliers, provide or co-fund:

- Data-flow mapping from source records to submitted figures;
- Metering-gap assessments and priority meters;
- Reconciliation of utility, production, material, and financial records;
- Product and precursor allocation;
- Controlled spreadsheets or digital tools;
- Data-owner training;
- Internal controls and evidence repositories;
- Mock submissions and assurance-readiness reviews.

A new portal should not be mandatory where a secure, controlled spreadsheet process is adequate.

## 5.3 Decarbonization support

Potential projects include:

- Energy efficiency and waste-heat recovery;
- Furnace and process optimization;
- Electrification or fuel switching;
- Renewable electricity access;
- Captive-power optimization;
- Scrap and remelting improvements;
- Lower-carbon feedstocks;
- Steel DRI/EAF or other route-transition studies;
- Aluminum smelting and anode improvements.

Each project requires a baseline, expected physical emissions reduction, capital and operating costs, supplier contribution, implementation milestones, and a measurement plan.

# 6. Steel and aluminum controls

## Steel

The buyer should distinguish:

- Integrated blast furnace/basic oxygen furnace;
- Electric arc furnace;
- Induction furnace;
- Direct-reduced iron/electric furnace;
- Re-rollers and processors;
- Facilities using purchased slabs, billets, pig iron, DRI, scrap, coke, lime, or ferroalloys;
- Facilities with captive power.

Evidence should cover:

- Precursor supplier and installation identity;
- Production, heat, cast, batch, or line records where relevant;
- Energy and fuel data;
- Scrap quantities and origin;
- Mass-balance and yield reconciliation;
- Product allocation;
- Outsourced processing;
- Route and product-mix changes;
- Avoidance of double counting or omission of precursor emissions.

## Aluminum

The buyer should separately assess:

- Bauxite, alumina, and primary-metal sourcing;
- Primary smelting;
- Smelting electricity and captive generation;
- Anode consumption;
- Remelt and recycled production;
- Scrap origin and quantity;
- Casting, billet, rolling, extrusion, and finishing;
- Purchased upstream emissions;
- Product-family allocation;
- Recycled-content and mass-balance evidence.

Corporate renewable-energy claims, generic certificates, or group-level sustainability reports should not automatically substantiate a lower-emissions claim for a specific installation or product.

# 7. Data, assurance, and correction

## 7.1 Data classification

Data should be classified on two dimensions.

**Technical reliability**

1. Site-specific and independently reviewed;
2. Site-specific with documented controls;
3. Site-specific estimate supported by evidence;
4. Permitted default or fallback value;
5. Unsupported or unreliable data.

**Regulatory usability**

- Assessed by the buyer as meeting the applicable submission methodology;
- Usable subject to correction;
- Interim only;
- Requires legal or methodological review;
- Not usable.

Internal acceptance does not guarantee acceptance by an EU authority, verifier, or other competent body.

## 7.2 Assurance decision tree

For each data category, determine:

1. Is the information legally required?
2. Is independent verification legally required?
3. Is additional buyer assurance commercially justified?
4. What provider qualifications apply?
5. Who pays?
6. What installation, product, precursor, and period are covered?
7. What happens if deficiencies are found?

Buyer review, consultant review, assurance readiness, independent verification, and regulatory acceptance must remain separate concepts.

## 7.3 Correction and interim-data process

When information is incomplete:

1. Record the affected product, import, materiality, and data gap;
2. Issue a written correction request;
3. Set a supplier deadline;
4. Assess technical and regulatory usability;
5. Use an approved interim method only where legally permitted;
6. Record financial exposure and reserve requirements;
7. Decide whether the shipment may proceed;
8. Track historical corrections and invoice adjustments;
9. Escalate repeated or material failures.

Good-faith corrections should not automatically trigger penalties. Deliberate misrepresentation should trigger immediate suspension of reliance, investigation, and contractual remedies.

# 8. Commercial and financing model

## 8.1 Cost allocation

| Cost | Default treatment |
|---|---|
| Importer/declarant and certificate obligations | Remain with the legally responsible party; economic recovery only if agreed |
| Routine supplier reporting | Progressively becomes supplier responsibility |
| Initial measurement and readiness | Shared through capped, milestone-based support for priority suppliers |
| Assurance or verification | Shared initially where justified, then reviewed |
| Physical decarbonization | Project-specific finance, offtake, premium, or shared savings |
| Cost caused by unsupported supplier data | Allocated according to agreed responsibility and evidence |

Legal responsibility, economic incidence, accounting treatment, and supplier price adjustment must be documented separately.

## 8.2 Commercial decision hierarchy

1. Confirm scope and legal responsibility;
2. Select the best permitted emissions method;
3. Model actual, interim, and default-value exposure;
4. Allocate routine reporting costs;
5. Apply temporary support where justified;
6. Apply premiums only for substantiated performance;
7. Reconcile under the agreed formula;
8. Escalate material deviations or disputes.

The contract formula should address:

- Import volume;
- Embedded-emissions quantity;
- Certificate-price reference and timing;
- FX conversion;
- Carbon-price treatment;
- Default or fallback values;
- Assurance costs;
- Taxes;
- Corrections and restatements;
- Payment timing;
- Disputed amounts;
- Any agreed cap, collar, or sharing percentage.

The buyer should not make unilateral deductions based solely on imputed emissions without a credible route for the supplier to submit defensible data.

## 8.3 Financing safeguards

Every material funding proposal should include:

- Strategic rationale;
- Supplier financial assessment;
- Expected avoided cost or resilience benefit;
- Physical emissions and data outcomes;
- Supplier contribution;
- Funding instrument and amount;
- Milestones;
- Offtake or volume protection;
- Asset ownership and maintenance;
- Repayment or recovery rights;
- Insolvency and exit treatment;
- Legal, tax, FX, competition, sanctions, and anti-corruption approvals;
- Post-investment verification.

# 9. Regional implementation

## India

Differentiate integrated producers, EAF and induction facilities, re-rollers, remelters, and smaller independent plants.

Priorities include:

- Captive-power and group-company accounting;
- Plant-level rather than corporate-average data;
- Scrap and precursor traceability;
- Multiple plants reporting under one corporate entity;
- Trader-to-producer data flows;
- Practical tools for smaller mills;
- Local-currency and equipment-financing constraints.

Participation in an Indian energy-efficiency or carbon-market mechanism should not be assumed to qualify as a CBAM carbon-price deduction.

## Türkiye

Use a dedicated workstream reflecting its close integration with European supply chains and diverse exporter base.

Priorities include:

- Integrated, EAF, re-rolling, foundry, processor, and trader distinctions;
- Converting existing EU-facing sustainability systems into installation- and product-level data;
- Monitoring Turkish climate and emissions-trading developments;
- Clear producer-to-trader data flows;
- Avoiding assumptions that Customs Union integration or a developing domestic system eliminates CBAM exposure.

## North Africa

Create country annexes only for markets represented in the actual supply base, potentially including Morocco, Egypt, Algeria, and Tunisia.

Assess:

- Grid and captive-power emissions;
- Gas and electricity access;
- Renewable-power availability;
- Currency and foreign-exchange restrictions;
- Financing and enforcement;
- State ownership or government interface;
- Local assurance and engineering capacity;
- Customs and logistics;
- Permitting, labor, water, and community issues.

Country context should inform support design but never replace installation-specific evidence.

# 10. Fairness and relationship safeguards

## Supplier fairness compact

The buyer should commit to:

- Reasonable notice;
- Clear separation of legal requirements from buyer preferences;
- Consistent treatment of comparable suppliers;
- Consistent carbon-price review;
- Protection of commercially sensitive data;
- Good-faith correction without automatic penalty;
- No retroactive deduction based solely on unsupported assumptions;
- Staged remediation before exclusion;
- Transparent use of emissions data in sourcing;
- Review and appeal rights.

Suppliers should commit to:

- Timely, traceable data;
- Named data owners;
- Reasonable site and record access;
- Corrective actions;
- Notification of material operational changes;
- No unsupported environmental claims.

## Scorecard safeguards

The buyer should:

- Publish scoring dimensions and weighting;
- Keep data quality separate from emissions intensity;
- Notify suppliers of material score changes;
- Allow correction of factual errors;
- Record reasons for volume reduction or exit;
- Apply consistency checks across countries and routes;
- Require executive review before reducing strategic-supplier volumes;
- Prohibit unrelated use of emissions data unless contractually agreed.

## Advisory council

The council should provide a structured forum for discussing administrative burden, assurance costs, energy constraints, financing, methodology, carbon-price treatment, and impacts on smaller suppliers.

Competition-law controls are mandatory:

- Legally review agendas and minutes;
- Prohibit discussion of prices, bids, capacity allocation, market sharing, and customer strategy;
- Aggregate or anonymize supplier data;
- Do not share identifiable supplier information;
- Require participants to leave inappropriate discussions;
- Do not seek collective supplier commitments.

# 11. Phased roadmap

| Phase | Key actions | Readiness gate |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Scope and diagnosis** | Confirm products, CN codes, importers, declarants, installations, routes, suppliers, and criticality; issue readiness packs; establish governance and budgets | Every covered product and import flow has an approved scope decision, owner, installation, and data path |
| **2. Capability and pilots** | Complete data-flow and metering assessments; train data owners; pilot reporting; reconcile records; amend contracts; approve interim procedures and funding | Priority sites have minimum evidence, named owners, and corrective-action plans |
| **3. 2026 operations** | Collect recurring data; apply current legal timetable; complete required review or verification; review carbon-price claims; manage reserves, corrections, and cost-sharing; qualify alternatives | Each import has data assessed by the buyer as meeting the applicable methodology or an approved interim treatment |
| **4. 2027 onward** | Reduce fallback reliance; transition routine reporting costs; expand physical abatement, offtake, and performance finance; integrate emissions into sourcing | Supplier capability and physical emissions performance improve year over year |

The 2026 dashboard should assign named owners and dates for scope approval, authorization, supplier data, contracts, metering, assurance, treasury reserves, declarations, certificates, and alternative qualification.

# 12. Governance and KPIs

## Governance

The executive steering committee should approve budgets, exceptions, financing above delegated limits, commercial policy, volume restrictions, supplier exit, and contingency sourcing.

The PMO should coordinate implementation, supplier support, data, financing, grievances, regulatory updates, and reporting.

Named executives should be assigned for:

- Customs and product scope;
- Importer/declarant authorization;
- Supplier data;
- Assurance providers;
- Certificate and treasury exposure;
- Contracts and cost sharing;
- Supplier grievances;
- Alternative sourcing;
- Regulatory change.

## KPIs

| Area | Core KPI | Escalation trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Covered products with approved determinations | Any unresolved high-volume product |
| Responsibility | Import flows with documented importer/declarant | Any unidentified responsible party |
| Data | Complete, timely, source-reproducible data | Repeated late or unexplained submissions |
| Fallback exposure | Declining reliance on default/fallback values | Exposure exceeds approved reserve |
| Supplier capability | Priority sites with owners and corrective plans | Missing owner or overdue plan |
| Finance | Forecast versus actual CBAM exposure | Variance above approved tolerance |
| Supply | Strategic supplier retention and concentration | Unplanned exit or excessive concentration |
| Decarbonization | Route-specific physical emissions reduction | No progress after agreed investment period |
| Fairness | Grievances resolved within agreed timeframe | Material cases exceed aging limit |
| Cybersecurity | Unauthorized disclosure incidents | Any material incident |
| Funding | Support delivered against milestones and recovery performance | Missed milestones or unrecovered funding |

Targets should be set after establishing a baseline and labeled as internal management targets, not regulatory requirements.

# 13. Principal risks and mitigations

- **Incorrect classification or scope:** controlled product master and legal review.
- **Authorization or declarant failure:** transaction responsibility matrix and authorization register.
- **Unusable supplier data:** route-specific evidence, correction process, and interim-data approval.
- **Certificate-price or FX volatility:** treasury scenarios, reserves, and contract formulas.
- **Supplier disengagement:** fairness compact, local support, advisory council, and staged remediation.
- **Financing loss or insolvency:** credit review, milestones, security, offtake protection, and recovery rights.
- **Competition-law breach:** legally reviewed council rules and anonymized information.
- **Cyber or confidentiality incident:** role-based access, secure exchange, retention, backups, and audit logs.
- **Greenwashing:** defined product boundaries and substantiation review.
- **Labor, safety, water, or community impacts:** integration with existing EHS and responsible-sourcing controls.
- **Supply disruption:** alternative qualification, safety stock, dual sourcing, and emergency procedures.
- **Regulatory change:** owned legal change log and periodic refresh.

# 14. Immediate next steps

Within the next management cycle, the buyer should:

1. Appoint the executive sponsor and PMO.
2. Freeze and validate the in-scope product and import-flow register.
3. Confirm importer, declarant, customs-representative, and Registry responsibilities.
4. Produce the current 2026 legal, declaration, certificate, and treasury calendar.
5. Assess every supplier site by capability, willingness, strategic criticality, route, and financial need.
6. Issue the common Readiness Pack and fairness compact.
7. Launch pilots with priority steel and aluminum sites.
8. Approve interim-data, correction, and historical-restatement procedures.
9. Prepare standard contract clauses and a CBAM cost-reconciliation schedule.
10. Approve separate readiness and decarbonization funding envelopes.
11. Establish the supplier advisory council with competition-law safeguards.
12. Qualify alternatives for critical products without prematurely exiting cooperative suppliers.

This strategy provides a disciplined middle path: neither unilateral cost transfer nor open-ended subsidy, but a transparent partnership that protects compliance, relationships, supply resilience, and long-term industrial competitiveness.
