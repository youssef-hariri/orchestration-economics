# Global Framework for Geopolitically Driven Consumer Activism

## Executive purpose

Geopolitical consumer activism can affect demand, employee trust, franchise relationships, supply continuity, physical safety, regulatory exposure, market access, investor confidence, and reputation at the same time. The same corporate action may be praised in one market and boycotted in another.

The company should not seek identical statements everywhere or attempt to resolve political disputes. It should use a common operating system that:

1. Protects people and prevents unlawful conduct.
2. Establishes what is verified and what remains uncertain.
3. Determines whether the company caused, contributed to, or is linked to harm.
4. Applies consistent global standards.
5. Adapts implementation to local law, facts, language, culture, and operating conditions.
6. Supports employees, franchisees, suppliers, and affected communities.
7. Communicates only material, accurate, actionable, and safe-to-disclose information.
8. Measures consequences, delivers commitments, and corrects course.

### Central standard

> **Consistency means common standards for safety, dignity, legal rights, human-rights responsibility, evidence, decision rights, and accountability—not identical words or identical commercial actions in every market.**

---

# 1. Scope and assumptions

This framework applies when geopolitical or national-sentiment activism creates material risk involving:

- Boycotts or calls for brand withdrawal
- Alleged support for a government, political movement, or armed actor
- Alleged human-rights complicity
- Controversial donations, suppliers, partnerships, executives, or operating decisions
- Conflicting employee or franchisee demands
- Misinformation, impersonation, or coordinated inauthentic activity
- Protests, threats, vandalism, or violence
- Conflicting government or regulatory requirements
- Supply, staffing, payment, or market-access disruption

Routine customer complaints remain within normal customer-care processes unless an escalation threshold is met.

The framework assumes that:

- Laws governing sanctions, speech, labor, protest, donations, privacy, franchising, and disclosure vary by jurisdiction.
- Online activity is an imperfect proxy for customers, communities, or affected rights holders.
- Facts may be incomplete, disputed, or rapidly changing.
- The company cannot resolve the underlying political dispute.
- Legal permissibility is not the same as ethical adequacy.
- External legal, security, linguistic, or human-rights expertise may be required.
- The framework reduces risk but cannot guarantee public agreement, sales recovery, or freedom from political interpretation.

# 2. Operating principles

## 2.1 Protect people first

Employee, customer, franchisee, supplier, contractor, and community safety takes priority over short-term sales or uninterrupted operations.

## 2.2 Legal compliance is the floor

The company will comply with applicable law while maintaining the highest feasible level of its global standards.

Where law conflicts with a global commitment, the company will:

1. Comply with mandatory law.
2. Avoid unnecessary participation in or facilitation of harmful conduct.
3. Seek lawful alternatives that preserve the global standard.
4. Escalate the conflict to legal, ethics, human-rights, executive, and, where appropriate, board leadership.
5. Assess mitigation, suspension, or exit if severe harm cannot be responsibly addressed.

The decision record should state whether an action is legally required, commercially chosen, or ethically constrained.

## 2.3 Use role-based neutrality

The company will not endorse political parties, governments, armed groups, or contested territorial claims as a commercial actor.

This does not prevent the company from:

- Acknowledging verified human-rights concerns
- Condemning threats, violence, or unlawful discrimination
- Protecting affected people
- Reviewing business relationships
- Remedying harm it caused or contributed to
- Complying with sanctions or other legal requirements
- Acting where it has responsibility or leverage

Neutrality must not become moral indifference or false equivalence.

## 2.4 Determine responsibility before choosing a response

The company must assess whether it:

- **Caused** harm directly
- **Contributed** to harm through its conduct or relationships
- Is **linked** to harm through a business relationship
- Has **no credible connection** to the alleged harm

Campaign visibility does not establish corporate responsibility.

## 2.5 Respect lawful criticism

The company may protect people, data, facilities, and operations, but must not suppress respectful criticism or lawful protest merely because it is politically uncomfortable.

## 2.6 Do not stereotype

Regional decisions must be based on documented differences in facts, law, safety, exposure, operating conditions, or feasible remedies—not assumptions about nationality, religion, ethnicity, language, or political identity.

## 2.7 Prefer substance over symbolism

The company should not announce commitments it cannot implement, measure, or sustain. It should be candid about its limits:

> “We cannot resolve the underlying political dispute, but we can explain our conduct, protect people in our operations, and act where our business relationships create leverage.”

# 3. Global commitments and local adaptation

## 3.1 Non-negotiable global commitments

The following apply across markets:

- Human dignity and non-discrimination
- Employee, customer, partner, and community safety
- Freedom from retaliation for good-faith concerns and lawful expression
- Compliance with applicable law
- Truthful, evidence-based communications
- No intentional funding or facilitation of prohibited conduct
- Human-rights due diligence for relevant operations and relationships
- Responsible sourcing and supply-chain standards
- No threats, hate speech, harassment, doxxing, or incitement through company channels
- No unauthorized corporate political endorsements
- Consistent rules for brand assets, corporate accounts, premises, uniforms, and customer data
- Protection of personal and sensitive operational information
- Prompt correction of material errors
- Documented decision rights, escalation, commitments, and accountability

## 3.2 Permitted local adaptation

Regions may adapt:

- Language, tone, examples, spokesperson, and channel
- Timing and frequency of communication
- Employee-support delivery
- Community engagement
- Stakeholder-consultation methods
- Store hours and proportionate security arrangements
- Product or promotional decisions where lawful and operationally justified
- Explanations of local legal requirements
- Accessibility formats and offline communications

Regions may not alter:

- The human-rights baseline
- Safety or non-discrimination requirements
- Verified facts about company conduct
- Sanctions or other legal positions
- Material claims about donations, ownership, sourcing, or political activity
- Evidence standards
- Escalation criteria
- Rules governing corporate representation

## 3.3 Regional-difference test

A material regional difference is legitimate only when supported by one or more of:

1. Different law or regulatory requirements
2. Different verified facts or company relationships
3. Different employee, customer, franchisee, or partner safety risks
4. Different operating or humanitarian conditions
5. Different stakeholder exposure
6. Different feasible remedies or foreseeable consequences

Each difference should document:

- What differs
- Why it differs
- Which global standard remains unchanged
- Who approved it
- Whether and how it should be explained publicly

### Suggested explanation

> “Our underlying standards are the same in every market. The operational response differs because the legal, factual, safety, or operating conditions are different.”

Cultural sensitivity alone is not sufficient justification for different human-rights standards.

# 4. Incident decision model

Every material incident should follow eight steps.

## 1. Protect

Confirm safety, prevent retaliation and unlawful conduct, secure people and facilities, preserve records, and pause unsafe operations where necessary.

## 2. Classify

Identify whether the issue concerns:

- Perceived affiliation
- Corporate conduct
- Human-rights impact
- Misinformation
- Employee relations
- Franchise governance
- Regulatory conflict
- Physical security
- Operational disruption
- Market access

## 3. Verify

Classify material claims as:

1. **Verified**
2. **Substantially supported but incomplete**
3. **Unverified or disputed**
4. **False or materially misleading**

Record the source, date, language, evidence quality, confidence level, unknowns, and review owner. Use qualified local-language reviewers for sensitive content.

## 4. Assess impact

Assess:

- Human-rights severity
- Employee and public-safety risk
- Legal and regulatory exposure
- Operational and supply impact
- Financial materiality
- Speed and geographic spread
- Franchise and supplier fragmentation
- Misinformation velocity
- Reputational persistence
- Reversibility
- Potential harm caused by the proposed response

Safety and rights severity should outweigh revenue when setting response level.

## 5. Determine responsibility

| Classification | Meaning | Typical responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Company activity directly causes harm | Stop harm, prevent recurrence, support remedy |
| Contribution | Company enables or materially contributes to harm | Cease contribution, use leverage, remediate |
| Linkage | Business relationship connects company to harm | Conduct due diligence, use leverage, mitigate, consider suspension or exit |
| No credible connection | Allegation lacks reasonable substantiation | Correct material misinformation without overstating certainty |

## 6. Decide

Select the least harmful feasible response that protects people and meets the company’s responsibilities. Options include:

- No public statement with private engagement
- Factual correction
- Employee, franchisee, supplier, or community support
- Investigation or independent review
- Supplier or partner remediation
- Temporary operating change
- Humanitarian or community assistance
- Suspension or termination of a relationship
- Market closure or exit
- Public apology and remedy

The decision record should identify alternatives, expected benefits and harms, affected parties, costs, legal constraints, rights implications, reversibility, comparable cases, dissenting views, owners, and review date.

## 7. Communicate

Communicate only what is verified or clearly labeled as uncertain, material, actionable, safe to disclose, and appropriate for the audience.

## 8. Review

Track consequences, deliver commitments, remedy harm, reassess assumptions, and determine whether the response should continue, change, or end.

# 5. Response tiers and escalation

## Tier 1: Routine criticism

**Indicators:** Limited reach, no credible threat, and no material operational, legal, or rights concern.

**Owner:** Local customer-care and communications teams.

**Action:** Normal response, approved FAQs, routine monitoring.

## Tier 2: Emerging campaign or allegation

**Indicators:** Trending boycott, repeated material allegation, growing employee or franchisee concern, or local operational pressure.

**Owner:** Regional response lead.

**Action:** Verify facts, notify regional leadership, issue approved Q&A, brief managers and franchisees, and increase monitoring.

## Tier 3: Material cross-market event

**Indicators:** Multiple markets affected, significant sales or franchise impact, regulatory attention, executive involvement, or contradictory regional messaging.

**Owner:** Incident executive and response council.

**Action:** Establish one source of truth, coordinate global and local communications, assess rights and operational effects, brief relevant investors, and track commitments.

## Tier 4: Crisis

**Indicators:** Violence, credible threats, serious rights allegations, major operational disruption, market-access crisis, or potential material disclosure.

**Owner:** Full crisis-management structure.

**Action:** Activate security and continuity plans, provide frequent updates, engage affected parties and authorities lawfully, and notify the board where appropriate.

### Automatic escalation triggers

Immediate escalation is required for:

- Threats to life or violence
- Attacks on facilities
- Serious human-rights allegations
- Government investigation or contradictory legal direction
- Major supply, payment, production, or store disruption
- Coordinated franchise closures
- Major employee walkout or safety concern
- Unauthorized senior-executive political statement
- Evidence that company conduct caused or contributed to harm
- Material financial or disclosure implications
- Regional statements causing material public confusion

Automatic triggers override ordinary tier discretion.

# 6. Governance and decision rights

## 6.1 Geopolitical Activism Response Council

The standing council should include representatives from:

- Executive leadership
- Legal and regional legal teams
- Corporate affairs and communications
- Human resources and employee relations
- Security and business continuity
- Operations and supply chain
- Risk, compliance, and internal audit
- Ethics, sustainability, and human rights
- Franchise and market-development leadership
- Finance and investor relations
- Consumer insights and data governance
- Relevant regional leadership

The council sets standards, approves high-consequence actions, resolves conflicts, and oversees lessons learned. It should not approve routine local customer responses.

## 6.2 Incident executive

Each material incident must have one accountable executive who controls:

- Activation and cadence
- Workstream assignments
- Resources
- Cross-functional decisions
- Regional coordination
- Escalation
- The decision log and commitment tracker

## 6.3 Authority types

- **Advisory authority:** provides analysis or recommendations.
- **Approval authority:** authorizes the decision.
- **Execution authority:** implements the decision.
- **Emergency authority:** may act immediately to protect people or comply with law, with documentation afterward.

## 6.4 Decision-rights matrix

| Decision | Local market | Region | Global function | Executive/board |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Routine customer response | Approve | Informed | Informed | No |
| Translation or adaptation of approved content | Approve within guardrails | Review | Informed | No |
| Local support within approved budget | Recommend/approve | Approve | Review if sensitive | Sometimes |
| Temporary operating change | Recommend | Approve within threshold | Operations/legal review | If material |
| Local misinformation response | Lead | Review | Support | No |
| Statements on war, sanctions, governments, armed groups, or alleged atrocities | Input | Review | Approve | Often |
| Geopolitically sensitive donation | Recommend | Review | Legal/ethics approval | Usually |
| Market exit, prolonged closure, or major withdrawal | Recommend | Recommend | Assess | Executive/board |
| Emergency closure or evacuation | Act immediately | Coordinate | Support | Notify |
| Material apology or admission | Input | Review | Approve | Executive approval |

# 7. Stakeholder-specific guidance

## 7.1 Customers and boycott participants

The company should:

- Recognize concern without validating unsupported claims.
- Explain verified facts about ownership, funding, sourcing, donations, or operations.
- State the relevant corporate commitment.
- Describe concrete action where warranted.
- Avoid attacking critics or dismissing boycotts as illegitimate.
- Provide a clear information channel.

## 7.2 Franchisees and licensees

Provide:

- Approved facts and local-language Q&A
- Security and incident-reporting guidance
- A named corporate contact
- Manager and customer-service scripts
- Temporary operating flexibility where justified
- Clear brand and social-media rules
- Support for reasonable security or continuity costs
- Guidance for handling activist contact without confrontation

Corporate directives must identify whether they are:

- Legally mandatory
- Safety mandatory
- Required for brand integrity
- Recommended
- Optional support

Material new costs should have a defined allocation, reimbursement, or support mechanism. Master franchisees should be subject to equivalent governance standards.

### Graduated enforcement

1. Guidance and correction
2. Remediation plan
3. Temporary restriction on brand or channel use
4. Suspension
5. Termination where justified by law, contract, safety, or material brand harm

Enforcement must be documented, consistently applied, locally lawful, and subject to review or appeal. Support must not depend on ideological agreement.

## 7.3 Employees

### Employee standard

> “The company will not require employees to affirm a corporate geopolitical position, nor will it tolerate retaliation, coercion, harassment, or discriminatory treatment based on lawful expression or identity.”

The company should:

- Respect lawful personal expression and local labor rights.
- Apply viewpoint-neutral workplace rules.
- Prohibit threats, harassment, coercion, discrimination, and disruption.
- Set clear rules for uniforms, symbols, company systems, branded channels, and customer-facing conduct.
- Respect legally protected collective action.
- Require managers to remain neutral in employment decisions.
- Provide confidential reporting, mental-health support, and safety assistance.
- Give employee-resource groups a voice without making them corporate spokespeople.
- Provide a written response after consultation.

Any restriction must be lawful, necessary, proportionate, viewpoint-neutral, and consistently applied.

## 7.4 Suppliers and contractors

The company should:

- Avoid shifting crisis costs or deadlines unfairly.
- Assess worker impacts before suspension or termination.
- Require human-rights, safety, non-discrimination, and incident-reporting standards.
- Provide grievance channels for supplier workers and contractors.
- Assess whether exit would worsen conditions.
- Apply equivalent standards to third-party security providers.

## 7.5 Investors and lenders

Where material, communications should address:

- Financial and operational exposure
- Safety and continuity actions
- Legal and regulatory developments
- Human-rights review status
- Franchise and supply-chain implications
- Material costs and commitments
- Scenario ranges and uncertainty
- Governance and decision criteria

## 7.6 Regulators

Provide a factual record, explain compliance measures, cooperate with legitimate inquiries, and avoid speculative political commentary. Legal counsel should coordinate required disclosures.

## 7.7 Affected communities and rights holders

Engagement should be:

- Safe
- Accessible
- Appropriate to local conditions
- Clear about what input can influence
- Designed to minimize retaliation risk
- Supported by grievance or remedy channels

The company should record how input affected the decision without exposing vulnerable people.

## 7.8 Media

Use designated spokespeople. State what is known, unknown, and under review. Explain the company’s role and limits. Distinguish corporate policy from franchisee or employee opinion.

# 8. Communications framework

## 8.1 Six-part message structure

1. **Recognition:** Acknowledge the concern.
2. **Relevant principle:** State the applicable commitment.
3. **Verified facts:** Explain what the company has and has not done.
4. **Concrete action:** Describe what is being changed, reviewed, or supported.
5. **Limits and rationale:** Explain what the company cannot responsibly claim or do.
6. **Next step:** Give the next update, milestone, or contact channel.

For Tier 3 and Tier 4 incidents, communications should identify:

- What is known
- What is unknown
- What is being reviewed
- Who owns the review
- What action has already occurred
- What decision will follow
- When the next update or milestone will occur

### Illustrative global statement

> “We understand that people are concerned about this issue. Our standards on human dignity, safety, non-discrimination, and lawful operations apply to our business in every market. Based on the information currently verified, [state facts]. We are [state action], and we will provide a further update by [date or milestone]. We will not speculate about disputed political claims or disclose information that could put employees, customers, or partners at risk.”

### Illustrative accountability statement

> “Our review found that [specific company action] contributed to [specific impact]. We are taking responsibility for that impact. We have [stopped or changed the relevant activity], are providing [remedy or support], and have assigned [owner] to prevent recurrence. We will report progress by [date].”

### Illustrative regional-difference statement

> “Our underlying standards are the same in every market. The response in this market differs because [specific legal, safety, factual, or operating condition]. This difference does not reflect a different view of human dignity, safety, or non-discrimination.”

All sample language is illustrative and requires local factual, legal, and linguistic review.

## 8.2 Communication controls

Controls may apply to:

- Corporate accounts
- Official spokespeople
- Branded channels
- Brand assets
- Corporate premises
- Customer-facing materials

They do not authorize suppression of lawful personal expression outside those channels.

## 8.3 When not to speak

Private engagement or silence may be appropriate when:

- The issue is minor and declining.
- A response would amplify harmful content.
- Facts remain insufficiently verified.
- Public disclosure could endanger people.
- The matter is being addressed directly with affected parties.

Silence is inappropriate when it could imply confirmation of a material allegation, abandonment of employees, concealment of a safety issue, or refusal to address a serious rights concern.

If an update cannot be delivered as scheduled, the company should explain the delay, state what has been learned, identify what remains unresolved, and provide a revised milestone.

# 9. Misinformation and monitoring

Monitoring may identify:

- Credible threats
- Impersonation
- Doxxing
- Coordinated inauthentic activity
- Material operational risk
- Rapidly spreading factual claims

It must not be used to:

- Infer or profile political beliefs
- Target lawful activists
- Suppress respectful criticism
- Retaliate against employees or customers
- Collect unnecessary personal data
- Make employment decisions based on political activity
- Replace human review with automated sentiment scores

Use strict purpose limits, access controls, retention periods, vendor controls, and cross-border transfer safeguards.

A public correction should:

- State the accurate fact first.
- Avoid unnecessarily repeating the false claim.
- Use evidence proportionate to the harm.
- Acknowledge uncertainty.
- Appear on relevant channels where practical.
- Avoid attacking individuals or movements.
- State any action being taken.

# 10. Human-rights due diligence and remedy

For serious allegations, the company must conduct a rights-based review rather than a communications-only response.

## Minimum process

1. Define the alleged harm and affected groups.
2. Assess cause, contribution, linkage, or no credible connection.
3. Review relevant suppliers, franchisees, donations, logistics, technology, and commercial relationships.
4. Consult affected people or legitimate representatives where safe.
5. Assess leverage and mitigation.
6. Determine whether remedy is required.
7. Assign owners, deadlines, and monitoring.
8. Publish an appropriately bounded summary.

Independent review is required or strongly preferred where allegations involve severe abuses, senior leadership, major partners, conflicts of interest, or compromised internal processes.

## Exit-impact assessment

Before exiting a market, supplier, or partner, assess:

- Worker income and conditions
- Franchisee viability
- Small suppliers and local businesses
- Customer access
- Vulnerable communities
- Retaliation risk
- Whether withdrawal addresses the harm
- Whether continued engagement could produce better outcomes
- Reversibility and re-entry criteria

Exit is not automatically the most ethical or effective response.

# 11. Security and operational continuity

## 11.1 Proportionality standard

The company should distinguish:

- Peaceful protest
- Nuisance or obstruction
- Credible threats
- Violence

Security responses must:

- Use the least restrictive effective measure.
- Prioritize de-escalation.
- Comply with local law.
- Avoid discriminatory treatment.
- Include human-rights and non-discrimination training.
- Require incident reporting and review.
- Apply standards to third-party security providers.
- Record the basis for closures or operating restrictions.

## 11.2 Operational actions

Temporary closure, product withdrawal, route change, or market adjustment may be justified where:

- Safety cannot reasonably be maintained.
- Legal compliance is uncertain or impossible.
- Supply continuity requires unacceptable risk.
- The company materially contributes to serious harm.
- Continued operations expose people to disproportionate retaliation.
- A pause enables credible investigation or remediation.

Every major action should assess:

- Who loses income or access
- Whether harm is reduced or displaced
- Retaliation risk
- Supply and price effects
- Reversibility
- Reopening or reinstatement criteria
- Financial, insurance, and accounting implications

A safety-driven pause must not be misrepresented as a political endorsement.

# 12. Legal, ethical, and conduct guardrails

High-risk actions must be reviewed for:

- Sanctions and export controls
- Anti-boycott and anti-terror-financing rules
- Anti-corruption and political-contribution law
- Employment, labor, protest, and collective-action law
- Anti-discrimination requirements
- Consumer-protection and advertising rules
- Defamation and false-statement risk
- Privacy, surveillance, and data-transfer restrictions
- Franchise and commercial-contract law
- Competition law
- Product-labeling and origin rules
- Securities-disclosure obligations
- Charity and aid regulations
- Workplace safety and duty-of-care obligations
- Insurance and business-interruption requirements

The company must not:

- Make knowingly false or misleading statements.
- Conceal material facts where disclosure is required.
- Falsely label criticism as misinformation.
- Pressure employees or franchisees to adopt personal political views.
- Use threats, retaliation, discriminatory treatment, or unlawful surveillance.
- Evade sanctions, anti-boycott rules, or other legal restrictions.
- Expose aid recipients, activists, employees, or partners unnecessarily.
- Manufacture public support or manipulate sentiment.
- Use humanitarian language to disguise commercial motives.
- Shift material crisis costs unfairly to franchisees or suppliers.

## Legal triage

- **Level 1:** Pre-approved routine local response
- **Level 2:** Regional legal review
- **Level 3:** Global legal, compliance, ethics, human-rights, and communications review
- **Level 4:** Executive or board review for market exit, serious rights issues, major disclosures, or politically sensitive transactions

For each level, define the responsible reviewer, maximum review time, approval authority, and actions permitted under pre-approved language.

Counsel should determine privilege, litigation holds, retention, access, and cross-border transfer requirements.

---

# 13. Partisanship and comparable-treatment review

Before approving a high-risk action, ask:

1. Does the action advantage or disadvantage a political actor?
2. Is it grounded in company conduct, law, safety, or rights responsibility?
3. Would comparable facts involving another constituency receive equivalent treatment?
4. Are factual differences documented?
5. Is the same human-rights standard being applied?
6. Could the action create a foreseeable appearance of selective concern?
7. Can the company explain the action without relying on identity or nationality?
8. Is the difference based on evidence or merely political pressure?
9. Has a relevant precedent or comparable case been reviewed?

Political interpretation cannot always be avoided. The objective is principled, proportionate, evidence-based, lawful, explainable, and accountable conduct.

# 14. Crisis timetable

## First 24 hours

- Activate the response tier.
- Confirm safety and address urgent threats.
- Appoint the incident executive.
- Preserve records.
- Verify the core allegation.
- Establish one source of truth and a decision log.
- Identify legal, rights, and operational constraints.
- Brief employees, managers, franchisees, and relevant suppliers.
- Restrict unauthorized corporate or branded statements where necessary.
- Prepare audience-specific holding language.
- Set the next decision time.

## First 72 hours

- Complete the initial impact and causation assessment.
- Determine whether independent review is required.
- Decide whether public acknowledgment is necessary.
- Provide localized FAQs and support.
- Implement proportionate security and continuity measures.
- Assign owners, deadlines, and measures for commitments.
- Brief senior leadership, the board, regulators, or investors where material.
- Assess financial, insurance, and accounting implications.

## Closure

An incident closes only when:

- Immediate safety risks are addressed
- Legal and regulatory matters have owners
- Corrective or remedial actions are underway
- Employees and franchisees are briefed
- Public commitments are tracked
- Operating changes have review or reopening criteria
- Lessons are documented
- Monitoring is intentionally reduced
- Independent or board reporting is complete where required

# 15. Illustrative scenarios

## Scenario A: One action praised in one region and boycotted in another

**Situation:** A humanitarian initiative is welcomed in one market and criticized as partisan in another.

**Response:**

- Tier 3 if multiple markets and material brand impact are involved.
- Verify funding sources, eligibility criteria, recipients, governance, and independent oversight.
- State the humanitarian purpose and limits of the initiative.
- Apply the same eligibility and governance standards everywhere.
- Permit different local examples, language, and community engagement.
- Document why any regional operating response differs.
- Monitor whether recipients, employees, or local partners face retaliation.

**Metrics:** Reach of factual explanation, recipient safety, completion of promised oversight, employee and franchisee understanding.

## Scenario B: Franchisees demand opposing political statements

**Situation:** Operators in two markets request mutually incompatible endorsements.

**Response:**

- Decline requests that exceed the company’s commercial role.
- Provide a shared statement on dignity, safety, non-discrimination, and lawful operations.
- Permit local operational information and community support within guardrails.
- Provide security, employee, and customer-service assistance.
- Apply brand rules consistently and document any variance.
- Do not condition support on public endorsement.

## Scenario C: Employees demand a corporate boycott

**Situation:** Employees call for market withdrawal and organize collective action.

**Response:**

- Determine whether the action is legally protected.
- Protect employees from retaliation and colleagues from harassment or coercion.
- Conduct a causation and human-rights assessment.
- Explain decision criteria, legal constraints, and what can be influenced.
- Provide a written response and a review milestone.
- Consider operational action only after assessing rights, safety, feasibility, and consequences.

## Scenario D: Viral claim links the brand to prohibited funding

**Situation:** A post claims customer purchases directly fund a sanctioned actor.

**Response:**

- Verify financial flows, donations, suppliers, franchise arrangements, and compliance records.
- Classify the claim as verified, incomplete, disputed, or false.
- Coordinate with legal and compliance teams.
- Issue a concise correction if materially false and harmful.
- Explain relevant evidence without exposing sensitive information.
- Report impersonation or credible threats through appropriate channels.
- Investigate any real disclosure gap underlying the allegation.

## Scenario E: Protests create a physical-security risk

**Situation:** Demonstrations near stores become confrontational.

**Response:**

- Escalate immediately if threats or violence are credible.
- Distinguish peaceful protest from threats and violence.
- Use de-escalation and the least restrictive effective measure.
- Adjust hours or close temporarily only where justified.
- Support employees and franchisees.
- Explain accurately that the decision is safety-based.
- Establish reopening criteria and review the effects on workers and customers.

## Scenario F: Major supplier faces severe rights allegations

**Situation:** A key supplier is accused of forced labor, while immediate termination could harm workers and disrupt supply.

**Response:**

- Conduct an independent, rights-based review.
- Assess whether the company caused, contributed to, or is linked to the harm.
- Protect workers and preserve evidence.
- Use leverage for immediate safeguards and remediation.
- Develop an alternative-sourcing plan.
- Conduct an exit-impact assessment before termination.
- Suspend or exit if severe harm cannot be mitigated responsibly.
- Report the scope and outcome without exposing vulnerable workers.

# 16. Metrics and accountability

Metrics should measure protection, accuracy, execution, trust, and unintended consequences—not merely sales recovery or lower online activity.

## Leading indicators

- Time to acknowledge a material incident
- Time to verify core claims
- Threat reports and response times
- Employee hotline and retaliation complaints
- Employee and franchisee briefing completion
- Unauthorized corporate or branded statements
- Regulatory inquiries
- Supplier and logistics alerts
- Customer-service escalations
- Evidence status and reach of material claims
- Participation in listening sessions
- Completion of legal and human-rights reviews

## Outcome indicators

- Employee injuries, threats, absenteeism, and retention
- Store closures and operational downtime
- Franchise retention and variance requests
- Supply-service levels
- Customer retention and sales by market
- Investor and stakeholder confidence
- Resolution of verified misinformation
- Completion of remedy and corrective actions
- Legal claims, fines, or investigations
- Effects on workers, communities, aid recipients, or vulnerable groups
- Recurrence of the same control weakness

Every material commitment must have:

- Executive and operational owner
- Deadline
- Success measure
- Reporting method
- Review date
- Escalation consequence for delay

Post-incident reviews should be led partly by a function not responsible for the original decision and should record dissenting views on high-consequence actions.

# 17. Implementation roadmap

## Before activation

- Establish the response council and incident-executive model.
- Approve global standards and role-based neutrality language.
- Create decision rights, thresholds, escalation contacts, and emergency authority.
- Map geopolitical exposure by market.
- Review franchise, supplier, security, communications, insurance, and continuity arrangements.
- Establish threat, retaliation, grievance, and rights-reporting channels.
- Create message templates, evidence protocols, and a conflict-language review process.
- Designate local and global spokespeople.

## First 90 days

- Complete regional stakeholder and rights-holder mapping.
- Establish legal triage and records-management procedures.
- Create the regional-difference documentation form.
- Establish franchisee cost-allocation and variance procedures.
- Create employee manager guidance.
- Train managers, franchisees, customer-care teams, social teams, and security providers.
- Run exercises involving opposing market pressure, supplier harm, protected employee action, contradictory government requirements, and sensitive donations.
- Build the commitment tracker and dashboard.
- Test crisis notification, continuity, accessibility, and offline communication channels.

## Ongoing

- Refresh the geopolitical risk register.
- Conduct periodic tabletop exercises.
- Audit regional communications and franchise enforcement.
- Review employee-expression and non-retaliation practices.
- Test threat, grievance, and remedy channels.
- Review human-rights due diligence and exit-impact decisions.
- Conduct cross-market comparability reviews during active incidents.
- Report material incidents and systemic findings to senior leadership and the board.
- Update the framework for legal, platform, and geopolitical developments.

# Final decision standard

When markets pull in opposing directions, the company should ask:

1. What is happening, and what is verified?
2. Who faces the greatest immediate risk?
3. What is the company’s actual connection to the alleged harm?
4. Which global standard applies?
5. What can the company lawfully and credibly change?
6. What response is least harmful and most effective?
7. Why might implementation legitimately differ by market?
8. How will the decision be communicated, measured, reviewed, and corrected?

The company’s credibility will come less from finding universally popular language than from applying a repeatable system of **safety, dignity, evidence, responsibility, local awareness, lawful conduct, and accountability**.
