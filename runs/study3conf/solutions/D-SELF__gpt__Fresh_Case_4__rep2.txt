# Global Flexible-Work Policy

**Policy owner:** Global People and Workplace  
**Applies to:** Company employees in all twelve countries of operation  
**Effective date:** The date stated in the approved implementation notice. Country implementation begins only after the relevant country addendum and required consultation and approvals are complete.  
**Review cycle:** Quarterly governance review and formal review at least annually, or sooner following material legal, collective-agreement, operational, security, or business changes.

> **Important:** This policy establishes global minimum standards. It does not replace applicable law, collective agreements, works-council or union arrangements, employment contracts, statutory rights, or approved workplace adjustments. Country addenda provide the legally reviewed operating rules for each location.

---

## 1. Executive summary

Our company operates across twelve countries with different laws, client expectations, cultural norms, and views about office presence and visible commitment. No single work pattern is appropriate for every role or market.

This policy provides a common global framework for:

- remote and hybrid work;
- flexible start and finish times;
- compressed workweeks;
- reduced-hours arrangements;
- job sharing and temporary flexibility;
- right-to-disconnect protections;
- client and operational coverage;
- career and performance fairness.

The policy combines employee choice with clear business requirements. Flexibility is not an entitlement to a particular schedule or location, and it is not a reward for loyalty. Decisions must be based on documented role, client, security, safety, legal, or operational requirements.

### Global commitments

**Mandatory requirements**

- Approved flexibility must not create a career or compensation disadvantage.
- Physical visibility, long hours, online status, or out-of-hours responsiveness must not be used as stand-alone measures of commitment or performance.
- Working time, overtime, rest, leave, health and safety, accessibility, privacy, and security obligations must be protected.
- Routine work communications should not require a response outside agreed working time.
- Decisions must use objective criteria, be documented, and be reviewable.
- Employees must have reasonable and comparable access to information, development, recognition, and progression.
- Country differences must be legally reviewed, proportionate, and transparent.

**Recommended practices**

- Use predictable team rhythms rather than ad hoc attendance.
- Design office days around clear purposes.
- Use asynchronous documentation and regional handoffs.
- Rotate inconvenient meeting times.
- Review arrangements regularly against service, workload, wellbeing, and equity outcomes.

**Country-specific confirmation required**

Before implementation, each country must confirm its rules on:

- working-time limits, breaks, rest, and overtime;
- right to disconnect;
- consultation and employee-representation requirements;
- leave, public holidays, pay, benefits, and payroll;
- home-working safety and expenses;
- privacy and monitoring;
- cross-border work;
- tax, immigration, and social-security matters.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 Fairness without forced uniformity

Employees in different countries, roles, or work patterns may have different arrangements. This is fair when differences are based on documented requirements and employees retain comparable access to opportunity and protection.

Fairness does not require identical schedules, office attendance, or benefits where roles differ. It does require:

- consistent decision criteria;
- clear explanations for differences;
- proportional restrictions;
- reviewable decisions;
- monitoring for unequal outcomes.

### 2.2 Flexibility is work design, not a loyalty test

Managers must not use office attendance, extended hours, informal networking, or rapid out-of-hours responses as proxies for ambition, reliability, or commitment.

### 2.3 Outcomes over visibility

Performance must be evaluated using role-relevant evidence, including:

- delivery and reliability;
- quality and customer impact;
- collaboration and knowledge sharing;
- technical or professional contribution;
- leadership and development of others;
- compliance, security, and conduct.

### 2.4 Sustainable performance

Flexible work must not result in:

- hidden overtime;
- missed breaks or rest;
- unsafe fatigue;
- five days of work compressed into four;
- recurring availability outside agreed hours;
- unreasonable workload transfer to colleagues.

### 2.5 Local context with global safeguards

Local implementation may reflect communication norms, religious and public-holiday calendars, employee-representation structures, and client practices. Cultural preference alone must not justify unnecessary attendance, excessive availability, or unequal career access.

## 3. Governing standards and scope

### 3.1 Governing instruments

Applicable law and binding collective or representative arrangements govern the relevant subject. Employment contracts and approved workplace adjustments apply to the extent lawful and applicable to the individual.

Country addenda, this policy, and team working agreements must not waive, reduce, or contradict statutory, collectively agreed, contractual, or approved adjustment rights.

Managers may not disregard or alter a contractual term, collective provision, or approved adjustment on the assumption that it conflicts with this policy. Any proposed change must be reviewed by HR and Legal and follow required consent, consultation, amendment, or accommodation procedures.

A departure from a global standard is permitted only where documented evidence shows that compliance would be:

- unlawful;
- technically impossible after reasonable technical, organizational, staffing, scheduling, accessibility, and task-segmentation alternatives have been considered; or
- inconsistent with a binding and applicable collective or representative arrangement.

General managerial preference, budget pressure, historical practice, or convenience is not sufficient.

### 3.2 Scope

This policy applies to company employees, subject to local law.

Contractors, agency workers, consultants, and other non-employees remain governed by their contracts and the requirements of their employing or supplying entity. The company will communicate applicable health, safety, security, confidentiality, and site-access requirements without altering their legal status.

## 5. Work-pattern options

Flexibility is available where compatible with role requirements, employee circumstances, client commitments, security, team coverage, and applicable law. Approval is not automatic, but every request must be considered fairly.

### 5.1 Role classifications

Each role family must be classified using objective criteria as:

- **Remote-eligible:** Work can generally be performed away from a company office.
- **Hybrid-eligible:** Regular remote work is feasible, with office attendance for defined purposes.
- **Office-led:** Physical presence is substantially required because of equipment, secure facilities, client, regulatory, service, or safety requirements.
- **Location-restricted:** Work must occur in a specified country, region, site, or secure environment.

Each classification must state:

- the job requirements;
- evidence supporting the classification;
- permitted flexibility options;
- responsible approver;
- review date.

A role classification creates a presumptive eligibility framework. An individual denial must identify an additional role-specific, assignment-specific, security-specific, coverage-related, or documented performance-support reason. Repeating a general preference for office attendance is insufficient.

Before classifying an entire role family as office-led or location-restricted, the company must consider task segmentation, secure remote facilities, technical controls, regional handoffs, and other alternatives.

Role classifications must be reviewed at least annually and following material changes to the role, technology, security controls, client obligations, or operating model.

### 5.2 Remote and hybrid work

Where remote or hybrid work is approved:

- office days must have a documented purpose;
- attendance must normally be communicated at least 10 calendar days in advance;
- shorter notice requires a documented emergency, legal, or genuinely unforeseeable operational reason;
- repeated short-notice requirements must be reviewed;
- remote participants must be able to contribute effectively to important meetings;
- decisions and relevant context must be recorded in shared systems;
- employees must have reasonable and comparable access to development, recognition, senior leaders, and high-value work.

Minimum office-presence requirements must be necessary, proportionate, consistently applied, accessible, and reviewed periodically.

Client preference alone is not sufficient unless it reflects a documented contractual, service-level, regulatory, security, relationship-management, or materially evidenced business requirement.

### 5.3 Flexible start and finish times

Employees may request varied start, finish, or break times. Approval is required where the arrangement affects contractual hours, scheduled coverage, client commitments, security, or team operations.

Teams must publish:

- core collaboration hours;
- response windows;
- meeting norms;
- handoff procedures;
- coverage arrangements.

Flexibility must not create an unnecessarily broad span of expected availability.

### 5.4 Compressed workweeks

A compressed schedule distributes the same contractual hours over fewer days, such as:

- four longer days;
- a nine-day fortnight;
- another locally lawful pattern.

A compressed schedule does not automatically reduce total hours, pay, or workload. It must not:

- breach working-time or rest limits;
- remove legally required breaks;
- create unsafe fatigue;
- create hidden overtime;
- impair client or critical-system coverage;
- transfer unreasonable work to colleagues;
- become an informal expectation for non-participants.

Before approving longer workdays, the company must consider commute, caregiving, health, safety, night work, rest, workload concentration, and incident risk.

### 5.5 Reduced-hours arrangements

A reduced-hours arrangement decreases contractual hours and is distinct from compression.

Written terms must specify:

- hours and schedule;
- compensation and benefits;
- workload and objectives;
- meeting, service, and escalation responsibilities;
- leave and public-holiday treatment;
- bonus, equity, pension, and social-security treatment where relevant;
- decision authority;
- handoffs and coverage;
- review, withdrawal, and return arrangements.

Objectives, meeting load, service coverage, decision responsibilities, and escalation duties must be adjusted proportionately unless a documented role analysis establishes otherwise.

### 5.6 Job sharing and temporary flexibility

Job sharing, part-time work, temporary remote work, and other arrangements may be approved where lawful and operationally viable. They must include clear accountability, workload allocation, handoffs, coverage, and a review date.

## 6. Requests and approvals

### 6.1 Tiered process

The company will use:

- a **standard process** for low-risk arrangements within an approved role and country category; and
- an **enhanced process** for cross-border work, reduced-hours arrangements, unusual schedules, security-sensitive work, client-critical roles, and arrangements with significant legal or payroll implications.

Employees are not required to provide information the company can reasonably obtain from existing role, team, security, or coverage records.

Employees may propose coverage solutions, but managers remain responsible for assessing and designing workable team coverage.

### 6.2 Request information

A request should include, where relevant:

- proposed location and schedule;
- requested duration;
- work pattern;
- role and client implications;
- coverage and handoff considerations;
- technology or security considerations;
- adjustment or accessibility needs through the confidential process.

Employees must not be required to disclose unnecessary medical or personal information.

### 6.3 Manager assessment

Managers must consider:

- essential role requirements;
- client and service obligations;
- team coordination and time zones;
- security, privacy, and approved work locations;
- health, safety, fatigue, and accessibility;
- working-time and rest compliance;
- measurable performance outcomes;
- impact on colleagues and coverage;
- less restrictive alternatives.

“Operationally viable” means supportable by measurable service, coverage, working-time, security, safety, workload, or client requirements. Managerial convenience, historical practice, visibility, trust, or cultural preference is not sufficient.

HR review is required before denying flexibility on the basis of a documented performance or supervision need.

### 6.4 Decision standards and timelines

The decision must:

- use applicable country and role criteria;
- be approved by the designated authority;
- be documented in writing;
- state the effective date and review date;
- comply with consultation, consent, and representative requirements.

The decision period begins when the designated process owner receives the minimum required information.

- Standard requests should normally be decided within **15 calendar days**.
- Enhanced requests should normally be decided within **30 calendar days**.
- Any delay caused by missing information, consultation, or specialist review must be explained with a revised target date.

Country addenda may establish different periods where required by law or justified by the process.

A denial or modification must explain:

- the specific requirement affected;
- the evidence supporting it;
- why the requested arrangement would not meet that requirement;
- alternatives considered;
- any available alternative arrangement;
- appeal rights.

## 7. Working time, overtime, leave, and rest

### 7.1 Working-time protection

Employees and managers must comply with applicable:

- working-time limits;
- breaks and daily or weekly rest;
- leave requirements;
- overtime rules;
- health and safety requirements;
- time-recording obligations.

Managers must not encourage workarounds to legal limits, require under-recording, schedule recurring meetings during protected rest, or treat excessive hours as commitment.

### 7.2 Time recording

Each country addendum must specify:

- which employee categories must record time;
- what activities count as working time;
- treatment of meetings, messaging, training, travel, incident response, standby, and call-out work;
- the approved recording system;
- correction and approval procedures;
- retention periods;
- escalation routes.

Employees must record working time accurately where required. Managers must not reject accurate records or instruct employees to omit work.

Failure to obtain prior overtime authorization does not remove any obligation to record or compensate working time where required by law. Any conduct response to unauthorized overtime must be separate from the employee’s entitlement and must consider whether the employee was directed, encouraged, or pressured to work.

Managers must periodically certify that:

- known work outside scheduled hours has been recorded;
- employees have not been instructed to omit time;
- recurring overtime has been addressed.

### 7.3 Overtime and recurring excess hours

Overtime must be necessary, authorized where required, accurately recorded, and compensated or offset according to law, contract, collective agreement, and local policy.

Recurring overtime requires action involving staffing, prioritization, scope, process, or deadlines. It must not be solved through informal unpaid work.

### 7.4 Leave, public holidays, and benefits

Every compressed or reduced-hours arrangement must state how it affects:

- annual leave;
- public holidays, including holidays falling on non-working days;
- sick, family, and other statutory leave;
- overtime thresholds;
- benefits;
- bonuses and equity;
- pension and social-security contributions;
- payroll;
- office closures and holiday coverage.

## 8. Right to disconnect and availability

### 8.1 Definitions

- **Agreed working time:** Contractual, rostered, scheduled, or formally approved working periods, including approved on-call periods where legally treated as working or availability time.
- **Ordinary availability:** Reachability during scheduled working time without a special out-of-hours obligation.
- **On-call:** A formally assigned duty requiring a specified response during a defined period.
- **Standby:** A restriction on the employee’s freedom during a defined period, whether or not a call occurs.
- **Call-out:** Work actually performed in response to an on-call or emergency requirement.
- **Emergency response:** Unplanned work necessary to protect people, security, critical systems, clients, or legally protected interests.
- **Right to disconnect:** The right, subject to applicable law and defined exceptions, not to monitor, read, respond to routine work communications, or attend routine work activities outside agreed working time.

Where contractual, rostered, scheduled, and approved periods differ, the written country or individual arrangement must identify the controlling schedule. Historical work patterns do not create additional agreed working time unless formally approved.

### 8.2 General rule

Outside agreed working time, employees are not expected to:

- read or respond to routine communications;
- attend routine meetings;
- monitor collaboration tools;
- remain continuously available.

Messages sent outside working time do not create an expectation of immediate response. Delayed delivery and clear priority labels should be used where tools permit.

Managers must not create routine out-of-hours expectations through informal messages, recurring late meetings, time-zone practices, or implied performance norms.

### 8.3 Exceptions

Limited exceptions may include:

- health, safety, security, major incidents, or critical-system emergencies;
- scheduled on-call or support duties;
- time-critical client or regulatory obligations;
- pre-approved cross-time-zone work;
- other lawful arrangements specifically documented under the country addendum.

Each exception must state:

- duration;
- affected employees;
- responsible manager;
- response expectations;
- compensation or time off;
- rest protections;
- escalation route;
- expiry or review date.

Insufficient staffing, time-zone convenience, general client preference, or a cultural expectation of responsiveness is not sufficient grounds for a recurring exception.

## 9. Coverage and client service

Coverage is a management responsibility. It must not depend on continuous availability by every employee.

Each flexible team must maintain an accessible working agreement covering:

- working patterns and time zones;
- core collaboration hours;
- response times by channel;
- urgent and routine communication methods;
- primary and backup contacts;
- on-call arrangements;
- handoffs;
- absence and holiday coverage;
- escalation routes;
- office-day purposes.

Coverage may be achieved through:

- regional handoffs;
- rotations;
- scheduled overlap;
- backup staffing;
- service rosters;
- automation;
- shared documentation;
- asynchronous work.

Cross-time-zone meeting burdens must be rotated where practical. Repeated inability to rotate must be documented and reviewed.

A team working agreement may not:

- alter contractual hours;
- create recurring on-call or standby duties;
- impose new attendance obligations;
- waive rest or disconnect rights;
- reduce an approved adjustment;
- create unpaid availability obligations,

unless the required legal, HR, representative, and employee-approval processes are complete.

HR or the country policy owner must approve working agreements that establish recurring attendance, fixed availability windows, on-call duties, recurring out-of-hours meetings, holiday coverage, or material handoff obligations.

## 10. Performance, career, and visibility protections

### 10.1 Performance

Performance must be assessed against agreed, role-appropriate objectives. The following may not be stand-alone performance indicators:

- office attendance;
- webcam use;
- online status;
- message volume;
- workday length;
- instant responses outside working hours;
- attendance at unnecessary meetings.

Flexibility requests, approved work patterns, appeals, and disconnect complaints must not be used as performance or promotion factors except as necessary to administer an arrangement, investigate misconduct, or comply with law.

### 10.2 Comparable access

Comparable access means:

- timely access to relevant information;
- a genuine opportunity to provide input;
- participation during paid working time;
- consideration under the same published criteria;
- accessible alternatives where timing or location prevents attendance;
- follow-up access to decision-makers;
- no dependence on unpaid socializing or out-of-hours networking.

Managers must provide reasonable and comparable access to:

- important meetings and decisions;
- senior leaders;
- mentoring and sponsorship;
- training;
- stretch assignments;
- recognition;
- promotion and succession processes.

Informal office networking must not be the primary route to high-value work.

For stretch assignments, high-profile projects, and succession opportunities, managers must record:

- selection criteria;
- eligible pool;
- selected employee;
- business rationale.

### 10.3 Audits and corrective action

Global People must review unexplained differences by country, role, level, gender, disability where lawful, and work pattern.

Reviews must cover:

- performance ratings;
- promotions and succession;
- compensation;
- recognition;
- stretch assignments;
- mentoring and sponsorship;
- senior-leader access;
- attrition and involuntary exits.

A material disparity must have a named owner, remediation plan, target completion date, and follow-up measurement.

## 11. Responsibilities

### Employees

Employees must:

- follow approved schedules, coverage, security, safety, and time-recording requirements;
- use approved work locations and technology;
- raise workload, fatigue, accessibility, safety, or disconnect concerns;
- participate in required handoffs;
- protect confidential information;
- request approval before working cross-border.

Employees may request flexibility, appeal decisions, and raise concerns without retaliation.

### Managers

Managers must:

- apply objective eligibility criteria;
- assess outcomes rather than visible effort;
- document decisions and attendance requirements;
- set clear objectives and response expectations;
- protect rest and disconnect periods;
- design coverage and handoffs;
- provide comparable career access;
- support accommodations;
- address recurring overtime;
- complete required training.

Managers may not impose informal attendance rules, reward late-night responsiveness, withhold assignments because of reduced visibility, or use surveillance as a substitute for management.

Repeated breaches may affect leadership assessments and may result in corrective or disciplinary action under applicable procedures.

### HR and People teams

HR is responsible for:

- maintaining country addenda;
- advising on local employment and representative requirements;
- administering requests, appeals, and accommodations;
- monitoring consistency and equity;
- training managers;
- maintaining the policy register;
- escalating systemic concerns.

### Legal, Privacy, Security, Tax, Immigration, Payroll, Finance, and Occupational Health

These functions provide required review or approval for:

- country implementation;
- working-time and pay rules;
- accommodations;
- monitoring and data use;
- security-sensitive work;
- cross-border work;
- tax, immigration, payroll, and social-security risks;
- equipment, expenses, and health and safety.

### Executives and country leaders

Leaders must provide resources, enforce the global standard, approve necessary local differences, and address material disparities, recurring overtime, failed pilots, and repeated manager noncompliance.

No executive decision may override an applicable legal, representative, privacy, or security requirement.

## 12. Accessibility, accommodations, health, safety, and privacy

Requests related to disability, health, pregnancy, family responsibilities, caregiving, religious observance, or other protected grounds must be assessed under the applicable legal and company process. They must not be treated as ordinary discretionary flexibility where a separate accommodation, equality, leave, health-and-safety, or pregnancy process applies.

The company will consider reasonable adjustments promptly and in good faith, subject to lawful limitations such as essential functions, undue hardship, safety, security, and client requirements.

Client requirements may limit an adjustment only where the requirement is documented, lawful, necessary, and cannot reasonably be met through reassignment, secure access, scheduling, substitution, staffing, or another accommodation.

Disability and medical requests must receive independent review by the designated accommodations team, occupational-health provider, or HR specialist. The process must provide:

- acknowledgment within five business days where practicable;
- prompt interim-risk assessment;
- interim measures where appropriate;
- limits on requested documentation;
- a decision or status update normally within 15 calendar days where practicable;
- confidentiality controls;
- an appeal route.

Medical and personal information must be:

- collected only when necessary;
- kept separate from ordinary performance records;
- accessed on a need-to-know basis;
- retained only as long as necessary;
- processed lawfully.

Each country addendum must define:

- minimum equipment;
- replacement and repair;
- connectivity and expense reimbursement;
- ergonomic support;
- remote-work safety;
- alternative worksites;
- incident reporting;
- business-continuity arrangements.

No monitoring or analytics tool may be deployed, expanded, or used for employment decisions before required Legal, Privacy, Security, and representative review is complete and required employee notices are issued.

Managers may not use calendar presence, message timestamps, login data, meeting attendance, status indicators, keystrokes, webcam use, or message volume as individual productivity or commitment measures unless expressly authorized for a defined, legally reviewed purpose.

Meeting recordings and transcripts must comply with local requirements for notice, consent, objection, alternatives, retention, access, and automated transcription. They may not be used as generalized performance evidence or participation surveillance.

## 13. Cross-border work

Working from another country or an unapproved location requires prior authorization under the mandatory cross-border-work standard.

The standard is:

- owned by the Cross-Border Approval Owner;
- version-controlled;
- maintained in the policy register;
- incorporated into country addenda;
- provided to employees before approval.

It must define:

- application and approval lead times;
- permitted duration thresholds;
- prohibited countries and locations;
- prohibited data and roles;
- immigration and work authorization;
- payroll, tax, social-security, and permanent-establishment analysis;
- privacy and security controls;
- emergency travel;
- remediation for unauthorized work.

Emergency approvals must identify:

- the emergency approval contact;
- maximum initial duration;
- permitted activities;
- prohibited data or roles;
- employee instructions;
- tax and immigration escalation;
- follow-up approval deadline.

The company will distinguish deliberate unauthorized work from good-faith mistakes or emergencies. Remediation may include stopping work from the location, payroll or tax correction, immigration advice, security review, or reimbursement of required correction costs where appropriate. Discipline must be proportionate to intent, notice, circumstances, and actual risk.

## 14. Pilots and four-day-week trials

Four-day-week, reduced-hours, and other material schedule pilots require written approval from Global People, the business sponsor, and all required Legal, Finance, Payroll, Security, Privacy, Occupational Health, and employee-representative stakeholders.

A pilot plan must specify:

- eligible and excluded roles;
- transparent selection and waiting-list rules;
- accessibility and accommodation arrangements;
- treatment of employees who decline;
- whether hours are compressed or reduced;
- pay, benefits, leave, holidays, bonus, equity, and pension treatment;
- work that will stop, be automated, reassigned, or deprioritized;
- output, service, and staffing assumptions;
- coverage and handoffs;
- overtime, on-call, fatigue, and rest protections;
- success and stop criteria;
- withdrawal and return arrangements;
- performance and career safeguards;
- named pilot owner;
- independent stop authority.

Participants must not be expected to complete five days of materially unchanged work in four days without redesign and resourcing. Nonparticipants must not be penalized, and participation must not confer an improper career advantage.

Participants may withdraw where permitted by the pilot terms and applicable law without adverse performance or career consequences. Return to a standard schedule must state the effective date, notice period, pay, benefits, leave, objectives, and coverage treatment.

Required monitoring includes:

- baseline data before launch;
- early safety review within 30–60 days;
- monthly operational monitoring;
- midpoint review;
- final review.

Immediate suspension triggers include:

- legal breach;
- unsafe fatigue;
- material service failure;
- recurring unplanned overtime;
- serious security or safety risk;
- discriminatory or retaliatory effects.

## 15. Exceptions and emergency changes

### Individual exceptions

Temporary or permanent exceptions may be considered for:

- disability, health, pregnancy, or rehabilitation;
- caregiving or family responsibilities;
- religious observance;
- emergencies;
- temporary relocation or immigration transition;
- recruitment, retention, project, or client needs.

Exceptions must be documented, confidential where appropriate, and reviewed for consistency and unintended inequity.

### Business exceptions

The company may temporarily modify arrangements because of:

- major incidents;
- security threats;
- disaster recovery;
- regulatory or client requirements;
- office or network failure;
- urgent organizational change.

Emergency modifications must be limited to the minimum scope and duration reasonably necessary. The company must identify:

- triggering event;
- affected employees;
- start date;
- expected end date;
- decision owner;
- review interval;
- coverage and health-and-safety controls.

Where prior consultation is legally required and cannot occur before action, the company will consult as soon as legally practicable and document the basis for interim action.

Pay, rest, overtime, safety, accessibility, and other legal rights remain protected.

## 16. Appeals, complaints, and remedies

Employees may challenge:

- a denial or modification;
- an attendance requirement;
- an inaccurate role classification;
- retaliation or career disadvantage;
- excessive or recurring out-of-hours work;
- unequal development or promotion access;
- failure to implement an approved arrangement;
- accessibility, safety, privacy, or security concerns.

Available channels include:

- the manager;
- HR or People;
- a second-level manager;
- Employee Relations;
- a confidential alternative channel;
- statutory, collective, whistleblower, discrimination, health-and-safety, wage-and-hour, or data-protection channels.

Employees do not need to confront the original decision-maker where that is uncomfortable, unsafe, or impractical. Country communications must provide appropriate local-language and, where lawful, anonymous or protected reporting options.

Appeals must be reviewed by a decision-maker who was not responsible for the original decision and has no material conflict of interest.

- Appeals should be acknowledged within five business days.
- Appeals should normally be decided within 15 calendar days after complete information is received.
- Any delay must be explained with a revised target date.

Each formal case must have:

- a case owner;
- issue classification;
- acknowledgment date;
- target resolution date;
- written outcome;
- remedy owner;
- completion confirmation;
- retaliation follow-up where relevant;
- systemic-review trigger.

Possible remedies include:

- rescinding unsupported attendance rules;
- correcting performance or promotion records;
- restoring development opportunities;
- adjusting workload or schedules;
- compensating improperly recorded work where required;
- retraining managers;
- auditing similarly situated employees;
- corrective or disciplinary action.

Confidentiality will be maintained to the greatest extent practicable and lawful. Information may be shared on a need-to-know basis to investigate, protect safety, comply with law, administer an adjustment, consult representatives, or provide due process.

- required consultation.

Managers may not receive identifiable sensitive-characteristic data unless strictly necessary.

Flexibility requests, approved patterns, appeals, and disconnect complaints must not be used to infer ambition, loyalty, reliability, or promotion readiness.

A material disparity, recurring overtime pattern, or repeated manager breach must have a named owner, remediation plan, target completion date, and follow-up measurement.

### Decision rights

- Legal controls questions of statutory compliance.
- Privacy controls personal-data processing.
- Security controls security-risk acceptance.
- Employee representatives retain applicable consultation or approval rights.
- Global People controls consistency with this policy.
- Business leaders control operational design within these requirements.

No business or executive decision may override an applicable legal, representative, privacy, or security requirement.

### Phase 1: Validate

- establish the global steering group;
- confirm the twelve-country scope register;
- inventory legal and collective requirements;
- create role eligibility and restriction matrices;
- complete country addenda;

- establish the policy register;

## 20. Decision test for managers

Before approving, restricting, or denying flexibility, the decision-maker must identify:

1. the specific role or team requirement;
2. the evidence supporting it;
3. why the restriction is necessary and proportionate;
4. less restrictive alternatives considered;
5. how working time, rest, workload, accessibility, security, privacy, and coverage will be protected;
6. how employees using another approved arrangement will retain comparable access to opportunity;
7. the decision owner and review date;
8. applicable legal, contractual, collective, and representative requirements.

> **Cultural preference, physical visibility, perceived loyalty, managerial convenience, insufficient staffing, or assumptions about commitment alone are not sufficient grounds for restricting flexible work.**

### Governing principle

The company will be globally consistent on legality, dignity, rest, accessibility, career fairness, outcome-based management, transparency, privacy, and non-retaliation. It will remain locally adaptable on working hours, consultation, disconnect procedures, expenses, work locations, office rhythms, and client coverage where required by law, binding arrangements, security, or genuine operational necessity.
