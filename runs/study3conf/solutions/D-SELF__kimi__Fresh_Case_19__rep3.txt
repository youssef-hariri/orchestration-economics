**PRODUCT STRATEGY: SMALLHOLDER PRECISION SERVICES**
*Ecology-Up Market Entry Blueprint for India, Kenya, and Vietnam*

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

We are not exporting “precision agriculture” to smallholders. We are building a **decision-service utility** designed for the ecology of fragmented, tenanted, intercropped farms managed by cash-constrained households where women perform the majority of field labor. The product is not an asset; it is a **channel-delivered advisory subscription** paid for when farmers have money, not when they are buying seed.

**Strategic North Star:** A sub-\$120 sensor node (at ≥100,000 annual volume, with localized assembly) that a trained village agent can install and calibrate in four minutes, that delivers three years of tropical service life, and that turns biophysical and social data into a timely, actionable message—SMS, voice, or app—telling the farmer exactly what to do this week, conditional on whether they have water, capital, and control over the decision.

**The Shift:** We move from an asset-sale, dashboard-centric model to a **shared-infrastructure, gender-intentional, harvest-aligned service**. The smallholder does not buy a sensor; they buy **reduced risk and lower input cost**, delivered through networks they already trust.

**Financial Snapshot:** At scale, the service targets a blended revenue of \$38–\$48 per farmer per year across a multi-sided model (farmer SaaS, aggregator B2B, climate MRV, channel efficiency), against a fully loaded hardware and service cost of \$28–\$33 per farmer per year. Margins are thin and require flawless execution; the model is therefore gated by rigorous pilot stage-gates and explicit kill criteria.

**Board Ask:** Authorize Phase 1 anchor pilots in India, Kenya, and Vietnam, with capital releases contingent on pre-commitment milestones: (1) EMS BOM validation at ≤\$90; (2) signed Letters of Intent from at least one input manufacturer per market; and (3) executed pilot partnership agreements with verified cooperatives or inclusion-conditioned aggregators.

## 2. STRATEGIC DESIGN PRINCIPLES

| Principle | Implication |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Ecology-Up** | Start with tenancy, intercropping, gendered labor, and seasonal liquidity; hardware and analytics follow. |
| **Portable & Tenant-Safe** | The node is farmer property, not a land fixture; it moves with the cultivator. |
| **Textural Realism** | Agronomic thresholds are calibrated to soil class and water-control capability, never universal averages. |
| **Agency Preservation** | Advisories are recommendations with bounded liability; prescriptions are reserved for contract aggregators and explicitly labeled as such. |
| **Harvest-Aligned Economics** | Revenue collection mirrors smallholder cash flow. Hardware is never sold outright; it is subscribed, financed, or subsidized. |
| **Gender-Intentional** | The service targets the household member who executes fieldwork, using female agent networks, dual-phone registration, and gender-appropriate UX. |
| **Financially Closed Loop** | Unit economics must demonstrate a path to positive contribution margin after accounting for tropical hardware losses, reverse logistics, and channel margin. |

## 3. PILLAR 1: HARDWARE — FRAGMENTED-FARM PHYSICS

### 3.1 Design Philosophy
*“Portable, Textural, and Tropical-Honest.”*

Smallholder holdings are rarely single polygons. A 0.8 ha “farm” often comprises three to five non-contiguous parcels, intercropped and held under seasonal tenancy. The device must be **relocatable**, **legally portable**, and **agronomically valid** across heterogeneous soil textures.

### 3.2 Sensing Architecture

**Multi-Plot, Multi-Depth Reality**
A single shallow moisture point cannot proxy for scattered parcels or deep-rooted crops (sugarcane, cotton, coffee). The revised node uses a **dual-depth printed capacitive probe** (15 cm + 30 cm) integrated into a single PCB stem, adding <\$4 to the bill of materials.

- **Shallow layer:** Used for paddy, vegetables, and surface-sensitive crops.
- **Deep layer:** Used for root-zone stress in cane, cotton, maize, and coffee. If deep moisture is unavailable or unreliable, the system defaults to a localized evapotranspiration model rather than emitting false confidence.
- **Soil texture calibration:** At installation, the agent performs a 2-minute jar-test protocol to classify soil as clay, loam, or sand. This sets plot-specific approximations of field capacity and wilting point. “Dry / Okay / Wet” thresholds are texture-class dependent, not universal.

**Intercropping & Tenancy**
- The node is registered to the **farmer’s mobile account**, not the land parcel. QR-tied ownership allows seasonal relocation.
- A single subscription covers up to four discrete plots >0.2 ha each. Smaller or distant plots rely on cooperative weather and model interpolation.
- Tenancy disputes are avoided: the device is the farmer’s personal property, retrieved at season-end if land access expires.

### 3.3 Power, Durability, and Environmental Specification

**Battery: Tropical-Honest Lifecycle**
The fantasy of a five-year primary battery in tropical field conditions is abandoned. We target a **three-year guaranteed service life** under tropical derating (ambient >70°C, humidity impedance, and periodic 2G fallback at high TX power).
- **Chemistry:** Industrial-grade lithium thionyl chloride (LTTC), sized for 3.5 years in temperate zones, with a **scheduled replacement covenant at 30 months** in hot climates.
- **No solar:** Solar surfaces attract theft, dust fouling, and cable loss. The annual service fee includes the 30-month battery swap executed by the channel agent.

**Structural & Environmental Hardshell**
- **Mechanical load:** Crush-rated to 800 kg static load, achieved through a low-profile stamped-steel ground collar that deflects power-tiller blades and livestock hooves. No visible reflective bands that attract tampering.
- **Moisture and flood:** The device is rated for temporary immersion (rain splash, flooded furrows), not prolonged submersion. In Vietnam’s Mekong Delta, nodes are mounted on **1.2 m elevation stakes** above the monsoon flood layer, or attached to cooperative building pillars. Hermetic sealing with sediment-purge ports protects against humidity and salt air.
- **Corrosion:** Vietnam coastal deployments use polymer-coated galvanized steel spikes, avoiding marine-aluminum galvanic failure in acidic paddy soils. India uses white UV-stable polycarbonate upper housing with Gore-tex venting; operating spec validated to +85°C ambient.

### 3.4 Lifecycle Economics and Reverse Logistics

For an \$85 BOM device, rural reverse logistics can exceed unit replacement cost. We abandon the “return-to-depot for repair” model in favor of a **salvage-biased float model**:

- Each dealer maintains a **15% spare-node float** for instant swap.
- A failed node is swapped in the field; dead units accumulate in a lockbox at the dealer.
- **Quarterly consolidation caravans**—piggybacking on existing input-distributor routes—move failed units to a regional depot.
- At the depot, units are triaged: battery and modem are harvested for refurbishment; welded housing and probe are locally recycled. The unit economics assume a **22% annual field-loss rate in Year 1**, actively mitigated toward **12% by Year 3** via anti-theft firmware locks (bricked if stolen), low-profile concealment, and SHG social enforcement. This loss rate is absorbed into the subscription margin.

**BOM Trajectory and Localization**
- **Target BOM:** \$85 at ≥100,000 units/year.
- **Pilot BOM:** ~\$110 at 5,000–10,000 units.
- **India:** PCB assembly and housing molding are localized through an Indian EMS partner, avoiding 25–40% import duties (BCD + GST) and preserving the sub-\$120 channel price point.

### 3.5 Connectivity by Terrain

**Primary Modem:** Quad-band 2G fallback + LTE-M/NB-IoT. No WiFi, no Bluetooth.
**Geolocation:** Cell-tower triangulation and commune-level geofencing logged by the dealer at installation. On-device GPS is eliminated to comply with Vietnam’s Decree 85/2021/ND-CP and to reduce cost.

**Kenya Highlands / Remote Areas**
- LoRaWAN cooperative hubs are **roof-mounted externally** with grid power and battery backup, never placed indoors.
- Where terrain blocks sub-GHz line-of-sight, the system falls back to LTE-M direct.
- In deep shadow valleys, nodes store 14 days of data. If the backlog exceeds 14 days, the system defaults to cooperative weather interpolation and disables plot-specific advisories until connectivity resumes. **There is no “data mule” expectation placed on farmers.**

### 3.6 Country-Specific Hardware Nuances

| Market | Critical Adaptation | Rationale |
|--------|---------------------|-----------|
| **India** | White reflective housing; Gore-tex vent; +85°C spec; NB-IoT with mandatory 2G fallback; localized EMS assembly | Heat, dust, monsoon, and import-duty mitigation |
| **Kenya** | Low-profile steel collar; anti-theft firmware lock; strictly LTTC battery (no solar); external LoRaWAN hubs where needed | Livestock, theft, and highland terrain |
| **Vietnam** | 1.2 m elevation stake or building mount for Mekong floods; polymer-coated galvanized spike; marine-grade sealing; cell-tower geolocation only | Monsoon submersion, saline-acidic soils, geospatial data regulations |

## 4. PILLAR 2: PARTNERSHIPS & TRUST ARCHITECTURE

### 4.1 Go-to-Market Archetypes

| Archetype | Role | Constraint |
|-----------|------|------------|
| **Women’s Self-Help Groups (SHGs) / Women’s Cooperative Wings / Women’s Union** | Trust brokers, distribution nodes, training agents, and social enforcement. | Require structured training budgets and per-retention incentives, not just per-sale commissions. |
| **Cooperatives (Verified Check-Off Only)** | Collective buyer and payment rail. | Only viable where statutory deduction authority exists (KTDA tea, Brookside dairy, Vietnam state-linked HTX). |
| **Input Dealers / Agrovets** | Physical stock, installation, Tier-1 support. | Profit historically depends on input volume, creating conflict with advisory-driven reduction. |
| **Contract Aggregators / Exporters** | Subsidy provider for traceability; buyer of record. | Will cherry-pick larger farmers unless structurally prevented. |
| **Agri-Fintech / Development Finance** | Seasonal credit provider; bears default risk. | Required wherever post-harvest payment or input financing is used. |
| **Climate-Finance Intermediaries** | Opportunistic third-party payer for MRV data. | Methodologies are nascent; treated as upside, not base-case revenue. |

### 4.2 Restructured Partnership Models

#### A. Gender-Intentional Delivery: The Field Agent Network
To correct endemic gender erasure in agri-tech distribution, we establish a dedicated **Field Agent** cadre recruited through SHGs (India), women’s cooperative wings (Kenya), and the Vietnam Women’s Union.
- **Recruitment:** Female agents are selected for local credibility, not technical degrees.
- **Capability building:** Each agent receives a **3-day certification** covering jar-test calibration, node installation, relocation, and basic troubleshooting. A mobile app prompts photo-audit of every installation and texture test; cooperative agronomists spot-check 10% monthly.
- **Intra-household targeting:** Onboarding explicitly captures **two phone numbers**: the *decision phone* (typically the person managing day-to-day labor, often female) and the *payment phone* (often male). Advisories default to the decision phone.
- **Compensation:** Agents earn a fixed fee per installation plus a **retention bonus** at 6 and 12 months, aligning their incentive with service quality, not mere activation.
- **Social enforcement:** In India, SHGs act as co-guarantors for device float and conduct group listening sessions for weekly voice advisories.

#### B. Cooperatives: “Verified Check-Off or Seasonal Credit”
- **Where check-off is real** (Kenya dairy/coffee/tea, Vietnam state-linked HTX with procurement authority): The cooperative buys nodes and deducts \$2–3/month from member proceeds.
- **Where check-off is weak** (India FPOs, Kenya grain/vegetable cooperatives): Hardware is bundled into **seasonal input-financing packages**. A financing partner (agri-NBFC, exporter, or DFI) provides seed/credit + sensor; the farmer repays in a lump sum post-harvest. The company is paid by the financing entity, not the individual farmer.
- **Dashboard access:** Cooperative managers receive a block-level web dashboard; farmers receive SMS/voice only.

#### C. Input Dealers: “Outcome-Aligned Program Bundles”
The dealer conflict-of-interest—profiting from input volume while sensors optimize reduction—is resolved through structural incentive redesign:
- **Program bundling:** The dealer sells a **“Crop Protection Program”** (sensor + approved actives + timing protocol). The dealer margin is a **fixed per-hectare program fee**, identical whether the protocol calls for two sprays or four.
- **Implementation reality:** This transition requires **anchor demand pull** and co-investment from an input manufacturer seeking differentiation. The pilot phase tests this with one anchor manufacturer per country.
- **Consignment terms:** First 30 days are company risk. Thereafter, the dealer bears shrinkage/theft risk up to a capped annual exposure, backed by a modest float deposit. This prevents careless storage while acknowledging working-capital constraints.

#### D. Aggregators: “Inclusion-Conditioned Subsidy”
To prevent adverse selection:
- Company co-invests in hardware subsidies only if the aggregator contract requires that **≥60% of covered farmers manage <2 ha** and **≥30% of plots are female-managed**.
- **Agency preservation:** Advisories to farmers are labeled “Recommended Practice.” Contractual procurement standards are set by the aggregator; decision-support is provided by the company. A liability firewall separates the two.
- **Revenue:** Aggregator pays \$40–\$60/farmer/year for compliance traceability, yield forecasting, and ESG audit trails.

#### E. Climate-Finance: Opportunistic MRV Layer
Carbon and ESG monitoring are introduced **only at the cooperative or aggregator level**, never as a farmer-facing feature.
- **Revenue model:** Donors and carbon buyers may subsidize node deployment in exchange for standardized MRV data (soil organic carbon, rice methane, agroforestry). This is treated as **third-party upside revenue (target 15% of total)**, not as a base-case subsidy required to close unit economics.

### 4.3 Country-Specific Delivery Execution

**India**
- **Physical channel:** Agri e-commerce platforms (DeHaat, Gramophone) are used strictly for **lead generation and demand aggregation**. The last mile is executed by **SHG agents or local dealer staff** with stake-driver kits.
- **Payment:** UPI auto-debit for post-harvest settlements; SHG group guarantees for credit. Cash remains an option at the dealer counter.
- **Regulatory:** Localized PCB assembly and molding avoid crushing import duties. Pilots align with the government Soil Health Card scheme to build institutional trust.

**Kenya**
- **Check-off realism:** Strictly limited to dairy, coffee, and tea cooperatives with processor-linked ledgers. For maize and horticulture, the default is **M-PESA “pay-after-harvest”** or exporter-led input credit.
- **Agrovet density:** A master-distributor model with county-level depots. Consignment stock includes the 15% swap float.
- **Data protection:** Explicit farmer opt-in for every data-sharing event with aggregators, compliant with the Data Protection Act 2019.

**Vietnam**
- **Government extension:** Engagement with Provincial Sub-Departments of Crop Production proceeds via **12–18 month MoU cycles**. The sensor is framed as a decision-support tool *for* extension officers, feeding their existing VietGAP demonstration KPIs rather than displacing them.
- **Geospatial compliance:** No on-device GPS logging. Cell-tower triangulation and commune-level geofencing are used. All map data is stored onshore with a licensed local partner.
- **Exporter channel:** Lead with dragon fruit and coffee exporters, but enforce inclusion quotas for smallholders and female-managed plots.

## 5. PILLAR 3: ANALYTICS — CAPABILITY-AWARE & SCALABLE

### 5.1 Framework: Conditional, Ground-Truthed, and Decaying Human-in-the-Loop

Smallholders do not need dashboards; they need **recommendations that respect their control over water, capital, and labor.** Every feature is filtered through three questions:
1. Does the farmer have the infrastructure to act on this?
2. Is the data ground-truthed enough to be trustworthy?
3. Is the message delivered to the person who will execute the action?

**Automation Roadmap:** Years 1–2 rely on human-in-the-loop calibration (jar-tests, trap counts, farmer rain reports). Years 3+ transition to model-driven confidence, reducing manual ground-truthing frequency as local training datasets mature.

### 5.2 Feature Portfolio: Justified Choices

| Data Output | Decision | Smallholder Value Proposition & Rationale |
|-------------|----------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Soil Moisture (Dual-Depth)** | **Prioritize** | 15 cm + 30 cm, calibrated to soil texture class. Suppresses irrigation advisories if farmer has not confirmed pump/canal access. Avoids false “Dry” alerts in clay vertisols via texture-specific thresholds. |
| **Irrigation Scheduling** | **Conditional** | Offered only after farmer confirms irrigation access. In canal-dependent areas, replaces “irrigate now” with “canal likely dry in 3 days—fill storage if possible.” In rain-fed Kenya maize, feature is hidden. |
| **Weather & Spray Timing** | **Prioritize** | Ensemble model: cooperative station + radar + **farmer ground-truthing** (USSD “Reply 1 if it rained” for airtime credit). Advisories include conservative 3-hour buffers. Farmer input closes the hyperlocal gap left by 5 km station spacing. |
| **Pest/Disease Risk** | **Prioritize with Ground-Truthing** | Rule-based model (weather + crop stage + regional pressure) is **validated by a network of low-cost pheromone traps** counted weekly by cooperative scouts and farmer SMS reports (“Worms seen? Reply YES”). **No trap network = no advisory.** Scout labor cost is modeled into the cooperative SaaS fee. |
| **Fertilizer Recommendation** | **Modify** | Single, conservative flat-rate recommendation per growth stage based on soil texture + crop model. Explicit liability disclaimer; never prescribes beyond soil-test correlation. |
| **Satellite Vegetation Index** | **Drop per-farmer; Limit block-level** | 10 m resolution cannot resolve sub-hectare plots. **Eliminated from all farmer-facing output.** Cooperative agronomists may receive **100 m block-level anomaly maps** only to target manual scouting. No individual SMS derived from satellite. |
| **Market Prices** | **Conditional / Modify** | **India:** Local trader *gate-buy rates* for distressed/uncleaned produce, not APMC modal prices. **Kenya:** Cooperative-gate milk/grain prices. **Vietnam contract farmers:** Replaced with **quality/grade prediction** to maximize contract bonus. |
| **Pest AI Camera** | **Drop** | Hardware and connectivity cost prohibitive. Replaced by trap network + farmer IVR. |
| **Variable Rate Application Maps** | **Drop** | Irrelevant where equipment and plot size do not support VRA. |
| **Carbon / ESG MRV** | **Reintroduce at Cooperative Level** | No farmer-facing feature. Aggregator/cooperative dashboard for donor-funded credit verification. Creates opportunistic third-party revenue. |
| **Raw Data / API** | **Drop for farmers; Retain for B2B** | Farmers receive sentences; cooperatives and aggregators receive structured exports. |

### 5.3 Gender-Intentional & Literacy-Inclusive Delivery

| Channel | Content | Target User |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| **SMS (160 char, local language)** | Binary/ternary action: “Spray fungicide before Thu.” | Literate decision-maker |
| **IVR Voice (female voice default)** | 60-second advisory in local language; replayable. | Low-literacy farmers; women who prefer audio |
| **WhatsApp / Zalo** | Simple image of pest symptom + text. | Younger farmers, women managing household budgets |
| **USSD** | On-demand status check (*144*NodeID#). | Feature-phone users |

**Intercropping Logic:** Where plots are intercropped (maize+beans, coconut+pepper), the farmer selects a **dominant crop** during onboarding. Moisture advisories default to the shared root zone; pest and fertilizer advisories prioritize the crop with the highest susceptibility during the current growth stage.

### 5.4 Country Analytics Customization

**India**
- **Highest value:** (1) Canal/power-timing advisories where irrigation exists; (2) trap-validated pest alerts for cotton bollworm, rice blast, pink bollworm; (3) gate-level trader prices.
- **Integration:** Recommendations framed as digital extensions of the Soil Health Card scheme.
- **Validation:** Hardware and analytics must be validated during **kharif (Jun–Sep)** monsoon conditions before national scaling; rabi-only pilots are insufficient.

**Kenya**
- **Highest value:** (1) Planting-window alerts tied to verified cooperative rain gauges; (2) fall armyworm / locust risk with trap validation; (3) dairy fodder moisture for applicable cooperatives.
- **Delivery:** Voice-first in Kiswahili, Kikuyu, Kalenjin. Cooperative agronomist receives risk map; farmer receives “Plant now” or “Spray now” voice message.

**Vietnam**
- **Highest value:** (1) Spray-timing to meet export residue limits; (2) salinity/flood pivot alerts in the Mekong; (3) export compliance (VietGAP/GlobalGAP spray record logging via automated advisory trail).
- **Delivery:** Zalo Business for younger farmers; IVR voice for older growers. Contract farmers receive **grade-prediction advisories** to maximize bonus payments.

## 6. PILLAR 4: COMMERCIAL ENGINE & UNIT ECONOMICS

The prior product-down approach failed because it assumed hardware losses and subscription pricing could meet without a closed financial loop. This blueprint presents a **reconciled model** that treats the 22% field-loss rate as a Year 1 operational reality to be mitigated, not ignored.

### 6.1 Unit Economics Framework

| Cost/Revenue Driver | Base Case Assumption |
|---------------------|----------------------|
| **BOM** | \$85 at scale (≥100k units/year); \$110 at pilot |
| **Localized assembly (India)** | Avoids 35% all-in import duty, preserving margin |
| **Annual field loss / write-off** | 22% Year 1 → mitigated to 12% Year 3 via firmware lock, SHG enforcement, low-profile design |
| **Battery swap cycle** | 30 months (absorbed into annual service fee) |
| **Fully loaded hardware cost per farmer/year** | ~\$28–\$33 over 3-year life (includes loss, swap logistics, channel float) |
| **Blended revenue per farmer/year** | ~\$38–\$48 (multi-sided mix) |

### 6.2 Multi-Sided Revenue Model

To close the gap between hardware cost and smallholder ability to pay, revenue is deliberately stacked:

| Revenue Stream | Share of Total | Source |
|----------------|----------------|--------|
| **Farmer SaaS / Subscription** | 40% | Post-harvest or seasonal payments; input-financing bundles |
| **Aggregator / Exporter B2B** | 35% | Compliance, traceability, yield forecasting (\$40–\$60/farmer/year) |
| **Climate / Impact MRV** | 15% | Opportunistic; donor-funded carbon/soil credit verification |
| **Dealer / Co-op Service Margin** | 10% | Equivalent value capture from channel efficiency and bulk input optimization |

### 6.3 Pricing to Farmer (End-User)

| Market | Farmer Price Point | Payment Mechanism |
|--------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **India** | ₹1,000–1,500/season (\$12–\$18) | Post-harvest UPI; input-financing bundle; SHG group guarantee |
| **Kenya** | KES 250–400/month (\$2–\$3) | Check-off (dairy/coffee only); exporter credit; M-PESA deferred |
| **Vietnam** | VND 200K–300K/season (\$8–\$12) | Exporter subsidy; seasonal dealer bundle |

### 6.4 Financing & Risk Architecture

Because smallholders are cash-constrained at planting and liquid at harvest, the company does not extend credit directly. Instead:
- **India:** Partner with agri-NBFCs or FPOs to embed the sensor fee into seasonal input loans.
- **Kenya:** Use aggregator pre-finance or M-PESA deferred-payment plans for post-harvest settlement.
- **Vietnam:** Exporters pre-pay for nodes as part of contract-farming overhead.

**Default risk is borne by the financing partner**, not the company. The company is paid by the aggregator, cooperative, or funder upon deployment.

### 6.5 Currency & Macro Hedging
- Subscriptions to farmers are priced in local currency.
- B2B contracts with aggregators and climate financiers are denominated in USD or indexed to local-currency floors with annual escalation clauses.
- India manufacturing and Vietnam onshore data hosting provide natural hedges against import-duty volatility and geospatial regulatory risk.

## 7. PILLAR 5: LIABILITY, COMPLIANCE & ACCOUNTABILITY

The zero-touch, channel-led model diffuses accountability; this framework reconsolidates it.

### 7.1 Agronomic Liability
- All farmer-facing advisories carry an explicit **“Recommendation, Not Prescription”** label.
- The company provides decision support; the farmer, cooperative, or aggregator retains execution choice.
- **Crop insurance linkage:** In India and Kenya, sensor adoption is recognized as a “good agricultural practice” for premium discounting, but the sensor provider does not underwrite weather or pest loss.

### 7.2 Data Governance

| Market | Requirement | Operational Response |
|--------|-------------|----------------------|
| **India** | Data residency; IT Act fiduciary obligations | Farmer data stored in-country; company acts as data fiduciary |
| **Kenya** | Data Protection Act 2019; purpose limitation | Granular opt-in for every data-sharing event with aggregators |
| **Vietnam** | Decree 85/2021/ND-CP; cybersecurity law | No on-device GPS; cell-tower triangulation only; map data onshore with licensed local partner |

### 7.3 Warranty & Dispute Resolution
- **Hardware:** 30-month replacement guarantee against tropical field failure.
- **Advice:** Disputes are escalated to the local cooperative or SHG mediator before legal channels. The company maintains a **rural call center** in each country staffed in local languages, with female agents.

## 8. IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP & STAGE-GATES

### Phase 1: Anchor Pilots (Months 0–12)
**Objective:** Validate hardware under tropical field conditions and prove channel economics in controlled geographies.

| Country | Pilot Anchor | Crop / Segment | Seasonal Alignment | Key Validation |
|---------|--------------|----------------|--------------------|----------------|
| **India** | 3 FPOs + 1 input manufacturer | Sugarcane, cotton, grapes (Maharashtra/Gujarat) | **Kharif monsoon** (Jun–Sep) essential | Jar-test calibration accuracy, dealer consignment uptake, post-harvest UPI collection |
| **Kenya** | 2 dairy/coffee cooperatives with check-off | Dairy (Brookside-linked), coffee (KTDA) | Short rains / early long rains | Voice-first retention, M-PESA integration, hub connectivity |
| **Vietnam** | 1 dragon fruit exporter + 1 rice miller | Mekong Delta rice, Central Highlands coffee | Wet-season validation | Elevation-stake flood survival, Provincial MoU navigation, Zalo delivery engagement |

**Phase 1 Capital Release Gates (All must be green):**
1. EMS teardown validates pilot BOM ≤\$110 and scale BOM ≤\$90.
2. Signed LOI from ≥1 input manufacturer per country for Crop Protection Program bundles.
3. Executed partnership agreement with anchor cooperative or inclusion-conditioned aggregator.
4. 30-day farmer retention ≥60% and agent calibration audit pass rate ≥85%.

### Phase 2: Channel Productization (Months 12–24)
**Objective:** Transition from company-led installation to 100% channel-led delivery.

- Finalize **“Agent-in-a-Box”** kit: pre-configured nodes, calibrated probe testers, stake driver, photo-audit app, training videos.
- Launch **aggregator dashboard** for export compliance traceability and ESG MRV.
- Standardize **seasonal input-financing templates** with agri-fintech partners.
- Expand from 3 pilot anchors to 15 channel partners per country.

**Phase 2 Kill Criteria:**
- If 90-day retention in dealer-consignment channel falls below 40%, abandon dealer consignment and pivot to FPO bulk ownership or aggregator subsidy models in that district.
- If annual field-loss rate remains >18% after anti-theft firmware and SHG enforcement, pause scaling and redesign housing/mechanical security.

### Phase 3: Platform Scale (Months 24–36)
**Objective:** Reach operating leverage and reduce human-in-the-loop dependencies.

- Transition pest and weather models from majority manual ground-tr
