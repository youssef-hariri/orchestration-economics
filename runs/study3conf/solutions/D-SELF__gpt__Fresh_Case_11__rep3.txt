# 2026–2029 Cross-Border Financial-Operations Strategy

## Executive summary

The firm should pursue **selective expansion of specific products, not broad regional expansion**, using a federated operating model.

The correct decision unit is:

> **Legal entity → country pair → product → funding method → payout method → settlement currency → partner route → customer segment**

This is necessary because licensing, safeguarding, KYC, liquidity, settlement, fraud, and economics can differ materially within the same country pair.

### Recommended decisions

1. **Expand** products with clear legal permission, protected customer funds, lawful settlement, sufficient liquidity, resilient partners, scalable controls, and positive risk-adjusted economics.
2. **Maintain** profitable products where growth is constrained by partner capacity, licensing cost, technical maturity, or market size.
3. **Redesign** products with strong demand or gross margin but excessive FX, compliance, manual-processing, prefunding, or settlement costs.
4. **Restrict or pause new exposure** where legal, safeguarding, settlement, capital-control, liquidity, or compliance-capacity conditions are uncertain.
5. **Exit** products that remain uneconomic or unsafe after a defined remediation period.

Regional recommendations are **initial portfolio hypotheses**. Final capital allocation requires product-level evidence, local legal validation, and approved risk limits.

The governing rule is:

> No volume target overrides licensing compliance, sanctions controls, customer-fund protection, lawful settlement, adequate liquidity, reliable reconciliation, scalable controls, or positive risk-adjusted economics.

# 1. Strategic operating model

## Centralize

Central functions should own:

- Group risk appetite and minimum controls
- KYC, AML, sanctions, fraud, and case-management technology
- Treasury policy and exposure methodology
- Common transaction identifiers and data standards
- Reconciliation standards
- Partner due-diligence methodology
- Pricing and profitability analytics
- Capital allocation
- Regulatory-change governance
- Enterprise incident management
- Business-continuity standards
- Internal-audit methodology

Centralization applies only where funds, data, decision rights, and outsourcing arrangements can legally and operationally be centralized.

## Localize

Local or regional teams should own:

- Licensing interpretation and regulator engagement
- Local regulatory reporting
- Safeguarding mechanics
- Bank, wallet, cash, and payout relationships
- Permitted settlement currencies and accounts
- Capital-control and repatriation procedures
- Local tax and accounting
- Country-specific KYC documentation
- Consumer disclosures and complaints
- Local-language support
- Partner and subcontractor oversight
- Operational exception handling

## Control hierarchy

When objectives or resources conflict, priorities are:

1. Sanctions, licensing, and legal compliance
2. Customer-fund protection
3. Settlement and ledger integrity
4. Fraud and AML effectiveness
5. Customer communication and service recovery
6. Growth and marketing

Commercial teams cannot override a Legal or Compliance stop arising from sanctions, licensing, safeguarding, or unlawful-settlement concerns.

# 2. Product approval and portfolio segmentation

## 2.1 Hard gates

No product may launch or materially expand until the following are documented and approved:

- Correct legal entity and licensing basis
- End-to-end funds-flow and title-flow diagram
- Role of each bank, agent, sponsor, wallet, aggregator, correspondent, and subcontractor
- Customer-fund ownership and safeguarding treatment
- Permitted settlement currencies, accounts, and conversion routes
- Capital-control, purpose-code, quota, and repatriation treatment
- KYC, beneficiary, AML, sanctions, fraud, and reporting rules
- Consumer disclosures, cancellation, refund, and complaint procedures
- Data-transfer, localization, and outsourcing analysis
- Partner due diligence and enforceable contract protections
- Liquidity, FX, prefunding, and counterparty limits
- Reconciliation design and test evidence
- Contingency, refund, and wind-down plans
- Economics appropriate to the approval stage

Unresolved licensing, sanctions, safeguarding, customer-fund access, or settlement-legality issues are automatic stop conditions.

## 2.2 Portfolio statuses

| Status | Criteria | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| **Expand** | Hard gates cleared; economics, controls, liquidity, capacity, and resilience demonstrated | Increase volume, limits, marketing, or partner investment |
| **Maintain-normal** | Profitable and compliant but no immediate scale case | Optimize pricing, service, and partner economics |
| **Maintain-with-limits** | Sound model but temporary constraint or concentration | Cap exposure and complete remediation |
| **Redesign** | Strong demand or gross margin but inefficient or exposed | Change pricing, payout, currency, partner, or process |
| **Pause-new-originations** | New exposure is unsafe or uncertain; existing servicing remains lawful | Stop new funding while resolving the issue |
| **Service-existing-only** | Existing transactions require controlled completion or refunds | No new customer originations |
| **Exit** | Persistent negative economics or unacceptable risk | Orderly wind-down |

Management should monitor status monthly, formally reclassify quarterly, and act immediately on hard-stop events.

## 2.3 Decision hierarchy

1. Assess hard-stop conditions.
2. Test liquidity, counterparty, concentration, reconciliation, and compliance-capacity limits.
3. Assess risk-adjusted economics.
4. Assign portfolio status.
5. Approve an owner, limit, remediation plan, deadline, and expiry.

A commercial score cannot override a hard gate or breached risk limit.

# 3. Regional corridor recommendations

These are **initial hypotheses**, not conclusions that a region is uniformly attractive.

## 3.1 European Union

### Recommendation: Initial scale candidate, product by product

Prioritize EU-linked products only where entity authorization, receiving-side legality, realized economics, liquidity, and operational resilience are evidenced.

### Prioritize

- High-volume euro-funded bank-transfer routes
- Automated bank or payment-institution rails
- Products with high straight-through processing
- Routes with low fraud, return, and manual-review rates
- Products with sufficient volume to absorb fixed compliance costs

### Operating approach

- Centralize euro liquidity only where legally transferable and operationally accessible.
- Automate screening, monitoring, ledgering, and reconciliation.
- Retain entity-specific safeguarding, reporting, consumer, outsourcing, and regulator-access controls.
- Maintain API and secure-file fallbacks.
- Monitor fraud, account takeover, and correspondent-bank dependence.

### Trade-off

The principal commercial risk is likely to be price compression. Higher volume does not compensate for poor receiving-side economics. Cost separately for funding, FX, payout, local liquidity, capital controls, repatriation, compliance, and customer support.

### Decisions

- **Expand:** liquid, automated, high-volume products.
- **Maintain:** profitable but price-compressed products.
- **Redesign:** single-bank, high-fraud, or manual-review-heavy products.
- **Pause:** products with unresolved receiving-side authorization or customer-fund treatment.

---

## 3.2 Gulf

### Recommendation: Maintain core routes; expand selectively

Each Gulf country requires separate validation of licensing, sponsorship or agency arrangements, settlement currencies, bank access, source-of-funds requirements, repatriation, and safeguarding.

### Prioritize

- High-repeat remittance segments
- Bank or wallet payout with reliable status reporting
- Routes with liquid settlement currencies
- Premium services where customer willingness to pay is demonstrated

### Operating approach

- Maintain separate views of local-currency balances, hard-currency settlement, prefunding, collateral, restricted cash, and repatriation queues.
- Set maximum balances and holding periods.
- Use staged prefunding.
- Require enforceable settlement, reconciliation, audit, incident, and transition provisions.
- Use lower limits and more frequent reconciliation where no second partner is available.

### Trade-off

Customer fees and FX spreads may be attractive, but net margin can be consumed by sponsor costs, cash commissions, local liquidity, collateral, enhanced due diligence, conversion delays, repatriation delays, and partner concentration.

### Decisions

- **Expand:** products with lawful conversion, reliable payout, adequate liquidity, and tested contingency.
- **Maintain:** profitable cash or bank products with limited technical maturity.
- **Redesign:** products where manual work or source-of-funds review consumes margin.
- **Pause:** products with uncertain repatriation, safeguarding, or unprotected single-partner exposure.

---

## 3.3 Southeast Asia

### Recommendation: Country-level pilots, then scale digital winners

The region should not be treated as one licensing, currency, or technical market.

### Prioritize

- Licensed bank, wallet, or instant-payment routes
- High-repeat customer segments
- API-enabled payout with transaction-level confirmation
- Products where digital delivery lowers total cost without increasing safeguarding or partner risk

Wallet payout is not automatically superior. Bank, wallet, and cash options should be compared using total cost, adoption, settlement, safeguarding, liquidity, fraud, and recovery risk.

### Operating approach

- Use country-specific KYC, beneficiary, limits, and reporting rules.
- Support APIs, secure files, and controlled manual fallback.
- Automate duplicate prevention, status confirmation, beneficiary correction, and returns.
- Use country- and payout-specific delivery promises.
- Monitor wallet partner liquidity, safeguarding, settlement aging, and financial condition.

### Trade-off

Small average tickets make fixed costs decisive. A corridor should not scale if compliance, integration, payout, exception, refund, and customer-service costs consume the margin.

### Decisions

- **Expand:** legally clear, API-enabled bank or wallet products.
- **Maintain:** profitable bank-transfer products with limited growth.
- **Redesign:** low-ticket, manual, high-contact products.
- **Pause:** products with unclear wallet safeguarding, payout legality, or capital-control treatment.

---

## 3.4 Latin America

### Recommendation: Selective expansion with strict FX and liquidity limits

Each country requires separate analysis of licensing, tax, currency convertibility, inflation, devaluation, capital controls, repatriation, payout reliability, and customer-fund protection.

### Prioritize

- High-volume routes with transparent local settlement
- Bank or wallet payout with reliable reconciliation
- Same-day or short-duration conversion
- Repeat customers willing to pay for reliable delivery
- Partners with demonstrable liquidity

### Operating approach

- Minimize overnight local-currency holdings.
- Use staged prefunding and short quote-validity periods where permitted.
- Separate safeguarded customer funds from operating cash.
- Use only lawful conversion, netting, and repatriation structures.
- Set country, currency, partner, and settlement-age limits.
- Reprice or restrict when realized FX losses exceed tolerance.

### Trade-off

Wide quoted FX spreads are not durable margin unless execution cost, slippage, hedge cost, devaluation, funding, tax, and trapped cash are deducted.

\[
RealizedFXMargin =
QuotedFXRevenue
- ExecutionCost
- HedgeCost
- Slippage
- DevaluationLoss
- FundingCost
- TrappedCashCost
\]

### Decisions

- **Expand:** reliable digital products with transparent conversion and short exposure.
- **Maintain:** profitable products with manageable FX and payout risk.
- **Redesign:** cash-heavy or long-duration FX products.
- **Pause or exit:** products with unpredictable conversion, trapped funds, repeated outages, or realized losses that eliminate margin.

# 4. Economics and pricing actions

## 4.1 Risk-adjusted contribution margin

For product \(p\):

\[
RACM_p =
Revenue_p
- FXCost_p
- BankFees_p
- PayoutFees_p
- ComplianceCost_p
- FraudLoss_p
- CustomerServiceCost_p
- LiquidityCost_p
- PartnerIncentives_p
- ExpectedSettlementLoss_p
- VariableTechnologyCost_p
\]

Revenue must use realized FX revenue, not merely the quoted spread.

Report three views:

1. **Incremental contribution:** whether an additional transaction is profitable.
2. **Stand-alone economics:** whether the product covers directly attributable fixed costs.
3. **Fully allocated economics:** whether it covers shared licensing, technology, compliance, treasury, and regional costs.

## 4.2 Liquidity cost

\[
LiquidityCost =
AverageFunding \times TransferPrice
+ PeakLiquidityCapitalCharge
+ CollateralOpportunityCost
+ TrappedCashPremium
+ HedgeCarry
\]

Customer funds cannot be treated as operating liquidity unless legally available for that purpose.

## 4.3 Expected settlement loss

\[
ExpectedSettlementLoss =
\sum_i(PD_i \times LGD_i \times Exposure_i)
+ DelayCost_i
+ ReturnCost_i
+ OperationalErrorCost_i
\]

Exposure includes prefunding, funds in transit, unsettled customer liabilities, partner receivables, unrecoverable collateral, restricted balances, and common correspondent exposure.

Expected partner-credit, fraud, operational, return/refund, and delay losses should be modeled separately to avoid double counting. Stress or tail losses should be reported separately from expected loss.

## 4.4 Risk-adjusted return on operating capital

\[
RiskAdjustedROOC =
\frac{RACM - AllocatedFixedCost - ExpectedLossCapitalCharge}
{TimeWeightedOperatingCapital + IncrementalLiquidity + ComplianceInvestment}
\]

Capital should be measured using defined, non-overlapping components:

- Unrestricted operating cash
- Partner prefunding
- Collateral
- Restricted or trapped cash
- Regulatory capital or reserves
- Incremental compliance capacity
- Technology investment
- Contingency funding commitments

## 4.5 Hurdles and capital allocation

Do not impose a universal margin target. Annual hurdle bands should reflect:

- Weighted average cost of capital
- Liquidity and currency premium
- Country and partner risk
- Regulatory and safeguarding burden
- Volatility and tail risk
- Required investment
- Strategic value
- Availability of substitutes

Allocate growth capital in this order:

1. Mandatory licensing, safeguarding, and control remediation
2. Treasury, reconciliation, and settlement resilience
3. Compliance capacity and automation
4. Core products with proven risk-adjusted returns
5. Digital growth products with credible payback
6. Strategic redesigns
7. New high-complexity products

Regional percentages should not be fixed in advance. Capital allocation should use RiskAdjustedROOC, liquidity consumed, evidence confidence, strategic value, and remediation need.

## 4.6 Pricing actions

- Price bank, wallet, cash, and instant payout separately.
- Recover compliance, liquidity, manual, partner, settlement, and refund costs.
- Use dynamic FX pricing only within approved quote-lock and disclosure rules.
- Define who bears FX risk during compliance review and settlement delay.
- Reprice or reject transactions below the approved margin floor.
- Pass through material partner-cost changes where permitted.
- Apply higher pricing or limits to high-risk and manual-review-heavy segments.
- Review volatile products monthly and stable products quarterly.

Pricing changes require Finance and Product approval, Treasury review of FX and liquidity effects, and Legal/Compliance review where customer disclosures or regulated terms are affected.

# 5. Compliance and licensing approach

## 5.1 Product regulatory dossier

Each product must maintain a current dossier covering:

- Legal entity and regulated activity
- Funds-flow and title-flow
- Customer and beneficiary eligibility
- Funding, payout, and settlement route
- Partner and subcontractor roles
- KYC and beneficial-owner requirements
- Source-of-funds and enhanced-review triggers
- AML, sanctions, fraud, and transaction-monitoring rules
- Reporting obligations
- Safeguarding and customer-fund treatment
- Settlement currencies and account structures
- Capital controls, purpose codes, quotas, and repatriation
- Consumer disclosures, cancellation, refunds, and complaints
- Data localization and cross-border access
- Outsourcing and regulator-access requirements
- Tax and accounting
- Suspension and wind-down process
- Legal opinion date, assumptions, and renewal triggers

High-risk dossiers should be refreshed at least annually and after any material legal, product, partner, currency, or transaction-flow change.

## 5.2 KYC and beneficiary rulebook

Rules should be configurable by:

- Legal entity
- Country
- Customer and beneficiary type
- Product
- Funding method
- Payout method
- Transaction size and velocity
- Risk tier

Each rule must specify:

- Required data
- Verification method
- Timing
- Refresh trigger
- Escalation condition
- Retention period
- Responsible entity
- System control
- Evidence generated

Separate controls are required for:

- Third-party KYC reliance
- Digital-ID assurance
- Beneficial owners
- Non-face-to-face onboarding
- Source of funds
- Beneficiary screening
- Beneficiary changes
- Cash-pickup identification
- Failed or incomplete KYC
- Cross-entity record access

Reliance on partner KYC requires a legal basis, access to underlying evidence, quality testing, refresh responsibility, and a fallback if records become unavailable.

## 5.3 AML, sanctions, and fraud

Global minimum controls include:

- Screening at required transaction and party events
- Customer and beneficiary identification
- Transaction monitoring
- Fraud and account-takeover controls
- PEP and adverse-media processes where appropriate
- Suspicious-activity escalation and reporting
- Record retention and evidence integrity
- Independent testing

Beneficiary-change, multiple-sender, high-risk payout-location, and unusual velocity controls should connect fraud and AML monitoring.

Critical sanctions failures require immediate restriction or suspension and are not subject to an economic review cycle.

## 5.4 Regulatory-change management

| Classification | Required response |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | Same-day legal assessment; restrict affected activity if legality is uncertain |
| **High** | Implement before effective date with executive oversight and control testing |
| **Standard** | Implement through normal product and release governance |

Process:

1. Identify the change.
2. Assign affected entities and products.
3. Obtain legal interpretation.
4. Assess operational, technology, customer, tax, and liquidity effects.
5. Assign owner and deadline.
6. Configure and test controls.
7. Train affected staff.
8. Approve implementation.
9. Update the dossier and disclosures.
10. Validate post-implementation effectiveness.

## 5.5 Consumer protection

By country and product, document:

- Fees and FX disclosures
- Quote validity and price-lock rules
- Delivery-time representations
- Cancellation and refund rights
- Error correction
- Compliance-hold communications
- Payout-method disclosures
- Complaint channels and deadlines
- Language and accessibility
- Customer-fund disclosures

Dynamic pricing must not transfer post-funding losses to customers where prohibited.

# 6. Settlement and liquidity model

## 6.1 Separate liquidity pools

Daily reporting must distinguish:

- Safeguarded customer funds
- Unrestricted operating cash
- Partner prefunding
- Collateral
- Funds in transit
- Restricted or trapped cash
- Legally transferable liquidity
- Same-day usable liquidity
- Committed facilities

Only legally and operationally usable funds count toward operating liquidity coverage.

## 6.2 Currency profiles

Every settlement currency requires:

- Approved banks and account locations
- Convertibility classification
- Holding limit and maximum holding period
- Conversion and repatriation process
- Purpose codes and supporting documents
- Open-position limit
- Stress depreciation assumption
- Permitted hedging instruments
- Treatment of blocked conversions
- Customer-disclosure implications
- Escalation owner

An offshore or hard-currency alternative is not automatically lawful or economically preferable.

## 6.3 Liquidity requirement

\[
RequiredLiquidity =
PeakStressedNetOutflow
+
RefundRequirement
+
OperationalReserve
+
CurrencySpecificBuffer
\]

The methodology must specify:

- Forecast horizon
- Intraday and end-of-day requirements
- Gross versus net settlement flows
- Netting eligibility
- Confidence or stress level
- Holiday and cutoff treatment
- Customer-refund obligations
- Legal availability of facilities
- Currency-conversion assumptions
- Partner-delay assumptions

Stress scenarios:

- **Moderate:** three-day partner or clearing delay
- **Severe:** ten-day delay or loss of the primary route
- **Extreme:** capital-control restriction, trapped cash, or inability to convert

## 6.4 Refund reserve

For each material product, document:

- Responsible legal entity
- Minimum unrestricted refund reserve
- Maximum partner exposure
- Refund prioritization
- Funding source if partner balances are trapped
- Customer communications
- Regulatory notification
- Recovery claim against the partner

## 6.5 Treasury controls

- Daily cash and exposure reporting
- Intraday monitoring for material products
- Partner and currency limits
- Dual approval for material funding
- Independent reconciliation
- Forecast-versus-actual variance analysis
- Trapped-cash escalation
- Quarterly stress testing
- Automatic funding stops when limits or reconciliation tolerances are breached
- No speculative currency inventory

Any hedging program must define approved instruments, counterparties, tenor, collateral, basis risk, effectiveness, accounting treatment, and hedge-failure procedures.

# 7. Partner strategy

## 7.1 Full-chain due diligence

Assess direct and indirect exposure to:

- Bank or payout partner
- FX provider
- Correspondent bank
- Local clearing participant
- Wallet or cash agent
- Subcontractor
- Technology provider
- Common owner or infrastructure
- Country and currency

For material partners, obtain evidence covering:

- License and permitted services
- Ownership and financial condition
- AML, sanctions, fraud, and consumer controls
- Safeguarding and fund ownership
- Settlement and reconciliation history
- Liquidity and collateral requirements
- Technical uptime and recovery
- Cybersecurity and data
- Business continuity
- Insolvency, recovery rate, and recovery time
- Audit and regulator-access rights

Where audited statements are unavailable, use regulatory returns, bank references, settlement history, external credit information, management accounts, enforceable guarantees, and legal recovery analysis.

## 7.2 Partner tiers

| Tier | Capability | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | API, reliable status, automated reconciliation, strong controls | Primary scale route |
| **Tier 2** | Secure files, adequate controls, some manual exceptions | Secondary or controlled volume |
| **Tier 3** | Manual or semi-manual processing | Low-volume coverage or contingency only |

Tier 3 partners require lower transaction and balance limits, frequent reconciliation, dual controls, and higher pricing or reserve requirements.

## 7.3 Performance management

| Status | Trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Green** | Within settlement, reconciliation, compliance, financial, and exposure limits | Normal service and scale eligibility |
| **Amber** | Deterioration or isolated breach | No limit increase; cap volume; remediation deadline |
| **Red** | Material outage, unresolved balance, licensing, financial, or control concern | Stop prefunding; restrict or suspend; activate fallback |

Amber status must have a defined remediation deadline and maximum duration. Red status requires immediate executive review and a customer-impact assessment.

## 7.4 Concentration and contingency

Concentration limits should reflect:

- Unsecured exposure
- Partner financial strength
- Recovery time and rate
- Substitute availability
- Common correspondent or technology dependencies
- Country and currency concentration
- Customer impact
- Alternative route capacity

A second partner is preferred for material products but is not mandatory when unavailable or uneconomic. Compensating controls must be documented and time-limited.

A backup route is operational only when it has:

- Legal and Compliance approval
- Contractual availability
- Currency and account access
- Technical connectivity
- Sufficient capacity
- Tested reconciliation
- Pricing and disclosure readiness
- Refund capability
- Defined activation time

# 8. Risk controls and scenario triggers

## 8.1 Capital-control tightening

**Triggers:** new quotas, purpose codes, settlement-currency restrictions, conversion limits, or repatriation delays.

**Actions:**

1. Stop new exposure where legality or conversion is unclear.
2. Preserve, segregate, and reconcile customer funds.
3. Convene Legal, Compliance, Treasury, and Operations.
4. Confirm permitted currencies, quotas, purpose codes, and repatriation.
5. Reduce limits and prefunding.
6. Do not adopt offshore settlement without approval.
7. Communicate realistic delay or refund timelines.
8. Reopen only after updated legal, treasury, operational, and customer-control approval.

## 8.2 Partner failure or bank de-risking

**Triggers:** settlement outage, account-closure warning, credit deterioration, license loss, or unexplained restrictions.

**Actions:**

1. Stop additional prefunding.
2. Cap or suspend new originations.
3. Activate a validated fallback route.
4. Reconcile balances and funds in transit.
5. Invoke recovery, audit, and transition rights.
6. Fund refunds from approved unrestricted liquidity.
7. Notify customers and regulators where required.
8. Reassess concentration before restoring service.

## 8.3 FX shock or conversion failure

**Triggers:** currency movement beyond approved limits, inability to convert, hedge failure, or material variance from quoted FX economics.

**Actions:**

1. Shorten quote validity.
2. Reduce open positions and local inventory.
3. Convert same day where lawful.
4. Apply approved pricing adjustments.
5. Stress-test customer-fund and operating liquidity separately.
6. Escalate trapped cash or losses above tolerance.
7. Restrict or pause if conversion, liquidity, or economics are unreliable.

## 8.4 Compliance-volume spike

**Triggers:** alert backlog, sanctions hits, fraud, or manual-review time exceeding capacity.

**Actions:**

1. Stop growth marketing.
2. Add approved review capacity.
3. Reduce limits for higher-risk segments.
4. Change rules only through model governance.
5. Monitor false positives, abandonment, and quality.
6. Resume growth only when backlog and quality return within limits.

## 8.5 Ledger, cyber, or technology failure

**Triggers:** duplicate-payment risk, missing records, unavailable screening, ledger outage, or unauditable processing.

**Actions:**

1. Stop affected transactions.
2. Activate continuity and controlled manual procedures.
3. Preserve transaction and audit data.
4. Reconcile customer, bank, partner, and internal ledgers.
5. Notify affected parties where required.
6. Complete independent post-incident review before full restoration.

## 8.6 Wind-down

A wind-down plan must define:

- Maximum duration
- Maximum new exposure
- Exposure-reduction target
- Open-transaction treatment
- Refund reserve
- Customer-claim process
- Partner-balance recovery
- Regulatory notifications
- Record retention
- Data return or deletion
- Staff and vendor continuity

Immediate safety issues require immediate restriction. Economic exit decisions may use a two-quarter remediation period only where safety and customer-fund integrity remain sound.

# 9. Governance and decision rights

## 9.1 Corridor Investment and Risk Committee

Members:

- CEO or executive sponsor
- CFO
- Chief Risk/Compliance Officer
- General Counsel
- Treasurer
- COO
- CTO
- Product leader
- Regional leads
- Customer-service lead

The Committee approves:

- New products, corridors, entities, and settlement currencies
- Material partners
- Exposure and liquidity-limit increases
- Pricing exceptions
- High-risk customer segments
- Expand, Restrict, Pause, Redesign, and Exit decisions
- Strategic risk acceptances
- Major technology investments

## 9.2 Decision rights

| Decision | Accountable owner | Required control input |
|---|---|---|
| Legal perimeter and licensing | General Counsel | Compliance and regional lead |
| KYC, AML, and sanctions rules | Chief Compliance Officer | Legal, Product, Technology |
| Liquidity and currency limits | Treasurer | CFO and Risk |
| Partner exposure limits | Treasurer | Compliance, Legal, COO |
| Product profitability | CFO | Product and Treasury |
| Final product status | Committee chair | Legal, Compliance, Treasury, Finance |
| KPI data quality | Finance/Data owner | Internal Audit oversight |
| Regulatory-change implementation | Relevant control owner | Legal and Compliance |
| Reopening after legal or sanctions stop | General Counsel/CCO | Committee |
| Reopening after liquidity or partner event | Treasurer/COO | Committee |
| Customer-fund recovery plan | CFO/COO | Legal and Compliance |
| Emergency service restriction | CEO delegate or COO | Immediate control-function notification |

## 9.3 Emergency authority

- **Treasurer:** stop funding and reduce exposure for liquidity, currency, or counterparty breaches.
- **Legal or Compliance:** stop or hold transactions for licensing, sanctions, AML, or safeguarding concerns.
- **COO:** activate failover and controlled manual processing.
- **CEO or delegate:** approve temporary operational restrictions, but cannot override a legal or sanctions stop.
- **Committee:** review emergency action within five business days.
- **Board or Risk Committee:** receive notice for material safeguarding, liquidity, regulatory, or partner events.

- Risk-adjusted return on operating capital
- Cost per transaction
- Liquidity cost

- Manual transactions per shift
- Peak staffing coverage
- Complaint rate
- Refund completion time
- First-contact resolution
- Straight-through-processing rate

## Data and model governance

Maintain:

- Source-system ownership

- Timestamp and FX-rate standards
- Entity and currency mapping
- Manual-adjustment controls
- Data lineage
- KPI certification
- Access controls
- Change logs

AML, fraud, FX pricing, liquidity, stress-testing, and product-scoring models require named owners, approval authority, validation frequency, change control, performance monitoring, override logging, independent validation, and retirement criteria.

# 11. Implementation roadmap

## Phase 1: Days 0–30 — Protect urgent exposures

**Owners:** CFO, CRO, General Counsel, Treasurer, COO.

- Inventory material entities, products, currencies, partners, accounts, and payout methods.
- Identify licensing, safeguarding, trapped-cash, single-partner, and reconciliation exposures.
- Set emergency limits and stop-processing triggers.
- Confirm emergency decision rights.
- Build preliminary RACM for highest-volume and highest-risk products.
- Create initial funds-flow and regulatory dossiers.

**Exit criteria:** every material product has an owner, legal status, exposure view, interim limit, and emergency procedure.

## Phase 2: Days 31–90 — Establish decision visibility

**Owners:** Finance, Treasury, Compliance, Operations, Data.

- Complete cost, FX, liquidity, and partner exposure attribution.
- Separate customer-fund, operating, restricted, and partner balances.

# 12. Facts, assumptions, and required local confirmation

## Facts from the business context

- The firm operates licensed remittance corridors between the EU, Gulf, Southeast Asia, and Latin America.
- Licensing, KYC, and settlement-currency rules differ by bloc.
- Several corridors face new capital-control measures.
- Corridor margins differ materially.
- Local banking partners have uneven technical maturity.

## Strategic assumptions

The recommendations assume that:

- The firm can adjust pricing, transaction limits, payout methods, and partner routes.
- Some material products can support multiple banking or payout options.
- Customer-fund balances can be identified by legal entity and currency.
- The firm can obtain adequate transaction, FX, liquidity, partner, and compliance data.
- Management can suspend or limit products when legal, liquidity, sanctions, safeguarding, or settlement conditions deteriorate.
- Central technology and control platforms can be configured for local requirements.

These assumptions should be assigned confidence levels and validated before capital allocation.

## Matters requiring local counsel or regulatory confirmation

Before launch, material expansion, settlement-route change, or product redesign, obtain jurisdiction-specific advice on:

- Licensing, registration, passporting, agency, sponsorship, and branch models
- Whether the activity constitutes payment services, money transmission, FX, stored value, wallet, or another regulated service
- KYC, beneficial-owner, source-of-funds, and beneficiary requirements
- Third-party KYC reliance
- Suspicious-transaction reporting
- Sanctions blocking, rejection, and return requirements
- Consumer cancellation, refunds, disclosures, and complaints
- Safeguarding, trust, reserve, segregation, and insolvency treatment
- Permitted settlement currencies and account structures
- Capital controls, quotas, purpose codes, conversion, and repatriation
- Offshore settlement, netting, and correspondent arrangements
- Hedging and derivatives permissions
- Data localization, cloud, outsourcing, and cross-border access
- Tax, withholding, invoicing, transfer pricing, and local reporting
- Wallet, cash-payout, aggregator, and subcontractor requirements
- Enforceability of security, set-off, audit, step-in, and recovery rights

## Final recommendation

The firm should direct near-term attention to product-level screening, exposure reduction, regulatory certainty, treasury visibility, reconciliation, and partner resilience.

Subject to evidence:

- **EU-linked products** are the strongest initial scale candidates where liquidity, automation, legal permission, and receiving-side controls are demonstrated.
- **Gulf products** should be maintained and expanded selectively only after local settlement, repatriation, safeguarding, and partner resilience are confirmed.
- **Southeast Asian products** should be piloted country by country, prioritizing digitally scalable routes with sustainable small-ticket economics.
- **Latin American products** should expand selectively under strict FX, liquidity, conversion, and trapped-cash controls.

The strategy succeeds when the firm can grow profitable products while retaining the ability to **stop, reroute, refund, reconcile, and wind down** safely under regulatory, liquidity, partner, currency, or technology stress.
