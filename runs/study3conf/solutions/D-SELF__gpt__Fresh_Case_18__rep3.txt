# Global Health-Feature Rollout Strategy

## Executive summary

Adopt a **market-specific, claim-controlled, evidence-gated rollout**. The company should not market a single globally uniform “medical health platform.” Each feature and jurisdiction should have a controlled configuration covering intended use, claims, technical version, evidence, privacy, distribution, support, and post-market controls.

### Recommended sequence

1. **Build shared governance and validation infrastructure first.**
2. **Launch lower-claim features selectively**—fall detection, defined respiratory trends, connected-cuff integration, and authorized glucose-data integration—only where local review and safety operations are ready.
3. **Make sleep-apnea screening the primary medical program** and largest clinical-validation investment.
4. **Validate AF notification in parallel**, but do not automatically launch it next.
5. **Use the EU and Japan as potential first medical markets**, subject to formal local confirmation and readiness; do not assume either must be first.
6. **Keep the US on three separate paths:** genuinely non-medical wellness, appropriately reviewed research/clinical pilot, and medical authorization.
7. **Treat emerging markets country by country**, not as one regulatory category.
8. **Expand only after post-launch evidence confirms acceptable safety, subgroup performance, user comprehension, clinical utility, and economics.**

### Executive decisions requested

Approve:

- Sleep apnea as the highest-priority medical-validation program.
- AF as the second-highest medical-validation priority.
- Fall detection as a potentially earlier commercial launch, but safety-critical.
- Wellness-first respiratory, blood-pressure, and glucose-data strategies with tightly controlled claims.
- A global claims committee and feature-by-market release controls.
- Readiness-based launch gates rather than fixed geographic or calendar commitments.
- Separate wellness, research, and medical revenue forecasts.
- No launch without qualified, jurisdiction-specific regulatory, clinical, privacy, and legal confirmation.

# 2. Product-track and governance model

Operate three distinct tracks.

| Track | Definition | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| **Wellness** | Genuinely non-medical observations or trends | Consumer insight without disease-screening, diagnosis, treatment, or medical-referral implications |
| **Medical** | A jurisdiction-specific configuration that has completed applicable authorization, conformity, registration, labeling, advertising, and post-market requirements | Regulated screening, notification, or decision-support claims |
| **Research/clinical pilot** | Restricted activity under an appropriately reviewed research or clinical-investigation framework | Evidence generation or clinician workflow testing; not a regulatory workaround |

“Medical configuration” is an internal operating term, not a uniform legal category.

## Feature-by-market control matrix

For every feature and market, document:

- Intended purpose and target population
- Classification and market-entry rationale
- Hardware, sensors, firmware, algorithm, and cloud version
- Signal-quality rules and thresholds
- Approved and prohibited claims
- Supporting evidence and known limitations
- Warnings and exclusions
- Privacy, hosting, retention, and transfer model
- Referral or emergency workflow
- Complaint and adverse-event routing
- Post-market thresholds
- Local representatives, importers, authorization-holders, and distributors
- Revalidation requirements

Material changes to intended purpose, target population, sensors, model weights, thresholds, notifications, or clinician workflow require cross-functional review.

# 3. Claims and communications principles

## Claims hierarchy

| Level | Example | Required posture |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Observation | “Shows selected overnight breathing-related signals.” | Lower-risk starting point, subject to local review |
| 1. Wellness insight | “Helps users understand changes in sleep patterns.” | Wellness configuration |
| 2. Risk indication | “May identify patterns associated with elevated risk.” | Stronger evidence and qualification required |
| 3. Screening/referral | “Screens for possible sleep apnea and recommends professional evaluation.” | Medical pathway likely |
| 4. Diagnosis/treatment | “Diagnoses sleep apnea” or “guides treatment.” | Avoid initially |

The central claims committee should approve:

- Product and feature names
- Packaging and labeling
- App screens, notifications, and visual risk indicators
- App-store and retailer listings
- Websites, advertising, and press releases
- Physician materials
- Influencer and affiliate content
- Sales and customer-support scripts

Each launch requires a version-controlled claims pack covering screenshots, videos, result flows, insufficient-data states, help content, retailer listings, influencer materials, clinician reports, and support scripts.

### Prohibited communication principles

Do not claim or imply that the product:

- Diagnoses or rules out disease unless specifically authorized and supported.
- Replaces polysomnography or a validated clinical test.
- Guarantees detection or prevention of complications.
- Makes medication or treatment decisions.
- Provides guaranteed emergency response.
- Has FDA approval or equivalent status when that status has not been obtained.
- Is “medical-grade” in a way that implies all features are medically validated.

A disclaimer cannot cure an otherwise prominent medical implication created by feature naming, design, notifications, or marketing context.

# 4. Feature-portfolio sequencing

| Feature | Initial positioning | Validation priority | Commercial sequence |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Sleep apnea** | Screening/referral or narrowly bounded wellness insight | 1 | First major medical launch |
| **AF notification** | Possible irregular-rhythm notification, not diagnosis | 2 | Parallel evidence; launch only after gates |
| **Fall detection** | Possible fall detection and designated-contact notification | High safety priority | Potentially early, subject to safety case |
| **Respiratory trends** | Precisely defined respiratory-rate, oxygen, or signal trend | Moderate | Early wellness learning if output is defined and validated |
| **Blood pressure** | Connected-cuff, user-entered, or bounded trend data | Moderate-high | Integration/trend version first |
| **Glucose** | Authorized external-sensor data or user-entered records | High if autonomous | Defer autonomous claims |

### Important distinction

- **Validation priority:** sleep apnea, then AF.
- **Potential commercial priority:** fall detection and defined respiratory trends may launch earlier if safety and claims controls are ready.

Do not assume that a feature is low risk merely because it is positioned as wellness. Risk depends on the total presentation, likely user behavior, failure consequences, and available follow-up.

# 5. Sleep-apnea validation and evidence plan

## 5.1 Intended-use boundaries

A possible initial medical intended use is:

> The feature analyzes selected wearable signals to identify patterns that may be associated with elevated risk of sleep apnea and recommends evaluation by a qualified healthcare professional. It is not intended to diagnose sleep apnea, exclude disease, replace polysomnography or a validated home sleep-apnea test, or guide treatment.

The final wording must be market-specific and supported by the evidence.

Initially consider limiting use to:

- Adults
- A defined clinical target, such as obstructive sleep apnea or a specified severity threshold
- Supported devices and firmware
- Minimum valid data and signal-quality requirements
- Studied sleep duration and wear conditions
- Populations represented in validation

Exclude or separately validate children, pregnancy, known central sleep apnea, major cardiopulmonary disease, and materially unrepresented body-size, skin-tone, comorbidity, or device-fit groups.

The output should provide:

- Positive result
- Negative result
- Indeterminate or insufficient-data result

A negative result must not imply that sleep apnea has been ruled out.

## 5.2 Core clinical study

Use a prospective, multisite study against an appropriate reference standard. The protocol must specify:

- The target condition and severity threshold
- Primary unit of analysis: user, night, or recording
- When polysomnography is required
- When a validated home sleep-apnea test is acceptable
- Handling of technically inadequate or discordant reference tests
- Independent or blinded reference interpretation
- Minimum positive and negative cases
- Severity distribution
- Representative versus enriched sampling
- Device, firmware, and algorithm versions
- Prespecified statistical analysis

Include, where feasible:

- Multiple nights
- Realistic home-use conditions
- Motion and poor-fit conditions
- Missing data and sensor-contact variation
- Relevant device generations
- Prespecified subgroup recruitment targets

Report:

- Sensitivity and specificity
- PPV and NPV under realistic market prevalence
- AUC and calibration
- Per-user and per-night performance
- Indeterminate and unusable-night rates
- Night-to-night repeatability
- Performance by severity
- Device, firmware, and signal-quality effects
- Subgroup performance with confidence intervals
- Consequences of false positives and false negatives

A case-control study alone should not be treated as sufficient for a broad consumer-screening claim because it may distort predictive values and real-world usability.

## 5.3 Quantitative decision thresholds

Before unblinding, the clinical, statistical, medical-affairs, quality, and regulatory teams should approve:

- Minimum sensitivity and specificity
- PPV and NPV assumptions
- Maximum indeterminate and unusable-data rates
- Minimum subgroup sample sizes
- Maximum permitted subgroup performance gap or review trigger
- Confidence-interval precision
- Maximum acceptable referral burden
- User-comprehension threshold
- Safety stop rules
- Conditions requiring narrower labeling, additional studies, or termination

Exact numerical values should be based on the target use, clinical consequences, and regulatory strategy; they should not be selected after results are known.

## 5.4 Human factors and clinical utility

Test whether users:

- Understand that a positive result is not a diagnosis.
- Understand that a negative result does not exclude disease.
- Respond appropriately to an indeterminate result.
- Seek care when symptoms persist despite a negative result.
- Avoid changing medication or treatment based solely on the feature.
- Follow referral guidance.
- Experience excessive anxiety or false reassurance.

Measure:

- Referral completion
- Diagnostic yield
- Unnecessary testing
- Time to evaluation
- Delayed care
- Anxiety and distress
- Clinician interpretation time
- Clinician workload
- Incremental value over ordinary or symptom-based referral

Before launch, model the expected volume of positive and indeterminate results and confirm sufficient local sleep-testing, primary-care, or telehealth capacity.

# 6. Regional classification and launch approach

## European Union

### Current position

The company currently assesses the medical sleep-apnea feature as a medical device in the EU. This is a **provisional planning position** pending formal classification and qualified EU confirmation.

A screening or clinical-referral claim may bring the software within the applicable EU medical-device framework, but the outcome depends on intended purpose, software role, claims, and output significance.

### Required work

- Documented classification rationale
- Quality-management system
- Risk-management file
- Software lifecycle documentation
- Clinical evaluation
- Usability engineering
- Cybersecurity
- Applicable conformity-assessment route, potentially involving a notified body
- CE marking where required
- Post-market surveillance and clinical follow-up
- Economic-operator and registration obligations where applicable
- Importer and distributor controls
- Vigilance and corrective-action procedures
- GDPR and national privacy assessment

Distinguish:

1. **EU regulatory status**, including applicable conformity and registration requirements; and
2. **Country-level commercial readiness**, including language, advertising, reimbursement, distribution, support, healthcare access, and privacy implementation.

After the applicable EU route is complete, commercial availability may still be staged by country.

### Launch approach

Use a controlled launch in selected countries only after:

- Applicable EU market-access requirements are complete
- Local-language labeling is validated
- Support and complaint systems are operational
- Referral capacity is confirmed
- Post-market monitoring is live

Do not present CE marking as proof of diagnosis, universal clinical effectiveness, or physician endorsement.

## Japan

### Current position

The company currently assesses the feature as a medical device in Japan. This is also a **provisional planning position pending Japanese confirmation**.

### Required work

- Product classification and authorization route
- Local authorization-holder or responsible-party structure
- Quality-system obligations
- Local clinical or bridging-data requirements
- Japanese labeling and advertising review
- Importation and distribution controls
- Post-market vigilance
- Software-update and change-control requirements

Use Japanese investigators, sleep specialists, hospitals, and patient representatives to assess:

- Terminology
- Risk-versus-diagnosis comprehension
- Report format
- Referral pathways
- Local care access
- Interpretation of positive, negative, and indeterminate results

Physician involvement must distinguish scientific advice, clinical investigation, and promotional endorsement. Disclose conflicts of interest and obtain approval before using names, titles, quotations, or institutional affiliations.

### Launch approach

Japan may launch alongside or after the first EU market if the local authorization route, evidence requirements, partners, labeling, support, and surveillance systems are ready. It should not be selected solely for prestige or assumed to accept EU evidence without a local transferability assessment.

## United States

### Current position

US classification is pending. The route will depend on intended use, total presentation, technical characteristics, relevant comparators, and whether the output is used for clinical decisions.

### Three-path strategy

1. **Wellness configuration**  
   Non-disease observation or trend claims only.

2. **Research or clinical-pilot configuration**  
   Restricted access under an appropriately reviewed framework.

3. **Medical configuration**  
   Pursue the applicable regulatory pathway once intended use and evidence strategy are sufficiently defined.

The research route is not a workaround. Before any pilot, document:

- Study type
- Oversight and consent requirements
- Whether results are returned
- Whether clinicians use the results
- Distribution and claims restrictions
- Safety reporting
- Data ownership and secondary use
- Termination authority

### Immediate actions

- Conduct a total-claims and total-presentation audit.
- Compare with relevant legally marketed products.
- Obtain qualified US pathway advice.
- Seek FDA pre-submission feedback if a medical route is likely.
- Maintain separate wellness, research, and medical forecasts.
- Review federal and state advertising, consumer-protection, privacy, product-liability, employer, health-system, app-store, and retailer exposure.

A US wellness version must not use disease-specific screening language or communicate medical risk through red-risk displays, disease names, clinical referral prompts, or support scripts.

Pending FDA discussions or submissions must not be described as approval or clearance.

## Emerging markets

Assess each country individually for:

- Device registration and import rules
- Advertising and consumer-protection requirements
- Local representatives
- Privacy, hosting, localization, and transfer rules
- Language requirements
- Distributor and influencer controls
- Healthcare and sleep-testing access
- Complaint and adverse-event capability
- Customer-support and escalation capacity

Classify each country as:

- Wellness launch
- Medical launch
- Research or pilot only
- Defer

# 7. Physician and consumer credibility program

## Physician credibility

Establish an independent advisory group including:

- Sleep physicians
- Primary-care clinicians
- Respiratory specialists
- Sleep technologists
- Statisticians
- Human-factors experts
- Patient representatives

Use a written charter covering:

- Conflicts of interest
- Scientific versus promotional roles
- Use of names and affiliations
- Documentation of dissenting views
- Decision rights
- Publication and data-access principles

Conduct specialist and primary-care pilots. Measure:

- Report-interpretation time
- Referral appropriateness
- Unnecessary referrals
- Clinician confidence
- Workload
- Report usability
- Compatibility with local care pathways

Clinician reports should show:

- Number of nights analyzed
- Signal quality
- Result and threshold
- Indeterminate status
- Relevant limitations
- Device and software version
- Recommended next step
- Statement that diagnosis requires clinical testing

Define whether reports are automatically transmitted, whether monitoring is expected, and who handles urgent concerns. Do not create clinical obligations the company cannot support.

## Consumer credibility

Provide:

- Plain-language distinction between screening and diagnosis
- Study population, performance, and subgroup limitations
- “Why did I receive this result?” explanations
- Non-alarmist positive-result messaging
- Clear insufficient-data handling
- Symptom-based and locally relevant guidance
- Privacy controls and data-use explanations
- Visible complaint and support routes
- Notice of material algorithm or labeling changes

Support teams must not diagnose, recommend medication changes, or provide inappropriate reassurance. Escalation scripts should address severe symptoms, negative results despite symptoms, repeated indeterminate results, contradictory results, inability to access testing, AF alerts, fall emergencies, and device failures.

The company should commit to balanced evidence communication, including material limitations, subgroup performance, indeterminate rates, and significant post-market findings.

# 8. Feature-specific safety controls

## Fall detection

Fall detection may be commercially earlier but is safety-critical. Test:

- Different fall types, speeds, positions, and surfaces
- Missed falls and false alarms
- Battery depletion
- Connectivity loss
- Device removal and poor fit
- Emergency-contact confirmation
- Cancellation and escalation
- Older users and accessibility needs
- Regional emergency-service availability

State precisely whether the system contacts emergency services, a monitoring service, a designated contact, or only the user. Do not imply guaranteed emergency response.

## Respiratory and oxygen-related trends

Select and validate the exact output before assigning launch priority:

- Respiratory rate
- Derived respiratory trend
- Oxygen saturation
- Signal-quality change
- Composite anomaly score

Do not use broad “respiratory health” wording to imply disease monitoring. If oxygen saturation is shown, validate performance under motion, poor contact, varying fit and placement, altitude, temperature, peripheral perfusion, relevant skin-tone groups, and sensor and algorithm versions.

## AF notification

Before launch, establish:

- Analytical and clinical performance
- False-alert and notification burden
- Signal-quality failure handling
- Repeated and inconclusive-alert handling
- User comprehension
- Follow-up and urgent-symptom instructions
- Whether results go to the user, clinician, or both
- Market-specific requirements

## Blood pressure

Initially provide:

- Connected validated-cuff integration
- User-entered readings
- Clearly bounded trend displays where permitted

Identify the external data source clearly. Do not support medication or treatment decisions based on an unvalidated wearable estimate.

## Glucose

Initially display data from an authorized external sensor or user-entered records. Defer autonomous glucose or metabolic claims until accuracy, clinical utility, safety, and regulatory economics justify the investment.

# 9. Privacy, cybersecurity, and algorithm governance

## Privacy

For each market and use case, document:

- Data collected
- Raw versus derived data retained
- Processing and storage locations
- Access rights
- Retention periods
- Account-closure and deletion effects
- Secondary use for model improvement
- Research versus product consent
- Vendor and cloud responsibilities
- Cross-border transfers
- User access, correction, withdrawal, and deletion rights

Consent alone should not be assumed to resolve all obligations.

## Cybersecurity

Implement:

- Threat modeling
- Encryption in transit and at rest
- Role-based access and audit logs
- Penetration testing
- Secure updates and rollback
- Vulnerability-disclosure process
- Patch timelines
- Third-party dependency monitoring
- Cloud-outage and offline behavior
- Disaster recovery
- Account-recovery controls
- End-of-support policy
- Incident communications

## Algorithm governance

Decide whether the product uses:

- A locked algorithm
- Periodic controlled releases
- Personalization
- Adaptive thresholds
- Continuous learning

Do not permit uncontrolled continuous learning in production. Model-improvement data require documented provenance, lawful basis or consent, training/test separation, approval for new uses, and traceability from data changes to model releases.

# 14. Decision gates and success metrics

## Gate A: Claims and classification

- Intended purpose documented
- Every claim mapped to evidence
- Market route reviewed by qualified professionals
- All user-facing content controlled
- Population, exclusions, and limitations defined

## Gate B: Clinical evidence

- Prespecified performance thresholds met
- PPV and NPV acceptable under realistic prevalence
- Indeterminate rate within limit
- Subgroup criteria met or reflected in restricted labeling
- User comprehension and behavior acceptable
- Clinical utility and referral capacity demonstrated

## Gate C: Regulatory and quality readiness

- Applicable market-entry requirements complete
- Quality, risk, usability, software, and cybersecurity records complete
- Local privacy and labeling requirements satisfied
- Update and change-control processes operational
- Local representatives, importers, authorization-holders, and distributors ready

## Gate D: Commercial and safety readiness

- Support and clinical escalation trained
- Referral or emergency workflows operational
- Complaint triage tested
- Cloud availability and rollback validated
- Distributor and influencer controls enforceable
- Follow-up guidance actionable
- Expected support and clinical workload resourced

## Gate E: Expansion or continuation

- Complaint and adverse-event rates within limits
- Stable subgroup performance
- No material delayed-care or false-reassurance signal
- Manageable anxiety and support burden
- Clinician acceptance
- Adequate referral capacity
- No unresolved serious privacy or cybersecurity incident
- Sustainable full-lifecycle economics

### Core dashboard

Track:

- Sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, and calibration
- Indeterminate and unusable-data rates
- Subgroup performance gaps
- Referral completion and diagnostic yield
- Clinician interpretation time
- User comprehension and appropriate behavior
- False-positive and false-negative complaints
- Serious and reportable-event rates
- Complaint-triage and rollback times
- Support contacts per 1,000 users
- Privacy and cybersecurity incidents
- Distributor and influencer violations
- Contribution margin after medical, regulatory, support, and surveillance costs

Exact thresholds must be feature-specific and approved before clinical unblinding or launch.

# 15. Contingencies

- **Strong performance and straightforward authorization:** Launch in the first ready medical market, publish balanced evidence, and prioritize referral infrastructure.
- **Subgroup disparities:** Narrow the population or claim, add warnings or indeterminate outputs, and collect targeted data.
- **High sensitivity but excessive false positives:** Recalibrate, consider repeat-night confirmation, measure referral burden, and defer broad screening claims.
- **US pathway remains uncertain:** Maintain only a genuinely wellness-limited product or appropriately reviewed research program; keep medical revenue forecasts separate.
- **EU or Japan require additional evidence:** Reprioritize the first medical market, conduct bridging or usability work, narrow claims where justified, and reapprove investment.
- **Follow-up care is unavailable:** Avoid aggressive positive-result notifications, provide symptom-based guidance, establish telehealth or primary-care partnerships, or defer launch.
- **Post-launch safety signal:** Pause advertising, restrict claims, add warnings, change thresholds, require repeat testing, suspend affected markets, roll back the algorithm, notify users or regulators, or conduct additional validation as warranted.

---

## Final recommendation

Proceed with a **sleep-apnea-led, six-feature portfolio**:

- Sleep apnea receives the largest clinical and regulatory investment.
- AF receives the next-highest medical-validation priority but does not automatically launch next.
- Fall detection may commercialize earlier, subject to a dedicated safety case and reliable contact workflows.
- Respiratory trends may provide earlier wellness learning only after the output is precisely defined and validated.
- Blood pressure begins with connected or user-entered data.
- Glucose begins with authorized external-data integration.

Launch country by country only when claims, evidence, market-entry requirements, clinical or emergency pathways, privacy, support, surveillance, and rollback capability are aligned.

This strategy is a management framework, not a legal classification, authorization, or clinical endorsement. All market-entry, research, claims, and regulatory decisions require qualified jurisdiction-specific confirmation.
