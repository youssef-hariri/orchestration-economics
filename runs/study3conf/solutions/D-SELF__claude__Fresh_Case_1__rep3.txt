# PLAYBOOK 1: NORTH AMERICA
## "Augment with Clarity—Sector-Specific Deployment"

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Objective:** Achieve 60–70% agent daily-use adoption across North America (US + Canada) within 18 months while managing job-anxiety concerns, liability exposure, and partner adoption resistance through transparent authority frameworks, shared-value mechanisms, and sector-differentiated rollout.

**Key Success Factors:**
- Publish Decision Authority Framework (Month 0) as trust-building artifact
- Formalize job-security guarantee in writing (Month 0)
- Implement bonus pool with transparent allocation formula (Month 1)
- Obtain E&O insurance approval for litigation practices (Month 2, gating condition)
- Segment rollout by sector adoption readiness (4 tracks with staggered timelines)
- Measure adoption & trust monthly; escalate if targets miss

**Expected Outcomes (18 months):**
- Overall adoption: 60–70% daily use
- Sector A (Fast Track): 75–85% daily use
- Sector B (Standard): 55–65% daily use
- Sector C (Extended): 40–50% daily use
- Sector D (Litigation): 35–45% daily use (or deferred indefinitely if insurance denied)
- Staff trust in agent accuracy: 4.2/5
- Client confidence in AI-assisted work: 4.4/5
- Junior staff attrition: <8%
- Agent error rate: <3%
- Zero regulatory/liability incidents

**Owner:** COO (North America) with Chief AI Officer; delegated to 4 Sector Directors

## SECTION 1: REGIONAL CONTEXT & TRUST ASSESSMENT

### 1.1 Current State Analysis

**Staff Perception Baseline (Pre-Rollout):**
- 35–40% early adopters (junior colleagues narrative): Tech consulting, operations teams; see agents as productivity tools
- 30–35% pragmatists: Finance, audit, operations; emotionally neutral; efficiency-focused
- 20–25% anxious: Junior professionals (0–3 years); fear role compression, credential devaluation
- 10–15% resistant: Senior partners, relationship-heavy roles (M&A, wealth management); see agents as threat to premium positioning

**Trust Deficits Identified:**
1. **Authority clarity gap:** Staff unsure of agent vs. human decision boundaries; fear agents will overstep or be overcontrolled
2. **Job-security gap:** 62% worried role will shrink; 7% optimistic role will expand (net anxiety despite public optimism)
3. **Value-capture gap:** Benefits seen as flowing to partners/shareholders, not frontline staff
4. **Fairness gap:** Concern agents will reinforce existing hiring/promotion biases against women, underrepresented minorities
5. **Liability gap (litigation only):** Uncertainty whether E&O insurance covers agent-assisted work

**Regional Regulatory Context:**
- No comprehensive federal AI governance; reliance on sector-specific rules (SEC, FINRA for finance; ABA Model Rules for law)
- State privacy laws (California, emerging others) require transparency on data use
- Professional liability insurance carriers now demanding AI risk assessments; some reserving on coverage
- Recent legal settlements over AI errors in discovery/contract review elevate firm scrutiny
- Labor law: At-will employment (most states); minimal union presence; risk of wage compression in junior roles without proactive action

### 1.2 Sector Segmentation

**Sector A: Fast Track (Tech Consulting, Operations, Finance)**
- **Characteristics:** Client-ready for AI; work is analytical, non-relationship-dependent; efficiency-driven culture; high automation comfort
- **Size:** ~250 FTE across 5 practice teams
- **Adoption readiness:** High; agents fit naturally into workflow
- **Rollout timeline:** Months 4–8 (compressed training, fast deployment)
- **Target adoption (18mo):** 75–85% daily use

**Sector B: Standard Track (Strategy Consulting, Corporate Advisory, HR Advisory)**
- **Characteristics:** Mixed relationship/analytical work; clients may care about AI use; measured skepticism; reputation-sensitive
- **Size:** ~350 FTE across 6 practice teams
- **Adoption readiness:** Moderate; requires client relationship management
- **Rollout timeline:** Months 8–14 (standard training, phased deployment with client feedback loops)
- **Target adoption (18mo):** 55–65% daily use

**Sector C: Extended Track (M&A, Restructuring, High-Stakes Transactions)**
- **Characteristics:** Relationship-critical work; high liability; partners conservative; clients sensitive to AI use
- **Size:** ~200 FTE across 4 practice teams
- **Adoption readiness:** Low; requires deep relationship building with partners and clients
- **Rollout timeline:** Months 9–18 (extended training, measured deployment with formal client pilots)
- **Target adoption (18mo):** 40–50% daily use

**Sector D: Specialized Track (Litigation, Regulatory, IP)**
- **Characteristics:** Highest liability; regulatory scrutiny; professional conduct rules; insurance complexity
- **Size:** ~180 FTE across 4 practice teams
- **Adoption readiness:** Uncertain; contingent on E&O insurance approval
- **Rollout timeline:** Months 6–14 (discovery phase); Months 14–24+ (deployment only if insurance approved)
- **Target adoption (18mo):** 35–45% daily use (trial period; may extend to 24+ months)
- **Gating condition:** No rollout without written E&O carrier approval by Month 2

## SECTION 4: AUTHORITY & DECISION-MAKING MODEL

### 4.1 Decision Authority Framework (Published, Legally Reviewed)

**Principle:** Agents propose; humans certify. Autonomy scales with task maturity, risk level, and historical accuracy.

#### **Six Authority Levels**

| Level | Task Type | Agent Role | Human Role | Escalation Trigger | Examples | Appeal Path |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Information Gathering** (Autonomous) | Routine data collection, organization, presentation | Collects, organizes, presents | Reviews for relevance/accuracy | Missing data, accuracy <90%, stakeholder concern | Market data; regulatory filing summaries; precedent gathering | None (spot-check acceptable) |
| **2. Analysis & Recommendation** (Autonomous with QC) | Financial projections, competitive analysis, legal assessment | Analyzes, proposes recommendation | Reviews reasoning, fact-checks, applies judgment | Error detected, >10% uncertainty, bias suspected | Financial modeling; competitive landscape; legal precedent assessment | Escalate to practice leader if staff disagrees after review |
| **3. Compliance Flagging** (Autonomous with verification) | Conflict checks, regulatory concern triggers, deal-breaker scenarios | Flags potential issue | Investigates + verifies + decides if real | False positive >5%, ambiguous trigger, high-stakes matter | Conflict of interest flags; regulatory concern flags; deal-breaker scenarios | Escalate to general counsel if flagging recurs or staff disagrees |
| **4. Drafting & Documentation** (Autonomous draft + human substantive edit) | Contract boilerplate, engagement letters, RFP responses, memo structure | Drafts client-ready document | Edits, personalizes, certifies completeness | Factual error, missing client-specific elements, tone misalignment | Contract boilerplate; engagement letters; RFP responses; research memos | Escalate to practice leader if material gap in draft |
| **5. Client-Facing Advice & Strategy** (Recommendation only; human decides) | Deal strategy, litigation strategy, regulatory advice, client recommendations | Recommends strategy/advice option | Makes final decision; certifies to client | NEVER autonomous | Deal strategy; litigation strategy; regulatory advice; client-facing recommendations | Staff can appeal to practice leader; practice leader final authority |
| **6. Hiring, Promotion, Discipline** (Information input only; human decides) | Candidate screening, promotion consideration, disciplinary recommendations | Provides data (credentials, performance metrics) | Decides; bias audit required post-facto | NEVER autonomous | Candidate screening; promotion consideration; disciplinary recommendation | HR + external advisor review if bias concern raised |

#### **Decision Value Thresholds**

- **<$50k impact:** Agent can operate at Level 2–3 autonomy (recommendation/flagging); no pre-approval required, but spot-checks mandatory (weekly QC random sample ≥10 decisions)
- **$50k–$500k impact:** Agent operates at Level 3 max (recommendation/flag); human verifies before execution
- **>$500k impact:** Agent at Level 1–2 (information/recommendation); human decides independently
- **Client-facing matters (any value):** Agent at Level 4–5 max (draft/recommendation); human certifies to client before delivery

#### **Escalation Protocol (Mandatory)**

**Step 1: Staff Detects Potential Error**
- Flag in system (time-stamped comment); document reason; assign to QC lead
- System auto-routes to QC dashboard

**Step 2: Auto-Escalation Rule**
- If same agent flagged >3 times in 30 days for same task type → auto-escalate to practice QC lead + Chief AI Officer

**Step 3: QC Review**
- QC lead investigates within 5 business days
- Documents finding: Error confirmed? False alarm? Systemic issue?
- Communicates outcome to staff (transparency)

**Step 4: Remediation**
- **If confirmed error:** Restrict agent autonomy in that task (demote from Level 2 to Level 3, e.g.); notify practice leadership
- **If false alarm:** Educate staff on agent limitations; reinforce correct use
- **If systemic issue:** Brief Chief AI Officer; agent retraining + model update; communicate timeline to affected staff

**Step 5: Appeal**
- Staff can appeal QC decision to practice leader (7-day window); practice leader decision final
- Appeal documented; tracked for patterns

#### **Quarterly Authority Review**

**Owner:** Chief AI Officer + practice leaders

**Process:**
1. Review error rates by task type; chart trends
2. If error rate >5% in any task → audit that task; consider restricting autonomy
3. If error rate declining <2% → consider increasing autonomy (promote from Level 3 to Level 2)
4. Publish "Authority Update Report" (what changed, why, impact, examples)
5. Solicit staff feedback: "Do these authority levels feel right for your work?"

**Deliverable:** Monthly trend chart (error rate by task type); quarterly report to Regional Implementation Council

### 4.2 Authority Governance: Changing Scope

**Who decides if agents get more/less autonomy?**

| Scope Change | Authority | Timeline | Escalation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Minor adjustment** (shift one task from Level 3 to Level 2 within existing domain) | Practice QC lead + Chief AI Officer | Immediate (after 2-week error monitoring) | None (informational to practice leader) |
| **Moderate expansion** (agent granted autonomy in new task domain within practice) | Practice leadership + Chief AI Officer | 2-week review + staff input | Practice leader decision final; documented in monthly report |
| **Firm-wide scope expansion** (new task type across multiple practices; e.g., agents now handle all <$50k document drafting) | Regional Implementation Council + Chief AI Officer | 1-month review + multi-practice input + bias audit | CEO if controversial or >6-month implementation delay |
| **Tier shift (e.g., Tier 5 strategy work becomes agent-recommended)** | AI Steering Committee | Quarterly review; >30-day decision window | Board AI subcommittee if high-risk (liability, regulatory, fairness) |

**Transparency:**
- Publish monthly "Authority Status Report" (errors, escalations, scope changes)
- Staff survey biannually: "Do you understand agent authority in your role?" (target ≥4/5)
- Quarterly town hall: address authority questions publicly
