## SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Strategic Context & Problem Statement

A mid-size payments firm operates 18 licensed remittance corridors across EU, Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC), Southeast Asia (ASEAN), and Latin America (LAC). Each bloc enforces distinct licensing requirements, KYC standards, settlement currencies, and increasingly stringent capital-control measures. Compliance-cost inflation (12–23% of corridor revenue) combined with settlement friction, banking-partner technical inconsistency, and regulatory volatility threatens corridor profitability and creates operational risk through regulatory contagion.

**Current State (2025–2026):**
- Corridor revenue: €4.14M annually (18 active routes)
- Gross corridor margin: 3.0–4.0% (post-banking-partner take-rate)
- Compliance costs: 15–23% of revenue (accelerating +2–3% YoY)
- Net corridor margin (pre-operations overhead): 0.2–2.8% (highly fragmented by corridor)
- Working capital locked in nostro buffers: €3.5M–€5M (2–5 day settlement reserve)
- **Critical Gap:** 9 of 18 corridors yield <1.5% net margin; structurally unprofitable without operational restructuring

### Five Core Vulnerabilities Remediated

1. **Regulatory Contagion Risk:** Fragmented legal structure allows Brazil COAF enforcement to cascade across all corridors. *Solution: Implement tiered legal-entity architecture (TIER 1–4) with independent capital and compliance segregation.*

2. **Hidden Compliance-Cost Overruns:** Flat-rate compliance allocation masks transaction-level KYC cost escalation and jurisdiction-specific AML friction. *Solution: Deploy tier-triggered KYC cost model (B1/S2/E3 tiers) with automated variance tracking.*

3. **FX Settlement & Liquidity Cascades:** Capital-control delays and FX volatility create multi-week settlement backlogs; nostro buffers undersized for stress scenarios. *Solution: Multi-currency liquidity pooling + stress-test-calibrated nostro sizing (€6M–€8M) + contingency credit facility.*

4. **Partner Technical Misalignment:** 6 of 18 corridors rely on legacy SWIFT partners (T+3–T+5 settlement); API-first integration sequencing not prioritized. *Solution: Tier banking partners by API maturity; prioritize Tier-1 partners (EU, GCC, Singapore); defer Tier-3 legacy partners to Phase 2.*

5. **Corridor Profitability Blind Spot:** Volume-based prioritization masks structural unprofitability of Brazil/Argentina/Vietnam corridors. *Solution: Rank corridors by compliance-cost-to-margin ratio; deprioritize <1.5% net-margin routes; exit Argentina entirely.*
