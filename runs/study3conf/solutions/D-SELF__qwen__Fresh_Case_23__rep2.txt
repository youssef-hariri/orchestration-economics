**CONFIDENTIAL EXECUTIVE BRIEF**

**TO:** Group Board of Directors and C-Suite Executive Committee  
**FROM:** Group Integration Management Office (IMO)  
**DATE:** October 24, 2023  
**SUBJECT:** Pan-African Banking Integration & Market Consolidation Strategy (2026–2031)  

---

### **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**
To successfully integrate our recent acquisitions in Nigeria, Kenya, and Morocco, the Group must abandon the traditional "rip-and-replace" M&A playbook, which historically destroys value and triggers regulatory friction. Instead, we will execute a **"Context-Aware Glocal" strategy**—achieving global scale in technology, data, and corporate governance, while preserving deep local resonance in customer experience, product design, and brand heritage. 

This 60-month integration roadmap is designed to protect local talent, ensure absolute regulatory compliance across three distinct jurisdictions, and systematically migrate legacy technologies without disrupting daily operations.

---

### **STRATEGIC PILLARS**

#### **Pillar 1: Regulatory Compliance & Corporate Structure**
*Regulators in our target markets have distinct priorities: the CBN (Nigeria) focuses on FX and inclusion; the CBK (Kenya) on open banking and mobile interoperability; and Bank Al-Maghrib (Morocco) on exchange controls and participatory finance. Compliance is our architectural foundation.*

*   **Mauritius-Domiciled HoldCo & Ring-Fenced Subsidiaries:** We will establish a Group Holding Company in **Mauritius** to facilitate consolidated reporting and capital efficiency, while maintaining the acquired entities as distinct, locally capitalized legal subsidiaries. This satisfies local ring-fencing requirements without triggering capital flight concerns.
*   **Hyper-Local Data Sovereignty:** We will deploy a "Local Data, Global Intelligence" architecture. Customer data will reside strictly in in-country sovereign cloud regions (compliant with Nigeria’s NDPA, Kenya’s ODPC, and Morocco’s CNDP). Group-level AI and analytics will utilize only anonymized, tokenized data via secure enclaves.
*   **Tailored Regulatory Engagement:** A Group Regulatory Affairs Council will deploy Country-Specific Liaisons to proactively manage localized mandates, including CBN recapitalization directives, CBK’s National Payment System Act, and BAM’s dual-language (Arabic/French) ledger requirements.

#### **Pillar 2: Technology Migration**
*Unifying three distinct core banking systems requires an **"API-First, Strangler-Fig"** approach. We will route all new digital traffic through a modern middleware layer, systematically bypassing legacy systems without immediate core replacement.*

*   **Digital Wrapper & Intra-Group Hub (Months 1–18):** We will freeze non-essential legacy core changes and deploy a cloud-native Digital Experience Layer and API Gateway. Concurrently, we will build a unified **intra-group payment hub** to optimize cross-border liquidity and reduce correspondent banking fees.
*   **External National Switch Integration (Months 19–36):** Recognizing the complex negotiation timelines with national telcos and regulators, deep integrations with external switches (NIBSS in Nigeria, M-Pesa Daraja in Kenya, and CMI/HPS in Morocco) will be executed in Phase 3, ensuring realistic compliance and API-access milestones.
*   **Risk-Adjusted Core Consolidation (Months 18–48):** Migration to a single Group Cloud Core will follow a sequenced, risk-adjusted wave approach:
    *   *Wave 1 (Kenya):* Months 18–24. Selected for agile regulation and manageable legacy data.
    *   *Strategic Buffer:* Months 25–27. Dedicated stabilization period to absorb unforeseen delays and prevent cascading schedule impacts.
    *   *Wave 2 (Morocco):* Months 28–36. Focused on corporate/commercial lines and complex dual-language regulatory reporting.
    *   *Wave 3 (Nigeria):* Months 37–48. The highest-volume, most complex migration, requiring extensive parallel-run testing.

#### **Pillar 3: Brand & Product Decisions**
*A unified digital presence must not compromise local market trust. We will deploy a revenue-driver-aligned brand architecture.*

*   **Unified but Localized Digital Products:** We will launch a single Pan-African app architecture with deeply localized UI/UX and feature toggles: USSD fallback and agency banking locators for Nigeria; seamless M-Pesa wallet linking for Kenya; and integrated Zakat calculators and Islamic finance windows for Morocco.
*   **Revenue-Aligned Brand Migration:** 
    *   *Nigeria & Kenya (Retail/SME-led):* Transition the retail and digital brand to *[Group] Bank* first to capture mass-market digital trust, while retaining the acquired brand for large corporate clients in Years 1-2.
    *   *Morocco (Corporate/Wholesale-led):* Transition the corporate and institutional brand immediately to leverage formal relationships, while retaining the local retail brand for up to 3 years to protect the conservative deposit base.
*   **Platform Harmonization:** We will standardize the Loan Origination System (LOS) and customer onboarding engines group-wide, while allowing local product factories to define bespoke credit policies and localized products (e.g., agro-finance, micro-credit).

#### **Pillar 4: Management & Cultural Alignment**
*Nigeria’s scale-driven, Kenya’s agile, and Morocco’s hierarchical cultures require deliberate, structured integration to prevent talent exodus.*

*   **"Two-in-a-Box" with Cultural Translators:** Local Country CEOs will be paired with Group Integration Leads. Crucially, we will appoint "Cultural Translators"—mid-level managers with multi-regional experience—to act as communication shock absorbers between local teams and Group HQ.
*   **IMO with Local Veto Power:** The centralized Integration Management Office (IMO) will grant local Country CEOs veto power over integration tactics that threaten local regulatory compliance or core client relationships, preventing "HQ imperialism."
*   **Targeted Cross-Border Secondments:** Talent will be deployed to close specific strategic gaps: Kenyan digital talent to Nigeria for UX scaling; Nigerian risk/compliance experts to Kenya for corporate book scaling; and Moroccan structured-finance experts to Nigeria and Kenya to elevate institutional capabilities.

### **60-MONTH EXECUTION ROADMAP**

#### **Phase 1: Stabilization & Foundation (Months 1–6)**
*   **Regulatory:** Submit M&A notifications; establish Group Regulatory Council; finalize in-country data sovereignty protocols.
*   **Technology:** Freeze non-essential local IT changes; deploy API Gateway; initiate deep-dive data mapping of legacy cores.
*   **Management:** Stand up the IMO; implement "Two-in-a-Box" leadership; appoint Cultural Translators.
*   **Brand:** Announce acquisition with a "Local Heritage, Group Strength" messaging strategy; retain all local brands.

#### **Phase 2: Digital Unification & API Layer (Months 7–18)**
*   **Technology:** Launch unified Digital Experience Layer and "[Group] One App"; deploy intra-group payment hub.
*   **Brand:** Introduce unified digital brand; begin phasing out local digital branding in Nigeria and Kenya.
*   **Management:** Launch cross-border talent secondments; harmonize HR policies and group-wide KPIs.
*   **Regulatory:** Pass local regulatory audits for new digital channels; secure "No Objection" letters for upcoming core migrations.

#### **Phase 3: Core Migration (Waves 1 & 2) & External Switches (Months 19–36)**
*   **Technology:** Execute Wave 1 (Kenya) core migration; utilize Months 25-27 as a strategic buffer; execute Wave 2 (Morocco) core migration; finalize external national switch integrations (NIBSS, M-Pesa, CMI).
*   **Brand:** Transition retail brand in Nigeria/Kenya; transition corporate brand in Morocco.
*   **Management:** Transition from "Two-in-a-Box" to standard local leadership in Kenya; shift IMO focus from integration to optimization.
*   **Regulatory:** Secure regulatory sign-offs for new core systems in Kenya and Morocco; ensure seamless continuity of dual-language reporting.

#### **Phase 4: Core Migration (Wave 3) & Synergy Realization (Months 37–60)**
*   **Technology:** Execute Wave 3 (Nigeria) core migration; decommission all legacy local cores; activate AI-driven group-wide credit scoring and cross-border liquidity management.
*   **Brand:** Complete transition to a single Pan-African masterbrand across all channels; launch unified corporate and loyalty programs.
*   **Management:** Realize full cultural integration; shift focus to aggressive cross-selling and intra-group trade finance.
*   **Regulatory:** Achieve full compliance with 2026+ Basel IV/local capital requirements; demonstrate systemic stability and financial inclusion benefits to regulators.

### **STRATEGIC GUARDRAILS & RISK MITIGATIONS**

To ensure the success of this integration, the Executive Committee must strictly adhere to the following operational guardrails:

1.  **Regulators are the Ultimate Critical Path:** In African banking, regulatory friction can delay technology migrations by years. Continuous, transparent, and highly localized engagement with the CBN, CBK, and BAM is non-negotiable. *Mitigation: No technology or brand change will be publicly announced before the respective regulator is fully aligned.*
2.  **Protect Local Talent and Relationships:** The primary value of these acquisitions lies in local market knowledge and entrenched client relationships. *Mitigation: By granting local CEOs veto power over disruptive HQ mandates and utilizing "Cultural Translators," we ensure local management feels empowered rather than steamrolled, mitigating the risk of key talent exodus.*
3.  **Respect Infrastructure Realities:** A unified digital application will fail if it ignores local technological infrastructure. *Mitigation: The "Context-Aware" product design ensures mandatory, flawless low-data USSD fallback for Nigeria and seamless M-Pesa API integration for Kenya, ensuring the product works in the reality of the end-user.*
