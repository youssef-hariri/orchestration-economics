**PAN-AFRICAN BANKING GROUP**
**POST-ACQUISITION INTEGRATION PROGRAM**
**Project North Star: Nigeria, Kenya & Morocco Subsidiary Consolidation**

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following the 2026 acquisition of licensed banking entities in Nigeria, Kenya, and Morocco, the Group will execute a **Sovereign-First Platform** integration. Rather than merging three licenses into a single balance sheet, we will abstract unity at the digital product, risk taxonomy, and capital-governance layers while preserving full jurisdictional sovereignty at the data, regulatory, brand, and infrastructure layers.

**Strategic Architecture**
*   **Three Locally Domiciled Technology Subsidiaries (LTS)** in Lagos, Nairobi, and Casablanca satisfy Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN), Central Bank of Kenya (CBK), and Bank Al-Maghrib (BAM) oversight, eliminating fragile cross-border outsourcing structures.
*   **Split-Lead Execution:** Kenya leads digital retail and DevOps template definition (Months 6–20); Nigeria leads corporate treasury and capital-markets convergence (Months 20–40); Morocco leads regulatory compliance, Shariah IT segregation, and Francophone localization (Months 28–48).
*   **Federated Data Sovereignty:** Raw transactional data never leaves its jurisdiction. Three in-country Data Anonymization Engines feed only differentially private aggregates to the Group Insight Layer, satisfying Nigeria’s NDPR, Kenya’s DPA 2019, and Morocco’s Law 05-20 simultaneously.

**Financial & Resource Envelope**
*   **Total Program Budget:** $420 million (60% capex / 40% opex), released via phase-gate triggers.
*   **Hard Currency:** $280 million (USD/EUR) including an 18-month, $95 million USD escrow for Nigeria vendor continuity.
*   **Group Centre Integration Office (GCIO):** 220 FTE at peak (Months 12–36), tapering to 60 FTE by Month 48, organized around Technology, Risk/Regulatory, Finance, and People towers.

**Boundary Conditions**
*   **Minimum Viable Integration (MVI):** Achieved by Month 48 (December 2029)—all markets on unified digital layers with core migration complete in Kenya and Nigeria, and Morocco fully approved with legacy core maintained as statutory archive if required.
*   **Full Convergence:** Month 52 (March 2030)—legacy decommissioned in Kenya and Nigeria; Morocco archive retained per BAM mandate.

## STRATEGIC CONTEXT

The Group’s expansion across Anglophone West Africa, East Africa, and Francophone North Africa creates a “Glocal” imperative: global-scale digital products delivered through locally licensed, linguistically native, and regulatorily compliant entities.

**Guiding Principles**
1.  **Regulatory Sovereignty First:** Each entity remains separately capitalized and licensed. No cross-border data or capital flows occur without explicit regulatory approval.
2.  **Customer Zero-Disruption:** Existing account numbers, branch codes, and digital credentials remain valid. All change is opt-in or invisible.
3.  **Digital-First, Core-Second:** Unify the customer-facing layer before migrating legacy core systems.
4.  **Cultural Translation, Not Imposition:** Group standards are articulated in locally meaningful terms.
5.  **Language as Infrastructure:** Arabic (RTL), French, English, Swahili, Pidgin, and Darija are architectural requirements from Day 1, not localization afterthoughts.

## COUNTRY-SPECIFIC INTEGRATION PLANS

### NIGERIA: Sovereign-Hybrid Convergence
**Lead Domain:** Corporate / Treasury / Capital Markets  
**Timeline:** Months 20–40 (Digital retail from Month 20; Corporate from Month 36)

*   **Regulatory & Capital:** Maintain CAR >20% (well above CBN 15% minimum) throughout integration. Pre-submit the LTS domestic investment notice to CBN pre-close; decouple the core migration notification to avoid related-party scrutiny delays. Secure a CBN comfort letter for the Technical Service Agreement (TSA) and 18-month USD escrow by Month 6.
*   **Technology:** Deploy an **Edge-Hybrid Mesh**—containerized microservices at state-level cluster hubs with asynchronous sync to Lagos primary and Abuja DR sites—to manage grid instability and rural VSAT/4G failover. Migrate in three tranches: (1) digital retail, (2) SME/commercial, (3) corporate/treasury. Maintain legacy core as read-only archive for 24 months post-migration.
*   **Corporate Tail Risk:** The top 100 corporate clients (representing ~$180 million annual revenue) require individual ERP/host-to-host remapping. A **$12 million client retention fund** supports transition bonuses, fee holidays, and dedicated relationship management during Tranche 3.
*   **Brand:** Retain the local legal name on all physical touchpoints indefinitely. Launch **[Group] Naija** as a digital-only skin (Pidgin English, BVN-linked instant onboarding). Full rebrand prohibited unless customer equity research exceeds 60% approval *and* CBN issues a no-objection letter.
*   **Labor:** Negotiate a **Framework Agreement for Integration** with NUBIFIE pre-close. Establish a [Group] Academy for reskilling to digital roles. Offer a Voluntary Early Retirement (VER) window before any compulsory redundancy. No branch rationalization without union sign-off.
*   **FX:** The 18-month USD escrow ($95M) is rebalanced quarterly. If USD/NGN exceeds 1,500, trigger pivot to 100% local vendor substitution for non-core layers.

### KENYA: Digital Template Factory
**Lead Domain:** Retail Digital / DevOps / UX  
**Timeline:** Months 6–20

*   **Regulatory & Capital:** Enter the CBK Regulatory Sandbox at Month 0–3 to pilot the unified digital wallet. Maintain CAR >14.5%. File DPIA with the Data Protection Commissioner. Leverage CBK’s innovation-friendly posture to fast-track approval.
*   **Technology:** Deploy target core alongside legacy in a public-cloud pipeline. Migrate retail and SME digital accounts first. **90-day parallel run** is mandatory; playbook freeze occurs only after two full monthly close cycles and reconciliation variance <0.01%.
*   **Ecosystem Integration:** M-Pesa buygoods, PesaLink, KENSWITCH, and airtime purchase must be embedded on Day 1. API latency must remain <200ms; app-store rating must exceed 4.2 for 90 consecutive days before any masterbrand branch signage pilot.
*   **Brand:** Most receptive to pan-African identity. Pilot **[Group] Kenya** branch signage in five Nairobi locations at Month 18 (performance-gated). Full masterbrand rollout by Month 30 if NPS remains stable.
*   **Labor:** Consult BIFU/KUDHEIHA under the Employment Act 2007. Redeploy branch staff to agency banking networks (per CBK 2010 rules) rather than terminate.
*   **Strategic Role:** Nairobi serves as the Group’s **East African Digital Centre of Excellence**. Kenyan engineers export standards; they do not serve as a laboratory for other markets.

### MOROCCO: Sovereign Private Cloud & Francophone Compliance
**Lead Domain:** Regulatory / Shariah IT / Francophone Product  
**Timeline:** Months 28–48 (Formal BAM submission at Month 8)

*   **Regulatory & Capital:** Maintain CAR >14% (BAM is unforgiving on shortfalls). Secure a pre-close **note de principe** from BAM Inspection Générale via GPBM. Submit the formal IT change package in French and Arabic at **Month 8** to accommodate an 18–24 month inspection cycle. All documentation submitted via GPBM; English-only materials are prohibited.
*   **Technology:** Deploy a **Kubernetes-based Sovereign Private Cloud** in Casablanca/Rabat (on-premise Tier-III DC) with BAM source-code escrow and encryption key escrow within jurisdiction. Native RTL Arabic UX (not CSS mirroring), Hijri calendar logic for Islamic profit distribution, and trilingual support: French (corporate), Modern Standard Arabic (Islamic/formal), Darija (chatbot/SMS). Isolate Islamic finance microservices in a separate namespace/cluster with distinct backup regimes to avoid *riba* contamination.
*   **Migration Sequence:** By product line—Retail Islamic first (smallest book, highest scrutiny), then retail conventional, then corporate. If Shariah Supervisory Board rejects profit-calculation segregation, rebuild and resubmit before expansion.
*   **Brand:** Retain the local *raison sociale* bearing “Banque” indefinitely; BAM and Francophone/Arabic identity demand it. Launch **[Group] Maroc** digital skin only. The Islamic window operates under a distinct endorsed sub-brand (e.g., **[Local Name] Islamic**) with segregated MSA/French terms and imagery.
*   **Labor:** The *Comité d’Entreprise* (CE) must be consulted on all organizational changes. Avoid selective “golden handcuffs” that violate *Code du Travail* equality principles. Use **Group-wide profit-sharing (*intéressement*)** covering the top three management tiers. Redundancy is prohibitively expensive; prefer internal mobility (*mobilité interne*), reskilling (*validation des acquis*), and natural attrition.
*   **Statutory Titles:** Retain **Directeur Général (DG)** as the sole legally recognized title under Moroccan corporate law. Never impose “Managing Director,” which creates ambiguity in BAM filings and the *Registre du Commerce*.

## TECHNOLOGY MIGRATION SEQUENCING

**Philosophy:** The “Strangler Fig” model—wrapping legacy cores with a cloud-native digital layer and migrating back-books in sequenced waves.

### Infrastructure Abstraction Layer (IAL)
To prevent unification at the application layer but fragmentation at the infrastructure layer, Group Centre mandates:
*   **GitOps Pipeline:** Flux/ArgoCD with identical Helm charts across all three substrates.
*   **Observability Stack:** Standardized metrics, logging, and tracing; local SRE teams operate distinct runbooks.
*   **Payment Adapters:** Country-specific NIBSS, M-Pesa, and SICG adapters sit within each LTS, not in a shared Group layer. The Group layer sees only normalized post-settlement events.

### Split-Lead Wave Sequence

| Wave | Market | Timing | Core Methodology |
|------|--------|--------|------------------|
| **Wave 1** | **Kenya** | Months 6–20 | Public-cloud target core alongside legacy. 90-day parallel run. Serves as Group DevOps and UX template factory. |
| **Wave 2** | **Nigeria** | Months 20–40 | Edge-hybrid mesh. Tranche migration: digital retail → SME → corporate/treasury. Client-by-client integration tracks for top 100 corporates. |
| **Wave 3** | **Morocco** | Months 28–48 | Sovereign private cloud. Product-line migration: Islamic retail → conventional retail → corporate. On-premise K8s with BAM escrow. |

### Interim Integration Layer (Months 0–24)
An **Enterprise Integration Bus (EIB)** deployed in-country only (Lagos, Nairobi, Casablanca), connected by encrypted point-to-point links. The EIB is temporary 24-month scaffolding; legacy core API contracts are frozen at Month 6 with zero new feature development.

### Rollback Protocols
*   **Kenya:** If customer attrition >3% or reconciliation variance >0.01% at Day 60 of parallel run, extend to 120 days or freeze playbook.
*   **Nigeria:** If NIBSS dark-launch reconciliation fails for 5 consecutive days, revert digital traffic to legacy via pre-staged DNS.
*   **Morocco:** If BAM inspection finds critical IT governance gaps at Month 30, halt and remediate in dev environment before pilot branch deployment.

## MANAGEMENT ALIGNMENT & CULTURE

### Governance: Federal-Unitary Hybrid
*   **Group Centre** sets non-negotiables: risk appetite, OpenAPI standards, cybersecurity baselines, brand guidelines, and capital allocation. Houses the GCIO.
*   **Country Heads** retain full P&L ownership, local regulatory relations, and staffing autonomy.
*   **Hub Council:** Chaired annually on a rotating basis by the Nigeria MD, Kenya MD, or Morocco DG. Advises on capital allocation; Group Centre retains budget authority to prevent fiefdoms.
*   **Joint OKRs:** Weighted 50% local P&L and 50% Group platform contribution (playbook exports, cross-border revenue).

### Group Centre Integration Office (GCIO)
| Tower | Peak FTE | Responsibility |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| Technology & Data | 55 | IAL governance; playbook freeze authority; sprint rollback decisions |
| Risk & Regulatory | 45 | Three autonomous regulatory pods; comfort letter acquisition |
| Finance & Capital | 35 | Phase-gate budget release; FX escrow; transfer pricing defense |
| People & Culture | 30 | Mobility charter; succession; union/CE oversight |
| Program Management | 20 | IMO orchestration; executive escalation |
| Country Integration Directors | 3×12 | Embedded in-country IMOs; solid line to GCIO |

### Talent Mobility & Immigration
*   **Nigeria ↔ Kenya:** 12-month secondments under ICT visa frameworks.
*   **Morocco ↔ Others:** Limited to 90-day business visas and remote bridge teams from Casablanca to avoid ANAPEC *Titre de Travail* quotas and 30% foreign worker caps. Permanent relocation capped at 2–3 critical executives per direction.
*   **Group Mobility Policy:** Tax gross-up, housing allowances, repatriation guarantees. Rotations capped at 24 months to avoid permanent establishment tax risks.

### Cultural Integration: “The Group Way—Three Expressions”
Five Group values (Integrity, Customer Obsession, Excellence, Collaboration, Accountability) are codified, with each country defining behavioral anchors in local context. Mandatory cross-cultural training for all senior leaders.

### Local Succession Roadmap
*   **Months 0–12:** Assessment of high-potential local successors.
*   **Months 12–36:** Successors lead sprint workstreams.
*   **Months 36–42:** Shadow P&L accountability.
*   **Months 42–48:** Promotion to Country Head/DG; Group Centre shifts to platform governance.

## IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP

*Theme: Do No Harm; Fund the Future*

*   Deploy in-country EIB/API gateways; complete Group cybersecurity baseline audit
*   Submit integration notifications to CBN, CBK, BAM; file DPIAs in Kenya and Morocco
*   Confirm Country Heads; launch IMO (50/50 veteran/Group staff); convene inaugural Hub Council
*   Sign NUBIFIE Framework Agreement and CE consultation MOUs
*   **Gate 1 (Month 6):** All LTS operational; CBN comfort letter in hand; BAM pre-submission completed; escrow funded

### Phase II: Digital Unification (Months 6–20)
*Theme: One Platform, Three Faces*
*   **Kenya:** Complete target-core migration with 90-day parallel run; freeze playbook at Month 20
*   **Nigeria & Morocco:** Deploy digital wrapper over legacy cores; launch localized app skins ([Group] Pesa / Naija / Maroc)
*   Kenya tests masterbrand pilot in select Nairobi branches (performance-gated); Nigeria and Morocco remain fully endorsed
*   Begin 12-month secondments; launch unified HRIS
*   **Gate 2 (Month 20):** Kenya customer attrition <3%; NPS stable; playbook signed off; Nigeria LTS fully staffed

### Phase III: Core Convergence (Months 20–48)
*Theme: Systems Merge; Clients Stay*
*   **Nigeria:** Tranche migration (retail → SME → corporate/treasury). Corporate Client Integration Tracks for top 100 clients.
*   **Morocco:** Sovereign private cloud build; BAM formal approval targeted Month 28; Islamic retail pilot → conventional retail → corporate.

*   Integrate back-office functions via LTS contracts (procurement, Group SOC).
*   **Gate 3 (Month 28):** Nigeria retail dark-launch reconciliation passing; BAM formal approval received; Morocco source-code escrow signed.

### Phase IV: Optimization & Scale (Months 40–52)
*Theme: One Platform, Local Souls*
*   Decommission legacy cores in Kenya and Nigeria; maintain Moroccan legacy as BAM-mandated statutory archive if required.
*   Group Centre transitions from integration command to platform governance; execute local succession to Country Head/DG roles.
