**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Supply Chain Management Team
**FROM:** Global Operations Strategy
**DATE:** May 22, 2024
**SUBJECT:** Strategic Framework: Regional Supplier Onboarding & Integration

Following the strategic shift of our supply base to Mexico, Morocco, and Vietnam, we have moved away from a "command-and-control" model. We are adopting a **Regional Partnership Framework** designed to minimize quality drift, ensure communication reliability, and cultivate long-term institutional trust.

### 1. Regional Integration Profiles
Success in these regions requires tailoring our management style to local operational and cultural realities.

| Region | Strategic Driver | Key Onboarding Protocol | Operational Buffer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mexico** | *Confianza* (Relational) | On-site residency + Labor retention audit. | Account for local turnover in 6-month ramp-ups. |
| **Morocco** | Hierarchy & Status | SOPs as "Quality Certification" (European alignment). | 10% timeline buffer for bureaucratic processes. |
| **Vietnam** | Consensus & Growth | "Back-briefing" (intent-based communication). | Align production to Lunar New Year cycle. |

---

### 2. Standardized Communication & Accountability
To bridge the gap between our specifications and local execution, we are instituting three non-negotiable protocols:

*   **"Early Transparency" Policy (No-Penalty Amnesty):** To prevent the concealment of issues, we are shifting from a punitive model to an incentivized one. Suppliers who report potential delays or defects via our "Early Warning Channel" within 2 hours of discovery will face zero penalty. We prioritize proactive notification over reactive correction.
*   **The "Back-Briefing" Requirement:** To resolve the "Yes" trap (where local teams agree to requests out of courtesy or deference despite misunderstanding), all technical briefings must conclude with the supplier lead restating the project intent—not just the data points—in their own words.
*   **Local Facilitation:** All "Expectation Alignment Workshops" will be moderated by independent, local supply-chain consultants. This removes the "outsider lecturing" dynamic and ensures our requirements are translated into local business logic.

---

### 3. Execution Roadmap

1.  **Phase I: The Bi-Directional Pre-Flight**
    Before the first purchase order, conduct an "Expectation Exchange." Instead of presenting only our requirements, we will ask: *"What operational challenges in your local environment should we be aware of to help you succeed?"* This frames the relationship as a true partnership.
2.  **Phase II: Regional Calendar Audit**
    All Master Production Schedules (MPS) must explicitly overlay local religious and public holidays. Production commitments made during these windows are prohibited unless specifically authorized by regional management.
3.  **Phase III: Strategic Knowledge Transfer**
    Once operations stabilize, we will initiate cross-regional workshops. We will task our Mexican teams to share their relational management techniques with Moroccan counterparts, and our Vietnamese teams to train Mexican hubs on Kaizen/Collaborative Problem Solving.

---

### 4. Defining Success
We are shifting our definition of success. A high-performing supplier is no longer one that simply meets a KPI. **Success is defined as the "Cycle of Collaboration":** the point at which a supplier autonomously identifies a process improvement and presents it to HQ.

**Next Steps:**
*   Supply Chain Leads are to schedule regional "Pre-Flight" workshops for all new suppliers by EOM.
*   The Procurement team is to distribute the updated "No-Penalty Reporting" policy to all regional vendor portals.
