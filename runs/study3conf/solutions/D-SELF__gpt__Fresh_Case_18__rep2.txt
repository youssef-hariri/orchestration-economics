# Global Health-Feature Rollout Strategy  
## Executive decision deliverable — 2026

## Executive summary

Approve a **foundation-first, market-specific rollout program**, not an unconditional global launch.

The company should establish one global backbone for quality, evidence, privacy, cybersecurity, claims governance, software lifecycle, and post-market surveillance. Product configurations, intended uses, claims, user journeys, and release controls should then vary by market according to local classification and risk.

### Recommended sequence

1. **2026 — Foundation and regulatory clarification**
   - Confirm product boundaries, intended use, classification hypotheses, clinical endpoints, and claims.
   - Engage EU, Japan, and US regulatory advisers and authorities as appropriate.
   - Build quality, clinical, privacy, cybersecurity, claims, and surveillance capabilities.
   - Define the five additional health capabilities before approving their development budgets.

2. **First regulated reference markets — selected EU countries and Japan**
   - Proceed only after the applicable conformity, authorization, evidence, care-pathway, labeling, support, and post-market requirements are complete.
   - Use controlled clinical-workflow pilots or limited launches before broader consumer distribution.

3. **United States — FDA-informed decision path**
   - Do not commit to a medical launch until classification, intended use, evidence, and economics are sufficiently clear.
   - Maintain 510(k), De Novo, narrowed-use, genuinely nonmedical, defer, and terminate contingencies.

4. **Emerging markets — selective, genuinely nonmedical deployment**
   - Activate only where the full experience is nonmedical and not misleading, local claims are supportable, privacy and support requirements are understood, and users have an appropriate next step if concerned.
   - Do not treat wellness positioning as risk-free or as a substitute for evidence.

5. **Future capabilities — independent stage gates**
   - Sequence each capability according to its own clinical risk, evidence requirements, regulatory pathway, care pathway, privacy burden, operational complexity, and commercial value.

### Governing rule

> Launch only where the exact intended use and claims are authorized or legally supportable, clinically substantiated, understandable to users, connected to a realistic next step, and monitorable after release.

### Approved now

- Foundation, evidence, and regulatory-clarification work;
- Clinical and workflow pilots;
- Market and care-pathway assessments;
- Medical/nonmedical configuration controls;
- Portfolio definition for the five additional capabilities.

### Not approved yet

- A fixed global launch date;
- Broad disease-oriented consumer marketing;
- A US medical launch before FDA pathway clarification;
- Disease-risk functionality in emerging markets based only on disclaimers;
- Full development budgets for the five unspecified capabilities;
- Claims that the feature diagnoses, rules out, treats, or monitors sleep apnea.

# 1. Strategic principles

These principles are recommended best practices and remain subject to local legal and regulatory confirmation.

1. **Classification follows the total presentation.**  
   Intended use, interface, result language, app-store content, advertising, customer support, influencer activity, clinician materials, and partner communications all matter—not only the algorithm.

2. **Separate four decisions.**  
   Regulatory classification, clinical validity, clinical utility, and advertising permissibility must be assessed independently.

3. **Use one evidence backbone with local overlays.**  
   A global study and shared platform evidence may be reusable, but they do not automatically satisfy every regulator or market.

4. **Make medical and nonmedical configurations genuinely distinct.**  
   Replacing “screening” with “wellness” is insufficient if the product still generates disease-directed outputs or behavior.

5. **Care access is a launch criterion.**  
   A disease-oriented result should lead to a realistic and appropriate next step. Markets without meaningful follow-up options may be unsuitable for disease-directed deployment.

6. **Evidence must precede claims and claims must precede promotion.**

7. **Every clinically meaningful output must be traceable** to the market, device, sensor configuration, firmware, algorithm version, threshold, data pipeline, and wording shown.

8. **Algorithm, firmware, sensor, hardware, threshold, labeling, and intended-use changes require impact assessment.**

9. **Post-market surveillance begins before launch.**

10. **Wellness positioning does not eliminate risk.**  
    Privacy, advertising, consumer-protection, product-safety, cybersecurity, and liability obligations may still apply.

# 3. Product and claims architecture

## 3.1 Draft medical intended use

The following is **draft language for regulatory evaluation only**, not approved global labeling:

> The feature analyzes specified wearable-derived signals to indicate that the user may have findings associated with an increased likelihood of sleep-disordered breathing and recommends consultation with a qualified healthcare professional. It is not intended to diagnose or rule out sleep apnea, replace clinical testing, monitor treatment, or guide treatment decisions.

Each market must determine whether the appropriate claim is:

- General sleep information;
- Risk indication;
- Screening;
- Detection;
- Referral support;
- Another regulated purpose.

These terms are not interchangeable.

## 3.2 Product-boundary assessment

Document the relationship among:

- Wearable hardware and sensors;
- Embedded firmware;
- Mobile application;
- Cloud processing;
- Algorithms and thresholds;
- Clinician-facing reports;
- APIs and data exports;
- Accessories;
- Future device generations.

The assessment should identify which component performs the health function and how changes to another component could affect the validated system.

## 3.3 Claims ladder

| Claim level | Illustrative wording | Deployment rule |
|---|---|---|
| General sleep insight | “Understand your sleep patterns.” | Potentially available subject to local review |
| General education | “Learn about sleep habits.” | Use only if genuinely nonmedical |
| Risk indication | “May indicate an increased likelihood of sleep-disordered breathing.” | Requires appropriate evidence and local support |
| Screening | “Screens for possible sleep apnea.” | Only where specifically authorized and substantiated |
| Diagnosis | “Diagnoses sleep apnea.” | Not planned |
| Treatment monitoring | “Determines whether treatment is working.” | Separate high-risk program |

Maintain a version-controlled claims library by market, configuration, algorithm version, intended user, and channel. It must govern packaging, app stores, onboarding, result screens, advertising, social media, influencers, customer support, clinician materials, distributor materials, press releases, and investor communications.

Every external claim should have a substantiation record containing:

- Exact wording;
- Audience and channel;
- Applicable market;
- Supporting evidence;
- Limitations;
- Approval owner;
- Review or expiry date.

Terms such as **screening, detection, risk, clinical-grade, validated, accurate, early warning, monitoring, normal, safe, prevention,** and **low risk** require claim-specific clinical, regulatory, and legal review.

## Phase 1 — European reference markets

The EU should be treated as a potential regulated reference region, not one uniform commercial market.

### Required regulatory work

Obtain written advice addressing:

- Applicable EU MDR classification and software analysis;
- Product boundaries across wearable, app, cloud, algorithm, and clinician report;
- Intended users and clinical recipients;
- Conformity-assessment route;
- Notified-body involvement;
- Clinical evaluation and potential formal clinical-investigation requirements;
- Usability, risk management, cybersecurity, and technical documentation;
- Post-market surveillance and post-market clinical follow-up;
- Economic operators, importers, distributors, and labeling;
- Implications of algorithm, firmware, hardware, sensor, and threshold changes.

Use precise terminology. Depending on the product and classification, obligations may include conformity assessment, an EU declaration of conformity, CE marking, registration, labeling, vigilance, and other market-placement requirements. These are not necessarily a centralized governmental authorization.

### Beachhead-country selection

Select two or three countries based on:

- Regulatory readiness;
- Confirmatory-testing availability;
- Sleep-clinic capacity;
- Primary-care workflow;
- Clinician acceptance;
- Referral completion;
- Language and support burden;
- Reimbursement or care-pathway potential;
- Privacy and hosting requirements;
- Commercial value;
- Evidence-generation value.

Begin with a clinical-workflow pilot or limited launch. Expand only after structured 30-, 90-, and 180-day reviews.

### Local confirmation

Notified-body and local legal review are required for classification, product boundaries, intended use, clinical evidence, conformity assessment, significant changes, advertising, healthcare-professional communications, and country activation.

## Phase 3 — United States decision path

The US should be managed through explicit options and decision dates, not a fixed launch promise.

### FDA pre-submission topics

Seek feedback on:

- Classification;
- 510(k), De Novo, or another pathway;
- Predicate suitability, if relevant;
- Intended-use wording;
- Target condition and severity;
- Reference standard;
- Clinical endpoints;
- Study population and prevalence;
- False-reassurance risks;
- Human factors;
- Cybersecurity;
- Software lifecycle;
- Algorithm and threshold changes;
- Clinical-utility expectations.

FDA feedback is advisory and does not guarantee clearance or acceptance.

### US options

| Option | Description | Continue if |
|---|---|---|
| 510(k) | Predicate-based strategy | Predicate, evidence, timing, cost, and substantial-equivalence strategy are supportable |
| De Novo | New classification for a novel moderate-risk device | Special controls and investment are commercially justified |
| Genuinely nonmedical configuration | Disease-directed functionality and presentation are removed or constrained | The complete presentation is supportable after legal and regulatory review |
| Defer, narrow, or terminate | Change intended use or pause the program | Requirements exceed approved thresholds |

Set in advance:

- FDA pre-submission and feedback-review dates;
- Intended-use and study-design lock dates;
- Continue/narrow/defer/terminate date;
- Maximum incremental budget;
- Maximum acceptable delay;
- Minimum viable US product and claim;
- Minimum expected commercial value.

If FDA feedback is partial, delayed, or inconclusive, do not infer clearance. Convene a cross-functional review to decide whether to seek additional consultation, narrow the claim, continue evidence generation, or defer.

Any research-only US deployment must be separated from commercial availability and promotional claims, with appropriate privacy, ethics, participant-communication, and data-use controls.

# 6. Validation and investment priorities

## 6.1 Evidence layers

### Analytical and platform validation

For all configurations, assess:

- Sensor and signal quality;
- Data completeness and unusable-result rates;
- Device fit and movement;
- Device generations and firmware;
- Repeated-night performance;
- Skin tones, ages, sexes, body types, positions, and comorbidities;
- Failure modes;
- Software verification and validation;
- Cybersecurity, privacy, and data lineage.

### Clinical validity

Before study lock, define:

- Target condition;
- Clinically meaningful severity threshold;
- Intended population;
- Reference standard;
- Primary and secondary endpoints;
- Prevalence and sampling strategy;
- Sample-size rationale;
- Missing-data rules;
- Subgroup plan;
- Model-lock process;
- Statistical analysis plan.

Polysomnography and home sleep-apnea testing should not be treated as interchangeable without scientific and regulatory justification. Specify handling of inadequate tests, discordant results, and night-to-night variability.

Use prospective, multi-site evidence where appropriate, with separate training, tuning, validation, and locked-test datasets. Report sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, AUC, calibration, confidence intervals, subgroup performance, and indeterminate rates.

### Human factors and clinical utility

Test whether users and clinicians:

- Understand positive, negative, and inconclusive results;
- Do not interpret a negative result as ruling out disease;
- Select an appropriate next step;
- Understand uncertainty and limitations;
- Avoid unsafe delay or unnecessary alarm;
- Can use reports in relevant languages and accessibility formats.

Measure:

- Referral completion;
- Confirmatory-test completion;
- Confirmatory-test yield;
- Unnecessary-referral burden;
- Time to appropriate follow-up;
- User anxiety or harm;
- Clinician acceptance;
- Healthcare-system burden.

## 6.2 Claim-specific evidence table

Before pivotal-study lock, link each claim to:

- Target condition and severity;
- Population;
- Reference standard;
- Sampling design;
- Primary endpoint;
- Required performance and precision;
- Required subgroups;
- Human-factors endpoint;
- Clinical-utility endpoint;
- Intended markets;
- Post-market evidence requirement.

Thresholds must be approved and frozen by clinical, statistical, regulatory, quality, privacy, and commercial leadership before pivotal results are unblinded.

## 6.3 Indicative investment priorities

For the first 18 months, an indicative allocation is:

| Priority | Indicative share |
|---|---:|
| Clinical studies and biostatistics | 35% |
| Regulatory, quality, and technical documentation | 20% |
| Safety, cybersecurity, privacy, and human factors | 15% |
| PMS and data systems | 10% |
| Medical affairs and clinical-community programs | 10% |
| Consumer transparency and comprehension research | 10% |

These are planning assumptions, not approved budgets. Additional US or portfolio funding should require a formal value, cost, timing, and risk review.

# 7. Credibility-building plan

## Clinicians and clinical institutions

### External medical advisory board

Include sleep physicians, primary-care clinicians, respiratory specialists, biostatisticians, patient representatives, ethics expertise, and health-equity expertise.

The charter should address:

- Selection criteria;
- Expertise;
- Compensation;
- Conflicts of interest;
- Independence;
- Publication rights;
- Use of names and logos;
- Promotional restrictions.

### Clinical workflow pilots

Use selected EU and Japanese partners to:

- Test clinician reports;
- Assess interpretation and trust;
- Establish referral protocols;
- Measure confirmatory-test completion;
- Identify workflow and liability concerns;
- Generate clinical-utility evidence.

Separate research, advisory, implementation, and promotional relationships. Do not imply endorsement by a clinician, hospital, academic institution, or professional society without explicit, locally compliant permission.

### Medical education

Provide clinicians with:

- Study methods and limitations;
- Positive, negative, and inconclusive-result guidance;
- Subgroup performance;
- Confirmatory-testing recommendations;
- Sample reports;
- Device and algorithm-version information;
- Warnings against treating the output as a diagnosis.

## Consumers

Each result should explain:

1. What was measured;
2. What the result may suggest;
3. What it cannot determine;
4. The recommended next step;
5. Factors affecting reliability;
6. How to share the report;
7. When urgent symptoms require medical attention rather than reliance on the feature.

Use consumer research to test comprehension of qualitative and numerical risk. Provide clear privacy, retention, sharing, and deletion controls.

Create a remediation process for:

- Materially incorrect results;
- Changed thresholds;
- Model rollback;
- Previously viewed, exported, printed, cached, or shared reports;
- Clinicians who received affected reports;
- Users who did not reconnect;
- Devices operating offline.

Publications, clinical partnerships, advisory participation, certifications, and conference activity are voluntary credibility practices. They do not substitute for regulatory authorization or conformity.

# 10. Algorithm, hardware, and software change governance

Every clinically meaningful output must be traceable to:

- Device model;
- Sensor configuration;
- Firmware;
- Algorithm version;
- Threshold;
- Data-processing pipeline;
- Market;
- Displayed wording;
- Referral instructions.

Classify changes as:

1. Cosmetic or nonclinical;
2. Performance-neutral;
3. Performance-affecting within validated bounds;
4. Clinically meaningful or intended-use changing.

Categories 3 and 4 require formal impact assessment and may require revalidation, updated risk management, technical-documentation changes, regulator or notified-body engagement, amended labeling, or a new submission.

Do not silently deploy adaptive or continuously learning models.

A suspension or kill-switch process must address:

- Results already viewed;
- Exported, printed, cached, or shared reports;
- Offline devices;
- Users who do not reconnect;
- Inventory and products in transit;
- Regulatory notification;
- Corrective action and user communication.

Test geographic controls against travel, VPNs, account-country mismatch, app-store settings, family accounts, and device resale.

# 12. No-go, pause, and pivot criteria

## Pre-launch no-go

Do not launch if:

- Classification is materially unresolved;
- Claims exceed evidence or authorization;
- False reassurance is not adequately mitigated;
- Follow-up care is unavailable or unrealistic;
- Required clinical evidence is incomplete;
- Subgroup performance is unknown or materially inadequate;
- Traceability is incomplete;
- PMS, privacy, cybersecurity, or support controls are untested;
- Local-language support is inadequate;
- Partners cannot be controlled;
- Launch volume exceeds support or monitoring capacity.

## Post-launch pause or rollback

Investigate, warn, roll back, or suspend if:

- Serious or unexpected adverse events occur;
- False-positive, false-negative, or indeterminate rates exceed predefined limits;
- Material subgroup disparities emerge;
- A model, threshold, firmware, hardware, or sensor change affects validated performance;
- User misunderstanding exceeds predefined limits;
- Appropriate follow-up is not occurring;
- Regulators challenge classification, claims, or labeling;
- A significant privacy or cybersecurity incident occurs;
- Unauthorized partner claims recur;
- Previously delivered results require correction and remediation cannot be completed.

## US-specific pivots

Reassess the US program if:

- FDA requires a materially different intended use;
- A different reference standard or endpoint is required;
- A substantially larger clinical program is needed;
- The pathway exceeds the approved cost or timing envelope;
- Claims are narrowed below the minimum commercially viable product;
- The global study design becomes unsuitable;
- FDA feedback remains materially inconclusive after planned consultation.

Potential pivots include narrowing the intended use, conducting additional consultation, maintaining only a genuinely nonmedical configuration, deferring the medical launch, using a separate medical identity, reallocating resources, or terminating the program.

## Critical path

1. Intended use and product boundaries;
2. Reference standard and clinical protocol;
3. EU, Japan, and US regulatory feedback;
4. Clinical-site planning and recruitment;
5. Data lock and statistical analysis;
6. Quality-system and technical-documentation readiness;
7. Local representation and labeling;
8. PMS, support, and referral readiness;
9. Controlled pilot or limited launch;
10. Post-launch continuation decision.

## Timing posture

Use readiness windows rather than public calendar commitments:

- **Earliest case:** only if classification, evidence, local obligations, support, care pathways, PMS, rollback, and remediation are complete.
- **Base case:** includes time for recruitment, regional bridging, authority questions, translation, remediation, and operational scaling.
- **Downside case:** assumes additional clinical work, divergent regional requirements, subgroup remediation, QMS gaps, poor referral completion, or insufficient support capacity.

Do not publish launch dates until the critical path, capacity model, and regulatory assumptions are documented.

## Final recommendation

Approve the **foundation, evidence, and regulatory-clarification program**.

Do not yet approve:

- A fixed global launch date;
- Broad medical claims;
- A US medical launch before FDA clarification;
- Disease-oriented wellness deployment without local confirmation;
- Full funding or sequencing for the five unspecified capabilities.

The company’s scalable model should be:
