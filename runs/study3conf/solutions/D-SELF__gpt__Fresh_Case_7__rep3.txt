# 2026 International Launch Strategy  
## Culturally differentiated, safety-governed AI emotional-wellbeing companion

## 1. Executive summary

Launch through a **phased, country-by-country program**, not a simultaneous four-region rollout.

The product should be positioned globally as:

> **An AI wellbeing companion for reflection, practical coping, and finding human support—not a therapist, doctor, diagnostician, religious authority, or emergency service.**

The strategy combines:

1. **One global safety and privacy foundation**
2. **Country-specific language, regulatory, safeguarding, and referral plans**
3. **User-selected tone, directness, modality, interaction mode, values content, memory, and sharing**
4. **Independent local review before public launch**
5. **Binding authority to pause a market, partner, or feature**

The four regions are useful planning groupings, but they are not homogeneous markets. All regional recommendations below are **launch hypotheses requiring country-, segment-, and language-level validation**.

### Strategic priorities

- Build trust through honest limits, not simulated intimacy.
- Make human support and care navigation central product features.
- Treat family involvement as optional; never assume family is safe.
- Offer religiously neutral support, with values or faith-compatible content only by user choice.
- Adapt language, tone, disclosure style, accessibility, and referral pathways locally.
- Do not optimize retention or engagement at the expense of privacy, safety, professional care, or healthy disengagement.

## 2. Cross-market principles

### 2.1 Global product promise and boundaries

The companion may provide:

- Reflective listening
- General wellbeing information
- Low-risk grounding, breathing, sleep, and routine exercises
- Journaling and structured reflection
- Help organizing thoughts
- Preparation for conversations with clinicians or trusted people
- Local professional, community, and crisis-resource navigation
- User-controlled summaries for voluntary sharing

It must not:

- Diagnose, treat, or clinically monitor mental-health conditions
- Recommend, change, or discontinue medication
- Claim to prevent suicide or guarantee safety
- Present itself as therapy or a substitute for professional care
- Encourage withdrawal from treatment, family, friends, or community
- Issue religious rulings or claim spiritual authority
- Promise absolute confidentiality
- Claim to have contacted emergency services unless it actually has
- Advise confrontation or disclosure in abuse situations where this could increase danger
- Use dependency-oriented language such as “I need you” or “I am your best friend”

Required disclosure, or a locally reviewed equivalent:

> “I’m an AI wellbeing tool. I can help you reflect and try practical coping steps, but I’m not a therapist or emergency service. I may misunderstand you, and human support may be more appropriate.”

The same restrictions apply to product copy, app stores, advertising, influencers, employers, universities, public agencies, and referral partners.

### 2.2 User autonomy

Users should control:

- Tool, coaching, or conversational mode
- Tone and degree of directness
- Text, audio, or voice interaction
- Reminder frequency and quiet hours
- Whether values or spirituality are included
- Memory and personalization
- Whether another person is involved
- What is shared
- What is retained and deleted

The system must not infer that family, religion, gender presentation, or a communication style is appropriate solely from location, language, age, or profile data.

### 2.3 What to localize—and what not to localize

| Localize | Do not localize downward |
|---|---|
| Language, dialect, script, formality, pronouns | AI identity and limitations |
| Emotional vocabulary, idioms, humor, and sarcasm | No diagnosis, treatment, medication, or cure claims |
| Self-harm, abuse, grief, and crisis terminology | User-controlled memory and deletion |
| Voice, accent, transcription, and modality | No covert family, employer, school, insurer, or authority access |
| Notification and discreet-mode design | No emotional-data advertising or consequential profiling |
| Pricing, payment, bandwidth, and access | Family is not presumed safe |
| Healthcare and crisis navigation | Religion is not presumed relevant or authoritative |
| Age, consent, and safeguarding procedures | Human support remains available |
| Accessibility implementation | Crisis exceptions are narrow, disclosed, and legally reviewed |
| Optional values or spirituality settings | Safety standards cannot be weakened for growth |

### 2.4 Trust and privacy commitments

At onboarding, explain plainly:

- What data is collected
- Whether conversations and voice are stored
- Whether content is used for model improvement
- Whether humans may review safety events
- How long data is retained
- How deletion and export work
- Whether data crosses borders
- What family, employers, schools, insurers, and authorities can and cannot access
- What may happen in a narrowly defined emergency

Obtain separate, revocable consent for:

- Conversation storage
- Memory and personalization
- Voice processing
- Research or model improvement
- Human safety review
- Sharing
- Notifications
- Messaging, wearable, or other integrations

Basic wellbeing use and crisis navigation must not require consent to advertising, research, persistent memory, or payment.

## 3. Regional comparison

| Region | Positioning hypothesis | Primary adoption opportunity | Key trust concern | Initial product emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **East Asia** | Discreet support for stress, sleep, focus, work, and study | Low-pressure digital self-management | School, employer, family, and social surveillance | Structured check-ins, stress/sleep tools, formality controls |
| **Middle East** | Private, respectful support for emotional balance and life challenges | Privacy-sensitive access and care navigation | Unsafe disclosure, religious overreach, family or device control | Discreet mode, dialect support, optional values settings |
| **Latin America** | Warm, affordable support that complements human relationships | Mobile-first access where professional care is uneven | Cost, data misuse, violence, and false intimacy | Low-bandwidth/audio access, affordability, human-support sharing |
| **Northern Europe** | Transparent, evidence-informed wellbeing and care navigation | Prevention and support while awaiting care | Commercial AI surveillance and unsupported claims | Privacy center, accessibility, evidence library, user control |

These are hypotheses, not universal cultural conclusions.

# 4. Regional strategies

## A. East Asia

### Scope and assumptions

Treat **Japan, South Korea, mainland China, Hong Kong, and Taiwan** as separate launch cells. Their regulatory, platform, language, healthcare, and data-governance environments differ. Assess Singapore separately if included.

**Assumption requiring validation:** Some users may prefer entering through stress, sleep, work, study, or focus rather than clinical mental-health language. Test this against direct mental-health framing by country, age, and use case.

### Positioning

> “A discreet daily space to manage stress, sleep, focus, and emotional balance.”

Example:

> “Sort through the day, at your own pace.”

Do not assume users prefer indirect communication. Offer both practical and direct entry points.

### Priority segments

- University students and early-career workers
- Urban professionals experiencing work stress or sleep disruption
- Caregivers and middle-aged users
- Parents seeking guidance for adolescents, without default surveillance

### Feature adaptation

Provide:

- Structured check-ins before open-ended conversation
- Stress, sleep, study, work, and focus modules
- Formality and honorific controls
- Text-first interaction
- Optional voice and audio
- Calm, clearly AI, non-anthropomorphic default design
- Tool, coaching, and conversational modes
- Private journaling without an immediate response
- Neutral notifications
- Strong separation from school and employer analytics

Test direct and indirect prompts, avatars, gendered voices, and humanlike interaction by segment.

### Family, privacy, and safeguarding

- Provide parent guidance separately from adolescent conversation content.
- Allow sharing with friends, clinicians, teachers, mentors, or family.
- Ask whether the selected person is safe and supportive.
- Do not assume parental access is appropriate for adolescents or adults.
- Protect against employer, school, and family surveillance.
- Apply country-specific minor-consent and safeguarding rules.
- Do not assume family or emergency-service involvement is safe.

### Localization

Support Japanese, Korean, simplified Chinese, traditional Chinese, and Cantonese separately where offered. Review:

- Honorifics and politeness
- Emotional vocabulary
- Relationship terms
- Idioms, sarcasm, and indirect expressions
- Self-harm and abuse terminology
- Notification behavior
- Local crisis and care directories
- Data-hosting and platform requirements

### Partnerships

Potential channels include:

- Universities and student counseling services
- Licensed counseling associations
- Sleep and primary-care providers
- Youth and caregiver organizations
- Telecoms and productivity platforms
- Carefully governed workplace pilots

Workplace distribution should proceed only after testing whether employer involvement undermines trust.

### Measures

Track:

- First-check-in completion and 30-day repeat use
- Comfort with directness and tone
- Usefulness of stress, sleep, study, and work modules
- Privacy comprehension
- Reports of surveillance or judgment
- Crisis-resource accessibility and successful connection
- Safety performance by language and subgroup

## B. Middle East

### Scope and assumptions

Select countries individually after reviewing regulation, service capacity, safeguarding conditions, language requirements, and partner quality. Do not launch a generic “Arabic” product.

Account for Arabic dialects and expatriate users who may prefer English, Urdu, Hindi, Tagalog, French, or other languages.

**Assumption requiring validation:** Privacy and values-neutral framing may matter more for adoption than explicitly religious positioning. Faith-compatible content should therefore be opt-in.

### Positioning

> “A private, respectful space for emotional balance, stress, sleep, and life challenges.”

Example:

> “Pause, reflect, and take a constructive next step.”

Offer secular, values-based, and faith-compatible settings. Neutral interaction should be the default.

The AI must not present itself as an imam, mufti, priest, scholar, or source of religious rulings.

### Priority segments

- Young adults and university students
- Women seeking private and accessible support
- Caregivers and parents
- Migrant and expatriate workers
- Users experiencing grief, loneliness, or life-transition stress

### Feature adaptation

Provide:

- Modern Standard Arabic for formal information
- Country-specific dialect support
- Right-to-left design
- Formality, pronoun, and voice choices
- Text-first privacy mode
- Neutral icon and notifications
- Quick exit
- Optional PIN or biometric lock
- Low-bandwidth exercises
- Audio options for accessibility and literacy
- Modules for caregiving, migration, grief, sleep, work, and life transitions
- Optional spiritual reflection only when requested

### Family, privacy, and safeguarding

- Use a trusted-person model, not family-first sharing.
- Screen whether a selected person is safe, supportive, and confidential.
- Offer clinicians, advocates, crisis services, and women’s organizations as alternatives.
- Do not automatically involve family, religious leaders, or police.
- Protect users from outing related to mental health, sexuality, gender, marriage, or relationships.
- Screen for domestic violence and coercive control.
- Vet community and faith-based partners for confidentiality and safety.
- Protect users on shared devices and household-controlled accounts.

### Localization

Review:

- Modern Standard Arabic and selected dialects
- Right-to-left typography
- Gender and formality settings
- Relevant expatriate languages
- Crisis, abuse, marital-conflict, and coercive-control terminology
- Voice and notification privacy
- Country-specific referrals
- Optional values settings

### Partnerships

Potential channels include:

- Licensed hospitals and clinicians
- Universities and youth organizations
- Women’s health organizations
- Domestic-violence services
- Migrant-support organizations
- Telecom operators
- Carefully bounded community and faith organizations
- Employers with large expatriate workforces, subject to strict privacy controls

Partners should be described as referral or awareness partners unless they have evaluated the actual product and approved the specific claims.

### Measures

Track:

- Comprehension of privacy and sharing controls
- Use and perceived safety of discreet mode
- Language and dialect comprehension
- Safe referral connection
- Reports of moral, religious, gender, or family-related harm
- Unauthorized or ambiguous family-disclosure incidents
- Safety performance for women, migrants, minorities, and non-Arabic-language users

## C. Latin America

### Scope and assumptions

Create separate plans for **Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Argentina**, and any additional markets. Brazilian Portuguese requires an independent language, pricing, and commercial strategy. Spanish should be localized by country.

**Assumption requiring validation:** Warmth, affordability, and human-support framing may improve adoption, but warmth must not become false intimacy, dependency, or excessive informality.

### Positioning

> “A warm, private companion for everyday stress, worry, loneliness, and emotional support.”

Example:

> “A private space to pause, talk things through, and find your next step.”

Emphasize:

> “Support between the conversations you have with people you trust.”

### Priority segments

- Young adults in urban and peri-urban areas
- People with limited access to professional care
- Caregivers and parents
- Migrants separated from family
- Frontline workers and employees where privacy is credible

### Feature adaptation

Provide:

- Country-specific Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese
- Formal and informal registers
- Voice notes and audio exercises
- Low-bandwidth mode
- Freemium, affordable subscription, telecom bundle, or sponsored access
- User-written support summaries
- Modules for financial stress, migration, grief, caregiving, parenting, relationships, and violence-related distress
- Free and low-cost care directories

Messaging-platform integrations require separate review of:

- Metadata exposure
- Consent
- Account security
- Vendor access
- Shared-device risks
- Data retention
- User understanding of the integration

### Family, privacy, and safeguarding

- Treat family as one possible support network.
- Include friends, clinicians, advocates, NGOs, and community organizations.
- Ask whether sharing is safe, especially where domestic violence or coercive control may be present.
- Offer help drafting a request for support.
- Do not describe the AI as replacing human connection.
- Provide crisis navigation regardless of payment status.
- Distinguish public, private, NGO, paid, and emergency services.

### Localization

Review:

- Country-specific Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese
- Slang, idioms, humor, and formality
- Voice accents and transcription
- Pricing and payment
- Mobile-data and low-bandwidth access
- Crisis and care directories
- Resources for rural, Indigenous, Afro-descendant, migrant, disabled, and low-literacy users

### Partnerships

Potential channels include:

- Public and private primary care
- Telehealth providers
- Universities and technical institutes
- Telecom and mobile-payment providers
- NGOs serving migrants, women, youth, and survivors of violence
- Psychologist associations
- Responsible local creators
- Employers with independently verified privacy controls

Partners and creators must not claim that the AI cures depression, replaces therapy, or guarantees safety.

### Measures

Track:

- Activation and repeat use by country and access tier
- Audio and low-bandwidth usefulness
- Affordability and payment completion
- Successful connection to human support
- Tone ratings for warmth, respect, and transparency
- Reports of false intimacy or dependency
- Safety outcomes for violence, coercion, and shared-device scenarios
- Equal access to crisis and human-support resources for paying and non-paying users

## D. Northern Europe

### Scope and assumptions

Create separate plans for **Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland, and Iceland**. Treat the Netherlands, United Kingdom, Ireland, and Baltic countries as separate assessments.

**Assumption requiring validation:** Users may value transparency, autonomy, accessibility, and evidence more than highly anthropomorphic branding, while some users experiencing loneliness may still prefer a relational mode.

### Positioning

> “A transparent, evidence-informed tool for everyday mental wellbeing and care navigation.”

Example:

> “Understand your patterns, protect your privacy, and take practical steps toward better wellbeing.”

Do not use “prevention,” “clinically effective,” or similar outcome claims without appropriate evidence.

### Priority segments

- Students and young adults
- Employees and managers
- People waiting for professional care
- People experiencing loneliness or seasonal difficulties
- Older adults and users with accessibility needs
- Migrants and minority-language users

### Feature adaptation

Provide:

- Detailed privacy center
- Evidence library with sources and dates
- Explanations of AI limitations, memory, and safety behavior
- Fine-grained reminders and quiet hours
- Structured reflection and care-navigation tools
- Screen-reader, keyboard, caption, contrast, font-size, and voice support
- Inclusive language for disability, gender, sexuality, relationships, and family structure
- Tool-like and moderately relational modes
- Aggregated institutional reporting only

### Family, privacy, and safeguarding

- Offer trusted-person sharing without assuming family involvement.
- Include friends, partners, clinicians, student services, and community organizations.
- Keep employer and university programs separate from personal conversations.
- Apply country-specific adolescent confidentiality and safeguarding rules.
- Support migrants and minority-language users.
- Do not imply public-health or clinical endorsement without formal evaluation.

### Localization

Review:

- National languages and crisis systems
- Healthcare eligibility and navigation
- Accessibility requirements
- Seasonal and daylight-related content without implying diagnosis
- Privacy rights and retention explanations
- Public-sector and occupational-health terminology
- Country-specific procurement requirements

### Partnerships

Potential channels include:

- Student health services
- Primary-care networks
- Public-health agencies and municipalities
- Mental-health charities
- Accessibility organizations
- Research institutions
- Unions
- Carefully governed occupational-health programs
- Public procurement channels

Independent evaluation is likely to be more valuable than influencer-led acquisition.

### Measures

Track:

- Comprehension of AI, privacy, and safety notices
- Ability to locate deletion, export, memory, and human-help controls
- Accessibility conformance
- Trust by interaction mode
- Safety and referral performance by subgroup
- Reports of deceptive anthropomorphism or surveillance
- Absence of identifiable institutional access to personal conversations

# 5. Safety, privacy, and operating requirements

## 5.1 Human safety review

Before launch, document:

- What triggers human review
- What minimum content is visible
- Who may review it
- Reviewer qualifications and training
- Whether reviewers are employees, contractors, clinicians, or safety specialists
- Whether and when users are notified
- Review response-time targets
- Retention and deletion of reviewed content
- How users report or challenge a harmful response

Human review must not become general-purpose emotional surveillance.

## 5.2 Data and encryption architecture

The company must publish one technically accurate description of:

- Where conversations are processed
- Whether voice is recorded, transcribed, and retained
- Which vendors receive content or metadata
- Where crisis detection occurs
- Whether humans can review safety events
- How messaging and wearable integrations affect privacy
- What encryption protects
- Where true end-to-end encryption is unavailable

Encryption in transit and at rest must not be marketed as universal end-to-end confidentiality.

## 5.3 Age and minor safeguarding

Each country launch plan must specify:

- Minimum age
- Age-assurance method
- Parental authorization requirements
- Adolescent confidentiality
- Parent-access limitations
- Child-abuse reporting duties
- Imminent-danger exceptions
- Shared-device and parent-controlled-account risks
- LGBTQ+ and forced-disclosure risks
- Retention and deletion for minors

Recommended product tiers:

- **Children:** unavailable or restricted unless a dedicated safeguarding model is approved
- **Young adolescents:** limited features and stronger safeguards
- **Older adolescents:** greater autonomy with clearly stated confidentiality limits
- **Adults:** full user-controlled privacy, memory, and sharing

Parents must not receive raw adolescent conversations by default.

## 5.4 Institutional access

Employer, university, public-sector, telecom, sponsored-access, and occupational-health programs require:

- Independent user accounts
- No identifiable conversation access
- No individual emotional-risk scores
- Minimum aggregation thresholds
- No re-identification
- No participation reporting without separate consent
- No adverse employment, academic, insurance, or service consequences
- Contractual and technical limits on sponsor access to usage metadata

# 6. Governance and accountability

Each country launch cell must name:

- Accountable country executive
- Clinical-safety owner
- Safeguarding lead
- Privacy and data-protection owner
- Regulatory counsel
- Security owner
- Localization owner
- Accessibility lead
- Crisis-resource owner
- Human-review operations lead
- Partner due-diligence owner
- Communications owner
- Market-pause decision-maker

### Binding decision rules

| Decision | Required approval |
|---|---|
| Controlled pilot | Country executive, clinical safety, privacy, safeguarding, regulatory |
| Public launch | Global launch authority after all launch gates pass |
| Crisis-resource approval | Clinical-safety and local resource owners |
| Partner approval | Legal, privacy, safeguarding, safety, and commercial review |
| Feature release affecting safety or data | Product, safety, privacy, and regulatory approval |
| Immediate feature pause | Pre-authorized safety or security authority |
| Market pause | Independent safety authority with executive notification |

Independent reviewers must disclose conflicts of interest. Commercial targets must not override safety or privacy decisions.

# 7. Regulatory workstream

Before each pilot and public launch, assess:

- Sensitive health-data protection
- Data-controller and processor obligations
- Cross-border transfers and data localization
- Applicable AI regulation, including EU AI Act requirements where relevant
- Medical-device or software-as-medical-device classification
- Consumer-protection and health-advertising rules
- Telehealth and clinical-practice restrictions
- Child safety and age assurance
- Accessibility obligations
- Mandatory reporting and safeguarding duties
- Emergency-disclosure requirements
- App-store, messaging, telecom, cloud, and vendor rules
- Employment, insurance, education, and public-sector restrictions
- Automated profiling and high-impact decision rules

Countries should be deferred or excluded where:

- Crisis resources are materially unavailable or unsafe
- Human escalation cannot operate safely
- High-risk language coverage is inadequate
- Regulatory requirements cannot be met
- Privacy or security architecture is not feasible
- Local support capacity is insufficient for intended claims

“GDPR-grade” and “GDPR-aligned” are not substitutes for specific legal and technical commitments.

# 8. Phased 2026 launch actions

## Phase 1: Country selection and discovery — Q1 2026

Select initial countries using:

- Regulatory feasibility
- Crisis-resource availability
- Clinical and safeguarding capacity
- Language readiness
- Privacy and security feasibility
- Accessibility readiness
- Distribution-partner quality

For each country:

- Conduct qualitative research across age, gender, socioeconomic, disability, migrant, and relevant minority groups.
- Test positioning, directness, tone, interaction mode, voice, and notification privacy.
- Test consent, deletion, memory, and trusted-person sharing.
- Review crisis, abuse, self-harm, idiom, sarcasm, and religious language.
- Validate pricing, payment, bandwidth, and referral access.
- Establish the country RACI and pause procedure.

Each hypothesis must have a named research method, decision owner, sample, and success threshold.

## Phase 2: Controlled pilots — Q2 2026

Recommended pilots:

- **East Asia:** university and carefully separated workplace-stress pilots
- **Middle East:** young-adult and women’s wellbeing pilots with discreet-mode testing
- **Latin America:** telecom, university, or NGO pilots focused on affordability and low bandwidth
- **Northern Europe:** student-health, public-health, research, or occupational-health pilots

Pilot controls:

- Limited geography and audience
- Human safety monitoring
- Weekly review of harmful or misunderstood outputs
- False-positive and false-negative analysis
- Resource-availability checks
- Accessibility and subgroup testing
- No broad paid marketing

## Phase 3: Public launch in approved countries — Q3 2026

Launch only where:

- Local crisis and referral pathways operate
- Language and safety review is complete
- Human escalation is staffed
- Consent, deletion, and memory controls work
- Privacy and security reviews are complete
- Accessibility and minor safeguards are ready
- Partner claims and data-use controls are enforceable
- Local customer support is operational

## Phase 4: Evaluation and selective expansion — Q4 2026

- Conduct independent safety, privacy, security, and cultural-quality audits.
- Publish pilot outcomes, limitations, and material incidents.
- Expand only where evidence and service capacity support it.
- Reassess voice, family, employer, messaging, values, and faith-compatible features.
- Roll back or pause features causing unsafe disclosure, surveillance concerns, or disproportionate harm.

# 9. Validation priorities

Before each country launch, validate:

### Product and cultural fit

- Direct versus indirect prompts
- Tool, coach, and companion modes
- Tone, warmth, formality, and degree of anthropomorphism
- Voice, accent, transcription, and shared-device privacy
- Notification and discreet-mode design
- Values and spirituality preferences
- Trusted-person sharing and family-safety checks

### Language and safety

- Emotional vocabulary
- Slang, humor, sarcasm, metaphor, and idioms
- Self-harm, suicide, abuse, grief, trauma, and crisis terminology
- False positives and false negatives by language and subgroup
- Unsupported-language fallback procedures
- Accessibility of crisis information

### Privacy and trust

- AI-identity comprehension
- Data-use and human-review comprehension
- Memory, deletion, export, and consent usability
- Understanding of emergency exceptions
- Perceived surveillance and cultural misunderstanding
- Trust in distribution partners

### Service access

- Crisis-resource availability
- Cost, eligibility, hours, language, and geographic coverage
- Referral contact attempts and completion
- Appointment scheduling and care access where measurable
- Fallback resources when primary services are unavailable

# 10. Success metrics and pause thresholds

## 10.1 Trust and usability

Report by country, language, age, disability, migrant status, and other relevant groups:

- AI-disclosure comprehension
- Privacy and emergency-disclosure comprehension
- Ability to find deletion, export, memory, and human-help controls
- Perceived judgment, surveillance, coercion, or cultural harm
- Tone ratings for warmth, respect, usefulness, and professionalism
- Accessibility failures
- Trust by interaction mode and modality

## 10.2 Safety

Track:

- Unsafe-advice rate
- False-reassurance rate
- Crisis false positives and false negatives
- Performance by language and subgroup
- Human-review response time
- Referral contact attempted, completed, appointment scheduled, and care accessed
- Resource availability and accessibility
- Unauthorized disclosures
- Unsafe family or trusted-person sharing
- Over-escalation and distress caused by crisis messaging
- Professional-care avoidance
- Healthy disengagement
- Over-reliance, dependency, or unhealthy anthropomorphism

## 10.3 Business and adoption

Track only after safety floors are met:

- Activation and first-check-in completion
- Repeat check-ins at 30, 60, and 90 days
- Feature adoption by mode, language, and modality
- Cost of acquisition by channel
- Conversion or sponsored-access utilization
- Referral-partner utilization
- Payment completion and affordability
- Retention and healthy discontinuation

Business targets should be country-specific, tied to baselines, and reported with confidence intervals. Retention, conversion, session duration, and feature adoption are not evidence of improved mental health.

## 10.4 Launch and pause criteria

A country may proceed to public launch only with:

- No known critical crisis-resource defects
- Successful simulated end-to-end referrals
- Acceptable language- and subgroup-specific safety performance
- Operational human escalation
- Verified deletion, export, retention, and backup processes
- Completed accessibility, minor-safety, privacy, and security reviews
- Contractually enforceable partner controls

Pause a market, partner, or feature for:

- Critical false negatives in imminent-danger scenarios
- Unsafe or abusive advice
- Unauthorized disclosure
- Broken deletion or consent controls
- Material data breach
- Repeated language-specific or cultural misclassification
- Partner misuse of emotional data
- Unavailable, unsafe, or materially misleading crisis resources
- Evidence of coercive institutional use

---

## Final recommendation

Approve a **controlled international program**, not an unrestricted four-region rollout.

The product should earn trust through:

- Honest clinical limits
- Strong and comprehensible privacy controls
- Culturally intelligible language and interaction
- Safe, locally verified crisis pathways
- Accessible human escalation
- User control over memory, sharing, and deletion

The company should scale only where the product is demonstrably:

> **Useful, private, culturally intelligible, clinically honest, accessible, and safe enough for emotionally difficult moments.**
