# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
## Global Compensation Policy for Distributed Workforce (30 Countries)

### STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE

Establish a **legally compliant, internally fair, cost-conscious** compensation framework that replaces opaque location multipliers with **transparent, published adjustment factors** tied to objective data sources. This policy enables consistent application across all countries while meeting diverse regulatory requirements (pay-transparency laws in EU, Canada, USA; pay-equity audits in 15+ jurisdictions).

### THE PROBLEM WE'RE SOLVING

**Current State Friction:**
- Hidden location multipliers (0.43x–1.0x) create perception of unfairness
- Public salary forums (Glassdoor, Blind, Levels.fyi) expose gaps → employee anxiety
- Pay-transparency laws (Germany, France, Spain, Canada, USA states, Australia) now force disclosure of multipliers previously hidden
- Regulatory audits threaten fines ($10k–€300k per violation), litigation, reputational damage
- No defensible justification for gaps when challenged

**Policy Solution:**
- **Tier A:** Published global base compensation by skill level (L1–L6) — transparent, same for all countries
- **Tier B:** Market-Rate Adjustment (MRA) indexed to regional labor markets (Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, LinkedIn Salary) — objective, published, defensible
- **Tier C:** Cost-of-Living Adjustment (COLA) for affordability crises (e.g., Lisbon housing crisis) — transparent threshold (Salary/Rent < 0.70)
- **Tier D:** Compliance Adjustments for legal minimums (minimum wage, collective agreements, pay-equity remediation) — jurisdiction-specific, documented
- **Dispute Mechanism:** Independent Compensation Appeals Panel; employee can challenge any factor; 15-day SLA

### KEY STRATEGIC SHIFTS

| Element | Old Model | New Model | Benefit |
|---------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **Location Adjustment** | Hidden multiplier (0.50x, 0.72x, etc.) | Published MRA with data sources (Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, LinkedIn) | Transparent; defensible in audits; reduces employee anxiety |
| **Fairness Justification** | "That's what market pays" (vague) | "Warsaw median L3 = €58k (per Glassdoor); we offer $57.5k = competitive 45th percentile" | Clear logic; employees understand "why" |
| **Cost of Living** | No adjustment (low-cost employees compress salary) | COLA +% applied when affordability crisis (Salary/Rent < 0.70) | Addresses Lisbon, SF, Zurich housing crises; fair purchasing power |
| **Compliance** | Post-hoc remediation (audit finds violation, we fix) | Built-in compliance adjustments (auto-check minimum wage, collective agreements, pay-equity floors) | Proactive; reduces fines + litigation risk |
| **Dispute Process** | Informal (email to manager, usually dismissed) | Independent Appeals Panel; documented decision; binding unless escalated to arbitration | Credible; reduces grievances + legal exposure |
| **Regulation Readiness** | Unprepared (policy document vague; multipliers unexplained) | Audit-ready (all factors published, data sources cited, compliance checklist embedded) | Passes regulatory audits; fines avoided; reputation protected |

### EXPECTED OUTCOMES

| Outcome | Baseline (Current) | Year 1 Target | Year 2 Target |
|---------|------------------|---------------|---------------|
| **Internal Pay Equity Perception** | 40% of employees "agree company pays fairly" (forum data) | 60% (transparency + clear rationale) | 75% (relocation policy + manager enablement in place) |

| **Audit Readiness** | Unready (regulatory audit = exposure) | Partially ready (core compliance in place; gaps in auditing process) | Audit-ready (annual audits institutionalized; external auditor engaged) |
| **Manager Consistency** | Low (each manager interprets policy differently) | Medium (quick-reference guides distributed; minimal training) | High (manager training completed; compensation committee oversight; <5% calculation errors) |

## CORE POLICY FRAMEWORK

### SECTION 1: POLICY PRINCIPLES

#### **Principle 1: Cost-of-Labor Realism**
Regional talent markets set wage ceilings. We pay competitive rates indexed to **peer-to-peer regional benchmarks**, not arbitrary multipliers. All adjustment factors backed by published data sources (Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, LinkedIn Salary).

#### **Principle 2: Internal Fairness via Explicitness**
Employees accept pay differences when **reasons are clear, consistent, and universal**. Each employee receives itemized "Pay Rationale Card" showing: Base Role Compensation + MRA + COLA + Compliance Adjustments = Final Salary.

#### **Principle 3: Legal Compliance by Design**
Proactive compliance prevents $10k–€300k fines per violation. All factors transparent, published, auditable. Annual compliance calendar institutionalized. Legal review before policy changes.

#### **Principle 4: Market Competitiveness Without Overpay**
Target **50th–60th percentile of regional benchmarks** (competitive but sustainable). Premium-market exception (SF, NYC, London, Tel Aviv): 60th–70th percentile (talent war necessity). Low-cost markets (India, Poland, LatAm): 45th–55th percentile (cost-constrained but above turnover threshold).

#### **Principle 5: Algorithmic Consistency, Limited Human Discretion**
Discretionary pay = inequity. All adjustments formulaic, published, auditable. Merit adjustments only within ±10% band (documented). Appeals process ensures accountability.

### SECTION 6: MERIT & PERFORMANCE ADJUSTMENTS

**Definition:** Individual adjustments within published bands; recognizes tenure, performance, or market scarcity.

**Rules:**
- **Range:** ±10% of (Base × MRA × COLA + Compliance Adjustments)
- **Criteria:** Performance rating (annual), tenure/scarcity premium, retention risk mitigation
- **Documentation:** All adjustments documented; subject to audit
- **Governance:** Manager proposes; HR validates against policy; Compensation Committee spot-checks

**Example:**
```
Warsaw L3 Engineer, 4 YOE, High Performer (3.8/5 rating)
Base: $115k × MRA 0.50 = $57,500
Band: $57,500 ± 10% = $51,750–$63,250
Performance Adjustment: +10% (high performer) = $63,250
Final Salary: $63,250
```

**Governance:**
- Documented in offer letter and pay statement
- Subject to annual performance review (can be reduced if rating declines)
- Spot-check audits (HR reviews 10% of merit adjustments Q1 for consistency)

### SECTION 8: RELOCATION POLICY (ADDRESSING CRITICAL GAP)

**Definition:** Salary adjustment rules when employee relocates to different country/market.

**Relocation Types:**

#### **Type A: Internal Relocation to Higher-Market Location (e.g., Warsaw → Berlin)**

**Salary Rule:**
```
New Salary = Base × Destination MRA × Destination COLA + Compliance + Merit
(Destination rates apply; no discount for tenure or origin)
```

**Example:**
```
Employee: L3 Engineer, relocates Warsaw → Berlin
Current Salary (Warsaw): €54.6k (MRA 0.50 + €900 rent, no COLA)
Destination Salary (Berlin): €78.2k (MRA 0.68 + €1,400 rent, no COLA)
Adjustment: +€23.6k (+43%)

Rationale: Relocation = new labor market. Employee now competing in Berlin talent market 
(higher demand, higher salary expectations). Salary adjusts to Berlin market rate.
```

**Communication:**
> "Your relocation to Berlin results in salary increase to €78,200 (Berlin L3 market rate). 
> This reflects higher market demand in Berlin, not a special increase. Your new salary is 
> competitive with Berlin peers hiring at your level."

---

#### **Type B: Internal Relocation to Lower-Market Location (e.g., London → Warsaw)**

**Salary Rule:**
```
New Salary = Max(Destination Market Rate, 85% of Current Salary)
(Floor of 85% of current salary; does not drop below this due to relocation)
```

**Rationale:** Company does not penalize relocation to lower-cost markets. If employee chooses to relocate (e.g., family reasons, quality-of-life preference), salary does not drop >15% due to market adjustment. Company absorbs difference as goodwill.

**Example:**
```
Employee: L4 Engineer, relocates London → Warsaw
Current Salary (London): £122k (MRA 0.95)
Destination Market Rate (Warsaw L4): €100k (MRA 0.65 × $155k = $100.75k ≈ €96k)
Floor: 85% of £122k = £103.7k (≈ €123k)
New Salary: €123k (floor applies; does not drop to market €96k)

Rationale: Prevents "salary imprisonment" in lower-cost markets. Employee who voluntarily 
relocates (personal reasons) retains 85% of prior salary; does not face 20%+ pay cut.
```

**Communication:**
> "Your relocation to Warsaw results in salary adjustment to €123,000 (floor of 85% of current). 
> This ensures you maintain earnings stability while relocating to a lower-cost market. 
> Your new salary is above Warsaw market rate, reflecting company commitment to your retention."

---

#### **Type C: Promotion + Relocation (e.g., L3 Berlin → L4 London)**

**Salary Rule:**
```
New Salary = New Base × Destination MRA × Destination COLA + Compliance + Merit
(Apply new level to destination market; no "blended" rate)
```

**Example:**
```
Employee: Promoted to L4; relocates Berlin → London
Old Salary (L3 Berlin): €78.2k
New Base (L4): $155k
Destination (London): $155k × 0.95 × (1 + 0%) = $147.25k (no COLA for London)
Adjustment: $147.25k vs prior €78.2k (€74k) = +$73.25k (+99%)

Rationale: Promotion + relocation = new opportunity in new market. Salary reflects new 
level + new market; not constrained by prior level salary.
```

---

**Governance:**
- HR coordinates with manager + employee
- Written relocation offer specifies new salary with full calculation shown
- Legal review (if crossing major tax jurisdiction or collective-agreement change)
- 30-day notice to payroll; effective date aligned with relocation
