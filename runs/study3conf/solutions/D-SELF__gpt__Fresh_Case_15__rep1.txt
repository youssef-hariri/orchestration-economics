# Executive Summary

Adopt a **market-segmented, local-currency royalty and franchise-financing model** for Turkey, Egypt, Nigeria, Indonesia, and Brazil. Replace routine fixed-USD royalties with:

- a percentage of verified local-currency gross sales;
- a capped, soft local-currency minimum;
- a complete affordability ceiling covering franchisor-controlled fees;
- separately documented cross-border cost recovery;
- objective, time-limited relief for viable but stressed outlets;
- local third-party financing before franchisor capital;
- formal, competition-compliant engagement with franchisee associations;
- clear recovery, transfer, insolvency, and closure pathways.

The recommended structure shares short-term currency, inflation, and demand risk without permanently abandoning brand economics. It should be approved initially as a **design, validation, and pilot program**, with contractual rollout and material franchisor financing subject to country-specific legal, tax, accounting, regulatory, payments, competition, franchise, lending, data, labor, and insolvency approvals.

---

# 1. Objectives and Principles

## Objectives

The program should:

1. Keep economically viable franchisees operating.
2. Reduce avoidable closures, defaults, and underinvestment.
3. Preserve the franchisor’s long-term participation in sales growth.
4. Align payment currency with franchisee revenue and operating costs.
5. Maintain funding for brand, technology, training, marketing, quality, and field support.
6. Provide credible responses to collective franchisee pressure without surrendering pricing discipline.
7. Limit franchisor credit, liquidity, tax, and regulatory exposure.
8. Provide orderly transfer, restructuring, or closure for structurally uneconomic stores.

## Principles

- **Local-currency alignment:** Ordinary royalties should be invoiced and settled in local currency.
- **Sales participation:** The core royalty remains a percentage of verified gross sales.
- **Shared risk:** Temporary relief is available, but permanent losses are not automatically transferred to the franchisor.
- **Soft minimums:** Minimums fund essential system costs but cannot become unmanageable fixed charges.
- **Cash-flow-based relief:** Cash flow and contribution margin determine distress treatment; they are not normally the royalty base.
- **Brand protection:** Food safety, audits, technology, training, maintenance, and core standards remain mandatory.
- **Owner participation:** Franchisee equity and shareholder support are first-loss capital.
- **No structural subsidization:** Financing should not preserve outlets that are persistently loss-making before royalty.
- **Local adaptability:** Global principles are standardized; rates, indices, thresholds, and payment mechanics are country-specific.

# 2. Market Segmentation

## 2.1 Country segmentation

| Country | Risk posture | Royalty posture | Financing posture | Engagement posture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Egypt** | Very high inflation, FX scarcity, remittance, and demand risk | Local-currency settlement; no routine fixed-USD royalty; strongest affordability protection | Supplier credit, equipment leasing, receivables finance; minimal franchisor exposure | Formal consultation, individual accession, strict verification |
| **Nigeria** | Very high FX volatility, inflation, interest-rate, and payment risk | Local-currency royalty; specified executable-rate mechanics | Receivables-backed and local bank finance; limited guarantees | Standard relief menu and strong data controls |
| **Turkey** | High inflation, lira volatility, refinancing risk, and sophisticated bargaining | Local-currency royalty; capped quarterly indexation; cash-flow override | Local bank, equipment, and controlled deferral facilities | Detailed commercial negotiation through a country council |
| **Brazil** | Moderate currency and inflation risk; deeper finance market; complex tax and legal environment | Local-currency royalty with transparent indexation; mainly store-specific relief | Banks, fintechs, leasing, receivables finance | Formal consultation with competition safeguards |
| **Indonesia** | Lower relative macro stress but significant regional and format variation | Standard local-currency model differentiated by format, city, and maturity | Local banks, leasing, supplier, and development finance | Consultation-led, targeted relief |

Country classifications should be reviewed quarterly and after material changes in currency, inflation, remittance access, regulation, financing conditions, or consumer demand.

## 2.2 Outlet segmentation

| Category | Characteristics | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Green** | Positive pre-royalty contribution margin, adequate cash flow, reliable reporting, compliant operations | Standard terms |
| **Amber** | Viable before royalty but temporarily cash constrained | Temporary relief or third-party financing |
| **Red** | Viable only after restructuring; distress is material but remediable | Turnaround plan, owner contribution, capped support, transfer fallback |
| **Black** | Structurally negative pre-royalty contribution margin, fraud, repeated noncompliance, or unreliable data | No continuing subsidy; transfer or orderly closure |

Association membership must not determine eligibility.

# 3. Royalty Architecture

## 3.1 Complete payment structure

For outlet \(i\) in month \(t\):

\[
P^F_{i,t}=R_{i,t}+MKT_{i,t}+TECH_{i,t}+X_{i,t}
\]

Where:

- \(R\) = ordinary royalty;
- \(MKT\) = marketing contribution;
- \(TECH\) = technology or system fee;
- \(X\) = documented cross-border cost recovery.

Statutory taxes and government charges are shown separately:

\[
P^{total}_{i,t}=P^F_{i,t}+T_{i,t}
\]

Taxes are not franchisor revenue, but they must be included in the outlet’s affordability and viability assessment.

## 3.2 Ordinary royalty

\[
R_{i,t}
=
\max\left[
0,\;
\min\left(
\max(r_{m,f}S_{i,t},F_{i,t}),
A^{royalty}_{i,t}
\right)
\right]
\]

Where:

- \(r_{m,f}\) = market- and format-specific royalty rate;
- \(S_{i,t}\) = verified gross sales;
- \(F_{i,t}\) = soft local-currency minimum;
- \(A^{royalty}_{i,t}\) = affordability ceiling for ordinary royalty.

The percentage rate should preserve the franchisor’s normalized economic participation while avoiding excessive payment increases for franchisees transitioning from fixed-USD terms.

## 3.3 Gross sales

Gross sales should include:

- in-store, drive-through, kiosk, and nontraditional sales;
- delivery and aggregator sales;
- app and digital sales;
- catering and institutional sales;
- vouchers, coupons, promotions, and bundled transactions;
- cash, card, mobile-money, and third-party payment receipts.

Permitted deductions should generally be limited to genuine customer refunds and taxes collected solely for government, subject to local advice.

The following are ordinarily non-deductible:

- labor;
- rent;
- food and packaging;
- delivery commissions;
- financing costs;
- ordinary operating expenses;
- franchisee income taxes;
- unnormalized related-party charges.

Sales must be reconciled across POS, bank, card-acquirer, mobile-money, inventory, purchasing, and aggregator data.

## 3.4 Soft minimum

\[
F_{i,t}
=
F_{0,m,f}
\left[1+\min(I_{m,t},C_{I,m})\right]
Q_{i,t}
\]

Where:

- \(F_{0,m,f}\) = opening minimum by market and format;
- \(I_{m,t}\) = cumulative approved index movement since the last reset;
- \(C_{I,m}\) = annual and cumulative indexation cap;
- \(Q_{i,t}\) = approved factor for format, maturity, location, seasonality, or temporary closure.

Each country schedule must define:

- index and publisher;
- observation period and lag;
- reset frequency;
- annual and cumulative caps;
- treatment of negative inflation;
- fallback index;
- treatment of index revisions;
- treatment of temporary closures;
- whether capped amounts expire, are waived, or become conditionally recoverable.

A minimum cannot rise if the affordability ceiling or stress-period cap prevents the increase. New stores should receive a six- to twelve-month minimum holiday or reduced minimum, subject to opening, reporting, safety, and brand-compliance milestones.

## 3.5 Affordability ceiling

All franchisor-controlled fees must fit within a complete payment-capacity test:

\[
P^F_{i,t}\leq A^{total}_{i,t}
\]

Illustratively:

\[
A^{total}_{i,t}
=
\max\left[
0,\;
\min\left(
\alpha_{m,f}S_{i,t}
+
\beta_{m,f}(CF_{i,t}-CF^{reserve}_{i,t}),
\;
\delta_{m,f}S_{i,t}
\right)
\right]
\]

Where:

- \(CF\) = standardized store cash flow before franchisor fees and senior debt service;
- \(CF^{reserve}\) = required operating reserve;
- \(\alpha,\beta,\delta\) = locally calibrated parameters.

The ceiling must preserve funds for:

- payroll and statutory labor obligations;
- rent and essential utilities;
- taxes;
- essential inventory and suppliers;
- insurance;
- food safety;
- required maintenance;
- senior secured debt service;
- minimum operating reserves.

A zero ceiling produces a zero payment, not a negative royalty or credit balance, and automatically places the outlet into formal turnaround status. Zero-payment periods should generally be limited to six months per approval, with no more than two renewals without escalation.

## 3.6 Stress-period royalty cap

During a formally declared stress period:

\[
R_{i,t}\leq R_{i,t-12}(1+C_{R,m})
\]

The cap applies to ordinary royalty growth and creates no automatic catch-up claim.

# 4. Fee Taxonomy and Payment Priority

## Fee taxonomy

| Fee | Purpose | Default treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Royalty | Brand, system, operating methods, continuing support | Local currency; sales-based |
| Marketing contribution | Approved system marketing | Local currency; separately accounted and reported |
| Technology fee | POS, digital, software, and systems | Local currency where possible |
| Training and field support | Training, quality, and field support | Included in royalty unless separately documented |
| Cross-border charge | Actual foreign-supplied services or imported costs | Separately documented and validated |
| Taxes | Government charges | Paid according to law; not franchisor revenue |

The franchisor must prevent double charging. Each service should be mapped to one fee category, with its economic purpose, provider, location, tax treatment, transfer-pricing support, relief treatment, and payment priority documented.

## Intended operating payment priority

Subject to local law, senior lender agreements, and insolvency rules:

1. Statutory employee, tax, safety, and essential operating obligations.
2. Rent and essential suppliers.
3. Senior secured debt.
4. Current royalty and mandatory system charges.
5. Current technology and safety-critical fees.
6. Marketing contribution.
7. Deferred franchisor charges.
8. Shareholder distributions.

The legally enforceable priority may differ by country. Each country addendum must distinguish the intended operating waterfall from statutory insolvency priority.

# 5. Financing Solutions

## 5.1 Capital hierarchy

Funding should generally be sought in this order:

1. Franchisee equity and shareholder support.
2. Local commercial banks.
3. Equipment lessors and approved suppliers.
4. Payment processors and receivables lenders.
5. Development-finance institutions.
6. Franchisor-supported facilities.

The franchisor should not become the default provider of ordinary working capital.

## 5.2 Financing products

| Product | Illustrative structure |
|---|---|
| Working capital | Local currency; 12–36 months; limited grace period with recovery plan |
| Equipment finance | Local currency; 36–60 months; approved equipment and milestone drawdown |
| Royalty support | Documented receivable or loan; 12–24 months; capped cash-flow repayment |
| Interest support | Temporary, budgeted, generally six–twelve months |
| Receivables facility | Merchant, card, mobile-money, or aggregator flows, subject to consent and local law |

## 5.3 Credit controls

Every franchisor-supported facility requires:

- 13-week liquidity forecast;
- monthly 24-month recovery model for facilities exceeding twelve months;
- minimum owner-equity contribution;
- positive or near-positive pre-royalty contribution margin;
- debt-service-to-cash-flow test;
- maximum leverage test;
- identified repayment source;
- security and lender-priority review;
- expected-credit-loss assessment.

Illustrative starting controls, subject to local validation:

- debt service not exceeding approximately 35–45% of normalized cash flow;
- franchisor-supported debt generally not exceeding 12–18 months of normalized pre-royalty cash flow;
- fixed maximum guarantee payouts and expiry dates;
- exposure limits by outlet, beneficial-owner group, country, and consolidated portfolio.

## 5.4 Loss allocation

1. Franchisee equity is first-loss capital.
2. Shareholder loans should be subordinated where required.
3. Senior secured lenders retain agreed priority.
4. Franchisor support is capped and may be subordinated.
5. Guarantees have fixed maximum payouts and expiry dates.
6. Debt forgiveness requires separate approval.
7. No financing is provided solely to preserve an accounting receivable.
8. Related-party financing requires independent approval and commercial documentation.

No facility may be issued until local advice confirms lending, licensing, interest-rate, security, payments, tax, related-party, and insolvency requirements.

# 6. Country-Specific Applications

## Egypt

- Eliminate routine fixed-USD royalties and minimums.
- Use local-currency sales royalties with the strongest affordability protection.
- Suspend, locally deposit, finance, or waive cross-border charges that cannot legally be remitted or delivered.
- Assume prolonged FX scarcity in downside planning.
- Prioritize supplier credit, equipment leasing, and receivables finance.
- Use the lowest franchisor exposure limits and highest approval scrutiny.
- Validate withholding tax, VAT, remittance, foreign-exchange, lending, security, and insolvency treatment locally.

## Nigeria

- Use local-currency royalties.
- Specify the FX source, fixing date, fallback, and treatment where the published rate is not executable.
- Reconcile cash, card, mobile-money, and aggregator sales.
- Use payment-availability, cash-flow, and real-demand triggers.
- Prioritize receivables-backed facilities.
- Limit guarantees by outlet, franchisee group, country, and global portfolio.
- Validate official versus executable FX rates, payment regulation, tax, security, lending, and insolvency rules.

## Turkey

- Use local-currency royalties with quarterly resets.
- Apply inflation caps and a cash-flow override.
- Use local bank, equipment, and receivables finance, recognizing high interest and refinancing risk.
- Offer relief only to compliant, viable outlets with credible recovery plans.
- Expect sophisticated commercial negotiation and document association engagement carefully.
- Validate foreign-currency, consumer-price, franchise, tax, and competition-law requirements.

## Indonesia

- Use the standard local-currency model.
- Differentiate by city, format, maturity, seasonality, and local demand.
- Use semiannual or annual indexation unless conditions deteriorate.
- Prioritize local banks, leasing, supplier credit, and development finance.
- Use targeted, event-driven relief rather than broad macro concessions.
- Validate franchise, foreign-investment, tax, local-content, payments, labor, data, and licensing requirements.

## Brazil

- Use local-currency royalties with transparent semiannual or annual indexation.
- Prefer store-specific relief and restructuring over broad currency concessions.
- Use bank, fintech, leasing, and receivables finance.
- Maintain transparent marketing-fund accounting and reporting.
- Apply strong controls for franchise, tax, labor, consumer, competition, data, and insolvency compliance.

# 7. Treatment of Stable-Currency Company-Owned Markets

Company-owned stores in stable-currency markets should not be charged artificial royalties merely to create accounting symmetry. They bear operating risk directly and retain store-level profit.

The group should:

- maintain existing company-owned economics unless separately approved;
- use a common scorecard covering sales, contribution margin, cash flow, maintenance, and brand standards;
- use company-owned stores as operational benchmarks, not direct royalty-affordability comparators;
- attribute shared brand, technology, and marketing costs transparently for management reporting;
- avoid using stable-market company-owned profitability to justify excessive stressed-market franchisee charges;
- use company-owned stores as test sites for menu, format, and productivity improvements;
- adopt a separate standard-currency franchise policy for future stable-market franchising.

This distinction should be explained as a difference in contractual risk allocation, not preferential treatment.

# 8. Relief and Recovery Framework

## Eligibility

Relief requires:

1. One external or operational shock.
2. One verified outlet-level impact.
3. Positive or near-positive pre-royalty contribution margin.
4. Complete POS, bank, tax, debt, and outlet-level information.
5. Reasonable owner equity or loss-sharing.
6. Continued food-safety, labor, insurance, technology, audit, and maintenance compliance.
7. No fraud, sales diversion, related-party leakage, or unauthorized sourcing.
8. A credible recovery, transfer, or closure plan.

## Triggers

### External triggers

- Rolling depreciation above the country threshold.
- CPI, food, wage, rent, or utility inflation above the stress band.
- Material official-to-executable FX divergence.
- Inability to obtain or remit an approved cross-border payment.
- Regulatory closure, disaster, civil disruption, or major supply interruption.

### Outlet-impact triggers

- Real same-store sales decline above the approved threshold.
- Contribution-margin compression.
- Cash-flow coverage below the required level for two consecutive periods.
- Franchisor-fee burden above the approved sales or cash-flow percentage.
- Inability to fund essential obligations despite reasonable owner contribution.

The normal rule should be **one external trigger plus one outlet-impact trigger**, subject to conduct conditions. No single macro event automatically grants relief.

## Relief options

- **Minimum reduction:** Reduce or suspend the soft minimum for three to six months.
- **Conditional deferral:** Transfer a defined amount to a deferred ledger with maturity, repayment cap, currency, interest, security, and insolvency treatment specified.
- **Graduated royalty:** Use rolling sales and cash-flow bands, with true-ups and anti-suppression controls.
- **Payment extension:** Provide limited additional settlement time; do not convert this into indefinite trade credit.
- **Operating restructuring:** Permit approved changes to hours, menu, format, location, management, ownership, or temporary closure.
- **Transfer or closure:** Facilitate transfer to an approved operator or orderly closure where recovery is not credible.

## Treatment of reduced or deferred amounts

At the time of concession, each amount must be designated as:

- permanently waived;
- deferred and recoverable;
- recoverable only from future excess cash flow;
- payable upon transfer or sale;
- extinguished upon approved closure; or
- converted into a documented financing receivable.

A waived amount cannot later be converted unilaterally into debt.

## Recovery formula

\[
D^{repayment}_{i,t}
=
\min\left[
D^{outstanding}_{i,t},
p_{m,f}\max(0,CF_{i,t}-CF^{reserve}_{i,t})
\right]
\]

Repayment pauses if the affordability test is breached again. Immediate full catch-up is prohibited unless separately approved.

# 9. Franchisee-Association Engagement

## Engagement principles

The franchisor should:

- recognize legitimate associations where legally appropriate;
- publish objective eligibility rules;
- share only aggregate or anonymized data;
- preserve differentiation by format, location, maturity, and distress;
- offer relief regardless of association membership;
- require individual addenda;
- avoid most-favored-nation commitments;
- make concessions time-limited and reviewable;
- verify representatives’ authority to speak for members.

## Permitted council subjects

Country councils may discuss:

- aggregate macroeconomic conditions;
- formula principles;
- implementation timing;
- anonymized historical benchmarks;
- operational continuity;
- dispute trends.

## Prohibited or high-risk subjects

Councils must not coordinate:

- consumer prices;
- supplier prices or allocation;
- territories;
- output or capacity;
- customer allocation;
- competitive strategy;
- current competitor-specific pricing or costs.

Each meeting should have:

- an approved agenda;
- defined information-sharing limits;
- competition counsel attendance where appropriate;
- minutes and document retention;
- escalation procedures if prohibited topics arise.

Association frameworks are nonbinding until valid individual amendments are executed. Coordinated payment withholding is a separate legal and credit event and does not automatically establish hardship.

# 10. Legal, Tax, Accounting, and Regulatory Cautions

## Mandatory country gates

| Workstream | Required validation |
|---|---|
| Franchise law | Amendment, disclosure, registration, renewal, transfer, and termination requirements |
| FX and payments | Local settlement, FX sources, remittance, local deposits, and payment-platform controls |
| Tax | Withholding, VAT/GST, deductibility, e-invoicing, stamp duties, and relief treatment |
| Transfer pricing | Arm’s-length support for royalty, IP, technology, services, financing, and guarantees |
| Competition | Lawful association consultation and information-sharing protocol |
| Lending and security | Licensing, interest limits, security perfection, priority, set-off, and insolvency |
| Accounting | Modification, receivable, financing, impairment, and disclosure treatment |
| Data | POS, payment, cross-border transfers, privacy, and cybersecurity |
| Operations | Labor, food safety, insurance, consumer protection, and licensing |

## Hard-stop rules

- No foreign-currency charge without FX and tax approval.
- No franchisor lending without lending and enforceability approval.
- No receivables sweep without payment-provider and lender consent.
- No association framework without competition counsel approval.
- No contract amendment without required consent and disclosure.
- No cross-border data integration without privacy approval.
- No related-party financing without independent approval.

Approvals must be refreshed after a material legal, regulatory, FX, payments, or tax change.

# 11. Implementation Roadmap

## Phase 1: Stabilize — 0–90 days

- Freeze automatic hard-currency minimum increases in acute-stress markets.
- Implement interim hardship rules without creating unintended permanent amendments.
- Segment outlets and franchisees.
- Collect at least 36 months of sales, margin, cash-flow, debt, and arrears data.
- Establish governance bodies.
- Identify urgent financing, transfer, and closure cases.
- Obtain country legal, tax, FX, competition, accounting, payments, data, labor, and insolvency advice.

## Phase 2: Design and validate — 90–180 days

- Calibrate rates, minimums, affordability parameters, triggers, and exposure limits.
- Separate royalty, marketing, technology, training, tax, and cross-border charges.
- Build deferred-royalty and impairment ledgers.
- Draft contract addenda and financing templates.
- Establish association protocols.
- Back-test historical, repeated, and correlated downside scenarios.
- Complete country sign-offs.

## Phase 3: Pilot — 6–12 months

Pilot representative Green, Amber, and Red outlets:

- Turkey: capped indexation and cash-flow override.
- Egypt: local settlement and remittance-contingency model.
- Nigeria: payment-channel reconciliation and receivables finance.
- Indonesia: format and regional differentiation.
- Brazil: local indexation and store-specific restructuring.

## Phase 4: Scale and normalize — 12–24 months

Scale only where pilot gates are met. Restore standard terms as affordability improves, recover deferred balances through capped cash-flow sweeps, and transfer or close structurally uneconomic outlets.

# 12. Governance

## Board or board committee

Approve:

- permanent royalty-rate changes;
- country and global exposure ceilings;
- debt forgiveness;
- material guarantees;
- related-party financing;
- exceptional concessions;
- association framework agreements;
- changes to core brand, safety, technology, or audit requirements;
- pilot stop-loss thresholds.

## Global Royalty and Resilience Steering Committee

Responsible for:

- formulas and country parameters;
- market classifications;
- financing partners;
- impairment assumptions;
- transition methodology;
- global concentration limits;
- cross-market consistency.

## Country committees

Approve routine relief, financing packages, operating restructurings, transfers, and closures within delegated authority.

## Independent review panel

Reviews disputed hardship, exceptional relief, and alleged unequal treatment using verified financial information.

No executive who negotiated a material concession should be the sole approver of a related-party loan or exception.

## Dispute process

1. Store-level review within 10 business days.
2. Country finance and operations review within a further 15 business days.
3. Country council or independent-panel review.
4. Mediation.
5. Arbitration or court proceedings where legally permitted.

Undisputed amounts remain payable. Disputed amounts may be escrowed or placed in a controlled account where legally available.

# 13. KPIs and Scenario Triggers

## KPIs

### Franchisee viability

- Real same-store sales.
- Pre-royalty contribution margin.
- Cash-flow coverage.
- Payroll, tax, and supplier arrears.
- Closures, transfers, and renewals.
- Migration between Green, Amber, Red, and Black categories.

### Franchisor economics

- Royalty collections in local, constant-currency, and real terms.
- Deferred royalty balance and recovery rate.
- Aged receivables and expected credit losses.
- Financing utilization, defaults, guarantees, and losses.
- Brand, technology, training, marketing, and field-support funding.
- Country, franchisee-group, and related-party concentration.

### Brand and compliance

- Food-safety incidents.
- Audit scores.
- Customer satisfaction.
- POS reporting completeness.
- Unauthorized sourcing or menu changes.
- Maintenance and training compliance.

## Illustrative pilot gates

Before launch, baselines must be frozen and, where feasible, compared with matched historical or control cohorts. Initial gates may include:

- closure rates no more than two percentage points above baseline;
- at least 20% reduction in aged royalty arrears within twelve months;
- independent verification of at least 95% of relief cases;
- credit losses below 5% of franchisor-supported exposure;
- deferred balances below approved country ceilings;
- no material deterioration in food safety, audits, maintenance, training, or customer standards;
- real royalty and brand-support funding above the board-approved floor;
- no material legal, tax, competition, accounting, or regulatory findings.

These thresholds should be calibrated by country and product before launch.

## Stop-loss triggers

Pause or redesign a country program if:

- deferred balances exceed 125% of the approved ceiling;
- credit losses exceed the approved limit;
- material reporting manipulation appears in more than 5% of relief cases;
- critical food-safety or audit failures increase materially;
- franchisor cash commitments exceed budget by more than 15%;
- country or franchisee-group concentration exceeds approved limits;
- coordinated nonpayment cannot be contained lawfully.

## Scenario triggers and responses

| Scenario | Trigger | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Severe depreciation | Defined rolling FX depreciation plus outlet cash-flow impact | Suspend hard-currency minimums; cap indexation; offer deferral or minimum reduction |
| High inflation | Inflation exceeds country band and sales lag cost growth | Reduce or suspend minimum indexation; apply affordability ceiling |
| Recession | Real sales and contribution margin decline without major FX shock | Activate sales and cash-flow relief; prioritize store-specific restructuring |
| Remittance freeze | Cross-border charge cannot legally or practically be paid | Local settlement, deposit, financing, suspension, or waiver |
| Recovery | Traffic and margins recover above defined thresholds | Restore standard rates gradually; repay deferred amounts through capped cash-flow sweeps |
| Collective withholding | Coordinated royalty nonpayment | Convene council; distinguish compliant and noncompliant outlets; preserve lawful remedies |
| Franchisor liquidity stress | Program exposure or losses exceed approved capacity | Pause new franchisor lending; prioritize third-party finance; tighten limits |

Stress testing should include correlated and repeated shocks, such as depreciation plus food shortages, high interest rates plus refinancing failure, remittance freeze plus supplier disruption, and simultaneous distress across multiple countries.

# 14. Key Decisions Required

Management and the board should approve:

1. Replacement of routine fixed-USD royalties with market-specific local-currency hybrid royalties.
2. The royalty formula based on percentage sales, a capped soft minimum, and affordability protection.
3. The complete franchisor-fee ceiling and payment-priority framework.
4. The country-specific segmentation of Egypt, Nigeria, Turkey, Brazil, and Indonesia.
5. Immediate interim relief in qualifying acute-stress markets.
6. Creation of the Franchise Resilience Finance Program.
7. The hierarchy of franchisee equity, third-party finance, and franchisor support.
8. Country and global exposure limits for deferrals, loans, guarantees, and interest subsidies.
9. Formal country franchise councils and competition-law protocols.
10. Treatment of stable-currency company-owned markets.
11. Transition rules from fixed-USD contracts, including phase-in caps and arrears treatment.
12. The minimum brand-support funding floor.
13. The six-month design, back-testing, and pilot phase.
14. Quantified pilot gates, stop-loss thresholds, and scale/no-scale authority.
15. Mandatory local legal, tax, accounting, FX, payments, competition, data, franchise, labor, lending, and insolvency sign-offs before implementation.

## Recommended end state

> **Local-currency participation in local sales, protected by a capped soft minimum, constrained by a complete payment-capacity test, supplemented only by documented cross-border cost recovery, and supported by disciplined financing and time-limited relief.**

This structure gives viable franchisees a practical path through currency depreciation, inflation, demand contraction, collective bargaining pressure, and expensive credit while preserving the franchisor’s long-term brand economics, operating control, brand funding, and portfolio risk discipline.
