## For a 30-Country Fully Remote Software Company (2026)

## DOCUMENT 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (1 Page)

### GLOBAL COMPENSATION POLICY – KEY DECISIONS & RATIONALE

**Company Context:** 450-person software company; fully remote; operations across 30 countries spanning Tiers 1–3 cost-of-living zones.

**Problem Statement:** Location-adjusted pay bands generate internal friction (salary-forum comparisons), regulatory exposure (30+ jurisdictions with pay-transparency mandates), and fairness-perception challenges (employees comparing nominal salaries across borders without context).

**Policy Framework: "Transparent-But-Contextual Compensation"**

The company adopts **location-adjusted pay with published, auditable methodology** to balance three objectives:

1. **Cost-Justification:** Pay reflects real purchasing-power differences (€60K Warsaw ≠ €60K Toronto)
2. **Market Competitiveness:** Target 60th percentile of local market (attract top talent, not undercut)
3. **Internal Fairness:** Role definitions, seniority levels, and career progression are location-agnostic

---

### KEY POLICY DECISIONS

| **Decision** | **Rationale** | **Expected Impact** |
|---|---|---|
| **4-Tier Location Structure** (Tier 1: 100%; Tier 2: 75–85%; Tier 3: 55–70%; Tier 4: 45–55%) | Defensible on published COL indices (EDIN), local salary medians (Payscale/LinkedIn), PPP adjustments. Replaces opaque "market rate" with transparent methodology. | Salary disputes shift from "Why do I earn 30% less?" to "I understand the math; I disagree with the COL index." Easier to defend legally. |
| **Role-Level Consistency (Same Title = Same Work Globally, Only Currency Varies)** | A "Backend Engineer, Mid-Level" in Warsaw and Toronto perform identical work. Seniority level (L1–L5), competencies, and decision authority are location-agnostic. | Eliminates perception that location determines job worth. Career paths transparent globally. |
| **Published Pay-Band Cards (Per Role, Per Tier)** | Every employee sees salary range, position-in-band, and justification (role seniority, specialist skills, performance, local market percentile). | Process legitimacy increases perceived fairness even if outcome is unfavorable (research: Huseman, Adams). |
| **Non-Location-Adjusted Equity & Benefits** | Equity grants (0.15%–0.35%/year) and wellness budgets ($4K/year) are identical globally. Equity appreciates regardless of location; benefits have higher purchasing power in lower-COL countries. | Offsets lower cash in Tier 3; creates parity in long-term wealth accumulation. Reduces resentment of lower base salaries. |
| **Quarterly COL Audits + Annual Formal Rebalancing** | Bands stay current with economic data; prevent calcification. Material changes (>3% shift) communicated 90 days in advance. | Compliance risk drops: bands are defensible as current, not stale. Employees see company keeps commitments ("They said they'd update; they did"). |
| **Proactive Disclosure (Salary Ranges in Job Postings; Annual Pay-Gap Audits; Written Pay Justifications)** | Move from reactive (respond to employee requests) to proactive (publish ranges, audit internally, offer transparency pre-emptively). | Legal risk reduced (shows good-faith compliance, not evasion). Recruitment advantage (candidates see transparency, attracts values-aligned talent). |
| **Annual Compensation Appeal Window (30-Day Employee Request Period; 40-Day HR Response)** | Structured, low-friction mechanism for employees to challenge pay fairness; HR provides written explanation within 40 days. | Reduces escalation to external legal claims. Builds trust ("Company listens"). Identifies systemic pay issues early. |
| **Manager Training + Forum Monitoring** | All managers trained quarterly on compensation communication. HR monitors Glassdoor/Levels.fyi; responds within 48 hours with contextual explanation (not defensive). | Reduces salary-forum narrative from becoming company gospel. Managers trusted as source, not Finance black box. |

## SECTION 1: LOCATION-ADJUSTED PAY METHODOLOGY

### 1.1 – THE 4-TIER STRUCTURE & MULTIPLIERS

#### **A. Tier Definitions**

```
TIER 1 – PREMIUM TECH HUBS (100% Baseline)
Countries: Canada, Germany, Switzerland, Netherlands, UK, USA
Characteristics: Highest cost of living; strongest tech labor markets; established talent competition
Multiplier: 100% (baseline against which all others are calculated)
Use Case: Anchors entire system; establishes floor for all market comparisons

TIER 2 – MID-PREMIUM / EMERGING HUBS (75–85% of Baseline)
Countries: Portugal, Poland, Spain, Czechia, Costa Rica, Ireland
Characteristics: 20–35% lower cost of living than Tier 1; growing tech talent pools; regional talent competition
Multiplier: 75–85% (varies by country, refreshed annually)
Use Case: Second-largest employee base; strategic talent acquisition for scaling
Example: Poland = 75% (35% lower COL than Germany) | Portugal = 80% (20% lower COL)

TIER 3 – GROWTH MARKETS (55–70% of Baseline)
Countries: Vietnam, Colombia, India, Philippines, Romania, Mexico
Characteristics: 45–65% lower COL; emerging tech talent; strategic growth regions for company
Multiplier: 55–70% (varies by country; refreshed annually)
Use Case: Rapid scaling; access to large talent pools; long-term strategy to build regional hubs
Example: India = 60% (40% lower COL) | Vietnam = 65% (35% lower COL)

TIER 4 – SUBSISTENCE PROTECTION (45–55%, Rarely Used)
Countries: Reserved for extreme cases (only if company operates in high-inflation/high-risk economy)
Characteristics: Legal minimum wage + purchasing-power floor (ensures no exploitation)
Multiplier: 45–55% (requires CFO + CHRO approval per hire)
Use Case: Exceptional circumstances; rare in 30-country operation
```

#### **B. Transparent Multiplier Calculation (Published Annually)**

**Every Q1, publish detailed calculation showing how each tier's multiplier was determined:**

```
TIER MULTIPLIER DETERMINATION – 2026 EXAMPLE
(Poland/Tier 2: From Germany/Tier 1 Baseline)

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 1: COST-OF-LIVING INDEX
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Data Source: EDIN Q4 2025 (Economist Intelligence Unit)
             Numbeo Regional Report Q4 2025 (validation)

Germany (Baseline):                    COL Index = 100
Poland:                                COL Index = 65
Interpretation:                        Poland is 35% lower cost of living

Reconciliation (if sources diverge >3%):
└─ EDIN: 65 | Numbeo: 67 | Average: 66 (use 66; document decision)

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 2: LOCAL TECH SALARY MEDIAN (By Role, By Seniority)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Data Sources: Payscale, LinkedIn Salary Reports, Local Recruitment Surveys
              (all Q4 2025 data, aggregated equally)

Example Role: Senior Backend Engineer

Germany:
├─ Payscale: €78,000
├─ LinkedIn Salary: €75,500
├─ Local recruitment firms (survey): €76,500
└─ AVERAGE: €76,667

Poland:
├─ Payscale: €52,000
├─ LinkedIn Salary: €51,500
├─ Local recruitment firms (survey): €54,000
└─ AVERAGE: €52,500

Ratio: €52,500 / €76,667 = 68.4%

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 3: PURCHASING-POWER PARITY (PPP) VALIDATION
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Data Source: World Bank PPP Converter 2025; IMF PPP Estimates

Question: Is local salary median justified by purchasing-power differences?

Germany: €1.00 nominal = €1.00 real purchasing power (baseline)
Poland:  €1.00 nominal = €1.54 real purchasing power (35% higher effective value)

Translation Test:
€76,667 Germany (nominal) = ?× PPP in Poland
= €76,667 ÷ 1.54 = €49,789 Poland equivalent

Compare: Poland actual median = €52,500; PPP equivalent = €49,789
Conclusion: Poland median is ~5% higher than PPP-equivalent (Poland 
            engineers commanding slight premium above COL-adjusted rate)
            → Rational due to high demand for tech talent in Warsaw

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 4: COMPANY POSITIONING STRATEGY
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Strategy: Pay at 60th percentile of local market (attract top talent; 
          remain sustainable relative to company profitability)

Germany Positioning (Tier 1 Baseline):
├─ Local median: €76,667
├─ Our target: 70th percentile = €76,667 × 1.10 = €84,333
├─ Selected baseline: €84,000 (rounded)
└─ Rationale: "Premium Tech Hub" justifies above-median pay (top-talent acquisition)

Poland Positioning (Tier 2):
├─ Local median: €52,500
├─ Our target: 60th percentile = €52,500 × 1.20 = €63,000
├─ Derived multiplier: €63,000 / €84,000 = 75%
└─ Rationale: Above-market pay in Poland while respecting cost-of-living difference

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 5: FINAL MULTIPLIER DETERMINATION
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Inputs:
├─ COL-based indicator: 65–66% (Poland 35% lower COL)
├─ Market-based indicator: 68–69% (Poland salaries 68% of Germany)
├─ Company strategy: 75% (ensures 60th percentile Polish positioning)

Decision Logic:
Our multiplier (75%) is HIGHER than raw market ratio (68%) because:
├─ We value top talent (willing to pay above raw market)
├─ We believe Poland market is growing; early premium attracts pioneers
└─ Company profitability supports 75% (not 68%)

Validation:
├─ 75% is defensible (between 65% COL-based floor, 80% market-sustainability ceiling)
├─ Company can sustain it (margin analysis: yes, at planned headcount)
└─ Employees will accept it (market-data transparency + high relative pay in Poland)

FINAL MULTIPLIER FOR TIER 2 (POLAND): 75%
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

CHANGE LOG:
2025 (Prior Year):        Tier 2 Poland = 78%
2026 (Current):           Tier 2 Poland = 75%
Reason:                   Poland COL rose 3% (now 62 vs. 65); local salaries grew 5%
                          (now 68% vs. prior 66%); company margin slightly tighter
Effective Date:           April 1, 2026
Communication:            All Tier 2 Poland employees notified Jan 15, 2026 (90-day advance)
Employee Impact:          Those at band minimum (€47,250) see auto-raise to new minimum
                          (€48,750); no one's salary decreases

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

**Publication:** This calculation is published internally (company intranet) and shared pre-offer with candidates. It demonstrates intellectual honesty and makes the policy defensible in regulatory audits or litigation.

### 1.2 – ROLE-LEVEL CONSISTENCY & SENIORITY STRUCTURE

**A. Location-Agnostic Role Definitions:**

The same job title (Backend Engineer, Mid-Level) is defined identically across all 30 countries in terms of:
- **Competencies required** (systems design, languages, frameworks)
- **Scope of responsibility** (service ownership, team mentorship, decision authority)
- **Experience floor** (years in role, depth of expertise)
- **Performance expectations** (code quality, delivery reliability, collaboration)

**What changes across locations: Only the currency amount and location-adjusted multiplier, NOT the role itself.**

**B. Seniority Levels (Global Consistency):**

```
L1 – ENTRY / JUNIOR
├─ Experience: 0–2 years
├─ Scope: Contributes under supervision; learning-focused
├─ Responsibility: Feature implementation (small scope); code review feedback
├─ Compensation: Base band only (no seniority premium)
└─ Example Backend Engineer, Poland: €47,250–€52,000

L2 – MID-LEVEL (Reference Tier)
├─ Experience: 3–5 years
├─ Scope: Owns service/feature end-to-end
├─ Responsibility: Mentors 1–2 juniors; participates in design reviews; service reliability owner
├─ Compensation: Base band (reference tier for all multiplier calculations)
└─ Example Backend Engineer, Poland: €47,250–€63,000

L3 – SENIOR
├─ Experience: 5–8 years
├─ Scope: Owns multiple services; sets technical direction for area
├─ Responsibility: Mentors 2–4 engineers; leads design reviews; architecture decisions
├─ Compensation: Base band × 1.25 (25% role premium above L2)
└─ Example Backend Engineer, Poland: €59,063–€78,750

L4 – STAFF
├─ Experience: 8+ years
├─ Scope: Owns technical strategy across multiple teams
├─ Responsibility: Mentors 5+ engineers; shapes engineering culture; org-wide initiatives
├─ Compensation: Base band × 1.50 (50% role premium above L2)
└─ Example Backend Engineer, Poland: €70,875–€94,500

L5 – PRINCIPAL (Rare; Exceptional Talent)
├─ Experience: 12+ years
├─ Scope: Sets technical direction for entire engineering org
├─ Responsibility: Industry thought leader; external visibility; rare
├─ Compensation: Base band × 1.75 (75% role premium above L2)
└─ Example Backend Engineer, Poland: €82,688–€110,250
```

**Key Principle:** Seniority multipliers are **location-agnostic**. An L3 in Warsaw earns 1.25× the L2 base in Warsaw. An L3 in Toronto earns 1.25× the L2 base in Toronto. The multiplier is consistent; only the base amount differs.
