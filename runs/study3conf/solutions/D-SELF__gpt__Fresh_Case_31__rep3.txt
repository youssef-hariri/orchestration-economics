# Global Compensation Policy — 2026

## Executive position

Adopt a **globally consistent, location-informed compensation system with mandatory country supplements**.

The company will use one global job architecture and a common decision framework, while allowing salary differences based on approved labor-market evidence, role scope, level, and work location. Pay will not be identical across countries, but differences must be:

- Documented and job-related;
- Consistently applied;
- Explainable to employees;
- Reviewed for internal and legal pay equity;
- Adapted where local law, contracts, collective agreements, or employee-representation rights require.

**Jurisdiction-specific legal validation is required before implementation in each country.** This policy is a global design standard, not a determination that any particular practice complies with the laws of a specific jurisdiction.

---

# 1. Objectives and policy hierarchy

## Objectives

The framework is designed to:

1. Attract and retain appropriately skilled employees.
2. Pay competitively against relevant labor markets.
3. Recognize legitimate geographic and employment-cost differences.
4. Reduce reliance on negotiation and manager discretion.
5. Support credible progression, promotion, and mobility.
6. Identify and remediate unexplained pay gaps and compression.
7. Provide meaningful transparency while protecting confidential data.
8. Improve budget predictability across 30 countries.

## Priority order

When objectives conflict, decisions should generally be prioritized as follows:

1. Applicable law, contracts, collective agreements, and employee-representation rights;
2. Equal-pay and nondiscrimination principles;
3. Internal consistency and explainability;
4. Sustainable labor-market competitiveness;
5. Recruiting and retention effectiveness;
6. Budget affordability;
7. Administrative simplicity.

Material decisions must document the competing objectives, evidence considered, outcome, decision owner, and any review date.

---

# 2. Guiding principles

## Equal or comparable work

The company seeks to provide equal pay for equal or comparable work. Differences must be supported by documented, job-related factors, including:

- Job family and level;
- Scope and sustained responsibilities;
- Relevant qualifications and experience;
- Sustained performance, where lawfully and reliably measured;
- Approved work location;
- Documented market scarcity;
- Legal, contractual, or collectively agreed requirements.

Pay must not be based on protected characteristics, retaliation, informal favoritism, manager preference, or salary history where prohibited or otherwise inappropriate.

Internal job levels are management tools and may not be identical to the legal comparator used in every country.

## Location informs pay but does not determine employee value

Location may affect pay because labor markets, recruiting competition, and employer costs differ. It is not a measure of capability, contribution, nationality, ethnicity, or personal worth.

The company will distinguish:

- **Market price of labor:** the primary salary-setting input;
- **Employer cost:** salary plus employer taxes, statutory benefits, payroll, and other employment expenses;
- **Cost of living:** relevant to mobility and employee-relations discussions, but not the primary salary-setting measure;
- **Strategic labor-market access:** whether the company competes locally, regionally, or globally for a role.

## Transparency and explainability

Employees should understand:

- Their job family and level;
- Their applicable salary band;
- How geography affects the band;
- How employees progress within the band;
- How promotions, market reviews, and relocations work;
- How to request a review or appeal.

## No automatic entitlement

A band is an approved range, not a guarantee of midpoint or maximum pay. Placement depends on capability, sustained scope, experience, market position, performance where validly measured, and internal equity.

## Local requirements control

This policy is subject to local law, contracts, collective agreements, employee-representation rights, privacy requirements, and payroll, tax, benefits, immigration, and remote-work rules. Where requirements differ, the applicable local process or rule controls following local legal review.

# 3. Global job architecture

Every role will be mapped to:

- Job family;
- Discipline or specialty;
- Career track;
- Level;
- Standard scope;
- Capability expectations;
- Decision-making and organizational impact.

Titles alone do not determine level or pay.

Illustrative engineering levels:

| Level | Typical scope |
|---|---|
| E2 | Delivers defined work with regular guidance |
| E3 | Independently owns components and solves routine-to-complex problems |
| E4 | Leads substantial technical work and influences adjacent teams |
| E5 | Sets direction across systems or multiple teams |
| E6 | Shapes company-wide technical strategy and standards |

Existing employees will be mapped through documented calibration involving managers, functional leaders, Compensation, and appropriate local or regional reviewers. Employees will receive their mapping and may request review.

# 4. Pay-band methodology

## Two-layer model

Each salary band will be based on:

1. A global reference midpoint for the job family and level; and
2. A country or approved labor-market adjustment.

A simplified internal model is:

\[
\text{Local midpoint}_{j,c}
=
\text{Global midpoint}_{j}
\times
\text{Geographic factor}_{j,c}
\times
\text{Approved market or scarcity adjustment}_{j,c}
\]

This is an internal governance model. It does not independently establish legal compliance or an employee’s entitlement.

## Role-based labor-market segmentation

| Role type | Default pricing approach |
|---|---|
| Locally abundant roles | Local country market |
| Regionally competitive roles | Regional market with local validation |
| Globally scarce roles | Global or multinational remote-market data with controlled location adjustment |
| Customer- or time-zone-dependent roles | Market relevant to supported customers or operations |
| Executive or highly specialized roles | Individual market assessment with enhanced governance |

The category must be documented by job family and level, not selected informally for individual employees.

## Market-data sources

Sources may include:

- Reputable compensation surveys;
- Recruiting outcomes and offer acceptance;
- Time to hire and candidate availability;
- Regrettable attrition;
- Local technology-market data;
- Remote-first and multinational employer data;
- Currency, inflation, and employer-cost information.

Each benchmark must record:

- Source and methodology;
- Survey date and effective date;
- Job-match quality;
- Sample-size limitations;
- Cash and equity treatment;
- Geographic relevance;
- Currency;
- Market percentile;
- Confidence rating.

Low-confidence data may inform review but should not be presented to employees as a precise economic fact.

## Data governance

The Head of Compensation owns the pricing methodology and market-data inventory. The operating standard must define:

- Approved sources and source hierarchy;
- Minimum data recency;
- Job-match requirements;
- Data-aging methodology;
- Treatment of conflicting sources and outliers;
- Minimum reliability standards;
- Currency conversion;
- Confidence ratings;
- Approval requirements for low-confidence data;
- Version control and audit history.

The methodology will be back-tested against offer acceptance, hiring time, attrition, and retention outcomes.

# 5. Geographic differentiation

## Country bands as the default

Country-level bands will be used initially because they are understandable and manageable across 30 countries.

A city or labor-market zone may be introduced only when:

- Reliable data demonstrates a persistent, material difference;
- The difference reflects the actual recruiting market;
- There is a sufficient employee or candidate population;
- Legal, fairness, budget, and administrative effects have been assessed;
- The Global Compensation Council approves the change.

## Geographic-factor controls

Before implementation, the company must approve:

- A normal factor floor and ceiling;
- A maximum annual movement;
- Enhanced approval for large changes;
- A review trigger for material differences between countries with substantial employee interchange;
- Minimum data-confidence standards;
- An adverse-impact review;
- Treatment of existing employees when factors change.

Exact values require company data, budget modeling, employee-impact analysis, and local legal review. Thresholds are management controls, not legal safe harbors.

## Overlapping bands

Bands may overlap intentionally. This supports:

- Progression within a role;
- Recognition of experience and scope;
- Mobility;
- Scarce-skill premiums;
- Reduced false precision.

A higher-paid employee in a lower-priced market may overlap with a newer employee in a higher-priced market. The difference must still be explainable and consistently applied.

## Scarcity and assignment premiums

A role-based scarcity premium, customer-market premium, or temporary assignment premium must be separately coded from the geographic factor. It requires eligibility criteria, evidence, approval, effective date, review date, and expiration or continuation rationale.

# 6. Compensation components

The company will separately define the treatment of:

- Base salary;
- Bonus and variable compensation;
- Equity;
- Benefits;
- Statutory allowances;
- Sign-on and retention awards;
- Mobility and assignment payments.

Local legal review must determine which components:

- Are included in equal-pay analysis;
- Must appear in candidate disclosures;
- Must be disclosed to employees;
- Are governed by collective agreements or statutory rules.

Benefits or equity must not be used as an unexplained justification for a base-pay difference.

---

# 7. Progression, merit, and promotions

## Within-level progression

Progression is based on sustained growth in:

- Capability;
- Scope;
- Independence;
- Impact;
- Relevant experience;
- Performance, where validly measured;
- Market position and internal equity.

Time in role alone does not guarantee an increase, but employees must receive clear development expectations.

## Annual review

Every increase must be coded by purpose:

- Merit;
- Promotion;
- Market;
- Inflation;
- Statutory or collectively agreed increase;
- Pay-equity correction;
- Compression correction;
- Mobility;
- Retention;
- Administrative correction.

This separation supports accurate budgeting and prevents equity corrections from being hidden in general merit budgets.

## Promotions

A promotion requires evidence that the employee consistently performs the higher-level scope or is entering a role with that scope.

The process includes job-architecture review, functional calibration, Compensation review, confirmation of the new band, and written employee communication. A promoted employee should not remain below the new range minimum without a documented remediation plan.

---

# 8. Hiring and offer governance

Offers must be based on:

- Approved job family and level;
- Approved work location;
- Applicable band;
- Relevant qualifications and experience;
- Documented market conditions;
- Internal equity;
- Approved total-reward structure.

Salary history must not be requested or used where prohibited. Even where permitted, it should not be the primary basis for an offer.

Out-of-band offers require:

- Written rationale;
- Market evidence;
- Compensation approval;
- Comparison with incumbents;
- Pay-equity assessment;
- Local legal review where required.

Repeated individual counteroffers should not replace a population-level market adjustment.

---

# 9. Internal fairness and pay equity

## Monitoring

The company will monitor:

- New-hire compression;
- Promotion compression;
- Market compression;
- Geographic compression;
- Employees below range minimum;
- Out-of-band salaries;
- Unexplained peer differences;
- Hiring, promotion, rating, and attrition patterns.

Compa-ratio and range penetration identify review populations; they do not automatically determine pay.

## Comparator lenses

The company will use three distinct comparator lenses:

1. **Internal compensation comparator:** job family, level, location, scope, and relevant experience;
2. **Legal comparator:** the jurisdiction-specific equal-pay or work-of-equal-value standard;
3. **Business comparator:** employees competing for the same labor market, customer market, or scarce skill.

The applicable lens must be documented for each review.

## Pay-equity analysis

At least annually, and more often where required or triggered, the company will assess:

- Unadjusted and adjusted pay gaps;
- Job family and level;
- Location;
- Experience and tenure;
- Employment status and hours;
- Performance, where valid;
- Hiring and promotion outcomes;
- Attrition;
- Access to high-visibility work.

Small populations, missing data, temporary premiums, unusual compensation structures, and legal restrictions on demographic data must be documented in the analysis.

## Remediation

A separate reserve will address:

- Unexplained pay gaps;
- Employees below range minimum;
- Severe compression;
- Promotion-related underpayment;
- Material market misalignment.

Each remediation plan must specify the affected population, amount, timing, owner, budget, legal and tax review, employee communication, and follow-up validation. Remediation may not be deferred indefinitely without documented executive approval.

---

# 10. Market updates, currency, and inflation

## Annual review

Bands and factors will normally be reviewed annually using:

- Updated market data;
- Recruiting and offer outcomes;
- Attrition;
- Inflation;
- Currency;
- Employer-cost changes;
- Role scarcity;
- Pay-equity results.

Changes must document the effective date, affected populations, budget effect, equity effect, and legal or consultation review.

## Currency

The company may maintain the compensation architecture in a reference currency while paying in local currency. Annual planning should use an approved rolling exchange-rate convention rather than a single spot rate.

Reports must distinguish:

- Local-currency salary movement;
- FX translation;
- Headcount effect;
- Geographic-mix effect;
- Employer-cost effect.

FX movement alone does not automatically create a salary adjustment.

## Inflation and statutory changes

Inflation is reviewed separately from merit and market movement. It may trigger a general review, market adjustment, temporary allowance, or no action depending on market evidence and budget.

Mandatory statutory or collectively agreed increases must be applied where required.

## Off-cycle reviews

An off-cycle review may be triggered by:

- Material labor-market movement;
- Significant inflation;
- Major currency movement;
- Statutory changes;
- Deteriorating offer acceptance;
- Regrettable attrition;
- Major changes in remote recruiting competition.

---

# 11. Transparency and legal handling

## Internal transparency

Subject to local law, employees will receive:

- Job family;
- Level;
- Current salary and currency;
- Applicable band;
- Location methodology;
- Pay-setting criteria;
- Progression and promotion rules;
- Review and appeal process.

The company may provide a pay-position indicator if the underlying data is accurate and clearly explained.

## Candidate transparency

Job postings should identify, where required and operationally feasible:

- Base salary range;
- Currency and pay frequency;
- Work-location assumptions;
- Variable-pay eligibility;
- Equity eligibility;
- Benefits summary;
- Circumstances in which an offer may differ from the range.

A multi-country posting must not obscure a jurisdiction-specific disclosure requirement.

## Country compliance matrix

The company will maintain a country and relevant subnational matrix covering:

- Salary-range disclosure;
- Timing and required content;
- Employee information and comparator rights;
- Pay-gap reporting;
- Salary-history restrictions;
- Pay-discussion protections;
- Consultation and representative rights;
- Local-language requirements;
- Recordkeeping;
- Privacy and data-transfer requirements;
- Reporting ownership and deadlines.

The matrix must be reviewed by qualified local counsel or an authorized local specialist before use.

## Country launch certificate

No country may launch until the country owner documents:

- Legal and transparency requirements;
- Consultation or collective-bargaining status;
- Contract-change constraints;
- Payroll, tax, benefits, currency, and immigration review;
- Privacy and data-transfer approval;
- Local-language and recordkeeping requirements;
- Market-data limitations;
- Required remediation budget;
- Employee communications and appeal process;
- Written legal review date and next review date.

# 12. Employee movement and remote work

## Approved work location

The company will distinguish:

- Primary work location;
- Residence;
- Tax residence;
- Payroll country;
- Employing entity;
- Temporary travel location.

The approved work location normally determines the applicable band, subject to role and market analysis.

## Permanent relocation

Before approving a move, the company must assess employment-entity feasibility, payroll, tax, social insurance, benefits, immigration, permanent-establishment risk, data security, customer requirements, contracts, consultation, and the destination band.

## Higher-cost relocation

A move to a higher-priced labor market may result in movement toward the destination band after the required review and at the first legally and operationally feasible payroll date.

## Lower-cost relocation

A lower-cost move does not automatically reduce salary.

The default global approach is:

1. Protect existing base salary for six months, subject to local law and contract terms;
2. Apply the destination band to future compensation decisions after the protection period;
3. If salary exceeds the destination maximum, use a lawful freeze or phased alignment;
4. Preserve level and promotion eligibility.

Any reduction requires country-specific review for consent, notice, consultation, collective-agreement, discrimination, and contractual requirements. Local rules may require a different approach.

## Temporary remote work

Temporary work from another country requires advance approval. The company must define permitted duration, tax and immigration checks, benefits and insurance treatment, data-security conditions, salary treatment, and the process for an unapproved permanent move.

# 13. Exceptions

Permitted exceptions include:

- Critical or scarce skills;
- Verified retention risk;
- Material role expansion pending re-leveling;
- Executive or highly specialized roles;
- Temporary assignment or market premium;
- Acquisition or integration protection;
- Legal, contractual, statutory, or collectively agreed requirements;
- Documented hiring constraints.

Every exception must record:

- Objective rationale;
- Supporting evidence;
- Affected compensation component;
- Comparable-employee review;
- Pay-equity effect;
- Approvals;
- Effective date;
- Expiration or review date.

Exceptions should normally expire within six to twelve months unless a continuing basis is documented. Individual retention adjustments require review of similarly situated employees.

# 14. Governance

## Global Compensation Council

The Council will include Compensation, People, Finance, Legal, Payroll, Tax, Recruiting, Global Mobility, Privacy, Employee Relations, and regional representatives. Works council or union specialists will participate where relevant.

Responsibilities include:

- Approving job architecture;
- Approving market methodology;
- Approving geographic factors and band widths;
- Reviewing pay equity and compression;
- Approving remediation;
- Reviewing exceptions;
- Approving global communications;
- Monitoring country readiness and risk.

## Decision rights

| Decision | Accountable owner |
|---|---|
| Job architecture | Head of Compensation |
| Market methodology | Head of Compensation |
| Geographic factors and band widths | Global Compensation Council |
| Annual budget | CFO or delegated executive |
| Out-of-band offer | Compensation |
| Promotion | Functional leader with Compensation calibration |
| Retention exception | Compensation executive |
| Relocation | Global Mobility |
| Country launch | Country compliance owner |
| Employee appeal | Independent review owner |
| Public disclosure | Legal and People |

Appeals must be reviewed by someone not involved in the original decision. A written escalation route must resolve disagreements among Compensation, Finance, Legal, and local leadership.

# 15. Communications

Managers will receive:

- Approved talking points;
- Individual pay-position guidance;
- A prohibited-statements list;
- Escalation procedures;
- Training on confidentiality, bias, and pay discussions.

Managers must not:

- Reveal another employee’s pay;
- Attribute pay to personal living costs or nationality;
- Use salary history informally;
- Make unverified scarcity claims;
- Promise future increases;
- Discourage lawful compensation concerns;
- Create undocumented exceptions;
- Speculate about legal rights.

Employees will receive:

- The policy summary;
- Their level and applicable band;
- Location methodology;
- Progression and review rules;
- Mobility rules;
- Appeal channels;
- Applicable country supplement;
- Notice of material changes affecting them.

## Public salary comparisons

The company will not improperly monitor or retaliate against employees for lawful pay discussions.

Concerns will be triaged as:

- Individual payroll or policy error;
- Band or methodology concern;
- Potential pay-equity pattern;
- Public misinformation.

The company will collect only necessary information, protect confidentiality, investigate through approved channels, correct confirmed errors, and communicate only information it may lawfully disclose.

# 16. Implementation phases

## Phase 1: Inventory and legal assessment — months 0–2

- Inventory countries, entities, employees, contractors, and representative bodies;
- Map salaries, benefits, currencies, contracts, and locations;
- Build the country compliance matrix;
- Assess privacy and cross-border data flows;
- Identify existing mobility and contractual constraints;
- Pause new ad hoc location formulas.

## Phase 2: Design and diagnostics — months 2–4

- Validate the job architecture;
- Map employees to levels;
- Select and score market data;
- Model geographic-factor alternatives;
- Run pay-equity, compression, and adverse-impact analyses;
- Estimate remediation cost;
- Model budget scenarios.

## Phase 3: Consultation and approval — months 4–6

- Consult works councils, unions, or representatives where required;
- Obtain local legal reviews;
- Approve bands, factors, mobility rules, exceptions, and reserves;
- Prepare local-language materials;
- Configure HRIS, payroll, case management, and employee self-service;
- Train managers.

## Phase 4: Tiered launch — months 6–9

- Launch first in Tier 1 countries;
- Provide individual employee information;
- Apply approved corrections;
- Update job postings;
- Activate review and appeal channels;
- Complete country launch certificates before each subsequent launch.

## Phase 5: Stabilization — months 9–12

- Audit compensation decisions;
- Review employee understanding and perceived fairness;
- Monitor offer acceptance, attrition, exceptions, mobility, and disclosures;
- Reassess geographic factors;
- Report outcomes to executives and the Compensation Council.

# 17. Metrics and success measures

The company will review at least annually, with quarterly monitoring for key populations:

## Market and workforce

- Offer acceptance;
- Time to hire;
- Regrettable attrition;
- Retention by compa-ratio and location;
- Critical-role vacancy duration.

## Fairness and compression

- Adjusted and unadjusted pay gaps;
- Employees below range minimum;
- New-hire compression;
- Promotion compression;
- Geographic-factor effects;
- Remediation completion;
- Exception volume and expiry.

## Employee experience

- Understanding of pay methodology;
- Perceived fairness;
- Appeal volume;
- Appeal resolution time;
- Public-comparison themes;
- Manager-training completion.

## Financial and operational

- Salary cost;
- Fully loaded employment cost;
- FX and geographic-mix effects;
- Remediation spend;
- Mobility cost;
- Budget variance;
- Disclosure accuracy;
- Consultation completion;
- Privacy or recordkeeping incidents.

Statistical results below a threshold do not by themselves prove that the system is fair. Legal analysis, process consistency, employee experience, and outcome trends must also be considered.

# 18. Principal risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Unsupported geographic differentials | Use validated data, confidence ratings, factor limits, and adverse-impact review |
| Perceived unfairness between countries | Publish methodology, use overlapping bands, monitor sentiment, and remediate gaps |
| New-hire compression | Review offers against incumbents and use population-level market adjustments |
| Manager discretion and favoritism | Standardized levels, reason codes, calibration, approval controls, and audits |
| Legal noncompliance | Country launch certificate and jurisdiction-specific counsel review |
| Consultation or contractual breach | Complete required consultation, consent, notice, or amendments before launch |
| Privacy or data-transfer failure | Data mapping, role-based access, minimization, safeguards, and local review |
| Abrupt pay reductions after relocation | Six-month protection default and no reduction without local review |
| Budget volatility | Separate merit, market, inflation, FX, mobility, remediation, and employer-cost pools |
| Overly complex administration | Country-level default, role segmentation, tiered rollout, and controlled exceptions |

Assume a fictional Level E4 midpoint of 100 units and hypothetical local midpoints:

| Illustrative location | Hypothetical midpoint | Hypothetical range at 80%–120% |
|---|---:|---:|
| Location A | 100 | 80–120 |
| Location B | 90 | 72–108 |
| Location C | 80 | 64–96 |
| Location D | 75 | 60–90 |

The policy decision for any real location would require:

- Job and level validation;
- Relevant market data;
- Data-confidence assessment;
- Recruiting and attrition evidence;
- Internal-equity analysis;
- Budget modeling;
- Adverse-impact review;
- Jurisdiction-specific legal validation.

An employee near the top of a band may have greater sustained scope or scarce skills. A newly promoted employee may be near the lower portion of a new band. Neither location nor band position alone explains an individual salary.

# Final policy position

The company should retain location-informed pay, but replace unsupported multipliers, salary-history dependence, and negotiation-driven outcomes with:

> **Global job value and governance, evidence-based labor-market pricing, controlled geographic differentiation, overlapping bands, meaningful transparency, local legal adaptation, and documented remediation.**

This approach balances cost-of-labor logic with fairness perceptions and transparency obligations. It allows legitimate differences across a 30-country workforce while requiring that those differences be explainable, reviewable, financially modeled, and legally validated in every jurisdiction before implementation.
