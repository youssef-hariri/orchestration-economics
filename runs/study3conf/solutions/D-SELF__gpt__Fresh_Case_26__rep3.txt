# Global Seasonal Communications Framework  
## Faith-Based and Cultural Campaigns

## 1. Objectives

This framework governs campaigns associated with Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Lunar New Year, Diwali/Deepavali, and other culturally or religiously significant occasions.

The objectives are to:

- Represent faiths, cultures, languages, and communities accurately and respectfully.
- Give local teams meaningful authority over cultural interpretation and execution.
- Ensure the brand has a legitimate, useful role rather than exploiting seasonal attention.
- Apply consistent global standards without forcing identical creative across markets.
- Protect consumers, communities, partners, creators, and the brand from avoidable harm.
- Detect, pause, correct, and learn from criticism rapidly across borders.

The governing model is:

> **One global standard of conduct, evidence, and accountability; many locally credible expressions.**

Ramadan, Eid, Lunar New Year, Tết, Seollal, Diwali, Deepavali, Bandi Chhor Divas, and other observances must be treated as distinct occasions. They must not be managed as interchangeable “festive” campaigns.

---

# 2. Mandatory global principles

## 2.1 Participate without appropriating

The brand may support hospitality, connection, generosity, food sharing, family, and useful community activity. It must not present itself as the owner, interpreter, or source of a faith or culture.

The brand must not imply that buying, eating, gifting, or displaying its product:

- Demonstrates religious devotion or cultural belonging.
- Provides blessings, luck, prosperity, forgiveness, purity, or spiritual merit.
- Is necessary for valid observance.
- Replaces prayer, worship, charity, or community participation.

Use language such as **participate, support, contribute, represent,** and **work with**. Avoid language suggesting the brand creates, owns, activates, or delivers the occasion.

## 2.2 Classify the participation

Every campaign must be classified as one or more of:

1. Religious observance.
2. Cultural or family practice.
3. Seasonal commerce.
4. Community or charitable activity.
5. Mixed participation.

The highest-risk element determines the required operating mode and approval controls. For example, an ordinary family meal may be an Expanded activation, but adding prayer, scripture, a deity, or a worship setting moves the work into Exceptional review.

Do not use “celebration” as a default description. Some occasions involve fasting, prayer, reflection, remembrance, or solemn observance.

## 2.3 Local meaning before global templates

Each market must establish:

- What the occasion means locally.
- Which communities and audiences are addressed.
- Whether the communication is religious, cultural, commercial, community-focused, or mixed.
- Which practices, foods, symbols, languages, and settings are relevant.
- Which references are sensitive or prohibited.
- Why the product has a legitimate role.

Translation, local casting, color changes, traditional clothing, food, or festive decoration do not constitute localization by themselves.

## 2.4 Human and community value before product

The product must support a credible human or community truth.

Required tests:

> If the product and logo were removed, would the story still express a meaningful truth?

> What specific consumer or community need does the brand meet, and what evidence shows that this role is welcome rather than merely commercially convenient?

Evidence may include consumer research, documented local food practices, community-partner feedback, or a concrete benefit such as affordability, convenience, access, local employment, or verified support.

If either test is weak, revise or reject the concept.

## 2.5 Protect sacred content

Sacred content is presumptively excluded from mass-market food advertising. This includes:

- Prayer and worship.
- Scripture and religious recitation.
- Sacred objects, texts, gestures, and rites.
- Mosques, temples, shrines, and worship spaces.
- Deities and religious figures.
- Ancestor rites.
- Calls to prayer or sacred audio.
- Fasting used as a promotional device.

Use of sacred content requires all of the following:

1. The approved communication objective cannot be achieved without it.
2. Less sensitive alternatives were considered and rejected.
3. It is not being used merely to signal recognizability or seasonality.
4. Reviewers with defined, relevant expertise have assessed it.
5. Rights, permissions, participant consent, and safeguarding requirements are complete.
6. Residual risk and accountability are documented.

Permission alone does not establish cultural appropriateness. For this food brand, exclusion remains the default.

## 2.6 Represent diversity without flattening it

Representation should reflect the specified market and story, not a generic idea of diversity. Consider:

- Region, ethnicity, language, and diaspora identity.
- Age, gender, disability, class, and family structure.
- Religious observance and nonreligious cultural participation.
- Urban, rural, migrant, interfaith, and mixed-heritage contexts.

Each person should have narrative purpose and be represented as an individual, not a cultural symbol. Do not present one person, family, creator, advisor, or organization as representative of an entire community.

## 2.7 Make claims provable

Evidence is required for claims concerning:

- Halal or other dietary compliance.
- Ingredients, preparation, manufacturing, and allergens.
- Tradition, cultural authority, local credibility, or authenticity.
- Charity and community outcomes.
- Sustainability, safety, luck, prosperity, blessings, or religious benefit.

Use terms such as **locally credible** or **community-informed** only when the record shows how that judgment was established.

# 3. Scope and market inventory

Before strategy or creative development, the market team must create a controlled scope record identifying:

- Occasion and preferred local name.
- Countries and territories included.
- Target communities and audiences in each market.
- Whether the work is local, regional, global, or diaspora-facing.
- Paid-media targeting boundaries and likely cross-border exposure.
- Languages, scripts, dialects, and platforms.
- Paid, owned, earned, retail, packaging, ecommerce, influencer, PR, and customer-service applications.
- Local observance dates, launch windows, blackout periods, and transition dates.
- Whether the occasion is named explicitly.
- Community, creator, and charitable partners.
- Risk level and operating mode.
- Named market owners, gate leads, escalation contacts, and coverage hours.

No regional asset may be distributed until every receiving market is listed and the required controls are complete. Media-targeting, asset-management, retailer, and ecommerce controls should block unreviewed distribution wherever feasible.

# 4. Regional adaptation

Regional labels such as **GCC, North Africa, Southeast Asia,** or **Asia** do not replace country-level review where cultural, legal, linguistic, certification, or platform requirements differ.

## 4.1 Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr

Ramadan is a month of fasting, prayer, reflection, charity, and community. Iftar and suhoor are important food and social moments but must not become the sole frame.

Eid al-Fitr is a separate observance with distinct religious, family, gifting, hospitality, dress, travel, and retail dynamics. A Ramadan asset must not become an Eid asset simply by changing the caption or date.

The brief must state whether it addresses Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, or both.

### Gulf markets

Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, and Oman require separate review of:

- Arabic register and dialect.
- Religious-content and advertising rules.
- Public and private expressions of faith.
- Gender, family, dress, and modesty conventions.
- Public-space and daytime media suitability.
- Food abundance, luxury, waste, and charity expectations.
- Local Eid greetings, gifting, and celebration practices.

Mandatory controls:

- Review outdoor, retail, television, digital, and daytime placements for depictions of eating or drinking.
- Do not use generalized “Arabian” décor, luxury interiors, costumes, or accents as cultural shorthand.
- Review music, percussion, calls to prayer, and religious audio separately.
- Do not use Qur’anic text, mosque interiors, prayer scenes, or religious invocations as decoration.
- Confirm local dates through official sources and account for moon-sighting differences.
- Confirm charitable and community partners are appropriately licensed and that outcomes are evidenced.
- Develop separate Eid treatment where content continues beyond Ramadan.

### North Africa

Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, and Egypt require separate review of:

- Modern Standard Arabic, local dialects, French, Amazigh, and other relevant languages.
- Local cuisine and serving practices.
- Family, neighborhood, and household routines.
- Economic sensitivity around food prices, waste, and abundance.
- Local class and cultural context.

Mandatory controls:

- Do not import Gulf architecture, clothing, dialects, or hospitality conventions.
- Validate claims about local traditions, food, and family practices.
- Do not use poverty or identifiable beneficiaries as emotional scenery.
- Obtain informed consent for filming community members and beneficiaries.
- Test terms such as **traditional, authentic, for every family,** and **bringing the nation together**.

### Southeast Asia

Indonesia, Malaysia, Brunei, and Singapore require country-specific treatment of:

- Halal certification and claims.
- Bahasa Indonesia, Bahasa Melayu, English, and other relevant languages.
- Local food, family, neighborhood, mosque, and community contexts.
- Malay, Indonesian, Chinese, Indian, indigenous, urban, and mixed-faith identities.
- Creator and platform behavior.

Mandatory controls:

- Verify product, ingredient, manufacturing, preparation, and cross-contamination claims.
- Do not treat Bahasa Indonesia and Bahasa Melayu as interchangeable.
- Do not substitute Gulf visual language for Southeast Asian Muslim representation.
- Do not portray fasting as a weight-loss challenge, stunt, or engagement mechanic.
- Maintain a country-level halal claims matrix for each product variant and channel.

### Ramadan-wide safeguards

- Do not imply that the product is required for a valid iftar or suhoor.
- Do not encourage waste, excess, or conspicuous consumption.
- Review the final ten nights separately where relevant.
- Use country-level calendars rather than one global launch date.
- Treat Ramadan and Eid as separate campaign contexts.

## 4.2 Lunar New Year

The brief must identify whether the campaign concerns:

- Chinese New Year.
- Tết.
- Seollal.
- Another Lunar New Year tradition.
- A genuinely multicultural audience using “Lunar New Year.”

If multiple traditions appear together, the brief must explain why the combination is locally meaningful and how each tradition is accurately represented. If that cannot be demonstrated, develop separate executions.

Mandatory controls:

- Use the locally preferred name.
- Verify the calendar date and zodiac reference.
- Verify script, greeting, pronunciation, honorifics, kinship terms, typography, colors, numbers, and gift conventions.
- Do not use a generic “Asian” aesthetic.
- Do not combine Chinese, Vietnamese, Korean, and other traditions merely for production convenience.
- Do not guarantee luck, wealth, prosperity, or fortune.
- Do not require consumers to imitate rituals or disclose ethnicity.

Specific requirements:

- **Chinese communities:** distinguish mainland China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore, Malaysia, and diaspora contexts where scripts, terminology, gifting, and political sensitivities differ.
- **Vietnam:** use Tết-specific language, food, family, and cultural references. Changing only the greeting on Chinese New Year creative is not acceptable.
- **Korea:** use Seollal-specific language, food, family customs, greetings, and visual conventions. Chinese motifs are not substitutes for Korean references.

## 4.3 Diwali and Deepavali

The brief must specify whether it addresses:

- Hindu observance.
- Jain observance.
- Sikh communities and related observances, including whether Bandi Chhor Divas is relevant.
- Broader cultural participation.
- India or a particular diaspora market.
- Diwali, Deepavali, or another local term.

Mandatory controls:

- Do not present Diwali as “the Indian Christmas.”
- Do not imply that all Indians, Hindus, South Asians, or diaspora communities celebrate identically.
- Review regional differences across North, South, East, and West India.
- Treat diyas, rangoli, puja, temples, religious text, mantras, deities, and sacred gestures as sensitive or sacred content.
- Do not use sacred imagery as product decoration or imply blessings, prosperity, or religious merit.
- Verify sweets, ingredients, utensils, serving practices, and dietary claims.
- Review candles, diyas, fireworks, pollution, animal welfare, and fire-safety implications.
- Avoid reducing India to clothing, color, dance, sweets, spices, and fireworks.

# 5. Campaign development

## Stage 1: Local cultural strategy brief

The local team must produce a market-specific brief covering:

- Occasion and participation classification.
- Audience and community definition.
- Local cultural insight and evidence.
- Desired consumer behavior.
- Legitimate brand role.
- Relevant and prohibited practices, symbols, foods, sounds, and settings.
- Language and accessibility requirements.
- Representation and casting rationale.
- Community, creator, and partner plan.
- Product, certification, legal, safety, and regulatory considerations.
- Historical sensitivities and prior brand issues.
- Risk level and operating mode.

## Stage 2: Cultural participation

Local people must influence the work before the concept route is selected.

Participation may include:

- Paid cultural or religious specialists.
- Native-language editors.
- Community organizations.
- Local practitioners.
- Local artists, chefs, historians, or food experts.
- Creators with relevant lived experience.
- Qualitative testing panels.
- Accessibility specialists.
- Community or charitable partners.

Participation must be:

- Paid fairly.
- Documented.
- Used early enough to change the work.
- Diverse enough to avoid one-person authority.
- Free from pressure to approve.
- Governed by clear rights, consent, attribution, and usage terms.

Community-developed music, designs, recipes, stories, and artwork require fair compensation, attribution where appropriate, and limited written usage rights. Standard contracts must not transfer broad or perpetual ownership of community cultural work.

## Stage 3: Creative development

Each market must provide:

- Strategic rationale.
- Cultural rationale.
- Product-role rationale.
- Local message and translation record.
- Casting and representation rationale.
- Visual, audio, food, and location references.
- Community and creator roles.
- Risk assessment.
- Channel-specific adaptation rules.

The lead agency may support coherence but may not override local cultural, legal, food, safety, accessibility, or certification decisions.

## Stage 4: Testing and red-team review

Test concepts before production is locked and final assets before launch with relevant local audiences, people with lived experience, native-language reviewers, and specialists where applicable.

Test for:

- Accuracy and respect.
- Product credibility.
- Tokenism and stereotypes.
- Translation, pronunciation, and layout.
- Sacred trivialization.
- Class, gender, disability, and representation concerns.
- Exclusion or overgeneralization.
- Misinterpretation without context.
- Risk from clipping, remixing, memes, or altered subtitles.
- Whether the brand’s wider behavior supports the message.

For medium- and high-risk work, the record must contain at least two relevant evidence sources, such as specialist review, local testing, community participation, documented research, partner feedback, or independent red-team assessment.

# 6. Approval safeguards

## 6.1 Operating modes

### Core seasonal activation

For ordinary hospitality, sharing, or community content with no sacred imagery, sensitive claims, or unusual product risks.

Required:

- Local cultural and language review.
- Product and legal checks.
- Basic local testing.
- Accessibility review.
- Monitoring and pause plan.

### Expanded cultural activation

For culturally specific symbols, traditional food claims, seasonal packaging, creators, community programs, or multi-market paid activity.

Required:

- Full market risk register.
- Early community and advisor participation.
- Specialist language and product review.
- Qualitative local testing.
- Creator and partner due diligence.
- Cross-market audit readiness.
- Extended monitoring coverage.

### Exceptional sensitive activation

For prayer, scripture, deities, worship spaces, sacred audio, rituals, controversial partnerships, or claims about luck, blessings, prosperity, or spiritual benefit.

Required:

- Narrow necessity test.
- Specialist review within defined expertise.
- Independent local red-team.
- Rights, permissions, and safeguarding review.
- Enhanced legal, product, safety, accessibility, and reputation review.
- Written residual-risk decision.
- Strong presumption against proceeding.

## 6.2 Approval gates

### Gate 1: Strategy and concept

Confirm:

- Correct occasion and participation classification.
- Local audience and cultural rationale.
- Legitimate product role.
- Early local participation.
- Representation rationale.
- Sacred and commercialization risks.
- Market risk register.
- Community and creator plan.
- Required operating mode.

### Gate 2: Script, design, and production

Verify:

- Language, translation, pronunciation, layout, and subtitles.
- Symbols, settings, clothing, gestures, music, and food.
- Product, ingredient, certification, allergen, and safety claims.
- Creator conduct, disclosures, and scripts.
- Partner permissions, safeguarding, and impact claims.
- Legal, regulatory, data-protection, and accessibility requirements.
- AI-generated content controls.

### Gate 3: Prelaunch

Confirm:

- Final versions across all channels.
- Correct timing and placements.
- Packaging, retail, ecommerce, customer-service, PR, influencer, and social consistency.
- Partner and creator readiness.
- Monitoring queries, staffing, coverage hours, and backups.
- Pause capability and escalation contacts.
- Holding statements and correction materials.
- Cross-border asset audit.

## 6.3 Decision rights

- Local cultural reviewers control cultural and religious accuracy within their documented expertise.
- Local legal counsel controls local legal compliance.
- Product and certification teams control food, dietary, and certification claims.
- Global reputation controls cross-border response and global pause decisions.
- The designated gate lead records the decision, conditions, dissent, and residual-risk owner.
- Outcomes are: approve, approve with conditions, revise, defer, or reject.
- Unresolved cultural or religious objections block launch unless an independent senior panel overturns them in writing.
- Schedule, budget, media commitments, and global consistency alone cannot justify reversal.
- A local team may pause or remove content immediately where credible cultural, legal, product, safety, or reputational risk exists.

Each reviewer must document expertise, market, scope, limitations, conflicts of interest, and whether they are providing advice, approval, or consultation. No individual or institution speaks for an entire religion, ethnicity, nation, diaspora, or community.

## 6.4 Stop-launch criteria

Do not launch if:

- A qualified local reviewer identifies unresolved cultural or religious inaccuracy.
- Legal, certification, safety, product, or accessibility evidence is incomplete.
- Translation, pronunciation, or layout remains disputed.
- Creator or partner due diligence is incomplete.
- A charity or impact claim cannot be evidenced.
- Sacred content is unnecessary or primarily decorative.
- The product’s role is not credible.
- Testing identifies material concern that has not been addressed.
- Monitoring, moderation, or pause capability is unavailable.
- The receiving market is not listed in the scope record.

# 7. Agency governance

## Roles

### Global team

Owns global standards, shared tools, brand platform, cross-border coordination, global reputation, minimum resourcing, and system-wide learning.

### Regional team

Coordinates regional risk, agency delivery, monitoring support, and lessons learned. It must not consolidate distinct countries merely for production efficiency.

### Local team

Owns cultural strategy, advisors, creators, language, casting, locations, food, timing, partners, local testing, first-line monitoring, and local response.

### Lead agency

Owns adaptation logic, production standards, version control, and global coherence through brand behavior and tone—not visual sameness.

### Local and regional agencies

Own local insight, concept development, language, production, creators, community recommendations, testing, and escalation of concerns about global work.

## Agency charter and contracts

Contracts and briefs must define:

- Master-concept ownership.
- Local interpretation and final-language ownership.
- Testing responsibility.
- Creator and partner due diligence.
- Final-file and version-history control.
- Pause and rejection authority.
- Funding for research, advisors, testing, rework, removal, reprinting, and relaunch.
- Record retention.
- Confidential escalation and non-retaliation.
- Takedown, correction, and termination obligations.

Global funds mandatory shared safeguards and tools. Regional or local teams fund market-specific execution. A central contingency fund should cover mandatory compliance rework and incident response. Local teams must not be penalized for required cultural, legal, product, safety, or accessibility rework.

Agency performance should reward early identification of risk, quality of challenge, and accurate escalation—not merely a low number of reported incidents.

# 8. Monitoring and escalation

## Monitoring requirements

Monitor by market, language, platform, asset, creator, and issue type:

- Local-language keywords, slang, dialect, and transliterations.
- Cultural, religious, food, halal, ingredient, allergen, and certification concerns.
- Community, scholar, journalist, partner, certifier, and regulator responses.
- Boycott, removal, apology, and complaint terms.
- Creator conduct.
- Retailer, ecommerce, marketplace, and customer-service complaints.
- Memes, screenshots, stitched videos, altered subtitles, and visual reposts.
- Employee concerns, harassment, doxxing, and safety threats.

Each market must have:

- Named monitoring owners and backups.
- Defined coverage hours and peak-period staffing.
- Emergency after-hours coverage.
- Tested queries and false-positive rules.
- Native-language capability.
- Image and video monitoring.
- A central incident number and timestamp.
- Time-zone handover procedures.
- An approved pause authority.

Machine translation may assist triage but must not replace native-language interpretation for sensitive issues or publish final sensitive responses.

## Standard event definitions

- **Detect:** first internal capture of a relevant signal.
- **Triage:** initial classification and severity assignment.
- **Pause:** paid or scheduled amplification stopped.
- **Acknowledge:** first public or direct response.
- **Correct:** factual or cultural amendment implemented.
- **Resolve:** all required corrective actions completed and formally closed by the risk owner.
- **Learn:** preventive action assigned, tracked, and incorporated into future practice.

## Severity overrides

Volume thresholds are triggers, not permission to wait. Immediate escalation applies to:

- Sacred or religious inaccuracy.
- Food, halal, certification, allergen, ingredient, or safety risk.
- Regulatory or certifier concern.
- Harm to children, vulnerable people, or beneficiaries.
- Credible threat, harassment, or doxxing.
- Formal objection from a relevant institution.
- Incorrect packaging or point-of-sale material already distributed.
- Evidence that the issue is spreading across borders.

Issues must be assessed at asset, creator, platform, market, campaign, and global levels.

## Escalation levels

### Level 0: Routine

Isolated criticism without a confirmed error or unusual spread.

**Action:** respond locally within eight hours during staffed coverage.

### Level 1: Emerging concern

Any of the following:

- Repeated substantive criticism.
- One credible local specialist, organization, journalist, partner, or employee identifies a specific issue.
- Translation, certification, or cultural accuracy is under review.
- Negative mention rate is at least twice campaign baseline for two hours.
- A creator or partner raises concern.

**Action:**

- Notify market communications.
- Pause scheduled amplification while investigating.
- Obtain local-language interpretation within 60–120 minutes.
- Set the next decision time.

### Level 2: Material issue

Any of the following:

- Confirmed inaccurate, offensive, exploitative, misleading, or noncompliant content.
- Formal concern from a regulator, certifier, retailer, partner, or community body.
- Rapid local boycott or removal calls.
- Prominent local specialist, creator, journalist, or organization raises the issue.
- Similar content exists in multiple markets.
- An incorrect physical asset has entered distribution.

**Action:**

- Pause affected paid and creator amplification within 60 minutes of confirmation.
- Notify regional and global reputation teams.
- Issue a local holding statement within two hours.
- Audit campaign, packaging, retail, ecommerce, creator, customer-service, and parallel-market assets within 12 hours.
- Decide whether to clarify, edit, remove, apologize, or cancel.

### Level 3: Crisis

Any of the following:

- Threats to people, premises, staff, creators, or partners.
- Legal notice, regulator intervention, or certification challenge.
- Major retailer or partner action.
- Spread across three or more markets.
- Clearly offensive use of sacred content.
- Rapid escalation beyond established crisis capacity.

**Action:**

- Activate global incident management immediately.
- Pause relevant content within 30 minutes where technically possible.
- Notify executives within one hour.
- Issue a reviewed holding statement within three hours.
- Provide a specific corrective update within 24 hours where facts permit.
- Provide interim updates when investigation takes longer.

Thresholds must be calibrated to audience size, expected reach, historical baseline, platform velocity, campaign sensitivity, physical distribution, and source credibility. A credible high-severity objection may trigger Level 2 or Level 3 without meeting numerical thresholds.

# 9. Response playbooks

## Response principles

- Address affected communities and audiences, not only international observers.
- Name the specific issue once confirmed.
- Do not lead with intent, good motives, reach, or sales.
- Do not call criticism oversensitivity or online noise.
- Use the affected audience’s principal language or languages unless a documented legal, safety, platform, or operational reason prevents it.
- Preserve evidence before editing or removing content.
- Do not reproduce offensive content unnecessarily.
- Brief agencies, creators, retailers, customer service, employees, and partners consistently.
- Do not instruct staff or creators to defend disputed content before review.
- Remove criticism only where it violates platform rules or creates a safety risk.

## Unconfirmed concern

- Acknowledge that the issue is under review.
- Pause scheduled amplification if the concern is credible.
- Avoid defending intent.
- State the next review time.

Suggested wording:

> We have paused this content while we review a concern about its [wording, image, or product claim] with relevant local specialists and our market team. We recognize the importance of this observance and will provide an update at [time/date].

## Confirmed inaccurate or offensive content

- Pause or remove the asset.
- Issue a specific apology.
- Explain what was wrong and what has changed.
- Audit related assets across markets and channels.
- Notify affected partners, retailers, creators, and advisors.
- Record the root cause and process change.
- Do not relaunch until the corrected version passes the applicable review and testing.

Suggested wording:

> We used [specific image, wording, claim, or execution] in a way that was inaccurate and disrespectful. We apologize. We have removed it, reviewed related materials, and changed our [briefing, translation, approval, or partner-review] process to prevent a recurrence.

## Misinformation

- Verify the facts.
- Correct calmly with evidence.
- Do not amplify fringe claims unnecessarily.
- Address any legitimate cultural, product, or community concern separately.

“Misinformation” must not be used to dismiss inconvenient or low-volume criticism.

## Creator or partner conduct

- Investigate promptly.
- Apply contractual pause, takedown, or termination rights.
- Separate the conduct from the campaign only where facts support doing so.
- Communicate confirmed information.
- Do not shift responsibility where the brand selected, briefed, or approved the partner.

## Product or certification issue

A food, allergen, halal, dietary, ingredient, or certification error must immediately involve product safety and legal teams.

Actions may include:

- Pausing affected claims and media.
- Notifying retailers, marketplaces, and partners.
- Correcting consumer-facing information.
- Assessing withdrawal, recall, or regulatory notification requirements.
- Auditing related products, packaging, and listings.

# 10. Correction and relaunch

A confirmed issue triggers review of:

- The affected asset and all versions.
- The same slogan, translation, symbol, image, product claim, creator, or production asset in other markets.
- Packaging, point-of-sale, retail, ecommerce, paid media, and marketplace listings.
- Influencer scripts, captions, live content, and discount codes.
- Customer-service, sales, and internal materials.
- Landing pages, metadata, hashtags, alt text, and subtitles.
- Previously published content likely to resurface.

Before relaunch:

1. Correct the asset.
2. Recheck language, cultural meaning, product claims, accessibility, safety, and legal compliance.
3. Update captions, subtitles, alt text, metadata, hashtags, retailer files, and creator instructions.
4. Confirm the old version is paused or removed where feasible.
5. Complete fresh local testing for material changes.
6. Notify affected partners and advisors.
7. Determine whether a public explanation is required.
8. Reactivate monitoring for renewed criticism.
9. Record the relaunch decision, remaining risks, and reopening authority.

No corrected asset may relaunch merely because it has been edited. It must pass the applicable risk gate again.

A full resolution requires evidence that all affected assets were addressed, stakeholders were notified, required product or legal actions were completed, monitoring was reviewed, and preventive action was assigned.

# 11. Measurement and accountability

Report commercial performance alongside cultural and operational quality.

## Required measures

- Local review completed before concept approval.
- Verification of religious, food, certification, charity, safety, and authenticity-related claims.
- Creator and partner due diligence.
- Translation, pronunciation, and layout error rate.
- Percentage of work materially shaped by local creators, advisors, or communities.
- Number of concerns raised before launch.
- Time from concern to disposition.
- Time to detect, triage, pause, acknowledge, correct, and resolve.
- Number and severity of escalations.
- Cross-market recurrence of issues.
- Accessibility compliance.
- Community-partner satisfaction, beneficiary feedback where safe, and verified outcomes.
- Completion of corrective and preventive actions.

## Target standards

- Zero unresolved high-risk issues at launch.
- Confirmed material issues acknowledged within two hours.
- Affected paid amplification paused within 60 minutes of confirmation.
- Cross-market audit initiated within four hours.
- Independent post-campaign cultural review completed within 30 days.
- Corrective actions closed by assigned deadlines or accompanied by a documented interim status.

Metrics must not reward under-reporting. Early concerns, documented dissent, and effective challenge are positive governance outcomes.

“Materially shaped by local input” means local input changed the central insight, product role, cultural reference, casting, setting, language, timing, community contribution, or route selection. Translation, local production, or final sign-off alone does not qualify.

# 12. Agency, partner, and employee readiness

Before launch, provide:

- Market-language employee FAQs.
- Moderator and customer-service guidance.
- Approved and prohibited response language.
- Creator and partner briefing materials.
- Confidential reporting routes for agencies, advisors, employees, and partners.
- Non-retaliation protections.
- Emergency contact and coverage directory.
- After-hours pause authority.

Community partners must be classified as appropriate—such as registered charity, faith institution, community group, informal initiative, commercial provider, or government body—and verified proportionately.

Required partner controls include:

- Legal and financial due diligence.
- Safeguarding and data-protection review.
- Beneficiary dignity and informed consent.
- Permission for names, faces, stories, locations, logos, and cultural work.
- Complaint and remedy procedures.
- Verification of delivered meals, funds, or services.
- No purchase or social-posting requirement for support.
- No implication that the brand personally performed a partner’s work.

- Cross-border audit.
- Market contact and coverage directory.

The full framework sets the standard; these tools are the required execution layer.

# 14. Final readiness standard

A campaign is ready to launch only when:

- The occasion, participation type, audience, and market scope are accurately defined.
- Local participants influenced the work early.
- The product has a legitimate and evidenced role.
- Language, food, certification, imagery, timing, safety, accessibility, and claims are verified.
- Sacred and sensitive references are controlled.
- Market testing and red-team review are complete where required.
- Risks, dissent, evidence, and residual ownership are documented.
- Monitoring, moderation, pause, escalation, and response systems are active.
- Cross-border distribution is controlled.
- Employees, creators, partners, retailers, and customer-service teams are prepared.
- The brand can correct, apologize, withdraw, and change without delay.

Authenticity is not a visual style. It is a documented operating practice in which local knowledge shapes the work, claims are provable, sacred elements are protected, agencies and partners are accountable, scrutiny triggers proportionate action, and every incident produces measurable improvement.
