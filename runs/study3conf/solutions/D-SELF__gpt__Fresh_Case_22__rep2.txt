# Gulf Grocery Expansion Strategy, 2026  
## Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Qatar

## 1. Executive recommendation

Enter the Gulf through **separate, country-specific joint ventures**, supported by a regional operating model. Avoid a single Gulf-wide operating company and do not rely on informal sponsorship or nominee arrangements.

The European retailer should retain control of:

- Brand, private-label standards, and customer proposition
- Food safety, quality, recalls, and product specifications
- Technology, data architecture, loyalty, and cybersecurity
- Procurement governance and supplier integrity
- Operating standards, training, financial controls, and audit
- Format design, performance management, and regional know-how

The local partner should contribute measurable value, such as:

- Real estate and landlord access
- Import, distribution, and cold-chain capability
- Local regulatory execution
- National-workforce recruitment and training
- Local supplier development
- Government, municipality, and community relationships
- Capital, where agreed

A family conglomerate should not receive control or broad exclusivity merely because of its reputation or perceived connections.

### Recommended sequence

1. **Saudi Arabia:** prioritize as the principal long-term growth platform, subject to regulatory feasibility and localization economics.
2. **UAE:** pursue an emirate-by-emirate model, emphasizing premium, neighborhood, convenience, and digital formats.
3. **Qatar:** enter cautiously through a concentrated portfolio and a partner with strong import, distribution, and labor-market capabilities.

Begin with a staged pilot:

- One principal physical format in each country
- A limited number of stores
- Store-based e-commerce only after operating readiness is demonstrated
- Expansion only after financial, regulatory, supply-chain, and workforce-localization gates are met

---

## 2. Strategic principles

1. Use country-specific entities and contracts.
2. Select one anchor partner only where it provides measurable value.
3. Prohibit informal sponsorship, nominee ownership, and undocumented control arrangements.
4. Use operator-led governance with clearly defined local execution responsibilities.
5. Pilot before scaling.
6. Build localization into the operating model from the start.
7. Treat Ramadan as a core annual trading cycle.
8. Use regional systems with country-specific compliance controls.
9. Maintain direct access to licenses, leases, bank accounts, data, and critical records.
10. Agree partner-performance, replacement, and exit rights before investing.

The ownership, licensing, tax, labor, data, agency, franchise, and competition positions below are strategic recommendations or working assumptions. Current local confirmation is required before implementation.

# 3. Regulatory-feasibility protocol

Before signing definitive agreements, obtain a written, dated legal and regulatory package for each country and each proposed activity.

## Required confirmation

Counsel should address:

- Foreign-ownership eligibility and control rights
- Investment and commercial-registration requirements
- Retail activity classifications and license codes
- Store, warehouse, and premises approvals
- Importer-of-record requirements
- Food, halal, labeling, weights-and-measures, and product-registration rules
- E-commerce, delivery, payment, advertising, and loyalty requirements
- Employment, visa, payroll, social-insurance, and localization rules
- Data protection, cybersecurity, hosting, and cross-border-transfer requirements
- Competition and merger-control requirements
- Commercial-agency, distribution, franchise, and management-agreement consequences
- Tax, withholding, transfer-pricing, customs, permanent-establishment, and repatriation implications
- Enforceability of reserved matters, dilution, call/put, step-in, and exit provisions

Each requirement should be classified as:

- **Confirmed:** supported by current law, official guidance, or written authority confirmation
- **Likely:** counsel’s current view but still requiring licensing or authority confirmation
- **Conditional:** dependent on activity, emirate, premises, product, or workforce category
- **Unconfirmed:** no implementation decision permitted

No final ownership or governance structure should be approved until this feasibility gate is passed.

# 4. Country strategies

## A. Saudi Arabia

### Market approach

Saudi Arabia should be developed as the largest potential growth platform, but it will require the strongest operating infrastructure for localization, licensing, labor compliance, and multi-city logistics.

Initial format hypotheses:

- Neighborhood supermarkets in dense residential areas
- Value and family-basket stores
- Selective larger stores where catchment economics justify them

These are commercial hypotheses requiring local testing.

### Structure

Use the following hierarchy:

1. Legally permissible European majority-owned operating JV
2. Minority JV with contractual operational control and protected IP
3. Management or franchise structure only if required by licensing or ownership constraints
4. Prohibited: informal sponsorship, nominee ownership, or undocumented beneficial control

The Saudi operating entity should generally handle stores, local employees, premises, customer transactions, and country compliance. The parties must separately determine which entity will handle imports, warehousing, e-commerce, private-label contracting, and regional services.

### Partner priorities

Prioritize partners with:

- Proven grocery or multi-site retail experience
- Strong Saudization and labor-compliance capability
- Real-estate and municipal execution
- Import and cold-chain capability
- Sufficient capital
- Ability to operate across several cities
- Transparent family governance and succession
- Verifiable, lawful government and landlord relationships

### Governance

The European retailer should appoint or control the:

- CEO or COO
- CFO
- Digital and information-technology lead
- Procurement head
- Food-safety and quality head
- HR and localization-compliance head

The local partner may lead real estate, government and municipality relations, local supplier development, recruitment networks, and community relationships. It should not override food-safety, payroll, anti-bribery, financial-reporting, or approved-procurement controls.

### Workforce localization

Saudi localization should be managed through a dynamic, role-by-role matrix covering:

- Occupation classification
- Required national headcount
- Wage and registration treatment
- Visa impact
- Hiring lead times
- Productivity ramp
- Training and promotion pathways
- Cost per role
- Compliance forecasts

Establish a Saudi retail academy and develop Saudi nationals in store management, supply chain, merchandising, IT, finance, e-commerce, food safety, and HR.

**Current confirmation required:** activity-specific Saudization/Nitaqat rules, qualifying roles, wage treatment, registration requirements, expatriate-visa effects, and penalties.

## B. United Arab Emirates

### Market approach

The UAE should be managed as several operating markets rather than one uniform jurisdiction. Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, and other emirates may differ in licensing, premises, municipal, retail, workforce, and commercial conditions.

Initial format priorities:

- Premium neighborhood supermarkets
- Convenience and fresh-food stores
- Digital ordering and delivery
- Curated European private label
- Selective larger stores in suitable residential or mall catchments

### Structure

Use a mainland retail operating company for mainland stores unless current advice supports another structure.

A free-zone entity may be appropriate for regional services, holding, technology, or logistics, but free-zone incorporation should not be assumed to authorize mainland retail, warehousing, delivery, or import activities.

The UAE partner should be retained only if it provides measurable value through:

- Mall and landlord access
- Emirate-level licensing execution
- Distribution and logistics
- Local supplier access
- Emirati recruitment and management networks
- Property development or institutional relationships

### Governance

A suitable baseline is:

- Five-member board
- Three European-appointed directors
- Two partner-appointed directors
- European appointment of the CEO or COO and CFO
- Audit and risk committee chaired by a European or independent director
- No chair casting vote on reserved matters
- Defined financial thresholds and response deadlines

The partner should have consent rights for transactions involving its affiliates, material changes to agreed property commitments, and actions that materially alter its legal obligations.

### Workforce localization

Emiratization should be assessed by entity, emirate, employer size, activity, and job category.

Build national career pathways in:

- Store leadership
- Digital commerce
- Category management
- Analytics
- Finance
- Supply chain
- Customer experience

**Current confirmation required:** applicable Emiratization thresholds, qualifying-employee criteria, salary and payroll evidence, pension or social-security requirements, and differences between mainland and free-zone entities.

## C. Qatar

### Market approach

Qatar should be entered through a focused, lower-risk platform. Initial formats may include:

- Premium neighborhood stores
- Convenience and fresh-food formats
- Selected family-basket stores
- Store-based online delivery

Prioritize Doha and carefully selected high-income residential, expatriate, and family catchments. A large hypermarket rollout should not be the default.

### Structure

Begin with one tightly governed operating JV, supported by local distribution or logistics capability where required.

Assess the partner’s ability to provide:

- Import and customs execution
- Retail premises
- Cold-chain distribution
- Local hiring
- Municipality and government processes
- Supplier development

The European retailer should retain control of brand, operations, quality, technology, data, and finance.

### Governance and exit

Because the market is smaller and concentration risk is greater:

- Limit initial capital exposure
- Use short, measurable development commitments
- Include stronger partner-replacement and exit rights
- Require approved interim budgets during the launch phase
- Permit conversion to a management or franchise model only after legal review

**Current confirmation required:** foreign-ownership routes, retail and import licenses, commercial-agency consequences, Qatarization rules, labor and visa requirements, and enforceability of exit rights.

# 5. Partner selection

## Mandatory disqualifiers

A partner should be rejected or placed on hold if it has:

- Unresolved bribery, sanctions, or serious regulatory concerns
- Unclear beneficial ownership
- No three-year audited financial history
- Unverified capital capacity
- Refusal to permit independent audit
- Material undisclosed related-party conflicts
- A record of fictitious employment or serious labor violations
- Inability to provide secure property or licensing rights
- Demands for informal sponsorship, undocumented fees, or unrestricted exclusivity

## Country-weighted scorecard

Score each candidate from 1 to 5. Require a minimum overall score of 3.5, with no score below 3 in a critical category.

| Criterion | Saudi Arabia | UAE | Qatar |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Grocery and multi-site operating capability | 20% | 15% | 15% |
| Real estate and landlord access | 15% | 25% | 20% |
| Import, distribution, and cold chain | 15% | 15% | 25% |
| Localization and training capability | 20% | 15% | 15% |
| Regulatory and compliance execution | 15% | 15% | 15% |
| Capital capacity and funding reliability | 10% | 10% | 15% |
| Technology and e-commerce capability | 3% | 10% | 3% |
| Family governance and succession | 2% | 5% | 2% |

## Verification process

Support the score with:

- Audited financial statements and bank references
- Beneficial-ownership, PEP, sanctions, and adverse-media screening
- Litigation and insolvency searches
- Site visits to stores, warehouses, offices, and staff facilities
- Interviews with landlords, suppliers, former partners, and senior employees
- Property title and encumbrance checks
- Verification of workforce-localization performance
- Review of consultants, commissions, government-facing payments, and related-party arrangements
- A written partner business plan and binding capital commitment

Each partner should sign anti-bribery, sanctions, audit, conflict-of-interest, beneficial-ownership, confidentiality, localization-support, and capital-commitment undertakings.

Exclusivity should be territory-specific, activity-specific, time-limited, performance-based, and terminable for corruption, misrepresentation, nonperformance, or regulatory failure.

# 7. JV governance and contractual design

## Board and management

Recommended baseline:

- Five-member board
- Three European-appointed directors
- Two partner-appointed directors
- European-appointed CEO or COO and CFO
- Country compliance and food-safety leads with functional reporting to regional headquarters
- Audit and risk committee
- Independent director or committee chair where appropriate

Routine management should not require unanimous shareholder approval.

## Reserved matters

European consent should be required for:

- Changes to constitutional documents or ownership
- Annual business plans and budgets
- Material budget deviations
- New stores, closures, acquisitions, and disposals
- Debt, guarantees, or capital commitments above agreed thresholds
- Related-party transactions
- Intercompany fees and dividends
- Appointment or removal of key executives
- Changes to brand, format, private label, or food-safety standards
- Customer-data commercialization or material data-architecture changes
- Outsourcing of core operations
- Material litigation, recalls, regulatory settlements, or investigations
- Changes to localization strategy
- Affiliate supplier arrangements
- IP transfers or licenses
- Material changes to public-sector commitments

All reserved matters should have defined thresholds, information requirements, response deadlines, and deemed-approval rules for routine items. Emergency action must remain available for food safety, payroll, customer protection, and regulatory compliance.

## Control mechanisms

- Dual-signature bank mandates
- Separation of procurement, approval, receipt, and payment
- Monthly management accounts
- Quarterly board reporting
- Annual external audit
- European internal-audit rights
- Direct access to licenses, leases, payroll, supplier, and customer-data records
- Independent whistleblowing channel
- Related-party register
- Approved supplier and tender lists
- Anti-bribery pre-approval for public-facing consultants
- Prompt escalation of cyber, regulatory, payroll, and food-safety incidents

## Deadlock and exit

Use a tiered process:

1. Management escalation
2. Board escalation
3. Mediation or expert determination for technical matters
4. Interim budget and status-quo rules
5. Structured sale, call/put, or orderly wind-down for fundamental deadlock

Include:

- Funding obligations for approved commitments
- Cure periods
- Independent valuation procedures
- Financing periods for buyouts
- Partner replacement rights
- Change-of-control triggers
- Remedies for corruption, insolvency, regulatory disqualification, and repeated underperformance
- Treatment of leases, employees, inventory, customer data, loyalty accounts, and private-label stock
- Transition services and IP-license termination provisions

- Initial equity and shareholder loans
- Store capex by format
- Distribution and third-party logistics costs
- Technology and e-commerce investment
- Pre-opening costs
- Inventory and Ramadan working capital
- Localization and training costs
- Ramp-up losses
- Contingency reserves

Partners should provide:

- Binding capital commitments
- Evidence of funds
- Funding deadlines
- Parent guarantees, escrow, bank guarantees, or other credit support where appropriate
- Default interest
- Dilution formulas
- Suspension of voting rights or distributions for non-funding
- Call or replacement rights for persistent default

The investment model should test sensitivity to:

- Rent
- Labor and localization costs

- Currency
- Shrinkage and waste

- Supplier disruption
- Delivery economics

Set country-specific hurdle rates for store-level EBITDA, payback, cash conversion, and return on invested capital.

Tax and legal advice is required on dividends, withholding tax, royalties, management fees, transfer pricing, customs, cash pooling, and permanent-establishment risk.

# 9. Format and proposition adaptation

| Market | Initial format priorities | Strategic rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Saudi Arabia | Neighborhood; value/family; selective larger store | Urban growth, family baskets, and scale potential |
| UAE | Premium neighborhood; convenience; digital; selective larger store | Dense urban catchments and premium/convenience potential |
| Qatar | Premium neighborhood; convenience; fresh food; limited family-basket test | Concentrated market and greater scale and labor constraints |

These are hypotheses to validate through location, customer, and unit-economics testing.

### Neighborhood format

- Fresh food and bakery
- Halal and regional products
- Family packs
- Ready-to-eat meals
- Arabic and English navigation
- Store-based rapid delivery

### Premium and import-led format

- European private label
- Specialty cheese, bakery, organic, and wellness products
- Transparent provenance
- Curated imports rather than excessive range
- Local premium products

### Value and family format

- Staples and key-value items
- Larger pack sizes
- Regional and Arabic brands
- Promotional bundles
- Efficient labor and checkout model

### Assortment governance

Country assortment councils should include commercial, category, food-safety, supply-chain, finance, and local-partner representatives.

European approval should remain required for:

- Private-label specifications
- Quality, food-safety, and halal standards
- Product claims and provenance
- High-risk suppliers
- Material substitutions
- Brand presentation

Track local and regional supplier share, imported SKU share, private-label penetration, fresh-food margin, availability, lead times, and seasonal performance.

Pricing governance may establish internal price architecture and key-value-item targets, but current competition-law advice is required for exclusivity, supplier restrictions, promotional arrangements, and pricing practices.

# 10. Supply-chain strategy

Each country plan must identify:

- Local versus imported categories
- Importer of record
- Ports, borders, and customs routes
- Distribution-center or 3PL model
- Cold-chain requirements
- Private-label manufacturing location
- Safety-stock and dual-sourcing rules
- Product registration and labeling
- Batch traceability
- Recall procedures
- Inventory ownership and product liability
- Last-mile delivery model

Recommended principles:

- Source fresh, culturally relevant, and short-shelf-life products locally where quality is reliable.
- Use regional or direct imports for European private label and specialist products.
- Dual-source critical staples, chilled products, packaging, and Ramadan lines.
- Increase safety stock before Ramadan and periods of elevated import risk.
- Conduct mock recalls and test imports before opening.
- Require supplier audits, certificates, halal documentation, and corrective-action plans.
- Establish substitute-SKU rules for import delays.
- Avoid dependence on a partner-owned distributor without benchmarking, audit rights, and termination alternatives.

Key supply-chain measures should include on-shelf availability, supplier OTIF performance, import-clearance time, inventory days, cold-chain excursions, waste, shrinkage, recall response time, supplier concentration, and landed-cost variance.

# 11. Ramadan and Islamic-calendar planning

Maintain a rolling 24-month calendar covering Ramadan, Eid, Hajj-related periods where relevant, national days, school calendars, and major retail events.

## Planning cycle

### Six to nine months before Ramadan

- Confirm expected dates
- Build country and format demand scenarios
- Review current working-hour and trading guidance
- Secure import capacity and supplier commitments
- Begin national recruitment and temporary-labor planning
- Agree promotional and media strategy

### Three to five months before Ramadan

- Finalize seasonal assortment
- Place long-lead orders
- Confirm store hours and staffing rosters
- Test delivery capacity
- Train teams
- Approve community and charitable activity

### One to two months before Ramadan

- Build inventory progressively
- Test customs routes and alternatives
- Conduct mock recall
- Confirm payment, app, loyalty, and customer-service capacity
- Complete opening-hours and workforce-compliance sign-off

### During Ramadan and Eid

- Monitor sales by hour, day, category, and format
- Replenish before evening peaks
- Protect fresh-food availability and quality
- Increase delivery and customer-service capacity
- Monitor waste, substitutions, and stockouts
- Avoid culturally inappropriate daytime activations
- Manage travel-related demand shifts and post-Eid normalization

Build financial scenarios covering sales uplift, category mix, basket size, demand timing, waste, inventory days, labor and transport costs, delivery capacity, and post-season normalization.

Current annual confirmation is required on working hours, overtime, breaks, night work, store-hour restrictions, advertising, sampling, and labor treatment.

# 12. Workforce-localization compliance

Create a regional Gulf Workforce Compliance Office with country leads.

## Core controls

- Role-by-role localization matrix
- Monthly headcount and quota dashboard
- Visa and permit tracker
- Payroll and wage-payment reconciliation
- Social-insurance and employee-registration checks
- Recruitment-agency due diligence
- Genuine-employment verification
- Training and promotion records
- Outsourced-worker compliance audits
- Worker grievance and whistleblowing channels
- Annual labor-law audit
- Revalidation before recruitment, each opening, annually, and after material regulatory changes

National employees should be hired into genuine, productive roles, supported by retail academies, apprenticeships, scholarships, supplier training, and clear management pathways.

Prohibit fictitious employment, passport retention, recruitment-fee charging to workers, and informal cash payroll.

## Accountability

- **Country CEO:** accountable for compliance
- **HR/localization lead:** responsible for execution
- **Local partner:** supports recruitment channels and government processes
- **European parent:** sets standards, training, reporting, and audit requirements
- **Legal/compliance:** validates current rules and escalates breaches

Track:

- National headcount and qualifying-role compliance
- Six- and twelve-month retention
- National promotion rates
- Training completion
- Productivity by role
- Absenteeism and turnover
- Pay parity
- Payroll accuracy
- Visa-processing time
- Contractor compliance
- Labor findings

Current rules should be revalidated before entity formation, recruitment, every opening, and at least annually.

# 13. Implementation roadmap

## Phase 0: Feasibility — Months 0–3

- Approve market hypotheses
- Identify potential partners
- Obtain preliminary legal classifications
- Build country investment models
- Map entity and function requirements
- Establish the regional program office

**Gate:** no major partner commitment until ownership, licensing, and economics are sufficiently viable.

## Phase 1: Partner and operating-model design — Months 3–6

- Shortlist and score partners
- Complete legal, financial, sanctions, property, and operational diligence
- Agree term sheets
- Select initial cities and formats
- Design supply chain and data architecture
- Model localization costs

**Gate:** approve partner, structure, capital envelope, and pilot plan.

## Phase 2: Build — Months 6–12

- Incorporate entities
- Secure licenses and premises
- Execute capital commitments
- Establish governance and bank controls
- Contract suppliers and 3PLs
- Recruit leadership and national employees
- Deploy systems and loyalty architecture
- Conduct test imports and mock recalls
- Complete pre-opening approvals

## Phase 3: Pilot — Months 12–18

- Open one initial format in each approved country
- Use a controlled assortment and soft launch
- Add e-commerce only after store and delivery readiness
- Review performance at 30, 90, 180, and 365 days

## Phase 4: Scale — Months 18–36

Scale only when:

- Regulatory compliance is satisfactory
- Localization is sustainable
- Store economics meet hurdle rates
- Supply-chain service levels are achieved
- Food-safety and shrinkage performance are acceptable
- Ramadan execution is proven
- Partner obligations are being met

No waiver may bypass licensing, food safety, payroll, immigration, data, or anti-bribery requirements.

# 14. Principal risks

| Risk | Early warning | Key mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership or licensing route unavailable | Activity classification remains unresolved | Maintain majority, minority, and management-model alternatives | General Counsel |
| Partner fails to fund | Delayed commitment or weak liquidity | Guarantees, escrow, dilution, and call rights | CFO |
| Partner controls critical premises or permits | Refusal to provide direct rights | Direct leases, step-in rights, alternative sites | Country CEO |
| Localization shortfall | Deteriorating monthly forecast | Early hiring, training academy, role redesign | HR lead |
| Visa or payroll failure | Warnings or rejected applications | Monthly reconciliation and escalation | HR/CFO |
| Food-safety incident | Audit failure or cold-chain breach | Supplier audits, traceability, mock recalls | Quality head |
| Import disruption | Clearance delays or geopolitical alerts | Dual sourcing, safety stock, alternative routes | Supply-chain head |
| Ramadan stockout or excess inventory | Demand materially diverges from plan | Scenario planning and allocation rules | Commercial director |
| Data or cyber incident | Unusual access or system alerts | Access controls and incident plan | CIO |
| Related-party corruption | Unusual fees or supplier pressure | Pre-approval, audit, whistleblowing, suspension rights | Compliance officer |
| Competition-law breach | Exclusivity or pricing complaints | Legal review of suppliers, landlords, and acquisitions | Legal |
| Partner deadlock | Missed approvals or funding disputes | Interim budget, escalation, expert determination, exit rights | Board chair |
| Weak store economics | Sales or margin below gate | Stop-loss, redesign, closure, or pause | CFO/Country CEO |
| National employee attrition | Falling six-month retention | Career pathways and manager accountability | HR lead |

Risk owners should report monthly during the build phase and at every board meeting after opening.

## Final position

The retailer should enter as a **controlled regional operator with capable local country partners**, not as a passive investor and not as an unsupported foreign entrant.

Saudi Arabia requires the strongest localization and regulatory-execution model. The UAE requires emirate-specific licensing and commercial discipline. Qatar requires focused capital deployment, strong import and distribution capability, and early protection against partner and market concentration risk.

Success will depend less on the nominal equity split than on enforceable operating control, direct access to critical assets and records, genuine partner performance, resilient supply chains, workforce planning, and disciplined expansion gates.
