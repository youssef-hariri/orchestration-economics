# 2026 Vaccine Cold-Chain Distribution Strategy  
## West and East Africa

## 1. Executive summary

Approve a **controlled Phase 1 validation and launch program**, not unrestricted regional deployment.

The recommended network is a hybrid model:

- **Powered cold storage** at approved gateways, national stores, and selected high-throughput regional hubs.
- **Qualified passive shippers** for most cross-border, remote, mixed-mode, and fragile-access movements.
- **Powered vehicles only on validated, high-volume domestic or hub-to-hub lanes** with pre-cooling, maintenance, secure parking, backup power, and continuous local temperature recording.
- **Ministry-confirmed receiving and last-mile readiness** as a dispatch condition, scaled to corridor risk.
- **Inventory buffers based on product shelf life, demand, campaign timing, usable storage, and observed lead-time variability**, rather than uniform regional rules.
- **Regional emergency stock, qualified backup storage, alternate routes, and contracted airbridge capacity** for fragile or weather-sensitive corridors.
- **Routine service only after product, facility, packaging, transport, regulatory, and handoff qualification.**

The strategy should begin with representative pilots in Ghana, Nigeria or Tanzania, the Mombasa–Nairobi–Kampala corridor, one coastal-to-Sahel lane, and one fragile-access operation. Final procurement, route guarantees, buffer policies, and service levels should remain conditional on lane data, product approvals, ministry agreements, equipment sizing, and cost analysis.

---

## 2. Planning assumptions and mandatory controls

### 2.1 Assumptions requiring validation

- Products may include refrigerated, frozen, or ultra-cold vaccines; these require separate workstreams.
- Ministries of health retain allocation authority and control much of the district and facility last mile.
- Candidate gateways, hubs, borders, and air routes require country-level regulatory and operational approval.
- Routine demand is more predictable nationally than at district level.
- Campaign and outbreak demand may materially exceed routine volumes.
- Electricity, customs, road, security, and receiving performance vary within countries; national averages are inadequate for lane design.
- Donor, procurement, data-sharing, and reporting requirements may differ by country and program.

### 2.2 Product master data

Before procurement, pack-out, or dispatch, Quality and the product owner must approve a matrix for every product and presentation covering:

- Approved storage and transport range.
- Freeze sensitivity and minimum-temperature limits.
- Frozen or ultra-cold requirements.
- Permitted excursions and stability references.
- Presentation, pack size, thermal mass, orientation, and light sensitivity.
- Diluent, VVM, open-vial, and reconstitution requirements, where applicable.
- Minimum remaining shelf life at receipt.
- Batch-release, import, and product-authorization requirements.
- Compatibility with other products shipped together.

No shipment may rely operationally on the generic assumption that all vaccines require +2°C to +8°C.

### 2.3 Route and facility status

Every gateway, border, hub, airbridge, and corridor should be classified as:

1. **Proposed** — planning option only.
2. **Legally approved** — regulatory and import requirements confirmed.
3. **Pilot** — operating under controlled validation.
4. **Routine** — qualification complete and performance accepted.
5. **Suspended** — unavailable because of security, regulatory, quality, or performance failure.

# 3. Corridor segmentation and sequencing

## 3.1 Corridor segments

| Segment | Typical characteristics | Preferred model |
|---|---|---|
| Stable domestic and urban | High volume, predictable roads, established stores, better communications | Powered hubs; selective powered vehicles; passive district delivery |
| Established cross-border trunk | Commercially active but exposed to customs and border variability | Powered storage at hubs; extended-holdover passive transport; border contingency |
| Coastal-to-Sahel or Great Lakes | Long distance, heat, border exposure, variable security and roads | Passive trunk transport, inland staging, higher buffers, alternative routes |
| Remote and weather-sensitive last mile | Poor roads, flooding, limited power, ministry-controlled transport | Vaccine carriers, passive shippers, smaller lots, pre-positioning |
| Fragile-access and emergency | Security restrictions, blocked roads, humanitarian or outbreak response | Regional emergency stock, passive air shippers, airbridge, secure staging |

## 3.2 Phase 1: controlled launch and learning — months 0–3

Pilot a limited set of representative lanes:

1. Ghana domestic or urban route.
2. Nigeria or Tanzania high-volume domestic route.
3. Mombasa–Nairobi–Kampala cross-border trunk.
4. One coastal-to-Sahel route, subject to current security and customs approval.
5. One fragile-access or airbridge operation where required.

Measure:

- Planned, actual, and tail transit times.
- Customs dwell.
- Temperature performance and holdover remaining.
- Packaging, vehicle, power, and communications failures.
- Receiving and ministry last-mile delays.
- Wastage and emergency recovery.
- Equipment utilization and return-cycle time.
- Cost per dose and risk-adjusted recovery cost.

## 3.3 Phase 2: qualified expansion — months 4–9

Candidate corridors include:

- Abidjan–Ouagadougou.
- Tema or Lomé–Ouagadougou, subject to validation.
- Mombasa–Nairobi–Kigali.
- Dar es Salaam–Mbeya and selected neighboring routes.
- Selected northern Nigeria, western Uganda, and rural Ethiopian routes.

Expansion requires:

- Approved product-specific packaging and transport configuration.
- Qualified facilities, vehicles, shippers, and monitoring.
- At least two representative operating cycles, including relevant seasonal or congestion exposure where practical.
- Complete customs, temperature, inventory, and handoff records.
- Confirmed receiving and last-mile arrangements.
- Tested storage, vehicle, route, or air contingency.
- No unresolved critical deviation.

Pilot completion does not replace formal qualification or, by itself, authorize routine service.

## 3.4 Phase 3: regional optimization — months 9–18

- Recalculate buffers, cadence, fleet, and shipper quantities using observed data.
- Introduce powered transport only where reliability and risk-adjusted cost justify it.
- Regionalize maintenance, spare parts, training, and emergency equipment.
- Establish legally compliant emergency stock pooling.
- Add telematics, remote monitoring, and automated exception alerts.
- Requalify lanes annually and after material changes.

# 4. Corridor-specific strategy

## 4.1 West Africa

### Ghana: Tema/Accra–Kumasi and domestic routes

**Recommended status:** Phase 1 launch candidate.

- Use powered cold rooms at approved gateway and national stores.
- Use powered vehicles for dense urban and intercity movements only after qualification.
- Use passive shippers and vaccine carriers for district, outreach, and outage recovery.
- Schedule congested urban deliveries early morning or overnight where practical.
- Model localized flooding, rural access, congestion, and power interruptions.
- Use a provisional 2–4 week buffer beyond planned demand only as a scenario input, subject to shelf life, storage, and observed lead times.

### Nigeria: Lagos–Abuja–Kano and zonal distribution

**Recommended status:** Phase 1 for selected zones; not one national lane.

Manage separate plans for:

- Lagos and the southwest.
- Abuja and central distribution.
- Kano and northern routes.
- State-to-district and hard-to-reach movements.

Use:

- Powered cold rooms at approved gateway and zonal hubs.
- Powered vehicles on validated, secure, high-volume routes.
- Passive equipment for state-to-district delivery, weak-grid areas, and security-sensitive routes.
- Solar direct-drive or battery-backed systems where maintenance and security are demonstrably available.
- State-level receiving appointments, batch reconciliation, and district delivery schedules.

Buffers should be assessed by state or zone. Zonal emergency stock is preferable to relying solely on a national reserve.

### Abidjan–Ouagadougou

**Recommended status:** Phase 1/2 pilot; routine only after qualification.

- Use Abidjan as the powered import, quarantine, and consolidation hub.
- Use inland staging only at a secure, approved site with qualified storage.
- Use extended-holdover passive shippers for the cross-border trunk.
- Use continuous measurement and local recording for long-haul shipments.
- Maintain backup vehicle and qualified transfer capability.
- Do not make reefer transport the default until pre-cooling, maintenance, secure parking, border handling, and backup power are proven.
- Pre-position only quantities supported by shelf life, storage, and consumption.
- Use inland or Burkina Faso-side stock before allowing a delayed shipment to become a temperature or stockout event.

### Tema/Lomé–Ouagadougou–Niamey

**Recommended status:** Conditional Phase 2/3; not a default routine lane.

Treat Burkina Faso and Niger as separate legs with separate:

- Permits and customs processes.
- Security assessments.
- Transit and holdover requirements.
- Buffers and receiving arrangements.
- Contingency routes.

Use smaller consignments, inland staging, passive transport, and contracted airbridge capacity for urgent or campaign volumes. Suspend dispatch when security or expected duration exceeds the qualified envelope.

### Dakar–Bamako

**Recommended status:** Tier 3; emergency or conditional use only.

Do not establish routine replenishment without current evidence on:

- Security and access.
- Customs and import processes.
- Road availability.
- Qualified storage.
- Ministry receiving and onward distribution.

Prefer secure regional emergency stock and airbridge options to large destination inventories. Use passive road shippers only where the full route and recovery plan are qualified.

## 4.2 East Africa

### Mombasa–Nairobi–Kampala

**Recommended status:** Phase 1 launch cross-border corridor, subject to approval.

- Use Mombasa for import staging, Nairobi for consolidation and contingency, and Kampala for destination national storage, subject to qualification.
- Use powered cold rooms at approved hubs.
- Use extended-holdover passive containers for the cross-border trunk unless reefer performance is proven.
- Pre-clear documentation and confirm Uganda-side storage, receiver, and onward delivery.
- Monitor port congestion, border dwell, traffic, long rains, flooding, and landslides by route segment.
- Maintain Uganda-side contingency stock and a legally approved, operationally tested alternative crossing or air option.

### Mombasa–Nairobi–Kigali and Great Lakes routes

**Recommended status:** Phase 2/3; qualify by country and crossing.

Treat these as more complex than the Kampala route because of additional borders, terrain, lake crossings, transshipment, and access constraints.

Use:

- Powered storage at approved hubs.
- Passive extended-holdover shippers for cross-border legs.
- Smaller shipment lots.
- Destination-side staging.
- Solar refrigeration only where maintenance, security, and spare parts are confirmed.
- Airfreight for urgent quantities where road-tail risk exceeds qualified holdover.

### Tanzania: Dar es Salaam–Dodoma–Mbeya and onward routes

**Recommended status:** Phase 1 domestic trunk; Phase 2 cross-border expansion.

- Use Dar es Salaam as powered import and consolidation hub.
- Use Dodoma or another approved central facility as an inland hub.
- Treat domestic Tanzania and cross-border extensions as separate lanes.
- Use powered vehicles only on qualified domestic segments.
- Use passive shippers for long-haul, district, and cross-border movements.
- Model heavy rain, flooding, long-distance exposure, and cyclone risk by route.
- Use solar direct-drive systems only at facilities with technical support and security.

### Addis Ababa and Ethiopian regional distribution

**Recommended status:** Phase 1 controlled hub; regional lanes individually gated.

- Use Addis Ababa for controlled national distribution and emergency airfreight.
- Segment regional routes by terrain, security, weather, and facility readiness.
- Use powered storage at Addis and approved regional stores.
- Use passive road and air-to-ground transfers.
- Pre-position at secure regional hubs before predictable access deterioration.
- Use humanitarian or ministry coordination for restricted or displaced-population areas.

### Horn of Africa, Somalia, South Sudan, and other fragile-access routes

**Recommended status:** Tier 3 emergency or conditional operation.

- Use Nairobi, Addis Ababa, or another approved secure hub.
- Use passive air shippers and vaccine carriers as the primary transport solution.
- Use portable powered units only at secure, staffed staging sites.
- Use solar refrigeration only where maintenance and security are proven.
- Maintain regional emergency stock and contracted airbridge capacity.
- Dispatch only with current security clearance, confirmed receiving capacity, and an alternate access plan.
- Suspend road movement when the route is not within the validated temperature and security envelope.

# 5. Equipment choices and decision rules

## 5.1 Passive systems

Passive equipment is the default where uncertainty exceeds the value of powered transport, including:

- Variable customs dwell.
- Multiple borders or transshipments.
- Unreliable electricity.
- Motorcycle, boat, 4x4, or mixed-mode last mile.
- Low-volume shipments.
- Overnight staging outside qualified storage.
- New or unproven lanes.
- Security or road conditions that may cause diversion.

Each pack-out must specify:

- Product and presentation.
- Minimum and maximum payload.
- Coolant or PCM type and conditioning method.
- Loading diagram.
- Monitor placement.
- Opening, inspection, and resealing procedures.
- Qualified duration and ambient profile.
- Cleaning, inspection, reuse, and retirement criteria.

The required qualified duration must cover planned transport, expected customs and handling dwell, destination waiting time, and a justified recovery margin. Statistical percentiles support routine planning; worst credible conditions define qualification.

## 5.2 Powered facilities

Powered cold rooms, refrigerators, freezers, solar systems, and batteries require:

- Usable capacity assessment, including quarantine, segregation, airflow, and FEFO space.
- Temperature mapping under representative load.
- Alarm and response testing.
- Generator changeover and load testing.
- Site-specific power-loss clock.
- Fuel, battery, solar, and spare-parts plan.
- Qualified passive transfer capacity.
- Named 24-hour escalation responsibility.

There is no universal fuel-autonomy rule. Required autonomy depends on local restoration time, transfer time, access conditions, and product risk.

## 5.3 Powered vehicles

Use powered vehicles only when the lane has:

- Stable and sufficient volume.
- Qualified pre-cooling and temperature mapping.
- Controlled loading and unloading.
- Reliable maintenance response.
- Secure overnight parking.
- Backup power during loading and stops.
- GPS and temperature monitoring.
- A qualified transfer procedure for breakdowns.

A qualified passive shipper is preferable where reefer maintenance, border handling, or overnight power is less reliable than passive holdover.

## 5.4 Equipment-sizing requirements

Before procurement, size equipment by product, corridor, and scenario using:

- Doses and pack dimensions.
- Thermal mass.
- Routine, campaign, outbreak, and recovery volumes.
- Shipment cadence.
- Peak simultaneous inventory.
- Usable cold-room capacity.
- Vehicle and shipper payload.
- Return-cycle and cleaning time.
- Equipment downtime.
- Spare capacity for simultaneous disruptions.

# 6. Operating model and governance

## 6.1 Responsibilities

| Party | Primary responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Firm | Network planning, packaging, trunk transport, monitoring, inventory visibility, exception management, training, and agreed equipment support |
| Ministry of health | Allocation, campaign planning, receiving authorization, district schedules, last mile, quarantine/release decisions as agreed |
| Customs broker | Permits, pre-clearance, document accuracy, inspection coordination, and border escalation |
| Carrier | Qualified vehicle or shipper, driver training, route execution, GPS, temperature records, and incident reporting |
| Depot or hub | Storage, power backup, receiving inspection, batch reconciliation, quarantine, and stock control |
| Quality unit | Qualification, deviations, excursion assessment, CAPA, recall, and authorized disposition |
| Security/weather function | Route advisories, closures, access restrictions, and alternate-route recommendations |

## 6.2 Ministry operating protocol

Before routine launch, agree in writing:

- Handoff points and custody transfer.
- Receiving hours and appointment requirements.
- Storage capacity and facility readiness.
- District, facility, and outreach schedules.
- Last-mile transport ownership.
- Data-sharing and electronic proof-of-delivery rules.
- Quarantine, excursion, release, and reallocation authority.
- Wastage, recall, and reverse-logistics responsibilities.
- Equipment ownership, return, and maintenance.
- Service-level scope, claims, remedies, and dispute resolution.

The firm should not guarantee facility-level delivery unless that responsibility is explicitly contracted.

## 6.3 Control tower

Operate a regional control tower with country cells including:

- Operations lead.
- Quality lead.
- Customs/regulatory lead.
- Ministry focal point and deputy.
- Primary and backup carrier.
- Depot representative.
- Last-mile coordinator.
- Security/weather contact.

Cadence:

- **Weekly:** campaigns, high-risk lanes, exceptions, holdover, and security.
- **Biweekly:** high-risk inventory and route review.
- **Monthly:** routine demand, equipment, carrier, broker, and wastage performance.
- **Quarterly:** lane classification, seasonal plan, buffer review, and contingency exercise.
- **Before dispatch:** documentation, receiving, route, monitoring, and contingency confirmation.

# 7. Seasonal schedule considerations

Each country should maintain a monthly, lane-specific calendar covering:

- Heat and rainfall exposure.
- Flooding and landslides.
- Bridge, ferry, and road availability.
- Cyclone exposure.
- Port and urban congestion.
- Public holidays and elections.
- Security restrictions.
- Campaign and outreach dates.
- Pre-positioning deadlines.
- Dynamic weather, road, and security triggers.

### Operating rules

1. Pre-position only quantities supported by shelf life, storage, and consumption.
2. Confirm roads, bridges, ferries, receiving staff, and storage before dispatch.
3. Increase monitoring during hot, rainy, flood, and cyclone periods.
4. Avoid major shipments immediately before known closures or severe weather.
5. Pre-book alternative carriers, storage, and air capacity for high-risk periods.
6. Reduce temporary buffers after disruption using FEFO.
7. Reconfirm conditions immediately before dispatch; route status can change rapidly.

West African coastal-to-Sahel lanes require particular attention to heat, rainy-season road deterioration, flooding, and security-related delay. East African Great Lakes and inland routes require additional attention to long and short rains, landslides, lake crossings, and flood isolation. Cyclone exposure must be modeled separately for Mozambique, Madagascar, coastal Tanzania, and affected Indian Ocean routes.

# 8. Temperature and data controls

## 8.1 Monitoring standard

Use the following terms consistently:

- **Continuous measurement and local recording:** required for long-haul, cross-border, campaign, emergency, and high-value shipments.
- **Real-time remote transmission:** preferred at high-volume hubs and priority routes where connectivity and response capability exist.
- **Manual fallback documentation:** permitted only under an approved fallback procedure.

Devices must be:

- Calibrated and within validity.
- Suitable for the transport mode, including air transport where relevant.
- Linked to shipment, product, batch, container, route, and handoff records.
- Placed according to the qualified pack-out or facility mapping.
- Retrieved and reviewed at receipt.

Freeze-sensitive products require minimum-temperature monitoring or an approved freeze indicator.

## 8.2 Data integrity

The control tower should maintain a shipment record containing:

- Product, batch, expiry, quantity, and status.
- Origin, destination, route, and milestones.
- Container, vehicle, seal, and logger identifiers.
- Dispatch, border, receipt, and final-delivery timestamps.
- Temperature data and exception history.
- Customs status.
- Proof of delivery.
- Quarantine, disposition, and CAPA records.

A missing, unreadable, or unexplained temperature record is a deviation requiring Quality assessment.

## 8.3 Excursion response

1. Quarantine affected product.
2. Preserve logger, seal, loading, route, and handoff records.
3. Notify Quality, ministry, carrier, and depot.
4. Assess exposure against approved stability information.
5. Release, return, relabel, or destroy only through authorized disposition.
6. Investigate root cause and implement CAPA.
7. Review related shipments if the cause may be systemic.

# 9. Inventory and spoilage-risk controls

## 9.1 Buffer policy

Use:

\[
\text{Required buffer}
=
\text{Demand during lead-time uncertainty}
+
\text{Campaign/emergency reserve}
+
\text{Recovery quantity}
\]

The calculation must include:

- Observed transit and customs variability.
- Product stability and shelf life.
- Pack size and wastage.
- Usable storage capacity.
- Campaign and outreach schedules.
- Minimum order quantities.
- Equipment downtime.
- Recovery time after disruption.
- Return and replenishment lead times.

### Provisional planning ranges

These are scenario inputs, not universal rules:

| Lane type | Initial planning approach |
|---|---|
| Stable urban/domestic | 2–4 weeks beyond planned demand |
| Established cross-border | 4–6 weeks |
| High-risk Sahel, Great Lakes, or weather-exposed | 6–10 weeks only where shelf life and storage support it |
| Fragile access | Smaller destination lots; emergency stock mainly at secure regional hubs |

Separate routine, campaign, emergency, and recovery stock. Apply FEFO, weekly short-dated review at high-risk hubs, batch reconciliation, recall traceability, and wastage classification.

## 9.2 Cadence

- Stable urban/domestic: weekly or biweekly.
- Established cross-border: every 2–4 weeks.
- Remote domestic: smaller shipments every 2–4 weeks aligned with outreach.
- High-risk corridors: smaller, more frequent, or access-triggered shipments.
- Campaigns: dispatch by district start date and confirmed storage capacity.

Do not delay a shipment solely to fill a truck if stock will fall below the approved reorder point. Do not ship a large load solely for utilization if storage or last-mile readiness is unconfirmed.

## 9.3 Spoilage-risk tree

| Risk | Preventive control | Detection and response |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature excursion | Qualified pack-out, monitoring, trained handlers | Quarantine, stability assessment, CAPA |
| Freeze exposure | Conditioned PCM, freeze indicators, correct loading | Quarantine and minimum-temperature assessment |
| Expiry | FEFO, shelf-life rules, short-dated review | Transfer or accelerated approved use |
| Open-vial wastage | Session planning and presentation-specific forecasting | Record by facility/session and revise allocations |
| Missing or incompatible diluent | Product matrix and shipment compatibility check | Block use, correct supply, report deviation |
| Damage or breakage | Packaging inspection and secure handling | Segregate, reconcile, investigate |
| Recall or quality defect | Batch traceability and recall procedures | Stop distribution, quarantine, trace, recover |
| Receiving refusal | Confirmed appointments and storage checks | Reschedule within qualified window or stage |
| Uncontrolled transfer | Approved transfer SOP and qualified equipment | Quarantine if evidence or temperature is compromised |
| Campaign cancellation | District-level planning and reverse logistics | Reallocate under authority and review expiry |
| Last-mile failure | Ministry operating protocol and backup providers | Use district stock, reschedule, or escalate |
| Monitoring/data loss | Calibrated devices and validated fallback records | Quarantine pending Quality assessment |
| Theft or security loss | Seals, custody records, GPS, route controls | Notify security, reconcile, investigate, recall if needed |

# 10. Contingency and emergency response

## Customs delay

- Keep cargo in qualified storage where possible.
- Monitor remaining holdover.
- Escalate through broker, customs, ministry, and gateway management.
- Release destination or regional stock if service is threatened.
- Reroute only through a legally approved and operationally tested route.
- Use airfreight for priority quantities rather than risking the full shipment.

## Vehicle breakdown or uncontrolled transfer

- Keep the shipper closed and secure.
- Deploy a qualified backup vehicle.
- Transfer under documented temperature, seal, and custody controls.
- Move to approved cold storage if repair exceeds remaining holdover.
- Quarantine if temperature evidence is lost or transfer is uncontrolled.

## Power failure

- Start generator or switch to battery/solar.
- Restrict door opening.
- Start the site-specific power-loss clock.
- Transfer before validated autonomy expires.
- Quarantine if product exposure becomes uncertain.

## Flood, conflict, or route closure

- Stop dispatch when expected duration exceeds the qualified envelope.
- Activate an approved alternate route, staging site, or airbridge.
- Release regional or ministry emergency stock.
- Reconcile and replenish emergency inventory.
- Update lane risk, seasonal assumptions, and CAPA.

## Compound disruption

For events such as border closure plus power failure:

- Prioritize products by public-health urgency and remaining stability.
- Move stock to the nearest qualified facility.
- Use regional emergency stock.
- Activate airbridge or alternate gateway.
- Escalate to the regional incident team.
- Record decisions, costs, allocations, and recovery actions.

## Minimum business-continuity scenarios

Test and resource at least:

1. Seven-day border closure.
2. Multi-day gateway cold-room failure.
3. Campaign surge during heavy rains.
4. Failure of primary and alternate routes.
5. Control-tower communications outage.
6. Reefer breakdown with incomplete temperature data.
7. Multi-country product recall.

Each scenario should specify decision authority, stock, storage and transport capacity, communication fallback, recovery-time objective, cost owner, and replenishment obligation.

# 12. Implementation roadmap

## First 30 days

Complete:

- Product temperature, freeze, diluent, VVM, open-vial, and shelf-life matrix.
- Lane register with origin, destination, borders, dwell, weather, security, power, storage, and last-mile data.
- Country customs and regulatory responsibility matrices.
- Status classification for gateways, hubs, borders, routes, and airbridge options.
- Ministry focal points and draft operating protocols.
- Cold-room, vehicle, passive-equipment, power, and monitoring audits.
- Quality, excursion, quarantine, recall, and emergency-release procedures.
- Shipment-ID, data, and monitoring standards.
- Organizational and 24-hour escalation plan.

**Gate:** Approve only the defined Phase 1 pilot scope after critical product, regulatory, receiving, and safety controls are complete.

## Days 31–90

- Qualify facilities, vehicles, passive configurations, and monitoring devices.
- Contract primary and backup carriers, brokers, storage, and emergency transport.
- Complete Phase 1 pilots.
- Train firm, carrier, depot, customs, ministry, and last-mile personnel.
- Launch the control-tower dashboard.
- Test customs delay, breakdown, power failure, excursion, communications loss, and route closure.
- Complete the vaccine-program annex covering VVMs, diluents, open-vial policy, reconstitution, ancillary supplies, recalls, pharmacovigilance, and wastage.

**Gate:** Move a lane toward routine service only after formal qualification, pilot review, deviation assessment, and ministry acceptance.

## Months 4–9

- Expand through lane-readiness gates.
- Install or rehabilitate backup power and selected solar systems.
- Add inland staging and border-side transfer capability.
- Pre-position for validated seasonal risks.
- Contract airbridge capacity for designated fragile corridors.
- Add remote monitoring and vehicle telematics.
- Complete ministry operating agreements.
- Complete equipment-sizing, fleet-capacity, and risk-adjusted cost models.
- Add compound-disruption resource tables and exercise them.

## Months 9–18

- Recalculate buffers, cadence, fleet, and shipper quantities from observed data.
- Add powered transport only where reliability and risk-adjusted cost justify it.
- Regionalize maintenance, spares, training, and emergency equipment.
- Establish compliant emergency stock pools.
- Automate temperature, dwell, holdover, power, inventory, and milestone alerts.
- Conduct annual lane requalification.
- Update contractual service levels using demonstrated performance.

# 13. Performance dashboard

Track by product, country, corridor, carrier, and handoff:

- On-time and in-full delivery.
- 90th and 95th percentile transit and customs dwell.
- Holdover remaining at receipt.
- Temperature and freeze-event rate.
- Complete temperature-data rate.
- Stockout days.
- Expiry, open-vial, and logistics-attributable wastage.
- Cold-room uptime and backup autonomy.
- Confirmed-receiving compliance.
- Ministry last-mile completion.
- Exception detection and resolution time.
- Carrier, broker, and depot deviation rates.
- Emergency shipments and root causes.
- Cost per dose and risk-adjusted recovery cost.
- Recall traceability and completion time.

### Provisional management objectives

These are management objectives, not universal guarantees:

- Stable lanes: 95–98% on time.
- Established cross-border lanes: 90–95% on time.
- Fragile-access lanes: measure access-window reliability, stockout avoidance, and emergency response time.
- Preventable temperature excursions: below 1%.
- Complete traceable temperature data for long-haul, campaign, emergency, and high-value shipments.
- Zero dispatches without confirmed destination storage.

Targets must be reviewed after the first two operating quarters and must not encourage unsafe dispatch.

# 14. Key risks, assumptions, and final approval conditions

The principal unresolved items are:

- Product-specific temperature and stability requirements.
- Actual lane transit and customs dwell data.
- Usable cold-storage capacity.
- Power and maintenance performance.
- Seasonal road and security calendars.
- Ministry receiving and last-mile capability.
- Regulatory and import approvals.
- Airbridge, backup storage, carrier, and alternate-route capacity.
- Equipment quantities and total cost.
- Custody, liability, wastage, and emergency-cost allocation.
- Staffing and 24-hour response capability.

## Final recommendation

Approve:

- The tiered operating architecture.
- Preparatory contracting.
- The limited Phase 1 validation program.
- Development of the required product, lane, quality, regulatory, equipment, cost, and ministry deliverables.

Do not yet approve:

- Unrestricted routine deployment.
- Final fleet or cold-room procurement.
- Universal buffer rules.
- Unvalidated alternate routes or airbridges.
- Contractual service guarantees based on provisional targets.
- Routine frozen or ultra-cold distribution under the standard refrigerated model.

## Practical priorities for 2026 execution
