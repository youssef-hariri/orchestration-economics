## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Following the 2026 acquisitions, the Group now operates three sovereign banks with heterogeneous core systems, divergent regulatory frameworks, and distinct cultural economies. The strategic mandate is to deliver unified digital product capabilities while preserving local licensure, customer trust, and supervisory relationships.

**Strategic Philosophy: *Sovereign-First Federalism***

African banking regulators are sovereign gatekeepers with veto authority over architecture, data flows, and management control—not project stakeholders to be consulted after design. Accordingly, the Group will operate three distinct, locally-licensed banks unified by capital discipline, product patterns, and risk governance. We will not attempt unified ledgers, cross-border data lakes, or masterbrand imposition.

**Financial Boundary Conditions**

| Metric | Commitment |
|:---|:---|
| Total Integration Investment | **$200–$230 million** over five years |
| Risk-Adjusted Integration NPV | **1.4x–1.8x** |
| Target Cost-to-Income Ratio (Year 5) | **54–58%** |
| Sovereign Synergy Run-Rate (Year 5) | **$27 million annually** |
| Program Contingency Reserve | **$15 million** |
| Nigeria FX-Trapped Operating Reserve | **$25–$40 million** (naira-denominated) |

The Sovereign-First model accepts a **$36 million annual complexity tax** versus theoretical full convergence, but raises the probability of regulatory execution from below 30 percent to an executable certainty. The Board must treat this tax as the premium paid for operational survival.

## 2. GOVERNANCE, CULTURAL ALIGNMENT & MANAGEMENT INTEGRATION

### 2.1 Joint Integration Veto Councils
Each country maintains an **Integration Veto Council** chaired by the Local CEO, with the Group CEO, Group CTO, Group Chief Legal, and an independent local regulatory advisor as members. The Council must approve:
*   Any architecture touching the core ledger or regulatory reporting.
*   Any customer-facing digital product launch.
*   Any management change affecting Risk, Compliance, Finance, or IT.
*   Any brand campaign referencing the Group.

**Veto Rule:** The Local CEO may veto any group initiative that, in their sole judgment supported by local counsel, creates regulatory jeopardy or material customer flight risk. Override requires a Group Board vote, not executive fiat.

### 2.2 Country-Specific Cultural Alignment Programs

**Nigeria — The Promoter-Director Alignment Program**
Nigerian banking authority flows through ethnic, political, and shareholder networks as much as formal titles.
*   Map and retain promoter-directors and the Managing Director’s coalition within 30 days.
*   Conduct private alignment sessions with each major shareholder bloc before any public integration announcement.
*   Appoint a **Country Regulatory Advisor** (former CBN Director level) reporting to the Group Chief Legal, insulating regulatory dialogue from technology optimism.
*   Cap expatriate IT architects at Ministry of Interior quota limits; build a Nigerian-led delivery team with group secondment support.

**Kenya — The "Silicon Savannah" Protocol**
Kenyan digital banking teams possess world-class expertise. Imposing a foreign-designed platform will trigger organizational rejection.
*   The **Kenyan CIO leads UX and mobile architecture** for the Kenyan Digital Sidecar. Group provides a *pattern library*, not a front-end blueprint.
*   An **"Innovation Respect" charter** commits the Group to preserving M-Pesa interoperability, local API standards, and Swahili-first microcopy as non-negotiable constraints.
*   The Group CPO must negotiate M-Pesa commercial terms directly with Safaricom before app architecture is finalized.

**Morocco — The Francophone Technocratic & Social Dialogue Program**
Moroccan banking culture is hierarchical, BAM-facing, and legally rigid.
*   All BAM submissions, IT architecture documents, and compliance filings must be in **French**, translated and notarized locally.
*   Appoint a **Moroccan CIO** with prior BAM IT examination experience and statutory authority to sign circulaires techniques.
*   **Tamazight (Berber)** language support is added to the digital roadmap (Months 12–18) to satisfy financial inclusion mandates.
*   A mandatory **Social Dialogue Protocol** with representative unions precedes any restructuring or management change.

## 4. TECHNOLOGY & DATA ARCHITECTURE

### 4.1 The Digital Sidecar Model
Legacy cores cannot be safely decoupled in six months:
*   **Finacle 10.x (Nigeria)** is batch-oriented; an API facade would create balance mismatches and fraud holes.
*   **Customized Temenos T24 (Kenya)** cannot be "upgraded" to cloud-native; migration to Transact is a multi-year data-model rebuild.
*   **Sopra / FIS Equation (Morocco)** is character-terminal legacy; wrapping it requires fragile screen-scraping middleware.

**The Solution:** A **Digital Sidecar** in each market—a new, regulated digital channel operating **alongside** existing e-banking.
*   New and migrating customers use the Sidecar (unified group UX, local brand skin).
*   Existing e-banking remains live for non-migrated customers.
*   The Sidecar writes to the legacy core via **in-country middleware** in batch or near-real-time, respecting the core’s actual technical capability.
*   This decouples **customer-experience velocity** from **core-migration risk**.

### 4.2 Infrastructure: Local Colocation, Not Cloud Abstraction

| Market | Primary Site | Disaster Recovery | Rationale |
|--------|--------------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Nigeria** | Rack Centre Lagos (CBN-certified Tier III+) | Secondary Lagos/Abuja site | CBN requires physical inspection rights. No hyperscaler primary. |
| **Kenya** | Wananchi / iHub partner Tier III+ | Alternate Nairobi zone | CBK expects physical DR within Kenya. |
| **Morocco** | Maroc Datacenter Casablanca | Casa Cloud / Rabat DR | BAM requires local hosting attestation with physical DR. |

### 4.3 Three Sovereign Data Lakes
There is **no Group Data Lake containing raw customer records**.
*   Each country operates a **Sovereign Data Lake**.
*   A locally-controlled **Analytics Export Layer** generates **irreversibly aggregated, statistically anonymized KPIs** (e.g., portfolio default rates, segment deposit growth, channel usage indices).
*   Only these flat, non-individual KPI files are transmitted to Group for consolidation.
*   **Legal sign-off is required in each jurisdiction** before any export layer is activated.

### 4.4 Cybersecurity Coordination
While PII cannot flow, threat intelligence must. A **Cybersecurity Coordination Treaty** governs the exchange of non-PII indicators of compromise, anonymized attack signatures, and coordinated incident response across the three banks without violating data sovereignty.

## 5. BRAND ARCHITECTURE

We adopt an **Endorsed Brand Architecture**, not a unified masterbrand.

| Element | Decision | Rationale |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| **Masterbrand** | Group name appears only as corporate endorsement: *"[LocalBrand] — A [GroupName] Company"* | Avoids liability confusion. Preserves local trust. Satisfies regulatory preference for clear local licensure. |
| **Local Brand Preservation** | Nigeria, Kenya, and Morocco retain existing customer-facing names for **36 months minimum.** | Nigeria’s ownership-sensitive depositors need continuity. Kenya’s digital users need local identity. Morocco’s prestige market needs French/local champion signaling. |
| **Digital App Branding** | Each Digital Sidecar carries the **local brand skin** (colors, iconography, language). Commonality is in UX *patterns* (navigation, security flows, accessibility standards), not visual identity. | Kenyan app must feel Kenyan. Moroccan app must feel Moroccan. |
| **Liability Clarity** | The Digital Sidecar is legally the local bank’s product. Terms & Conditions, dispute resolution, and fraud liability sit entirely with the local entity. | Prevents regulatory confusion over outsourcing and protects the Group from direct host-country banking liability. |

**Financial Impact:** The endorsed brand architecture is estimated to prevent **$8–$12 million** in avoided deposit flight in Nigeria alone, based on historical African banking M&A patterns.

### 6.1 The Federal Premium Framework
The Sovereign-First model materially lowers the synergy ceiling but raises execution certainty.

| Synergy Category | Full Convergence Fantasy | Sovereign-First Reality | Capture Rate |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| IT Infrastructure (one cloud, one core) | $18M/yr | $6M/yr (vendor consolidation only) | 33% |
| Procurement & Vendor Leverage | $8M/yr | $6M/yr | 75% |
| Treasury & ALM Optimization | $12M/yr | $3M/yr (Nigeria/Morocco liquidity trapped) | 25% |
| Cross-Border Trade Revenue | $15M/yr | $8M/yr (aggregated KYC/revenue data only) | 53% |
| Analytics & Credit Scoring | $10M/yr | $4M/yr (local models, local data) | 40% |
| **Total Annual Synergy (Year 5)** | **$63M** | **$27M** | **43%** |

**Integration Tax:** The Sovereign-First model imposes a **$36 million annual complexity tax** versus full convergence. However, the probability of full-convergence success is below 30 percent, yielding an expected value of only **$19 million/year**. The Sovereign-First **$27 million** is the superior risk-adjusted value creation path.

| Priority | Mechanism | Amount | Rationale |
|:---|:---|---:|:---|
| **P1: Nigeria FX-Trapped Operating Reserve** | Naira-denominated local capital injection | $25–40M | CBN may block software royalty repatriation. Nigeria must self-fund Sidecar opex for 18–24 months without cross-border invoicing. |
| **P2: Regulatory Capital Buffers** | Tier 1 capital injection to all three subsidiaries | +200 bps CAR | Mandatory per strategy. Nigeria especially vulnerable to CBN risk-weighting adjustments during migration. |

### 6.3 Intercompany Pricing Protocol
To satisfy CBN, CBK, and BAM related-party scrutiny:
1.  **Pattern Library Licensing:** Group licenses UX/security patterns to subsidiaries at **cost-plus-5%**, settled via local-currency equity injections where FX is restricted.
2.  **Management Fees:** Capped at **1.2% of local revenue** (market benchmark for African banking groups). Requires Office des Changes pre-approval in Morocco.
3.  **Data Analytics:** No cross-border data fee. Group pays subsidiaries for **anonymized KPI extraction** as a data vendor, avoiding service-fee scrutiny.

## 7. RISK-WEIGHTED SEQUENCING & PORTFOLIO DEPLOYMENT

We treat the three markets as a **venture portfolio**, not a uniform pipeline. Capital deployment follows a **"Prove → Scale → Defend"** cadence.

| Market | Risk-Adjusted IRR | Strategic Role | Capital Deployment Phase | Decision Logic |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Kenya** | **22–26%** | **Proof-of-Concept / Quick Win** | **Phase 1:** Months 0–18 | Lowest regulatory latency, highest digital maturity. Sidecar demonstrates group UX patterns to regulators and investors within 12 months. |
| **Nigeria** | **14–18%** | **Scale Engine / Earnings Anchor** | **Phase 2:** Months 6–36 (overlap with Kenya) | Highest absolute NPV but highest variance. Wave 0 capital front-loaded only after CBN Comfort Letter received. Hold back 40% of Nigeria Wave 1 budget until Gate 1 cleared. |
| **Morocco** | **4–8%** | **Strategic Option / Defensive Moat** | **Phase 3:** Months 12–48 | Longest regulatory cycle, lowest synergy yield. Cap exposure at $66M. Accept "permanent Sidecar" as base case; fund core retrofit only if BAM issues formal circulaire (low probability). |

### Staggered Parallel Execution
*   **Months 0–12:** All three markets run Wave 0 in parallel, but **capital release is tranched**:
    *   **Kenya:** 100% of Wave 0 budget released immediately (CBK approval high-confidence).
    *   **Nigeria:** 50% released at Month 0; remaining 50% gated on CBN Comfort Letter (expected Month 6–9).
    *   **Morocco:** 60% released at Month 0; 40% gated on formal BAM pre-consultation minutes.
*   **Months 12–30:** Kenya Sidecar launches first (Month 12 target). Nigeria Sidecar launches second (Month 18). Morocco Sidecar launches last (Month 24–30).
*   **Months 24+:** Nigeria core migration proceeds only if CAR and NDIC protocols are secured. Morocco core migration is **deprioritized** unless BAM signals explicit approval.
