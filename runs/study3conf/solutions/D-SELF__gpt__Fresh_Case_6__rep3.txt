# Sovereign-Cloud Transition Strategy

## Executive objective

Move from a single-region SaaS architecture to national or regional operating environments while preserving:

- Customer trust and retention
- Regulatory and contractual readiness
- Core functionality and performance
- Security and operational control
- Resilience and service continuity
- Predictable pricing
- Portability and exit rights

The transition should be presented as a **jurisdiction-specific operating program**, not as a universal legal requirement or a marketing relaunch.

> We will identify what is required, show what our architecture provides, disclose what remains outside our control, protect critical functionality and continuity, explain the cost, and provide practical remedies when commitments are missed.

Classify every customer move as:

- **Mandatory:** required by verified law, regulation, contract, accreditation, or customer policy
- **Recommended:** addresses a documented legal, procurement, operational, or customer-risk concern
- **Optional:** chosen for local performance, control, risk reduction, or customer preference

Do not describe a migration as a customer choice where no practical alternative exists.

---

## Universal commitments

These commitments should apply globally, subject to applicable law and contract.

### Transparency

- No unnotified migration
- Individual customer impact assessment before cutover
- Clear notice of data, processing, support, backup, logging, AI, and metadata locations
- Explicit disclosure of feature, performance, resilience, pricing, and support differences
- Version-controlled documentation and evidence
- Clear separation of legal requirements from provider design choices

### Customer protection

- No silent degradation of functionality
- Defined critical workflows and acceptance tests
- Tested migration, validation, and recovery procedures
- Rollback or forward-recovery plan for every workload
- Export, deletion, and exit procedures
- Commercial relief for provider-driven migrations
- Defined remedies for missed commitments
- Accessible escalation and post-migration support

### Truthful claims

Do not claim that local hosting automatically provides:

- Legal compliance
- Privacy
- Security
- Resilience
- Immunity from foreign law or government access
- National independence
- Full product parity

Use qualified language:

> The described controls may support the customer’s compliance assessment. They do not constitute a legal conclusion and do not, by themselves, guarantee compliance with all applicable requirements.

---

## Locally tailored approaches

Local teams may adapt:

- Language and terminology
- Legal and procurement references
- Contracting entities and partner disclosures
- Support hours and escalation routes
- Pricing and invoicing structure
- Examples and customer stories
- Government or regulator engagement
- Stakeholder sequence and communication channels

Local adaptation must not change the underlying facts, security commitments, scope of controls, or approved remedies.

Avoid politically loaded slogans such as “national independence” or “take back control” unless a formally defined policy term is required. Do not imply that domestic governments are inherently trustworthy or that foreign vendors are inherently unsafe.

---

# 1. Define sovereignty precisely

“Sovereign cloud” is not a universal legal category. Describe each environment through a capability profile:

1. Production data location
2. Backup and disaster-recovery location
3. Processing, logging, telemetry, and metadata location
4. AI prompt, inference, output, embedding, and model-log location
5. Administrator and support-personnel location, employer, and legal entity
6. Provider, partner, customer, emergency, and government access
7. Encryption and key custody, including limitations
8. Parent-company, hyperscaler, software, hardware, telecom, and partner dependencies
9. Applicable laws and legal authorities
10. Portability, export, deletion, and exit terms
11. Availability, RTO, RPO, maintenance, and recovery-test results
12. Local contracting, executive accountability, and regulator liaison

Use specific labels such as:

- Regional data residency
- National data residency
- Local processing
- Restricted foreign administration
- Local support operations
- Customer-controlled keys
- National disaster recovery
- Regional disaster recovery
- Local contracting entity

A local partner, local personnel, or customer-managed encryption keys should not be presented as proof of sovereignty without evidence of their actual scope and limitations.

---

# 2. Customer segmentation

Segment by regulatory exposure, operating model, trust concern, and migration complexity—not nationality alone.

## A. Mandated and highly regulated

Public sector, defense, financial services, healthcare, telecommunications, and critical infrastructure.

**Needs**

- Formal control evidence
- Restricted administration
- Regulator and procurement support
- Tested recovery
- Contractual remedies
- Executive governance

**Approach**

- Provider-managed migration
- Named executive sponsor
- Dedicated solution architect
- Legal and security annexes
- Migration funding
- Regulator-ready evidence

## B. Multinational enterprises

**Needs**

- Local data boundaries
- Global administration where permitted
- Consistent APIs and user experience
- Cross-border analytics and identity
- Country-by-country visibility

**Approach**

- Regional data planes with a global control plane only where permitted
- Explicit register for identity, billing, policy, telemetry, metadata, analytics, and support flows
- Policy-based workload placement
- Global executive steering group

## C. Sensitive commercial enterprises

**Needs**

- Optionality
- Price-performance clarity
- Procurement evidence
- Practical business value

**Approach**

- Offer regional and national options without artificial urgency
- Explain cost, functionality, resilience, and support trade-offs
- Avoid selling localization as inherently safer

## D. Smaller and less regulated customers

**Needs**

- Simple choices
- Low migration effort
- Predictable pricing
- Accessible support

**Approach**

- Recommended default environment
- Self-service and guided migration
- Bundled migration assistance
- Low-bandwidth tools
- Plain-language documentation

## E. Trust-sensitive customers

Classify concerns as:

- Foreign-jurisdiction exposure
- Domestic-government access
- Privacy or civil-liberties risk
- Local accountability
- Feature parity
- Price
- Resilience
- Procurement evidence

Match each concern to the appropriate evidence and remedy. Do not assume that customers in a country share one view of government or foreign vendors.

# 3. Regulatory and legal framework

Maintain a jurisdiction-and-sector matrix covering:

- Data categories in scope
- Storage and processing
- Backups and recovery
- Logs, telemetry, metadata, and support tickets
- AI prompts, outputs, embeddings, caches, and model logs
- Encryption and key management
- Personnel and administrator location
- Subprocessors, partners, ownership, and control
- Government and law-enforcement access
- Cross-border transfers
- Retention, deletion, and legal holds
- Incident notification
- Accreditation and procurement requirements
- Regulatory uncertainty and review dates

Classify each conclusion as:

- **Verified:** supported by law, regulation, or authority
- **Customer-reported**
- **Inferred**
- **Unresolved**

Use qualified local counsel for jurisdiction-specific interpretation. Do not state that an environment is legally compliant unless the specific service, configuration, customer use case, and applicable authorization support that conclusion.

## Evidence package

Each environment should provide:

- Architecture and data-flow diagrams
- Location of data, processing, backups, logs, telemetry, support, and AI artifacts
- Subprocessor and partner register
- Personnel and administrative-access model
- Key-management documentation
- Break-glass procedures
- Government-request process and disclosure limits
- Audit and certification evidence, including scope and exclusions
- Recovery-test results
- Data-export and deletion procedures
- Product parity matrix
- Residual-risk statement approved by legal and security leadership

# 4. Architecture and resilience choices

Use a common platform foundation with controlled regional variation:

- National or regional data planes
- Strong tenant isolation
- Region-bound storage and backups where required
- Regional logging and telemetry
- Policy-based workload placement
- Local or customer-controlled keys where appropriate
- Restricted administrative access
- Standard APIs and deployment tooling
- Local incident and support capability
- Global services only where legally and contractually permitted

Offer explicit recovery choices:

| Model | Benefit | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| National primary and national recovery | Strongest local-control profile | Potentially lower geographic redundancy |
| National primary and approved regional recovery | Better continuity | Transfer and legal complexity |
| Regional sovereign deployment | Greater scale and resilience | May not satisfy national requirements |
| Customer-managed backup | Greater autonomy | Greater customer responsibility |

Do not describe a national environment as more secure or resilient without measured evidence.

### Do not localize unnecessarily

For each market, compare:

1. National deployment
2. Regional deployment
3. Approved partner deployment
4. Contractual and technical controls on the existing platform
5. Temporary legacy hosting
6. Market limitation or exit

Select the least fragmented option that satisfies verified requirements and provides acceptable security, resilience, customer value, and economic sustainability.

# 5. Migration execution

## Migration paths

### Self-service

For low-complexity customers:

- Automated discovery
- Readiness checklist
- Test tenant
- Migration tools
- Export and import guidance
- Office hours
- Post-migration validation

### Guided

For standard integrations and moderate risk:

- Named migration manager
- Dependency assessment
- Integration testing
- Performance baseline
- Cutover plan
- Hypercare

### Provider-managed

For regulated, strategic, or complex customers:

- Provider-led execution
- Joint test cycles
- Dual-running where feasible
- Data-integrity validation
- Executive checkpoints
- Dedicated incident command

### Joint transformation

For critical workloads, multinationals, or material feature gaps:

- Executive steering group
- Architecture redesign
- Contract and procurement workstreams
- Formal acceptance criteria
- Dedicated engineering resources
- Alternative deployment or exception plan

## Mandatory migration inventory

Assess:

- Production data
- Backups and disaster recovery
- Logs, telemetry, and metadata
- Identity and administrator data
- AI prompts, outputs, embeddings, and model logs
- Support tickets and diagnostic data
- Integrations and private connectivity
- DNS, endpoints, and allowlists
- Legal holds and deletion
- Billing and tax records
- Export and exit requirements

## Customer responsibility statement

For every customer, document provider and customer responsibilities for:

- Data classification
- Network and identity changes
- Integration testing
- Key management
- User acceptance testing
- Downtime approval
- Legal and procurement review
- User communications
- Cutover sign-off
- Post-migration validation

Include estimated customer hours, required skills, dependencies, and additional costs.

## Recovery classification

Classify each migration as:

- Full rollback capable
- Point-in-time restoration
- Forward remediation
- Parallel continuation
- Customer-managed recovery

Document:

- Maximum rollback window
- Data-freeze requirements
- Treatment of post-cutover writes
- Reconciliation method
- Conditions making rollback impossible
- Recovery owner
- Customer approval point
- Alternative recovery plan

Do not promise reversibility where only forward remediation is realistic.

# 6. Product and pricing commitments

## Product parity

### Tier 1: Required before cutover

- Core workflows
- Authentication and authorization
- Critical APIs and integrations
- Import and export
- Audit logs
- Encryption
- Backup and restore
- Administrative controls
- Required reporting
- Incident and support processes

### Tier 2: Published delivery commitments

- Advanced analytics
- AI functionality
- Automation
- Marketplace integrations
- Specialized connectors
- Global administration
- Performance enhancements

### Tier 3: Potentially constrained

- Cross-border processing features
- Global benchmarking
- Certain AI models or training workflows
- Integrations dependent on unavailable local services
- Shared global services

A feature gap is material if it:

- Prevents a critical workflow
- Breaks a contractual feature
- Prevents required reporting
- Breaks a material integration
- Reduces performance below an agreed threshold
- Requires disproportionate manual effort
- Removes a feature used by an agreed proportion of users

Critical gaps automatically trigger a migration hold or executive-approved exception. Non-blocking exceptions require customer sign-off.

Every material gap must have:

- Impacted workflow
- Customer impact
- Workaround
- Owner and delivery date
- Migration restriction
- Commercial remedy
- Alternative deployment or exit path

Feature matrices incorporated into contracts must include version, date, scope, commitment status, and remedy.

## Pricing

Offer, where viable:

1. Regional hosting
2. Enhanced sovereignty controls
3. Regulated enterprise service

Itemize costs for:

- Duplicated infrastructure
- Local personnel
- Restricted support
- Assurance and audit
- Capacity
- Resilience
- Partner services
- Migration and dual-running
- Egress and exit

### Cost allocation

| Migration cause | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Provider-driven architecture change | Provider funds assessment, standard tooling, reasonable migration assistance, and agreed transition costs |
| Legal change independent of provider architecture | Allocate incremental costs according to contract and customer circumstances |
| Mixed cause | Provider funds baseline migration; customer-specific incremental controls are separately negotiated |
| Optional customer migration | Customer generally funds incremental services under transparent pricing |

For provider-driven migrations, offer:

- Migration credits or fee waivers
- Temporary price protection
- Dual-running where feasible
- No charge for controls already contractually promised
- Service credits for missed commitments
- Renegotiation or termination rights for material undelivered capabilities, subject to contract

Total cost of ownership must include migration labor, customer staffing, connectivity, integration changes, dual-running, key management, egress, taxes, currency exposure, workarounds, support changes, exit, and re-migration.

# 7. Security, access, and support

## Security controls

Minimum controls should include:

- Tenant isolation
- Encryption in transit and at rest
- Hardware-backed key protection
- Customer-managed keys where appropriate
- Privileged-access management
- Just-in-time administration
- Regional personnel restrictions where required
- Immutable audit logs
- Independent monitoring
- Vulnerability and patch management
- Secure software supply chain
- Insider-threat controls
- Tested incident response
- Secure deletion
- Controlled break-glass access with review

Explain separately:

1. Customer administrator access
2. Provider and subcontractor access
3. Emergency access
4. Government or law-enforcement access

Customer-managed keys may reduce access to specified content, but may not cover metadata, logs, memory, derived data, or every service component. State the exact scope.

## Support tiers

### Standard

- Local-language documentation
- Regional business-hour support
- Defined escalation path
- Support-data handling policy

### Enterprise

- Named technical account manager
- 24/7 incident coverage where required
- Restricted and recorded privileged access
- Customer-approved routine remote sessions where feasible
- Quarterly resilience and control reviews

### Restricted support

- Support personnel and ticket processing within permitted jurisdictions where required
- Locally controlled support tooling
- Separate administrative credentials
- Customer approval for routine privileged sessions
- Local incident commander
- Engineering escalation without unauthorized data export

Routine and emergency access must be governed separately.

# 9. Regional and cultural adaptation

These are planning hypotheses, not legal conclusions or universal cultural facts.

- **EU/EEA:** emphasize precise data flows, transfer safeguards, privacy obligations, subprocessors, government-access procedures, digital-autonomy concerns, and the distinction between residency and compliance.
- **United Kingdom:** address UK-specific processing, UK–EEA flows, public-sector procurement, financial-sector resilience, and UK-based support.
- **United States and Canada:** distinguish public-sector accreditation, defense requirements, sector obligations, commercial preferences, and parent-company exposure.
- **China:** clarify local entities, partners, licensing, cross-border restrictions, personnel, support, feature limitations, and foreign-parent dependencies.
- **Japan and South Korea:** prioritize reliability, detailed evidence, local-language materials, formal acceptance criteria, rehearsed migrations, and local accountability.
- **India:** emphasize sector-specific requirements, local support, partner transparency, price protection, performance, and practical evidence.
- **Southeast Asia:** offer regional and national options; do not assume one ASEAN model satisfies every jurisdiction.
- **Middle East:** address local accountability, critical infrastructure, local entities and partners, procurement, employment, and resilience choices.
- **Latin America:** emphasize affordability, currency protection, local-language support, latency, public-sector procurement, and country-specific analysis.
- **Africa:** address connectivity, bandwidth-efficient migration, cost, local participation, regional availability, and realistic resilience limitations.

Each priority market should have an implementation card covering:

- Verified obligations and sources
- Priority customer segments
- Regulatory and procurement stakeholders
- Support and language requirements
- Known product gaps
- Resilience options
- Pricing constraints
- Partner requirements
- Open questions
- Local approver
- Confidence level

# 10. Trust, communications, and churn prevention

## Communication sequence

### 1. Executive briefing

Explain:

- Verified legal or commercial drivers
- What is mandatory versus optional
- Roadmap and decision points
- Risk and resilience trade-offs
- Pricing principles
- Governance and accountability

### 2. Individual impact assessment

Show:

- Current and target locations
- Data and service scope
- Migration date
- Feature differences
- Performance and recovery changes
- Pricing and contract impact
- Customer responsibilities
- Available choices and constraints
- Escalation contacts

### 3. Legal and procurement pack

Provide:

- Control profile
- Data-flow diagrams
- Subprocessors
- Access model
- Certifications and limitations
- Contract annexes
- Pricing, remedies, continuity, and exit terms

### 4. Technical readiness pack

Provide:

- Reference architecture
- API and integration guidance
- Identity and network changes
- Test plan
- Performance expectations
- Recovery and rollback limits
- Troubleshooting guidance

### 5. End-user notice

Explain in plain language:

- Whether login or endpoints change
- Expected downtime
- Feature or performance changes
- Required user actions
- Support channels

### 6. Post-migration confirmation

Report:

- Completion
- Validation results
- Known issues
- Remaining commitments
- Support contacts
- Feedback channels

## Trust principles

- Lead with customer consequences, not infrastructure jargon.
- State what is known, unknown, and still under review.
- Explain trade-offs, including price and resilience.
- Use local legal, cultural, accessibility, and procurement review.
- Make difficult information as visible as positive news.
- Never use marketing language to obscure limitations.

## Churn intervention playbooks

| Concern | Required response |
|---|---|
| Price increase | TCO review, price protection, migration credit, executive approval |
| Feature gap | Workflow assessment, workaround validation, migration hold, product escalation |
| Foreign-provider concern | Parent-company, partner, key, personnel, and legal-exposure disclosure |
| Domestic-government concern | Access-model briefing, key options, logging, and legal qualification |
| Resilience concern | Alternative recovery design and test evidence |
| Procurement concern | Standard evidence annex, local entity, remedies, and exit terms |
| Deadline risk | Extension, phased migration, dual-running, or executive exception |
| Trust decline | Independent assurance briefing, executive outreach, and root-cause review |

Red accounts require:

- Named executive sponsor
- Retention budget
- Migration decision date
- Next customer action
- Escalation owner
- Alternative migration or exit plan

Low trust should be treated as a possible product, pricing, legal, resilience, or governance problem—not merely a messaging issue.

# 11. Support and escalation

## Escalation levels

### Local

- Local-language support
- Local incident commander
- Customer-facing updates
- Contractual contacts

### Regional

- Legal, security, product, and operations authority
- Ability to pause migrations
- Exception and remedy authority
- Regulator and strategic-customer engagement

### Global

Required for:

- Suspected cross-border exposure
- Missed legal deadlines
- Material feature gaps
- Repeated migration failures
- Major outages
- Government intervention
- Conflicting legal orders
- Strategic churn risk

## Government-access process

Document:

1. Request authentication
2. Legal and jurisdictional review
3. Scope minimization
4. Challenge or clarification where lawful
5. Technical access limitations
6. Parent-company and partner coordination
7. Customer notification where lawful and required
8. Record retention
9. Transparency reporting
10. Post-event review

Do not imply that technical controls defeat all lawful authority.

## Emergency migration

Define triggers such as:

- Immediate security risk
- Imminent infrastructure failure
- Binding regulatory direction
- Threat to life, safety, or critical services

For each trigger, define:

- Approval authority
- Minimum notice where practicable
- Temporary boundary controls
- Maximum time outside the target boundary
- Customer notification deadline
- Regulatory notification responsibility
- Data reconciliation and deletion
- Customer remedies
- Post-event review

# 12. Timeline and decision gates

The final schedule must be validated against the current readiness of each market. No market should be called ready solely because a calendar date has arrived.

## Immediate: establish the baseline

- Confirm verified legal and contractual scope.
- Identify affected customers and deadlines.
- Freeze unsupported claims.
- Complete market readiness tables.
- Identify Tier 1 feature blockers.
- Confirm budget, staffing, migration capacity, and support coverage.
- Establish baseline trust, churn, service, and migration metrics.
- Issue customer-specific choice-and-constraint statements.

## Build and prove

Before general availability:

- Complete environment capability profiles.
- Validate data flows, access, keys, logging, support, backup, and recovery.
- Complete representative pilots.
- Finalize procurement and contract packs.
- Validate local-language and accessibility requirements.
- Approve migration waves and remedies.
- Test deletion, export, recovery, and incident procedures.

## Execute migration waves

- Prioritize legal urgency, customer harm, readiness, and criticality.
- Migrate low-complexity and representative workloads first.
- Use managed or joint-transformation paths for critical customers.
- Maintain rollback or forward-recovery capacity.
- Provide hypercare.
- Review red accounts weekly.
- Pause waves when acceptance, recovery, security, capacity, or support criteria fail.

## Decommission only after sign-off

Legacy hosting should remain available where legally and contractually permitted for documented blockers. Decommission only after:

- Customer sign-off
- Data-integrity validation
- Export testing
- Deletion and legal-hold analysis
- Recovery validation
- Contractual approval
- Executive authorization

Define “transition complete” separately for:

1. Environment availability
2. Customer notification
3. Legally required migration
4. Feature stabilization
5. Legacy exception closure
6. Final legacy shutdown

# 13. Metrics and executive controls

Report by market, segment, and migration wave.

| Metric | Target principle | Escalation trigger | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer impact assessments | 100% before required notice | Any customer lacks assessment | Customer Success |
| Critical workflows validated | 100% before cutover | Any Tier 1 workflow unvalidated | Product |
| Migration failures | Below approved threshold | Repeated failures in one wave | Migration |
| Mandatory-migration churn | Within approved variance from baseline | Material variance by market | Commercial |
| Material feature gaps | Owner, date, workaround, remedy for every gap | No funded plan | Product |
| Restricted-support compliance | 100% within approved boundary | Unauthorized access | Security |
| Claims evidence | 100% current and approved | Expired or unsupported claim | Legal |
| Recovery testing | Passed before launch and major change | Failed exercise | Resilience |
| Notification compliance | Within approved notice standard | Late or unnotified migration | Program Office |
| Trust score | No material decline from baseline | Decline beyond threshold | Customer Success |
| Red accounts | Declining each reporting period | Increase for two periods | Executive Sponsor |

Track:

- Churn, contraction, renewal, and competitive displacement
- Migration completion, downtime, rollback, and data-integrity rates
- Tier 1 parity and feature-gap age
- Availability, RTO, RPO, and support performance
- Unauthorized transfer or access events
- Customer effort and total cost
- Pricing fairness
- Confidence in data-location accuracy
- Confidence in access and government-request handling
- Feature and resilience confidence
- Willingness to renew or recommend

Every metric must have a baseline, target, escalation threshold, reporting frequency, owner, required action, and decision deadline.

# 14. Responsible sovereignty and governance

Sovereign infrastructure can create risks involving surveillance, censorship, discriminatory access, or unlawful disclosure.

Use risk tiers:

- **Low:** documented mitigation and periodic review
- **Medium:** executive approval, enhanced monitoring, customer disclosure
- **High:** board or ethics review
- **Unacceptable:** decline deployment, suspend expansion, or offer an alternative where legally possible

Review:

- Government-access exposure
- Conflicting laws
- Customer confidentiality
- Partner and parent-company control
- Customer ability to exit
- Availability of meaningful technical safeguards
- Potential human-rights impact

Customer research and trust measurement must be:

- Voluntary
- Purpose-limited
- Minimized
- Anonymized or pseudonymized where appropriate
- Stored in approved locations
- Access-restricted
- Retained for defined periods
- Non-retaliatory for negative feedback

---

## Final executive recommendation

Approve the transition only as an evidence-based, market-specific program with:

1. Capability-based sovereignty descriptions
2. Verified legal and contractual scope
3. Current readiness and capacity baselines
4. National, regional, partner, contractual, and exit alternatives
5. Customer-specific choice-and-constraint statements
6. Provider-driven migration funding and protections
7. Tested product, recovery, support, export, and deletion controls
8. Contractually clear feature, pricing, service, and remedy commitments
9. Local legal, cultural, accessibility, and procurement review
10. Government-access, AI, supplier, human-rights, and research-governance controls
11. Trigger-based churn and trust intervention
12. Quantified metrics with accountable owners

Trust will be preserved when customers experience the transition as **accurate, fair, technically controlled, operationally continuous, commercially manageable, and locally intelligible**—and when the provider is candid about what localization solves, what it does not solve, and what customers can do if commitments are missed.
