# Royalty and Franchise-Financing Restructuring Strategy

## 1. Executive summary

The franchisor should replace fixed USD-denominated royalties with a **market-segmented, local-currency royalty framework**, supported by targeted financing, covenant relief, and controlled restructuring of distressed stores.

The strategy is designed to:

- align royalty obligations with the currency in which franchisees generate revenue;
- preserve the franchisor’s participation in nominal sales growth;
- provide temporary relief only to distressed but viable franchisees;
- avoid converting relief into an unpayable deferred balance;
- match franchisee debt currency to operating cash flow;
- protect food safety, brand standards, maintenance, technology, and data integrity;
- transfer, resize, refranchise, or close structurally unviable stores;
- preserve long-term brand economics through indexed floors, capped recovery, and disciplined capital allocation.

The objective is **risk-adjusted system value**, not preservation of an increasingly uncollectible USD invoice:

\[
NPV_{\text{system}} =
NPV_{\text{royalties and services}}
+ NPV_{\text{future outlet growth}}
- NPV_{\text{support and credit losses}}
- NPV_{\text{closures}}
- NPV_{\text{brand damage}}
\]

All rates, thresholds, formulas, financing terms, and legal mechanisms below are illustrative and require country-specific validation. No implementation should proceed without local legal, tax, accounting, foreign-exchange, competition, employment, banking, insolvency, and regulatory review.

---

## 2. Leadership decisions required

Leadership should approve:

1. The principle of replacing fixed USD royalties with a segmented local-currency model.
2. Creation of a capped Franchise Stabilization and Growth Facility.
3. Pilot implementation in Turkey and one of Egypt or Nigeria, subject to local readiness.
4. A limited, documented standstill for compliant and potentially viable stores.
5. The Green, Amber, and Red franchisee classification framework.
6. Country-level legal, tax, accounting, FX, competition, employment, and financing validation.
7. Board-approved limits for:
   - total support capital;
   - guarantees and first-loss exposure;
   - deferred royalties;
   - country and franchisee-group concentration;
   - expected and stressed credit losses;
   - franchisor liquidity exposure.
8. A six- to twelve-month validation period before broad, irreversible rollout.

Leadership should not approve broad binding amendments, direct franchisor lending, or material guarantees until the country execution packages and financing commitments are complete.

---

## 3. Guiding principles

### 3.1 Match obligations to cash flows

Royalties and debt should generally be denominated in the currency in which local sales are earned, unless a natural hedge or approved financial hedge exists.

### 3.2 Preserve revenue participation

A percentage-of-sales royalty allows the franchisor to participate in long-term nominal sales growth while reducing the fixed-burden risk created by a USD invoice.

### 3.3 Separate liquidity stress from structural failure

A viable store facing temporary macroeconomic pressure should receive liquidity and affordability support. A structurally unprofitable store should be remediated, transferred, resized, refranchised, or closed.

### 3.4 Use formulas and controls, not repeated discretionary waivers

Objective metrics, defined review triggers, standardized approvals, and documented exceptions should replace ad hoc country or franchisee concessions.

### 3.5 Protect the operating system

Food safety, critical brand standards, data integrity, maintenance, training, technology, and approved marketing remain non-negotiable.

### 3.6 Make relief temporary and recoverable where realistic

Relief should have a defined duration, a maximum balance, clear recovery terms, and a sunset. It should not create a debt burden that reduces eventual recovery.

### 3.7 Preserve optionality

The franchisor should retain the ability to recalibrate, refinance, restore, or restructure economics as markets stabilize, subject to contractual and legal requirements.

## 4. Market archetypes and country implications

| Archetype | Markets | Principal challenge | Royalty approach | Financing approach |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Severe volatility and convertibility constraint | Egypt, Nigeria | Currency depreciation, inflation, remittance and FX availability | Local-currency sales royalty; reduced or suspended floor; capped deferral | Local-currency working capital, supplier finance, leasing, capped guarantees |
| High inflation but financeable | Turkey | Inflation, high interest rates, refinancing risk | Local-currency royalty; CPI-linked floor; narrow affordability collar | Local-bank refinancing, leasing, revolving facilities |
| Moderate volatility and growth | Indonesia | Rupiah exposure, regional variation, expansion leverage | Local-currency royalty; modest floor; sales bands; location-specific relief | Local banks, equipment finance, supplier credit |
| Moderate volatility and mature financeability | Brazil | Inflation, rates, tax, labor, disclosure and litigation complexity | Near-normal local-currency royalty; indexed floor | Local-bank, receivables, development and private credit |
| Stable-currency company-owned markets | Internal benchmark | Capital allocation and transfer discipline | No franchise royalty; shadow royalty for comparison | Capped first-loss capital, guarantees and system investment |

Country classifications should be reviewed quarterly. Relief should generally be determined at store or franchisee-group level rather than automatically applied across an entire country.

## 5. Royalty architecture

### 5.1 Standard formula

For an ordinary store:

\[
R_t=\max(\tau_m B_t,F_t)
\]

Where:

- \(R_t\) = royalty payable in local currency;
- \(\tau_m\) = approved market- and format-specific rate;
- \(B_t\) = verified royalty sales base;
- \(F_t\) = local-currency minimum royalty.

For an approved hardship store:

\[
R_t^{H}=
\max\left(\tau_m(1-d)B_t,\ F_t(1-c)\right)
\]

subject to:

\[
R_t^{H}+M_t+T_t \leq A_t
\]

Where:

- \(d\) = temporary percentage-rate reduction;
- \(c\) = temporary floor reduction;
- \(M_t\) = marketing contribution;
- \(T_t\) = technology and system-services charges;
- \(A_t\) = approved affordability ceiling.

Relief does not arise automatically from a macroeconomic trigger. It requires verified data, eligibility approval, and an executed agreement.

If the affordability ceiling would reduce payments below the amount needed to fund essential brand services, the store must either receive separately approved support or enter a formal restructuring or exit process.

### 5.2 Royalty base

Each country agreement must define treatment of:

- dine-in, takeaway, drive-through, digital and delivery sales;
- third-party delivery platforms and commissions;
- delivery fees;
- VAT and other sales taxes;
- refunds and chargebacks;
- coupons and discounts;
- gift cards;
- loyalty redemptions;
- related-party sales;
- kiosks, pop-ups and nontraditional formats;
- foreign-currency sales;
- POS outages and manual reporting.

The initial default should be verified gross food and beverage sales, excluding collected sales taxes, bona fide refunds and chargebacks, subject to local validation.

Sales should be reconciled, where lawful, against POS records, bank receipts, tax filings, delivery-platform reports, inventory usage and payment processors.

### 5.3 Minimum royalty

The minimum should be denominated in local currency:

\[
F_t=F_0(1+\pi_t)^\alpha\left(\frac{FX_t}{FX_0}\right)^\beta
\]

Where:

- \(\pi_t\) = approved inflation index;
- \(FX_t\) = specified FX reference;
- \(\alpha\) = inflation-indexation factor;
- \(\beta\) = partial FX factor, normally zero unless linked to documented hard-currency costs.

Each agreement must specify:

- index and FX sources;
- fallback sources;
- averaging periods;
- treatment of multiple exchange rates;
- publication delays;
- rounding;
- treatment of negative index movements;
- suspension of indexation during stress;
- treatment of unpaid and deferred amounts.

Full automatic USD indexation should not apply to stores whose revenues are principally local currency unless a natural hedge exists and local law permits it.

### 5.4 Illustrative parameters

The following are modeling assumptions, not approved commercial terms:

- normal royalty: 3%–6% of the approved sales base;
- temporary relief: up to 25%–50% for 6–12 months;
- maximum rate step-up: 0.5–1.0 percentage points per review period;
- deferred royalty cap: no more than 50% of normalized annual store EBITDA;
- recovery period: generally 12–36 months;
- monthly calculation and monitoring;
- semiannual formal reset, with extraordinary reviews as required.

Final parameters must be calibrated using store-level economics, expected survival, tax leakage, brand-funding requirements, financing cost, and system NPV.

### 5.5 Affordability tests

Illustrative hardship indicators include:

- total brand charges above 25%–30% of normalized EBITDA;
- post-royalty DSCR below 1.15x;
- stressed DSCR below 1.10x–1.20x;
- currency depreciation above 20% over six months;
- annualized CPI above 30%;
- real transactions down more than 15% year-on-year.

These indicators initiate a review; they do not create automatic relief rights.

Normalized EBITDA must be governed by a written policy covering owner compensation, related-party rent, group allocations, unusual costs, maintenance deferral, FX gains and losses, transfer pricing, taxes, payroll and temporary disruption costs.

### 5.6 Deferred and recovery royalties

Relief should normally be applied in this order:

1. temporary percentage-rate reduction;
2. temporary minimum-floor reduction;
3. deferral into a subordinated royalty receivable;
4. contingent recovery when performance improves.

Possible recovery mechanisms include:

- repayment from excess cash flow;
- a temporary additional royalty of 0.5%–1.5%;
- 10%–20% of sales above an agreed baseline;
- a limited franchise-term extension;
- conversion into subordinated debt or preferred equity.

Recovery may begin when, for example:

\[
DSCR \geq 1.35x
\]

for two consecutive quarters, based on consistently normalized data.

Deferred amounts must have:

- a maximum balance;
- a fixed repayment period;
- no uncontrolled FX compounding;
- no automatic acceleration solely because of a macroeconomic event;
- clear treatment on sale, closure, termination and insolvency;
- a write-down or restructuring process if recovery is no longer realistic.

## 6. Fee separation and transparency

The commercial package should distinguish:

### Brand royalty

Payment for trademarks, operating systems, training, support and intellectual property.

### Marketing contribution

Contribution to approved national and global marketing. It should be transparently reported and, where feasible, ring-fenced.

### Technology and system services

Charges for POS, digital ordering, training, procurement, logistics and other services actually provided.

Each franchisee should receive a total-brand-charge schedule showing:

- royalty;
- minimum;
- marketing;
- technology;
- supply-chain charges;
- financing fees;
- tax and withholding effects;
- historical equivalent burden;
- temporary relief;
- recovery obligations.

No cost may be recovered twice. Separate charges must not be used to recreate the former unpayable USD obligation or shift taxable income without commercial substance.

## 7. Country implications

### 7.1 Egypt

**Archetype:** Severe volatility and convertibility-constrained stabilization.

**Royalty**

- Local-currency percentage royalty.
- No fixed USD minimum for qualifying stressed stores.
- CPI-based local floor, with limited FX adjustment only for verified hard-currency costs.
- Rolling three-month FX average where an FX reference is necessary.
- Local settlement, escrow or accrued local-currency payable only where legally permitted.
- Regulatory remittance delays separated from franchisee refusal or inability to pay.

**Financing**

- Local-currency working-capital facilities.
- Supplier and inventory finance.
- Equipment leasing.
- Development-finance or export-credit support for qualifying imports.
- Franchisor first-loss support rather than unhedged USD lending.

**Controls**

- Liquidity reporting for Amber stores.
- Capped deferred royalties.
- Approved pricing and sourcing plans.
- No USD borrowing for local operating costs without a documented hedge.
- Separate treatment of remittance, tax, banking and insolvency risks.

### 7.2 Nigeria

**Archetype:** High-volatility local-currency market with FX availability constraints.

**Royalty**

- Local-currency percentage royalty.
- No fixed monthly USD minimum for stressed stores.
- Three-month average of an approved FX source for any residual hard-currency component.
- Relief assessed using real transactions, margins and DSCR rather than nominal naira sales alone.

**Financing**

- Local-currency revolving facilities.
- Supplier finance.
- Equipment leasing.
- POS-linked receivables lending.
- Capped franchisor guarantees.
- No mandatory USD debt for local-revenue stores.

**Controls**

- Define the permissible FX reference.
- Document official-versus-commercial-rate differences.
- Confirm applicable remittance requirements.
- Distinguish FX availability problems from credit default.
- Validate tax, security, lending and insolvency treatment.

### 7.3 Turkey

**Archetype:** High inflation but financeable.

**Royalty**

- Normal local-currency percentage royalty.
- CPI-indexed minimum reset quarterly or semiannually.
- Narrow affordability collar.
- Relief based primarily on interest-rate shock, refinancing risk, DSCR and real transaction decline.
- FX component only for verified imported services or costs.

**Financing**

- Refinance legacy hard-currency debt where feasible.
- Local-bank term and revolving facilities.
- Equipment leasing.
- Interest-rate and refinancing-reserve requirements.
- Selective guarantees for verified multi-unit operators.

Turkey is the preferred pilot market for indexed floors, POS-linked royalties, local-bank refinancing and recovery royalties.

### 7.4 Indonesia

**Archetype:** Moderate-volatility growth market.

**Royalty**

- Standard local-currency percentage royalty.
- Modest indexed floor for mature stores.
- Lower introductory rates or revenue bands for new formats and lower-income locations.
- Store- or region-specific relief rather than an automatic national waiver.

Illustrative revenue bands:

\[
R =
\begin{cases}
2.5\%\times B_t, & B_t<S_1\\
4.5\%\times B_t, & S_1\leq B_t<S_2\\
5.5\%\times B_t, & B_t\geq S_2
\end{cases}
\]

Thresholds require calibration by province, format, rent, logistics, transactions and imported-input exposure.

**Financing**

- Local-bank loans.
- Equipment finance.
- Supplier credit.
- POS- and digital-sales-based underwriting.
- Expansion financing only where post-debt-service cash flow supports growth.

### 7.5 Brazil

**Archetype:** Moderate-volatility, locally financeable market.

**Royalty**

- Near-normal local-currency percentage royalty.
- Inflation-indexed local minimum.
- Targeted relief for regional disruption, occupancy stress or debt-service pressure.
- Quarterly review only on defined triggers.
- Transparent marketing fund.

**Financing**

- Local-bank working capital.
- Receivables-backed lending.
- Equipment leasing.
- Development-bank and private-credit alternatives.
- Portfolio refinancing after reliable POS and credit data are established.

Before launch, Brazil requires a specific legal and disclosure review covering applicable franchise disclosure requirements, Portuguese-language documentation, material amendments, tax treatment, cross-border payments, labor exposure and enforceability.

### 7.6 Stable-currency company-owned markets

Company-owned stores do not pay franchise royalties, but the franchisor should calculate:

\[
\text{Shadow royalty rate}
=
\frac{\text{brand, technology, marketing and support costs}}
{\text{store sales}}
\]

This is an internal benchmark, not a mandatory transfer-pricing rate.

Support may fund:

- first-loss reserves;
- capped guarantees;
- global technology and training;
- supply-chain resilience;
- temporary viability grants;
- local-currency financing support.

Every transfer requires board approval, country and group limits, use-of-funds restrictions, a sunset date, expected recovery or return, and legal, tax, accounting, solvency, fiduciary and capital-control clearance.

## 8. Franchisee classification

### Green: viable without material support

Typical criteria:

- normalized DSCR at least 1.50x;
- positive free cash flow after maintenance capex;
- current or approved arrangements for payroll, taxes and suppliers;
- accurate POS reporting;
- acceptable brand and food-safety performance.

**Treatment:** Standard royalty and ordinary financing access.

### Amber: distressed but viable

Typical criteria:

- positive normalized unit economics before temporary macroeconomic effects;
- DSCR approximately 1.15x–1.49x, or temporary stress below threshold;
- credible operator;
- reliable financial data;
- no serious safety, fraud, payroll or tax misconduct;
- approved turnaround plan;
- owner support or equivalent capital contribution.

**Treatment:**

- temporary royalty relief;
- capped deferral or recovery;
- local-currency financing or refinancing;
- covenant relief;
- monthly or quarterly monitoring;
- defined recovery milestones.

### Red: structurally unviable or non-compliant

Typical criteria:

- negative normalized unit economics before relief;
- persistent DSCR below threshold without a credible remedy;
- material underreporting or data fraud;
- serious food-safety or brand violations;
- deliberate non-payment of wages or taxes;
- misuse of financing;
- no credible owner or investor support.

**Treatment:**

- sale or transfer;
- relocation or format conversion;
- refranchising;
- selective company-owned conversion;
- orderly closure and de-branding.

Red stores are excluded from the ordinary financial-relief pilot and handled through a controlled-exit process.

### Relief conditions

Relief requires:

- POS, bank and tax reconciliation where lawful;
- written normalization analysis;
- maintenance and food-safety compliance;
- no unauthorized distributions;
- disclosure of debt and related-party transactions;
- approved turnaround plan;
- compliance with employment obligations or an approved statutory arrangement;
- audit access;
- executed individual amendment and financing documents.

Good-faith disputes, government-caused delays and approved tax or payroll arrangements must be distinguished from fraud or intentional non-payment.

## 9. Financing toolkit

### 9.1 Franchise Stabilization and Growth Facility

#### Working-capital window

- 6–18 month tenor;
- local-currency denomination;
- POS-linked borrowing base;
- supplier-direct disbursement where appropriate;
- minimum liquidity covenant;
- monthly reporting;
- cash sweep after minimum operating liquidity.

#### Capex and modernization window

- 3–7 year tenor;
- construction grace period;
- equipment leasing or export-credit support;
- milestone-based drawdowns;
- approved return-on-investment case;
- maintenance and digital-deployment covenants.

#### Restructuring and ownership-transition window

- store transfers, refranchising, relocations or partner buyouts;
- independent valuation;
- approved operator;
- subordinated debt, preferred equity or earn-out where appropriate;
- no funding of permanent operating losses.

### 9.2 Funding sources

Potential sources include:

- franchisor first-loss capital;
- local commercial banks;
- development-finance institutions;
- export-credit agencies;
- suppliers;
- equipment lessors;
- receivables-backed lenders;
- local private-credit funds;
- franchisee equity;
- stable-market company-owned-store cash.

Management must distinguish committed funding from exploratory discussions before approving the facility.

### 9.3 Currency and interest-rate policy

Debt should be denominated in the currency in which the store generates cash flow unless:

- revenues are hard-currency linked;
- the financed asset creates a natural hedge;
- an approved hedge exists; or
- explicit liquidity and risk approval has been granted.

Illustrative pricing:

\[
i_t =
r_{\text{local benchmark},t}
+s_{\text{credit}}
+s_{\text{country/liquidity}}
-g_{\text{guarantee}}
\]

Local-currency debt remains subject to interest-rate and refinancing stress tests.

### 9.4 Covenant relief

For Amber franchisees, approved relief may include:

- temporary DSCR waiver;
- interest-only period;
- maturity extension;
- principal amortization holiday;
- cure period for technical defaults;
- removal of unrelated-store cross-defaults;
- temporary royalty-covenant relief;
- cash-sweep suspension while liquidity is rebuilt.

Conditions include no dividends or related-party distributions, continued payroll and tax compliance, monthly reporting, owner support and milestone testing.

### 9.5 Guarantees and exposure limits

Before launch, leadership must approve:

- total facility size;
- country sub-limits;
- per-store and franchisee-group limits;
- first-loss cap;
- maximum guarantee exposure;
- expected and stressed loss budgets;
- liquidity reserve;
- concentration limits;
- support cost per preserved outlet;
- stop-funding triggers.

Guarantees must have a fixed amount, defined term, claim procedure, recovery rights, lender underwriting requirements, and local enforceability, tax, accounting and regulatory approval.

### 9.6 Security and recovery

Security should ordinarily be limited to:

- financed store assets;
- equipment;
- verified receivables;
- designated accounts;
- approved inventory;
- equity interests where lawful.

Broad cross-collateralization across unrelated stores should be avoided. Each country requires a recovery waterfall covering employee claims, taxes, landlords, senior lenders, suppliers, new-money financing, deferred royalties, guarantors and owners, subject to local insolvency law.

## 10. Franchisee-association and bargaining approach

### 10.1 Consultation structure

Establish:

- country franchisee consultation forums;
- a Global Franchisee Advisory Council;
- quarterly economic and operating reviews;
- an independent expert and mediation panel;
- documented agendas, minutes and escalation procedures.

Association consultation is non-binding unless the association has legal authority and the relevant franchisee executes an individual amendment or financing agreement.

### 10.2 Competition and information safeguards

Before meetings:

- provide competition-law training;
- use counsel-reviewed agendas;
- appoint a compliance monitor;
- prohibit discussion of franchisee-specific prices, margins, wages, suppliers, discounts, output, territories or expansion plans;
- use aggregated or anonymized data;
- use a clean team or independent data processor where needed;
- maintain secure records;
- stop and escalate prohibited discussions immediately.

The franchisor must not facilitate coordinated non-payment, collective price-setting, supplier boycotts, coordinated closures or other commercial coordination among independent franchisees.

### 10.3 Fairness and non-retaliation

The franchisor must not:

- terminate or threaten termination because of lawful association participation;
- deny relief based on association membership;
- offer preferential terms to association leaders to divide the group;
- force franchisor-affiliate financing where reasonable alternatives exist;
- condition essential system access on unrelated commercial concessions.

Differentiation must be based on documented economics, compliance, store characteristics, operator capability, financing risk or market conditions.

### 10.4 Limited standstill

Any enforcement standstill must:

- cover only identified compliant and potentially viable stores;
- have a fixed duration;
- preserve accrued rights;
- exclude fraud, food-safety failures, IP misuse, diversion and intentional underreporting;
- contain an express reservation of rights;
- be approved by the country or exceptions committee.

It must not create a blanket waiver or course-of-dealing precedent.

## 11. Documentation and required local validation

Use separate, coordinated documents:

1. Royalty amendment or restated franchise agreement.
2. Marketing and system-services schedule.
3. Hardship or stabilization agreement.
4. Financing agreement.
5. Guarantee, intercreditor or first-loss agreement.
6. Association consultation protocol.

Before launch in any country, validate:

- amendment and consent requirements;
- franchise disclosure and registration;
- language requirements;
- FX and remittance mechanics;
- inflation and exchange-rate references;
- VAT, withholding, transfer pricing and deductibility;
- variable consideration and revenue recognition;
- contract modification and impairment;
- expected credit losses and guarantee accounting;
- debt modification and forgiveness;
- banking and lending permissions;
- security and insolvency;
- employment and collective-dismissal matters;
- competition law;
- data protection and POS transfers;
- arbitration and court enforcement;
- regulatory-change and fallback-payment provisions.

No country may launch based solely on commercial approval.

## 12. Data controls and dispute resolution

### 12.1 Data architecture

Implement:

- POS-to-bank reconciliation;
- POS-to-tax-return reconciliation where lawful;
- channel-level reporting;
- inventory-yield analytics;
- audit rights;
- offline sales capture;
- manual fallback for outages;
- cybersecurity and local data-hosting controls;
- data-quality scoring.

Use proportionate requirements:

- simplified reporting for small, low-risk stores;
- standard reporting for ordinary stores;
- enhanced reporting for Amber stores and material borrowers.

### 12.2 Royalty calculation disputes

Each agreement should provide:

- monthly royalty statements;
- source-data access;
- defined objection period;
- payment of undisputed amounts;
- independent accountant determination;
- interim calculation rules;
- correction and refund procedures;
- allocation of expert costs.

### 12.3 Escalation

1. Local operational review.
2. Country implementation committee.
3. Independent accounting, FX or valuation determination.
4. Confidential mediation.
5. Court or arbitration, subject to local enforceability.

Urgent remedies remain available for food safety, fraud, IP misuse, data diversion and serious brand harm.

## 13. Implementation roadmap

### Immediate actions: 0–60 days

- Establish the limited standstill policy for identified viable and compliant stores.
- Create a temporary hardship protocol.
- Identify stores at immediate closure, payroll, supplier, tax or food-safety risk.
- Collect the store-level baseline.
- Quantify fixed-USD exposure, remittance delays and unhedged franchisee debt.
- Establish country consultation forums and competition-law protocols.
- Begin country legal, tax, accounting, FX, employment, banking and regulatory reviews.
- Identify committed and potential financing sources.
- Establish global and country governance bodies.

### Near-term actions: 60–150 days

- Finalize country execution matrices.
- Calibrate royalty rates, floors, collars, caps and recovery terms.
- Complete the normalization and eligibility policies.
- Establish the stabilization facility and underwriting rules.
- Prepare amendment, hardship, financing and guarantee documentation.
- Pilot in Turkey and one of Egypt or Nigeria, subject to legal and lender readiness.
- Implement POS, reconciliation and data-quality controls.
- Begin lender and franchisee consultation using aggregated information.

### Medium-term actions: months 5–12

- Execute required disclosures and individual amendments.
- Roll out the approved model by country and franchisee cohort.
- Refinance eligible unhedged hard-currency debt.
- Deploy local-currency working-capital and capex facilities.
- Apply recovery mechanisms to improving cohorts.
- Transfer, resize or close Red stores through controlled processes.
- Conduct quarterly country reviews and monthly risk monitoring.
- Evaluate initial pilot performance after two quarters.

### Long-term actions: months 12–24

- Complete the six- to twelve-month expansion gate.
- Restore indexed floors and step-up rates in recovering cohorts.
- Refinance the stabilization facility as credit quality improves.
- Shift support from liquidity stabilization toward capex and growth.
- Reassess market archetypes and country parameters.
- Consider portfolio financing only after collection, credit and data quality are proven.
- Review the shadow-royalty benchmark and long-term global economics annually.

Two quarters may support continuation or redesign of a pilot. Broad, irreversible rollout should normally require six to twelve months of validated operating and credit data.

## 14. Governance

### Global steering committee

Members should include finance, treasury, operations, franchise, legal, tax, risk, technology and country leadership.

Responsibilities:

- approve global principles and market segmentation;
- approve formula ranges and country limits;
- approve pilots;
- approve aggregate support and guarantee exposure;
- monitor system NPV, brand health and franchisor liquidity;
- suspend new commitments when risk limits are breached.

### Country implementation committees

Responsibilities:

- classify franchisees Green, Amber or Red;
- approve relief within published parameters;
- approve local financing within country limits;
- manage local filings, amendments and consultations;
- escalate exceptional or conflicted cases.

Association representatives participate only in a consultative capacity and receive no individual confidential data.

### Independent credit and exceptions committee

Required for:

- guarantees;
- related-party financing;
- large deferred balances;
- material waivers;
- company-owned-store transfers;
- eligibility exceptions;
- strategic closures or conversions.

The board or audit/risk committee should receive quarterly reporting on exposure, impairment, liquidity, litigation, regulatory developments, franchisee outcomes and brand risk.

## 15. Risk controls and scenario triggers

| Risk or trigger | Required response |
|---|---|
| Currency depreciation above 20% in six months | Extraordinary royalty and debt review |
| CPI above 30% annualized | Review floor and affordability parameters |
| Real transactions down more than 15% year-on-year | Store-level pricing and operating review |
| Post-royalty DSCR below 1.15x | Amber/Red review |
| Brand charges above 30% of normalized EBITDA | Hardship review |
| Cash royalty collection below 90% | Country cash-control review |
| Deferred royalties above approved cap | Stop new deferrals; restructure or exit |
| Guarantee utilization above approved limit | Stop new guarantees |
| Remittance delay caused by regulation | Apply documented accrual or escrow process; do not automatically treat as default |
| Critical food-safety, fraud, IP or data-integrity event | Immediate remediation and possible relief suspension |
| DSCR above 1.35x for two quarters | Begin recovery and step-up review |
| Franchisor liquidity below board threshold | Stop new support and escalate to the board |
| Material regulatory change | Reassess payment, disclosure, financing and amendment mechanics |

Triggers initiate review. They do not create automatic relief or automatic termination.

## 16. KPIs

### Franchisee health

- post-royalty EBITDA;
- free cash flow after maintenance capex;
- royalty and total-brand-charge burden;
- DSCR and stressed DSCR;
- real transaction growth;
- price-volume decomposition;
- liquidity and supplier arrears;
- payroll and tax status;
- deferred royalty balances;
- maintenance backlog;
- Green, Amber and Red population;
- closures, transfers, appeals and holdouts.

### Franchisor economics

- cash royalty collection rate;
- accrued versus cash royalties;
- constant-currency royalty revenue;
- deferred royalty recovery rate;
- expected credit loss;
- guarantee utilization;
- first-loss return;
- remittance days;
- support cost per preserved outlet;
- currency exposure;
- legal and accounting reserves;
- system NPV versus the status quo.

### Brand and workforce

- food-safety failures;
- critical operating violations;
- mystery-shopper results;
- customer complaints and satisfaction;
- product availability;
- employee turnover and labor disputes;
- maintenance and remodel compliance;
- digital-order penetration;
- supply-chain service levels.

### Governance

- amendment execution rate;
- holdout rate;
- relief decisions and appeals;
- exception frequency;
- association disputes;
- litigation and arbitration;
- regulatory findings;
- audit exceptions;
- percentage of arrangements with complete legal, tax, accounting and credit documentation.

---

## 17. Long-term brand protections

The following protections remain in force during and after the restructuring:

- verified POS and audit rights;
- transparent total-brand-charge disclosure;
- local-currency participation in sales growth;
- indexed or reset floors after stabilization;
- capped recovery mechanisms;
- ring-fenced or transparently reported marketing funding;
- minimum maintenance, technology and training requirements;
- critical food-safety, fraud, IP and data-integrity standards;
- relief sunset dates;
- no indefinite deferred balances;
- store-level viability testing;
- anti-gaming and clawback provisions;
- annual comparison with company-owned-store shadow economics;
- country, franchisee-group and franchisor exposure limits;
- no automatic precedent across countries or franchisees.

Minor or curable operating deficiencies should receive cure periods. Immediate action is reserved for critical safety, fraud, serious data-integrity, IP or comparable risks.

---

## 18. Final recommended end-state

- **Egypt and Nigeria:** local-currency sales royalties, low or conditional floors, remittance protocols, capped deferrals, and strong liquidity support.
- **Turkey:** indexed local-currency royalties, narrow DSCR-based relief, local-bank refinancing and recovery royalties.
- **Indonesia:** standard local-currency royalties with revenue bands, regional calibration and targeted growth support.
- **Brazil:** near-normal local-currency royalties with indexed floors and primarily third-party financing.
- **Stable company-owned markets:** shadow-royalty benchmarking and disciplined, capped capital support rather than open-ended subsidies.

The strategy converts an increasingly uncollectible fixed USD claim into a recoverable share of local sales and future growth while preserving brand funding, contractual discipline, financing control, operating standards, and the franchisor’s long-term economic position.
