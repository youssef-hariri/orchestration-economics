**GLOBAL COMPENSATION POLICY**
**Remote-First Pay Framework**
**Version 3.0 | Effective Date:** January 1, 2026

**Applies To:** All full-time employees and fixed-term contractors (mapped to employee levels for internal analytical parity only) across 30+ jurisdictions.  
**Policy Owner:** Chief People Officer  
**Governance:** Global Compensation Committee (GCC); Independent Compensation Review Panel (ICRP); Regional HR Business Partners; Legal (Employment, Privacy, Tax); Data Protection Officer

---

## 1. PHILOSOPHY: WHY WE PAY THIS WAY

We are remote-first. Talent may live and work from Warsaw, Lisbon, Berlin, or Toronto without being treated as second-class because of location. We are also intellectually honest: global labor markets are not identical. Pretending otherwise would either overextend the business or exclude talented people in lower-cost markets entirely. Neither is acceptable.

**Our Commitment**
- **Role value, individual impact, and verified market reality** drive base pay. We do not use cost-of-living or PPP adjustments to set salaries.
- **Difference is bounded.** Geographic differentials exist because labor markets differ, but we cap them, overlap them, and allow experience and impact to close—or reverse—them within a level.
- **Geography fades with seniority.** As scope becomes global, tiers compress and then disappear. Senior talent is hired on a global market.
- **Transparency is non-negotiable.** Every employee has the right to understand how pay is set, to challenge it, and to discuss it freely. We will never punish candor.
- **Continuous improvement.** This architecture is living. When market data shifts, laws change, or fairness tests fail, we adjust.

---

## 2. COMPLIANCE ARCHITECTURE: GLOBAL STANDARD, LOCAL PRECISION

We reject a naive “strictest global standard” because pay-transparency laws conflict across jurisdictions. We operate a **Global Transparency Standard (GTS)** with **Local Compliance Addenda (LCA)** and an overriding principle of **Statutory Supremacy**.

| Layer | Requirement |
|-------|-------------|
| **Global Transparency Standard (GTS)** | All employees may view internal job architectures, tier definitions, band midpoints, and methodology. |
| **Local Compliance Addenda (LCA)** | Where a jurisdiction mandates specific posting formats, pay-history bans, works-council consultation, or employee-representative co-determination, the LCA modifies external disclosure and process without altering underlying architecture. |
| **Statutory Supremacy** | No band, floor, or global target may undercut local statutory minimum wages, mandatory bonuses (e.g., 13th/14th month), sectoral collective bargaining agreements, or non-waivable employee entitlements. Local law governs where it exceeds our global standard. |

### 2.1 Good-Faith Local Range (GFLR) Protocol
We do not artificially narrow external posting ranges to mask our internal architecture. For every requisition, Legal and the GCC establish a **Good-Faith Local Range (GFLR)** representing the range we **reasonably and in good faith expect to pay** for that specific role, level, and location, given the qualifications of candidates we intend to attract.

- In jurisdictions requiring full viable range disclosure (e.g., Colorado, California, New York City, Washington, and EU jurisdictions under the Pay Transparency Directive), postings reflect that full range.
- Where local law permits a target range based on documented hiring intent, the range is validated by local counsel and accurately reflects realistic pay expectations—not a mechanical subset of the global band.
- All external ranges are attested by Legal before publication. Range suppression is prohibited.

### 2.2 EU Pay Transparency Directive Readiness
For EU jurisdictions, “categories of workers” for gender pay reporting are defined by **global job level and functional track** (e.g., IC4 Software Engineering, M3 Product Management), not by geographic tier. This prevents geographic concentration from creating proxy discrimination findings.

- We maintain a six-year readiness calendar aligned with the Directive’s phased implementation.
- Where unexplained gender pay gaps exceed regulatory thresholds, we conduct joint pay assessments with employee representatives and publish remediation plans.
- Employee requests for pay-level information in the EU are coordinated through Legal and Regional HR to ensure consistency with the GTS.

### 2.3 Data Privacy & Minimization
Transparency does not mean individual exposure. The internal Compensation Architect wiki displays **architecture, not individuals.** Band-position percentiles are suppressed from team-level dashboards where team size is fewer than five employees or where the combination of level, tier, and band width could allow re-identification. An annual Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) reviews access logs and re-identification risk in small jurisdictions.

## 3. GLOBAL ROLE & LEVEL ARCHITECTURE

We use one leveling framework worldwide. Level criteria are geography-agnostic and published.

| Track | Levels | Differentiators |
|-------|--------|-----------------|
| **Individual Contributor (IC)** | IC1 – IC6+ | Scope of technical ownership, architectural complexity, autonomy, mentorship, and business impact. |
| **Management (M)** | M1 – M5 | Team size, cross-functional scope, strategic accountability, and organizational multiplier effect. |

**Global Reference Point (GRP):** For every level, the GCC establishes a USD-denominated GRP representing the 65th percentile of Tier 1 labor markets. The GRP is the mathematical anchor for all tiers.

*Example: L4 Software Engineer*  
`GRP = USD 160,000`

## 4. THE ANCHOR-TIER BAND MODEL

### 4.1 Tier Rationale & Methodology
To preserve coherence without ungovernable fragmentation, we aggregate 30 countries into **four tiers** derived from log-linear regression of verified country tech-wage medians against the Tier 1 median, weighted for talent-market depth and competitive density. The full regression methodology, source weighting, and R-squared thresholds are maintained in the **Compensation Methodology Addendum**, reviewable by all employees and the ICRP.

| Tier | Definition (Illustrative) | Tier Target Multiplier (TTM) |
|------|---------------------------|------------------------------|
| **Tier 1** | Major global tech hubs with the deepest senior-talent liquidity and highest competitive pressure (e.g., US/Canada metros, Switzerland, UK, Nordics) | 1.00 |
| **Tier 2** | Established tech economies with strong infrastructure but lower wage pressure (e.g., Germany, France, Netherlands, Ireland, Australia, Japan, Singapore) | 0.80 |
| **Tier 3** | Maturing/high-growth markets with rising tech sectors and moderate wage pressure (e.g., Poland, Portugal, Czechia, Greece, UAE, Mexico, Chile, South Africa) | 0.65 |
| **Tier 4** | Emerging markets where local tech medians are materially lower, though top talent is globally competitive (e.g., India, Philippines, Nigeria, Argentina, Vietnam) | 0.50 |

**Maximum Spread:** At any non-converged level, the spread between Tier 1 and Tier 4 is capped at 2.0×.

**Thin-Data Protocol:** In markets with insufficient syndicated survey data (typically Tier 4), the GCC triangulates: (a) localized recruitment-market intelligence, (b) validated proxy data from structurally similar Tier 3 markets adjusted for labor-market scale and competitive density, and (c) local employer benchmarking consortiums. No band floor is set using a single data source.

### 4.2 Intra-Tier Calibration Protocol
When verified, syndicated market data for a specific country deviates by more than ±12.5% from its assigned tier midpoint for two consecutive annual cycles, the GCC may approve a **Country Calibration Adjustment (CCA)** of up to ±10%. The CCA modifies the local midpoint used for offers and merit planning without fragmenting global band architecture. CCA decisions are published to the ICRP and are challengeable under §7.4.

### 4.3 Band Construction, Overlap & Total Target Cash
Each tier band is **±25% around the tier-adjusted midpoint** (GRP × TTM × CCA, if applicable). This creates intentional overlap: a high-performing, experienced L4 in a lower tier can earn at or above the midpoint of the next tier up.

Base salary bands are the primary architecture. Each level also carries a **Target Annual Bonus (TAB)** expressed as a global percentage of base salary, uniform across all tiers for that level. **Target Total Cash (TTC)** equals base salary plus TAB.

*L4 Software Engineer Example (Base Salary)*

| Tier | Midpoint Calculation | Band (USD) |
|------|---------------------|------------|
| **Tier 1** | $160k × 1.00 = $160k | **$120,000 – $200,000** |
| **Tier 2** | $160k × 0.80 = $128k | **$96,000 – $160,000** |
| **Tier 3** | $160k × 0.65 = $104k | **$78,000 – $130,000** |
| **Tier 4** | $160k × 0.50 = $80k | **$60,000 – $100,000** |

**Overlap in Practice:** A top-tier L4 in Warsaw (Tier 3, $130k base) earns within the same base range as a mid-tier L4 in Berlin (Tier 2, $128k base) and above a newly promoted L4 in Toronto (Tier 1, $120k base). Impact and progression within a level can outweigh geography; geography alone does not cap potential.

### 4.4 Currency & FX Protocol
Bands are set in USD to preserve global coherence. Salaries are paid in local currency.

- **Reference Rate:** Annual salary is fixed using the USD/local currency rate on the first business day of the fiscal year.
- **Collar Protection:** If year-to-date currency fluctuation exceeds ±7.5%, the GCC authorizes an off-cycle true-up or temporary cost-of-currency allowance. This is a volatility guardrail, not a cost-of-living adjustment.
- **Transparency:** Employees see their band in USD and the local-currency equivalent on their compensation dashboard.

## 5. THE CONVERGENCE PRINCIPLE

As scope becomes global, the labor market becomes global. To reflect this, we apply **Convergence Bands** at senior levels.

| Level | Convergence Rule |
|-------|------------------|
| **IC1–IC4 / M1–M3** | Standard 4-tier model applies (with CCA if applicable). |
| **IC5–IC6 / M4** | **Compressed Tiers.** Two bands: “Global Metro” (Tier 1–2 blend, TTM 0.90) and “Global Emerging” (Tier 3–4 blend, TTM 0.75). Maximum spread: 1.2×. |
| **IC6+ / M5+** | **Single Global Band.** One USD band for all roles at this level. TTM = 1.00. These roles are hired on a global market and are expected to influence outcomes across time zones without geographic discount. |

## 6. EQUITY: LOCATION-AGNOSTIC OWNERSHIP

All equity-eligible employees participate in the same global program. Grants are denominated in shares and tied to level and individual impact rating, not geography.

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Grant Value** | Benchmarked at the 65th percentile of US venture-backed or public tech companies (as applicable), regardless of employee location. |
| **Vesting** | Standard 4-year vest with 1-year cliff; no geographic variation. |
| **Liquidity** | Same tender-offer and exercise terms for all equity holders. |
| **Tax Support** | Localized tax-briefing stipends for first-time option holders and RSU recipients to ensure they understand obligations in their jurisdiction. |

## 7. TRANSPARENCY, DISCLOSURE & EMPLOYEE VOICE

### 7.1 Internal Transparency: The Compensation Architect
An internal wiki available to all employees shows: (a) the tier list, country assignments, and any active CCAs; (b) the GRP, TTM methodology, and band for every level; (c) data sources and the annual review calendar; and (d) TAB percentages by level. Individual compensation data is never displayed.

### 7.2 External Postings & Legal Disclosure
- Job postings include the **Good-Faith Local Range (GFLR)** applicable to the specific requisition and location.
- Where local law mandates narrower formats or specific disclosure elements (e.g., benefits valuations, bonus targets), the LCA governs format while the GFLR governs substance.
- Works councils and employee representatives are consulted on local addenda where required by law.

### 7.3 Perception Management & Constructive Inversions
We do not police public conversations about pay. We equip managers to handle them with honesty.

**Inversion Context Protocol** (for scenarios where a senior peer in a lower tier earns more than a junior peer in a higher tier):
1. **Acknowledge** the concern without defensiveness.
2. **Explain** that within-level pay reflects verified impact, experience, and progression through overlapping bands—not passport.
3. **Contextualize** using Total Target Cash (base + TAB + equity).
4. **Escalate** to the ICRP if the employee believes their own level or tier is misaligned.

Quarterly **Compensation Literacy** sessions ensure all people leaders navigate these conversations with confidence and accuracy.

### 7.4 Level, Tier & CCA Challenge Process
Employees may initiate a formal challenge once per **annual merit cycle**.

- An **Employee Compensation Advocate**—an independent, non-managerial role appointed by the ICRP—is available to help employees assemble market data and frame their appeal.
- The GCC must respond in writing with data within **15 business days**.
- If unresolved, the employee may escalate to the ICRP for final review.

## 8. GOVERNANCE, DATA & REVIEW CADENCE

| Activity | Frequency | Owner | Output |
|----------|-----------|-------|--------|
| Market data refresh | Annual (Q4) | GCC + external vendor | Updated GRPs; tier reassignments if warranted (>15% median shift over 24 months) |
| FX true-up review | Quarterly | Global Payroll + Finance | Cost-of-currency adjustments |
| Bias & equity audit | Quarterly | People Analytics | Regression analysis of pay vs. gender, ethnicity, and national origin within each level; unexplained gaps >3% trigger automatic remediation |
| Architecture review | Annual (Q3) | CPO + Legal + GCC | LCA updates; new jurisdiction integration |
| Employee challenge resolution | Rolling (15-day SLA) | Regional HRBP + GCC + ICRP | Written determination with data |
| Exception review | Annual | CPO + Board Compensation Committee | Aggregation of all out-of-band exceptions |

**Band Adjustment Protections:** If market data requires a tier downgrade, existing employees are **red-circled**—pay is not reduced. New hires enter at the revised tier. Upward adjustments apply to all employees in the tier immediately.

---

## 9. GLOBAL MOBILITY & REMOTE WORK

### 9.1 Permanent Relocation
An employee who relocates permanently adopts the compensation tier of their new location, subject to:

- **No immediate downward adjustment** if moving to a lower tier. Salary is frozen at the current level until the new tier band catches up via normal merit increases, or for 24 months, whichever is shorter.
- **Immediate upward adjustment** if moving to a higher tier, provided the move is approved and work authorization is established.

### 9.2 Temporary Travel
Working remotely from a non-approved jurisdiction for more than 90 days in a rolling 12-month period requires pre-approval. Compensation does not change for temporary travel under 90 days. Extended stays may trigger local tax, permanent-establishment, and tier-review obligations.

---

## 10. EXCEPTIONS & ESCALATION

Exceptions to this policy (e.g., out-of-band offers for critical hires, M&A integrations, or unique tax equalizations) require:

1. Written business justification from the hiring manager.
2. Approval by the CPO and one GCC executive sponsor.
3. Documentation in the employee’s file with a sunset review date (typically 12 months).
4. Annual aggregation of all exceptions for board-level compensation-committee review.

---

## 11. ACKNOWLEDGMENT & EFFECT

This policy replaces all prior location-based compensation guidelines. It is a living document. We expect it to generate hard questions, and we welcome them. Our goal is not to eliminate every dollar of difference between markets—that would ignore economic reality—but to ensure that every dollar we pay is explainable, defensible, bounded by data, and subject to independent scrutiny.
