**GLOBAL FLEXIBLE WORK POLICY 2026**  
*FLEX-12 Framework: Differentiated Legality, Calibrated Culture, and Unified Operations*

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

In 2026, divergent “right to disconnect” statutes, four-day-week pilots, and cultural expectations toward visibility have created a fragmented global landscape. The FLEX-12 Framework replaces all prior remote-work and flexible-hours guidelines with a single, operationally unified system that is legally resilient, culturally coherent, and enforceable across twelve country operations.

**Strategic Premise.** Flexibility is a business capability, not a perk. It is distributed equitably but implemented differentially. No single schedule is imposed globally; instead, every employee is protected by a universal floor of rights, a ceiling of operational accountability, and a legally binding compliance pathway that must be cleared before any modality is activated.

**What the Board Is Approving.** This policy establishes:  
1. **Four regulatory clusters** (RVM, CHM, HPM, TEM) that map the twelve countries to calibrated implementation standards;  
2. **A legally gated rollout** requiring per-country counsel sign-off and, where mandated, works-council or union agreement before any compressed-week or hard-stop disconnect modality is offered;  
3. **A unified operating backbone** for global software teams, including Follow-the-Sun coverage hubs, asynchronous handover standards, and strict meeting taxonomy to prevent timezone fatigue;  
4. **An anti-bias architecture** that redacts schedule metadata from promotion committees and substitutes “contextual merit” for presence-based judgment; and  
5. **An Equity Assurance Dashboard** reviewed quarterly by the Global Flexible Work Council and biannually by the Board Risk Committee.

**Risk Posture.** Where local statute, collective bargaining agreements (CBAs), or works-council accords conflict with this policy, local law prevails. A Local Compliance Veto, held by each country’s Cluster Autonomy Officer, ensures no global provision is forced upon a statutory representative body. Deadlock is escalated to the Chief Legal Officer and CHRO for resolution within twenty-one days.

**Timeline.** Markets in Clusters A, B, and C commence enrollment following a 90-day legal clearance window. Cluster D operates a phased rollout beginning Month 4. Full global implementation is targeted for Month 9, subject to legal gating.

## I. PURPOSE & SCOPE

**1.1 Coverage.** This policy governs working-time arrangements, location flexibility, and digital disconnection rights for all **employees** across the Company’s twelve country operations. It does not create statutory rights for independent contractors or vendors; contractor arrangements remain subject to applicable commercial agreements and local employment-status tests.

**1.2 Core Premise.** Flexibility is a business capability distributed equitably but implemented differentially. No single schedule is imposed globally. Every employee is protected by a universal floor of rights, a ceiling of operational accountability, and a legally binding compliance pathway.

**1.3 Jurisdictional Primacy & The Local Compliance Veto.** Where this policy conflicts with local statute, CBAs, works-council accords, or regulatory guidance, local law prevails. Each country’s **Cluster Autonomy Officer** (see Section X) holds a **Local Compliance Veto**. They may suspend any global provision for their jurisdiction pending written justification to the Global Flexible Work Council.

**1.4 Deadlock Resolution.** If the Global Council disagrees with a Local Veto, the Chief Legal Officer and CHRO shall adjudicate within **21 calendar days**. If deadlock persists, the local veto prevails; the Company may then either modify the modality for that jurisdiction or withdraw it and offer the **next most flexible arrangement** available under the Global Menu (Section V), per the following fallback hierarchy:  
1. Full Global Menu eligibility (if veto applies only to a specific sub-modality);  
2. Restricted Menu (staggered shifts, remote days, seasonal flex);  
3. Interim Standard (core-hour autonomy + two remote days/week);  
4. Legacy arrangements preserved under prior terms.  

Under no circumstance may a global provision be forced upon a works council or statutory representative body.

**1.5 Transition Protection.** Employees with existing flexible arrangements retain those arrangements under legacy terms during the pre-implementation validation period. No employee may enroll in a new compressed-week, hard-stop disconnect, or location-flex modality until their country’s **Legal Clearance Gate** is signed by local employment counsel and, where required, by the relevant works council or union. If a Legal Clearance Gate is not achieved by the target date, the Chief Legal Officer must present an escalation plan to the Board Risk Committee; pending resolution, employees continue under legacy arrangements or the Interim Standard, whichever is legally permissible in that jurisdiction.

## II. FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLES

1. **Operational Integrity & Legal Sovereignty.** Client commitments and release cycles are designed around coverage architecture, but never by circumventing local maxima on hours, minimum rest, or collective-bargaining terms.
2. **Calibrated Disconnection.** Every employee enjoys a default right to disengage. *Managed Availability* roles (on-call, standby) are permitted only where **(a)** local law allows compensated after-hours work, **(b)** the employee has signed a digital opt-in accord before the assignment, and **(c)** a restitution premium (cash or leave, per local law) is pre-programmed.
3. **Equity of Opportunity through Contextual Merit.** Fairness is measured by equivalent access to advancement and compensation. Impact is primary, but “contextual merit” also recognizes documented relationship stewardship and mentorship where locally valued. Contextual merit is evaluated against the published rubric in Section VII.C, never assumed from physical presence.
4. **Anti-Bias by Documented Impact.** Performance assessment evaluates documented output. Presence is not a proxy for productivity; nor is availability a proxy for commitment.
5. **Cultural Fluency over Cultural Imperialism.** We do not export asynchronous workflow theology to high-context markets, nor do we allow presenteeism bias to colonize regulated markets. Each cluster defines “commitment” through locally co-designed norms that are explicitly documented and trained.
6. **Predictable Reliability.** All schedules are transparent. Handoff protocols are standardized so that flexibility produces reliability, not chaos.

| **India** | D (TEM) | State-level Shops & Establishments Acts; no federal RtD; overtime after 48 h in most states; IT sector exemptions. | State-by-state overtime-threshold lock in federated payroll engine; Shops Act registration for altered hours; deferred compressed week pending Karnataka/Maharashtra/Haryana clarification; IT-sector exemption mapping. |

**3.2 Payroll Architecture.** The Company maintains jurisdiction-specific overtime-threshold and benefits-accrual rules within the global HRIS. Where provincial or state variance exists (Canada, India), payroll rules are federated at the lowest administrative unit required by statute. All compressed-week arrangements are configured to auto-lock at statutory thresholds unless a pre-approved digital opt-in override is on file.

**3.3 Data Protection & Telemetry Safeguards.** Any automated time-telemetry deployed to enforce overtime caps or RtD windows is **aggregate and anonymized at source**; individual-level surveillance dashboards are prohibited. A Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is mandatory in GDPR and equivalent jurisdictions before system configuration. Managers receive only anonymized, team-level adherence percentages.

**3.4 Works-Council & CBA Timeline.** In countries requiring collective consultation (France, Germany, Spain, Netherlands, Brazil), the **pre-implementation consultation window is 90 days**. Rollout in these countries is gated by written agreement or completion of statutory deadlock resolution.
