# GEOPOLITICAL BOYCOTT NAVIGATION FRAMEWORK FOR GLOBAL CONSUMER BRANDS
## Executive Implementation Manual for 2025-2026

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This framework enables global consumer brands to navigate contradictory regional pressures from geopolitical boycotts while maintaining operational coherence, stakeholder trust, and business continuity. It provides:

1. **Clear decision criteria** distinguishing when to position vs. when to maintain neutrality
2. **Delegated authority architecture** that empowers regional teams while preventing brand fragmentation
3. **Explicit stakeholder communication protocols** that acknowledge tensions rather than hiding them
4. **Harm mitigation strategies** tied to specific dollar amounts and timelines
5. **Exception protocols** for when ground truth contradicts theoretical decision logic

**Core Principle**: Principle-driven positioning is sustainable only when brand is transparent about trade-offs, accepts that some stakeholder groups will be disappointed, and explicitly prioritizes whose demands it will satisfy when all demands cannot be met simultaneously.

# PART 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY FOR C-SUITE DECISION-MAKING

## 1.1 Three-Tier Principle Architecture (Replace Ambiguous "Values")

The framework eliminates vague commitments by categorizing all corporate positions into three tiers with distinct decision rules:

| **TIER** | **DEFINITION** | **REVISION POLICY** | **STAKEHOLDER IMPACT** | **EXAMPLE** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **UNIVERSAL PRINCIPLES** | Non-negotiable across all regions; no exceptions for profit or activism pressure | Revision only if core analysis was wrong (rare); requires Board approval | High stakes: Violations trigger market exit or public accountability | No child labor in supply chain; no discrimination in hiring; food safety standards |
| **CONTEXTUAL POSITIONS** | Grounded in principles but operationalized differently by region based on market conditions | Revisable annually if ground truth changes; requires Regional VP + CFO approval | Medium stakes: Implementation timeline/approach varies; core commitment stays aligned | "Living wage" levels vary by cost-of-living; supplier transition timelines vary by market maturity |
| **EXPLICIT NEUTRALITY** | Issues outside brand domain where no principle applies and no stakeholder is exposed | Non-revisable (if neutral today, stays neutral unless exposure changes materially) | Low stakes: Brand explains why neutral; protects employee personal activism | Candidate elections; religious doctrine; abstract geopolitical disputes not affecting operations |

**Immediate Action for CEO**: 
- Audit all corporate statements (mission, ESG, public speeches)
- Assign each to Tier 1, 2, or 3
- Challenge: Are Tier 1 items truly non-negotiable? (Most organizations have 3-5 true Universal Principles; anything more suggests mission creep)

## 1.2 Decision Authority Grid: Who Decides What

When geopolitical pressure emerges, stop asking "What should we do?" Start asking "Who has authority to decide this?"

```
DECISION AUTHORITY BY ISSUE TYPE
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

UNIVERSAL PRINCIPLES (Tier 1)
├─ Decision Authority: CEO + Board
├─ Regional VP can: Implement; explain locally; ask for timeline extension
├─ Regional VP CANNOT: Override; grant exceptions; negotiate principle away
├─ Franchisee can: Execute operationally within guardrails
├─ Franchisee CANNOT: Take contradictory local position; publicly oppose principle
├─ Exception Process: Only if new evidence shows principle analysis was wrong (rare)
└─ Example: If labor standards enforcement (Tier 1), all regions enforce. 
           Franchisee facing revenue loss gets Options B (financial support) or C (mitigation partnership), 
           not autonomy to position against labor standards.

CONTEXTUAL POSITIONS (Tier 2)
├─ Decision Authority: Regional VP + CEO
├─ Regional VP can: Adapt tone/emphasis; change implementation timeline; customize for local context
├─ Regional VP CANNOT: Contradict core principle; appear to take opposite position
├─ Franchisee can: Operational adaptation within regional strategy
├─ Franchisee CANNOT: Undermine regional position; take contradictory stance
├─ Exception Process: 2-week review if Regional VP reports ground truth contradicts filters
└─ Example: If gender equity positioning (Tier 2), Region A emphasizes "equal opportunity"; 
           Region B emphasizes "expanding talent pool"; same principle, different emphasis, approved by Regional VP.

OPERATIONAL/TACTICAL DECISIONS (Franchise Authority)
├─ Decision Authority: Franchisee
├─ Franchisee can: Choose HOW to implement principle locally (not WHETHER to implement)
├─ Franchisee CANNOT: Violate principle or create appearance of violation
├─ Example: Principle = "diverse hiring"; Franchisee decides: partner with women's orgs vs. 
           university recruiting vs. community center outreach (HOW; not WHETHER).

PERSONAL/POLITICAL MATTERS (Individual Authority)
├─ Decision Authority: Individual employee
├─ Brand role: Protect right to personal activism; maintain safe workplace for all perspectives
├─ Protected: Employee's private political activism, community involvement, personal charitable giving
├─ Professionalism required: No harassment of colleagues; maintain professional standards at work
└─ Example: Employee's own political activism is protected; cannot be retaliated against; 
           but cannot create hostile workplace for employees with opposite views.
```

**Immediate Action for Regional VPs**: 
- When franchisee makes demand, immediately classify: Is this Tier 1, 2, or 3 issue?
- If Tier 1: You cannot negotiate; explain authority limit; offer available options
- If Tier 2: You have flexibility in operationalization; discuss within 1 week
- If Operational: Franchisee decides; your role is guardrail compliance, not approval

## 1.3 The Harm Tolerance Framework (Explicit Trade-Offs)

Stop pretending positioning will be painless. Specify upfront: What harms will brand accept?

| **HARM TYPE** | **TOLERANCE THRESHOLD** | **MITIGATION APPROACH** | **ESCALATION TRIGGER** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Employee Safety** | ZERO tolerance | Immediate evacuation; relocation support; visa sponsorship | Any safety risk triggers CEO decision on positioning viability |
| **Franchisee Revenue Loss** | Up to 20% of annual profit | Years 1-3: Financial support (75%, 50%, 25% of loss); operational mitigation; extended timeline | Loss >20% triggers reconsideration of operationalization or market viability |
| **Worker Displacement** | Temporary acceptable if mitigation is robust | Retraining; income bridging; job placement; supplier transition loans | If >20% unemployment at 18-month mark, accelerate support or reconsider timeline |
| **Consumer Price Increase** | ≤5% without mitigation; ≤10% with mitigation | Absorption of some cost increase; lower-income consumer support | >10% triggers review of operationalization (may need longer timeline) |
| **Activist Boycott** | Up to 10% revenue loss from campaign | Expected; do NOT reverse position due to boycott | >10% loss triggers assessment of whether position is operationalized correctly |
| **Market Share Loss to Competitors** | ≤10% acceptable; >10% triggers review | May need to accept competitive disadvantage; revisit if >10% sustained | If >10%, assess market viability or operationalization approach |

**Immediate Action for CFO**: 
- For any positioning involving Tier 1 principle, calculate: What is maximum harm brand can absorb?
- Budget for mitigation explicitly (Years 1-3)
- Get Board sign-off: Do we accept harm up to this threshold for this principle?
- Do NOT proceed with positioning if harm tolerance exceeds Board comfort

## 1.4 Exception Protocol: When Ground Truth Contradicts Theory

**Scenario You'll Face**: Regional VP submits evidence that Filters 1-4 say "position required," but ground intelligence says "positioning will destabilize supply chain, trigger government pressure, create sectarian violence."

**2-Week Exception Review Process**:

```
STEP 1: Regional VP Submits Exception Request
├─ Includes: Evidence summary, risk assessment, recommended alternative
├─ Example: "Filters indicate labor standard enforcement is required. But supply chain audit 
│          shows 35% of suppliers cannot comply in operationalization timeline. Alternative: 
│          Graduated transition (3 years) instead of enforcement (18 months)."

STEP 2: Rapid Review (2 weeks)
├─ Decision-makers: CEO + Chief Strategy Officer + CFO + Regional VP
├─ Review criteria:
│  ├─ Is ground truth evidence credible? (Supply chain audit > anecdotes)
│  ├─ Does ground truth actually contradict filters, or is it just costly? (Contradiction ≠ expensive)
│  ├─ What alternative operationalization serves principle while accommodating ground truth?
│  └─ What accountability structure monitors alternative approach?

STEP 3: Decision
├─ OPTION A: DENY (Filters stand; Regional VP must implement)
│  ├─ Decision: CEO explains decision; offers enhanced support (budget, timeline extension, HQ team)
│  └─ Accountability: CEO owns outcome if predicted harms occur
│
├─ OPTION B: GRANT (Substitute alternative approach)
│  ├─ Decision: CEO documents exception reasoning
│  ├─ Alternative: [Graduated timeline] OR [Regional Contextual Position instead of Universal Principle] 
│  │             OR [Temporary market exit while conditions improve]
│  ├─ Communication: Brand announces revised approach; explains principle still applies
│  └─ Monitoring: Quarterly review; if alternative fails, escalate to decision point
│
└─ OPTION C: ESCALATE TO BOARD (If decision is strategic/brand-identity level)
   └─ Timeline: 1 week for Board review; decision within 2 weeks of submission
```

**Immediate Action for CEO**: 
- Identify if your organization has existing exceptions (positioning that's not fully universal)
- Evaluate: Should each exception be formalized within this framework?
- Communicate to Board: "We may need to grant regional exceptions. Here's our decision process for them."

# PART 2: STAKEHOLDER-SPECIFIC PROTOCOLS

## 2.1 Franchisee Negotiation Protocol: When They Demand Autonomy

**Scenario**: Franchisee in Region A says: "Your labor standards position will cost me 25% revenue. I want permission to take opposite position locally and say it's my choice, not brand's."

**Response Sequence**:

```
STEP 1: Acknowledge Conflict (Within 24 hours)
├─ Regional VP: "I understand this hurts your business. Your concern is legitimate. 
│               Let me explain why I may not be able to grant autonomy."

STEP 2: Explain Authority Limit (Within 48 hours)
├─ "Labor standards involve a Universal Principle (Tier 1). This requires brand consistency globally.
├─ If I grant you autonomy to position against labor standards, I'm signaling the principle is negotiable.
├─ Stakeholders in other regions will demand same exemption.
├─ Brand will fragment; investor confidence in ESG commitments will erode."

STEP 3: Offer Available Options (Your choice; not negotiable)
├─ OPTION B: Financial Support
│  ├─ Brand covers 75% of Year 1 revenue loss, 50% of Year 2, 25% of Year 3
│  ├─ Requires: Documentation of loss; franchisee stays aligned with brand position
│  ├─ Budget: $500K-$2M+ per franchisee (rare; max 2-3 franchisees per company to prevent precedent)
│  └─ Duration: 3 years; after Year 3, no further support (market should normalize)
│
├─ OPTION C: Operational Mitigation
│  ├─ Brand provides: Marketing support ($500K-$1M), employee training, supply chain redesign
│  ├─ No financial subsidy; significant resource investment
│  ├─ Franchisee stays aligned with brand position
│  ├─ Timeline: 12-18 months of intensive support
│  └─ Success metric: Revenue stabilizes or recovery trajectory is clear
│
└─ OPTION D: Orderly Market Exit
   ├─ Brand funds: Franchisee severance (6-12 months avg profit), employee transition support
   ├─ Community engagement: Explain why brand is exiting (principle-driven, not abandonment)
   ├─ Timeline: 12-18 months to wind down
   └─ Philanthropy: Brand may invest in community after exit (shows respect for region)

STEP 4: Document Decision (In Writing)
├─ Decision memo: Issue → Authority → Why autonomy cannot be granted → Option offered
├─ Franchisee signature: Acknowledges decision + option chosen
├─ Escalation: Copied to CEO, Chief Legal Officer, CFO
└─ Quarterly review: Is franchisee adapting or resisting? Adjust support if needed.

STEP 5: Accountability Governance
├─ If Option B chosen: CFO tracks expenses quarterly; at Year 3, decision point on renewal
├─ If Option C chosen: Regional VP tracks progress; escalate if not on trajectory by Month 12
├─ If Option D chosen: HR manages transition; communicate transparently to employees + suppliers
└─ If franchisee refuses all options: Contract review; may trigger franchise termination process
```

**Immediate Action for Regional VPs**:
- Pre-brief all franchisees: "Here's how we make decisions when global positioning conflicts with local interests."
- Be clear upfront: "Autonomy exists for operational decisions, not for taking position opposite to Universal Principles."
- Don't let this surprise them during crisis.

## 2.2 Employee Communication: Managing Identity-Level Threats

**Scenario**: Brand positions on issue that contradicts some employees' national/community identity. Employees fear: "Brand is saying my nation/community is bad."

**Pre-Announcement Protocol (72-96 hours before announcement)**:

```
STEP 1: Identify Vulnerable Employees
├─ Employees from communities likely to feel threatened by position
├─ Immigrant employees; employees with strong national identity; employees in conflict zones
├─ Assess vulnerability: Are they fearful? Do they have legal status concerns? Are they support network isolated?

STEP 2: Individual Outreach (1:1 conversation)
├─ Manager or HR + employee; phone or video call (not email)
├─ Message:
│  ├─ "We're announcing [position] on [date]. I wanted you to hear directly from us first."
│  ├─ "I know this affects you personally because of your [community] identity. I wanted to acknowledge that."
│  ├─ "Here's what we're NOT saying: We're not saying your nation/community is bad."
│  ├─ "Here's what we ARE saying: [Principle]. It applies to all suppliers globally, including those 
│  │ associated with your community."
│  ├─ "Your personal views are protected. You don't have to endorse this position. 
│  │  But we need you to maintain professionalism at work."
│  └─ "Here's what we commit to supporting you: [EAP access, ERG resources, flexible work options]"

STEP 3: Listening (Not consultation; genuine listening)
├─ Ask employee: "What concerns do you have? What would make this more manageable?"
├─ Don't promise outcome employee is hoping for (reversal of position)
├─ DO listen; document concerns; find operational support
├─ Example response: "I can't reverse our position because it reflects our principle. 
│                    But I can offer you [flexible hours / change of team / transfer to different unit]."

STEP 4: Ongoing Support
├─ EAP resources: Counseling access (free, confidential)
├─ ERG (Employee Resource Group): Connect employee with community-based affinity group
├─ Manager check-ins: Weekly for 1 month; then monthly for 3 months
├─ Anti-retaliation policy: Make clear—no punishment for personal views or activism outside work
└─ Escalation: If employee is experiencing harassment from colleagues, escalate immediately

STEP 5: Transparency Governance
├─ Track: How many employees from affected communities are experiencing distress?
├─ Measure: Are check-ins reducing stress levels? Is EAP utilization normal?
├─ Escalate: If >20% of employees from community show sustained distress, escalate to HR VP
└─ Adjust: May need to extend support, increase manager training, or revisit operationalization
```

**Immediate Action for CHROs**:
- Audit: Which positions are likely to create identity-level threat for employees?
- Pre-brief all managers: "Here's how we'll communicate positioning that affects employee identity."
- Ensure EAP capacity: May see increased utilization; budget accordingly
- Create ERGs for affected communities (even if not formalized before): Quick setup in crisis

## 2.3 Activist Response Strategy: Know When Engagement Works

**Critical Framework Principle**: Brand communication can persuade **good-faith stakeholders**. It cannot convert **activists with structural incentives to escalate**.

```
ACTIVIST CATEGORIZATION & RESPONSE STRATEGY
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

CATEGORY 1: GOOD-FAITH ACTIVISTS
├─ Profile: Genuinely concerned about issue; willing to dialogue; will accept good-faith progress
├─ Examples: Labor rights NGOs interested in supplier development; community groups seeking transition support
├─ Incentive: Issue resolution; not campaign longevity
├─ Response Strategy:
│  ├─ ENGAGE: Early dialogue; listen to concerns; incorporate feedback into operationalization
│  ├─ COMMUNICATE: Transparent progress updates; invite feedback on approach
│  ├─ PARTNER: Consider co-branding initiatives; give credit to activist groups
│  └─ SUCCESS INDICATOR: Activist group shifts from critical to collaborative; publicly supports approach
│
└─ Investment: Moderate resource; expect relationship to stabilize within 6-12 months

CATEGORY 2: ADVERSARIAL ACTIVISTS (Issue-focused but oppositional)
├─ Profile: Concerned about issue; convinced brand approach is wrong; will remain critical
├─ Examples: Activists focused on same principle but demanding more aggressive approach
├─ Incentive: Brand capitulation on their terms (not issue resolution)
├─ Response Strategy:
│  ├─ ENGAGE: Limited dialogue; clarify your position; don't negotiate principle
│  ├─ LISTEN: Understand their concerns; incorporate legitimate feedback (not demands for reversal)
│  ├─ SET BOUNDARIES: "We've made this decision. We're open to feedback on operationalization, 
│  │                   not on reversal."
│  ├─ COMMUNICATE: Quarterly updates (not intended to change their mind; for other stakeholders)
│  └─ ACCEPT: This group may remain critical; that's OK; maintain your position anyway
│
└─ Investment: Moderate resource; don't expect relationship to warm; campaign may persist 6-18 months

CATEGORY 3: STRUCTURAL-INCENTIVE ACTIVISTS (Funded to maintain campaign)
├─ Profile: Funded by governments/corporations to keep brand under pressure; incentivized by campaign longevity
├─ Examples: State-backed nationalist movements; competitor-funded campaigns; activist groups with government grants
├─ Incentive: Campaign continuation (not issue resolution); funding depends on staying active
├─ Response Strategy:
│  ├─ RECOGNIZE: This group's opposition is structural; communication won't change them
│  ├─ DON'T ENGAGE: Extensive dialogue signals vulnerability; minimal engagement is better
│  ├─ COMMUNICATE: To other stakeholders (good-faith activists, customers, media); not to this group
│  ├─ MAINTAIN POSITION: Do not reverse due to their pressure (reversals reward structural activism)
│  └─ ESCALATE IF STATE-BACKED: Escalate to CEO + Government Relations; may need political engagement
│
└─ Investment: Minimal; these campaigns often plateau at 12-18 months when funding is redirected

DECISION TREE: How to categorize activist group
├─ Does group engage in good-faith dialogue? YES → Category 1 or 2
├─ Will group ever be satisfied if brand maintains current approach? YES → Category 1 (good-faith); NO → Category 2 (adversarial)
├─ Does group appear funded by government/major corporation? YES → Category 3 (structural incentive)
├─ Has group's funding or rhetoric escalated with campaign intensity? YES → Likely Category 3
└─ Conclusion: Allocate engagement resources based on category; don't over-invest in Categories 2-3
```

**Immediate Action for Chief Communications Officer**:
- Map existing activist campaigns: Which category does each fall into?
- For Category 1: Increase engagement; treat as partnership opportunity
- For Category 2: Maintain clear position; accept ongoing criticism
- For Category 3: Identify funding source; escalate to CEO if state-backed; prepare for sustained campaign

# PART 3: IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP (First 90 Days)

## 3.1 Pre-Positioning Readiness Checklist (Weeks 1-2)

Before announcing any position on geopolitical issue:

```
CHECKLIST ITEMS (Complete all before announcement)

□ PRINCIPLE CATEGORIZATION
  □ Issue mapped to Tier 1 (Universal), Tier 2 (Contextual), or Tier 3 (Neutral)
  □ Classification verified by Chief Legal Officer + Chief Strategy Officer
  □ If Tier 1: Revision path specified (what would change our minds?)
  □ Board awareness: Board informed this will be positioned this way

□ FILTER ANALYSIS (Filters 1-4 from Strategic Framework)
  □ Filter 1 (Principle Test): Does this connect to stated principle? PASS/FAIL
  □ Filter 2 (Competency Test): Is this within brand's domain? PASS/FAIL
  □ Filter 3 (Asymmetry Test): Material harm to stakeholders? PASS/FAIL
  □ Filter 4 (Exposure Test): Our stakeholders directly exposed? PASS/FAIL
  □ If filters are inconclusive: CEO has explicitly decided to position or be neutral

□ STAKEHOLDER MAPPING
  □ Identified: Employees, Franchisees, Consumers, Suppliers, Regulators by region
  □ Assessed: Who benefits? Who is harmed? Who is neutral?
  □ Predicted: 3-5 likely activist responses (both pro and con)
  □ State-backed risk: High-risk geopolitical regions flagged for heightened monitoring

□ HARM ASSESSMENT
  □ Predicted harms by type: Safety, Revenue, Employment, Price increase, Boycott, Competition
  □ Quantified: % of franchisees affected, $ revenue at risk, # employees impacted, price % increase
  □ Tolerance check: Does any predicted harm exceed tolerance thresholds?
  □ Mitigation strategy: Designed for each significant harm type
  □ Budget approval: CFO has signed off on mitigation costs (Years 1-3)

□ REGIONAL CREDIBILITY AUDIT (Per major region)
  □ Media landscape: How will they frame this positioning?
  □ Historical precedent: Has brand positioned on this before? How was it received?
  □ Activist positioning: What will opponents say? What will supporters say?
  □ Credibility gap: % of stakeholders who will see this as principled vs. opportunistic
  □ If gap >20%: Narrative repair strategy designed

□ COMMUNICATION STRATEGY
  □ Global announcement: Core message, principle, rationale (drafted + edited)
  □ Regional variations: Localized messaging (different tone; same substance)
  □ Talking points: For managers, franchisees, media, employees
  □ Pre-announcement stakeholder schedule:
      □ Franchisees: 72 hours before
      □ Employees in affected regions: 48-72 hours before
      □ Suppliers (if supply chain affected): 48 hours before
      □ Investor relations: 24-48 hours before
      □ NGO partners: 24 hours before (if applicable)

□ OPERATIONAL READINESS
  □ If Tier 1 positioning: Implementation timeline specified
  □ If Tier 2 positioning: Regional variations clearly defined + approved
  □ If Tier 3 (Neutral): Neutrality explanation drafted + approved
  □ Supply chain: If affected, contingency plans for alternative suppliers
  □ Employee resources: EAP, ERGs, manager training materials ready

□ DECISION DOCUMENTATION
  □ Decision memo written: Issue → Principle → Filters → Rationale → Decision
  □ Signed: CEO, Chief Legal Officer, Chief Strategy Officer, CFO
  □ If Regional Exception requested: Exception decision documented + approved/denied
  □ Harm tolerance: CFO confirms budget; COO confirms feasibility; CEO confirms principle justifies cost

□ ESCALATION PROTOCOLS
  □ Franchisee override process: Defined + pre-briefed to Regional VPs
  □ Regional exception process: Defined + known to Regional VPs
  □ Employee distress escalation: HR has process for flagging concerns
  □ Activist campaign monitoring: PR team has monitoring + response triggers

CHECKLIST STATUS: 
├─ All items checked? READY for announcement
├─ 2-3 items unchecked? STOP; complete before announcement
├─ Any safety-related items unchecked? DO NOT ANNOUNCE (safety gaps are non-recoverable)
└─ CEO has final sign-off? All go
```

**Immediate Action for CEO**:
- Assign ownership: Who is responsible for completing each checklist item?
- Set deadline: All items completed by [DATE]. No announcement before this.
- Schedule: Readiness review meeting; go/no-go decision

## 3.2 Announcement Sequence (Week 3)

**Global Announcement (Day 1, Monday 8 AM ET)**:

```
ANNOUNCEMENT COMPONENTS

1. PRESS RELEASE (500 words)
   ├─ What we are doing (specific action)
   ├─ Why we are doing it (connect to principle; NOT marketing benefit)
   ├─ How we are doing it (operational steps, timeline, investment)
   ├─ What we acknowledge (harm, trade-offs, unknowns)
   ├─ What we commit to (measurement, transparency, accountability)
   └─ FAQ (3-5 likely questions + honest answers)

2. CEO VIDEO (1-2 minutes)
   ├─ CEO speaks in own words (not scripted corporate-speak)
   ├─ Tone: Confident but not arrogant; principled but not self-righteous
   ├─ Content: Same as press release, distilled to essentials
   └─ Delivery: Conversational; shows CEO understands complexity

3. MEDIA ENGAGEMENT (Same day, 4-6 hours after announcement)
   ├─ Journalists provided: Backgrounder, fact sheet, quotes, case studies
   ├─ Media contact: Same-day Q&A available
   ├─ Prepared responses to likely questions:
   │  ├─ "Why now?" (Not activist pressure; outcome of long planning)
   │  ├─ "What does this cost?" (Specific amount; material but budgeted)
   │  ├─ "Help or hurt business?" (Short-term cost; long-term value)
   │  └─ "What if competitors don't?" (That's their choice; we're competing on values)
   └─ Strategy: Let journalists tell story; don't over-message

REGIONAL ANNOUNCEMENTS (Week 1, Days 2-3, localized timing)
├─ Format: Regional VP + local interviews; regional media focus
├─ Messaging: Tailored to regional context (but NOT contradicting global announcement)
   ├─ Western regions: Emphasize values/principle (ESG framing works)
   ├─ Rising-power regions: Emphasize business rationale (local investment, local partnerships)
   ├─ Sectarian zones: Emphasize equal treatment (same principle applies to all communities)
│
└─ Timeline: Announcement in local language; same week as global announcement

STAKEHOLDER BRIEFINGS (Pre-announcement, 48-72 hours)
├─ Franchisees: 1-hour confidential briefing
│  ├─ Content: What's being announced; how it affects their business; what support is available
│  ├─ Q&A: Address their concerns directly
│  └─ Materials: Talking points, customer scripts, FAQ for their staff
│
├─ Employees (group briefing + individual outreach)
│  ├─ Managers brief: 1 hour, explain how to discuss with their teams
│  ├─ Employees hear: 20-min overview + Q&A session
│  ├─ Vulnerable employees: Individual 1:1 outreach (72-96 hours before)
│  └─ Materials: FAQ posters, 1-minute animated video (if geographically diverse)
│
├─ Suppliers (if supply chain affected)
│  ├─ Content: What's changing; how it affects them; transition support available
│  └─ Timeline: When does enforcement begin?
│
└─ Investors: 24-48 hours before
   └─ Message: This reflects ESG commitment; financially sustainable; long-term value creation
```

**Immediate Action for Communications Team**:
- Start drafting announcement immediately (Week 1)
- Schedule reviews: Each draft reviewed by CEO, Chief Legal Officer, Regional VPs
- Identify media contacts: Pre-brief key journalists 24 hours before announcement
- Prepare for day-of questions: Have talking points ready; rapid response capability

## 3.3 Post-Announcement Management (Weeks 4+)

**Ongoing Accountability Structure**:

```
WEEK 1-2: INITIAL RESPONSE MANAGEMENT
├─ Monitor: Media coverage, activist campaigns, customer/employee reaction
├─ Respond: To factual inaccuracies only (not to all criticism)
├─ Escalate: Coordinated campaigns (multiple groups, state backing apparent) to CEO
├─ Support: Franchisees, employees in affected regions; check-in calls from Regional VP
└─ Measure: Brand sentiment tracking; advocacy/opposition campaign intensity

MONTH 1: OPERATIONAL LAUNCH
├─ Supply chain: Notify suppliers of timeline; begin assessments if operationalization involves supplier changes
├─ Employee: Rollout EAP/ERG resources; monitor for distress signals
├─ Communication: Address most common stakeholder questions through FAQs, webinars, town halls
└─ Measurement: Establish baseline metrics (supplier compliance %, franchisee revenue impact, employee surveys)

MONTH 3: FIRST FORMAL REVIEW (Quarterly)
├─ Actual vs. Predicted Harm Assessment:
│  ├─ Is predicted revenue loss materializing? Higher or lower than expected?
│  ├─ Are employee distress levels stable or escalating?
│  ├─ Is activist campaign intensity declining or intensifying?
│  └─ If actual harm >20% higher than predicted: Escalate to Regional VP + CEO
│
├─ Mitigation Effectiveness:
│  ├─ Is Option B (financial support) helping franchisees stabilize?
│  ├─ Is Option C (operational mitigation) on track?
│  ├─ Do employees feel supported by company?
│  └─ Adjust if needed
│
├─ Operational Progress:
│  ├─ If operationalization involves supplier changes: % compliant; timeline on track?
│  ├─ If involves hiring changes: % from target demographic; on trajectory?
│  └─ Course corrections implemented
│
└─ Communication: Quarterly Transparency Report published
   ├─ What progress was made against operational milestones
   ├─ What harm was predicted vs. what actually occurred
   ├─ How mitigation investments are being deployed
   ├─ What we don't yet know; what we're still learning

MONTH 6: MID-COURSE CORRECTION POINT
├─ Decision: Are we on track to achieve principle operationalization?
├─ If YES: Continue; maintain current approach
├─ If NO: Options:
│  ├─ Extend timeline (slower operationalization = lower acute harm)
│  ├─ Increase mitigation investment (cost more, ease transition)
│  ├─ Revisit operationalization (maybe current approach is wrong, not principle)
│  └─ Regional Exception: If ground truth still contradicts filters, exercise exception protocol
│
└─ Communication: Transparency update addresses course corrections (shows brand is adaptive, not rigid)

MONTH 12: ANNUAL ASSESSMENT
├─ Business Impact:
