# Multi-Market Green-Bond Financing Strategy

## 1. Recommendation

Establish a **Group Green and Transition Financing Framework** with:

- **Core-green criteria** for projects with strong, measurable environmental benefits;
- a separately governed **transition-finance pool** for credible industrial decarbonization projects that may not satisfy strict green-taxonomy requirements;
- project-level classifications for each relevant taxonomy and market;
- one consolidated proceeds and emissions-data ledger;
- common claims, assurance, governance, correction and reallocation controls;
- country-specific legal, tax, listing and taxonomy supplements.

Do not describe all retrofit expenditure as universally green.

### Recommended financing sequence

1. Build the project, data, governance and proceeds-control platform.
2. Select the first market using an economic, legal, investor-demand and execution scorecard.
3. Prefer a **narrow EUR inaugural green bond** only if the scorecard supports it.
4. Use the **EuGB label only after current EU counsel and a qualified reviewer confirm compliance**. Otherwise issue an ICMA-aligned green bond expressly stated to be **not an EuGB**.
5. Establish a reporting and assurance record.
6. Enter selected Gulf and Asian markets individually.
7. Finance broader or less mature retrofit projects through a separately labelled transition, sustainability-linked, sustainability, project-finance, bank, export-credit or conventional instrument.

A smaller, defensible inaugural issue is preferable to a larger issue supported by weak data or overstated claims.

---

## 2. Financing and instrument model

### Default structure: parent-level issuance

Use a parent-level senior unsecured or otherwise appropriately supported bond, subject to:

- rating and leverage capacity;
- debt-incurrence and negative-pledge restrictions;
- liquidity and refinancing limits;
- tax and accounting treatment;
- intercompany-lending and guarantee capacity;
- foreign-exchange, remittance and convertibility rules.

This structure provides scale, liquidity and centralized allocation. Before funding non-EU subsidiaries, local counsel must confirm that proceeds can legally and economically reach the relevant plants.

Use subsidiary, project or local-currency issuance where local regulation, natural currency matching, tax, investor access or ring-fenced cash flows justify it.

### Instrument decision tree

| Financing need | Appropriate instrument |
|---|---|
| Ring-fenced projects with strong environmental evidence | Green bond |
| Industrial transition projects not meeting strict green criteria | Transition bond or loan |
| Green and social eligible expenditure | Sustainability bond |
| Group-wide, measurable targets where asset allocation is difficult | Sustainability-linked bond |
| Ring-fenced cash flows or project security | Project finance |
| Insufficient evidence for a sustainable label | Conventional debt |

A transition instrument is not an easier green bond. It requires an issuer-level transition plan, interim targets, sector pathways, lock-in controls and transparent residual-emissions treatment.

## 3. Project taxonomy and disclosure approach

Assign each project separate statuses:

1. **Core-green eligible under the Group Framework**
2. **EU Taxonomy eligible**
3. **EU Taxonomy aligned**
4. **Local-market green eligible**
5. **Transition eligible**
6. **Excluded**

These statuses must not be conflated. “Core-green eligible under the Group Framework” is an internal classification, not an external global standard.

### Core-green projects

Prioritize, where technically and legally eligible:

- renewable electricity generation;
- qualifying renewable-energy procurement;
- industrial electrification;
- replacement of fossil-fuel equipment with electric alternatives;
- waste-heat recovery;
- industrial energy-efficiency systems;
- renewable-linked storage;
- direct methane, nitrous oxide or other greenhouse-gas abatement;
- qualifying pollution-prevention, water-efficiency or resource-efficiency measures.

### Transition projects

Potential candidates include:

- coal or heavy-fuel-oil substitution;
- process optimization at high-emitting facilities;
- hydrogen systems;
- CCUS;
- biomass or biofuels;
- efficiency upgrades that may extend high-carbon asset life;
- projects with measurable reductions but incomplete taxonomy alignment.

Each transition project must disclose:

- pre- and post-project absolute emissions;
- intensity and production assumptions;
- remaining fossil-fuel use;
- expected asset life;
- lock-in and stranded-asset risk;
- compatibility with future electrification or lower-carbon fuels;
- relevant sector pathway;
- alignment with the Group transition plan;
- lifecycle emissions where material;
- residual emissions;
- reliance on subsidies, offsets or immature technology.

Exclude projects that materially increase unabated fossil capacity, rely on unsupported environmental assumptions, or cannot support a reproducible environmental claim.

### Required project register

For every project record:

- plant, country, legal owner and technology;
- project description, capacity and commissioning date;
- eligible expenditure type;
- environmental objective;
- applicable EU and local taxonomy activity;
- technical criteria, DNSH and safeguards;
- baseline and impact methodology;
- data-quality class;
- new financing, refinancing or reimbursement status;
- additionality and project-readiness assessment;
- co-financing and double-counting analysis;
- reviewer and assurance status;
- concentration exposure by country, technology and entity.

Taxonomy conclusions should distinguish:

1. activity identified;
2. activity eligible for assessment;
3. substantial contribution assessed;
4. DNSH assessed;
5. minimum safeguards assessed;
6. alignment concluded, not concluded or unable to assess;
7. percentage of proceeds aligned.

## 4. EuGB approach

Treat the EuGB as a regulated label, not enhanced marketing language.

Pursue it only if:

- the project pool meets applicable EU Taxonomy requirements;
- DNSH and minimum safeguards are evidenced;
- the applicable proceeds-allocation threshold can be met;
- required factsheets, allocation reports and impact reports can be prepared;
- the external reviewer meets applicable EU requirements;
- evidence for non-EU projects supports assessment against the relevant EU criteria.

The commonly referenced framework includes an **85% allocation threshold for relevant proceeds**, subject to applicable flexibility provisions. Current EU counsel and the qualified reviewer must confirm:

- issuer and instrument scope;
- the threshold and flexibility treatment;
- allocation timing and temporary allocation;
- treatment of non-EU projects;
- current technical screening criteria;
- reporting templates;
- reviewer registration and supervision;
- prospectus and liability implications.

No EuGB-oriented marketing should occur before this confirmation.

If EuGB is not supportable, issue an ICMA-aligned green bond and state prominently that it:

- is not an EuGB;
- does not imply full EU Taxonomy alignment;
- contains projects with individually disclosed taxonomy statuses; and
- follows the Group’s stated eligibility and evidence controls.

## 5. Emissions-data quality framework

### Data classes

| Class | Evidence profile | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Verified** | Metered or auditable data, stable boundaries, reconciliations and independent verification or assurance | Allocation and quantified impact claims |
| **B — Controlled estimate** | Partial measurement, documented engineering estimates, management review and quantified uncertainty | Allocation only by exception; qualified claims |
| **C — Screening estimate** | Proxies, outdated records, extrapolation or unsupported assumptions | Pipeline screening only |
| **D — Unreliable** | Missing boundaries, unexplained variances or irreproducible calculations | Exclude until remediated |

The inaugural core-green pool should contain only Class A and approved Class B projects. Class C and D projects should not receive initial core-green allocations or support quantified impact claims.

Before issuance, management must set:

- maximum percentage of proceeds for Class B projects;
- maximum percentage of reported impact based on estimates;
- maximum estimation percentage per project;
- permitted exceptions;
- minimum supporting evidence;
- remediation deadline;
- approval authority;
- public disclosure requirements;
- consequences if remediation fails;
- whether Class B projects may support EuGB allocation.

Every plant must have a named data owner, operations approver, finance reviewer, estimation percentage, materiality threshold, remediation date and assurance status.

### Group emissions manual

Adopt one manual covering:

- organizational and operational boundaries;
- subsidiaries, joint ventures, leased and contract operations;
- Scope 1 sources;
- location- and market-based Scope 2;
- emission factors and global-warming-potential values;
- PPAs, RECs and environmental attributes;
- baseline selection and recalculation;
- production, product mix and utilization;
- acquisitions, divestments, outages and process changes;
- biogenic emissions, CCUS, offsets and removals;
- estimation, uncertainty, double counting and restatement.

Report Scope 1, Scope 2 and relevant Scope 3 separately. Do not aggregate them into an unqualified “emissions avoided” figure.

RECs and PPAs are not offsets. Ownership, bundling, geography, timing, retirement and double-counting controls must be documented. Offsets and removals cannot substantiate direct retrofit reductions.

## 6. Baselines, additionality and claimability

Use the following baseline hierarchy:

1. measured pre-project baseline;
2. adjusted historical baseline;
3. peer or engineering benchmark;
4. regulatory or market counterfactual only where necessary.

Approve the baseline before commissioning or the first impact period. Do not change it solely to improve reported performance.

Normalize for:

- production and product mix;
- operating hours and utilization;
- feedstock quality;
- weather where relevant;
- maintenance and outages;
- major process changes;
- capacity expansion and production growth.

For each quantified claim disclose:

- baseline and post-project emissions;
- activity data and emission factors;
- project boundary;
- production assumptions;
- uncertainty range;
- ex ante or ex post status;
- gross versus net treatment;
- assurance or verification status.

Prefer **“emissions reduction against an identified baseline”** to “avoided emissions.”

Classify financing as new, refinancing, reimbursement, accelerated, expanded, legally required or already committed. Additionality is a Group credibility control, not automatically a universal legal or ICMA requirement. Do not claim that the bond caused or accelerated a project without evidence.

## 7. Proceeds management and assurance

### Proceeds controls

Create a consolidated ledger linked to:

- bond proceeds and bank accounts;
- project budgets, invoices and expenditure dates;
- legal entities and countries;
- currency conversions;
- new-financing and refinancing classifications;
- taxonomy and data classes;
- approvals and assurance evidence.

Controls must include:

1. eligibility-committee approval before allocation;
2. segregation of duties;
3. general-ledger reconciliation;
4. double-counting checks across bonds, loans, grants, subsidies, PPAs, RECs, offsets and other instruments;
5. restrictions on temporary investment;
6. a defined refinancing look-back period;
7. substitution and reallocation rules;
8. quarterly internal monitoring;
9. internal-audit testing;
10. no silent substitution.

The framework must specify whether it permits capex, directly related opex, maintenance, R&D, feasibility work, enabling infrastructure, working capital and refinancing. Local counsel must confirm consistency with each selected label and market.

### Assurance plan

Use separate scopes for:

- Framework alignment;
- EU and local taxonomy analysis;
- project-pool eligibility;
- EuGB review;
- transition classification;
- proceeds allocation;
- impact metrics;
- Shariah review, if relevant.

Use a risk-based matrix:

- assurance over material allocation controls;
- limited assurance for initially immature impact metrics;
- technical verification for material or high-risk baselines;
- plant-level verification for the largest or most emissions-intensive projects;
- stronger review for projects supporting EuGB alignment.

Each engagement must identify the applicable standard, scope, materiality, sampling, reliance on estimates, limitations, qualifications and assurance level. Reviewer independence and conflicts must be documented.

## 8. Governance and investor credibility protections

Create a board-approved Green and Transition Finance Committee comprising treasury, finance, sustainability, operations, legal, tax, investor relations, regional subsidiaries, risk and internal audit.

The Committee approves:

- framework and supplements;
- project classifications;
- taxonomy conclusions;
- data exceptions;
- label selection;
- reviewers;
- allocation and impact reports;
- substitutions and reallocations;
- public corrections.

Maintain a central claims register covering:

- offering documents;
- roadshows and oral statements;
- websites;
- annual and sustainability reports;
- social media;
- translations;
- third-party ESG databases;
- index questionnaires;
- bank and distributor materials;
- rating-agency submissions.

Each claim must identify its evidence source, owner, approval date, permitted jurisdictions, review date and status as expected, measured or verified.

Prohibit:

- presenting one taxonomy as equivalent to another;
- calling estimates verified;
- combining Scope 1, 2 and 3 without boundaries;
- using offsets as direct retrofit reductions;
- claiming unsupported additionality;
- using intensity improvements to imply absolute reductions;
- claiming the green label improves credit quality;
- implying that selected projects make the whole Group low-carbon;
- using “net zero,” “carbon neutral,” “zero-emission,” “clean” or “low-carbon” without separate substantiation.

Material errors, ineligibility or underperformance must trigger documented assessment of correction, restatement, reallocation, bondholder notification, reviewer reassessment and regulatory notification where appropriate.

## 9. Market sequencing

### Europe

Use EUR first only if the market scorecard demonstrates:

- sufficient investor demand;
- adequate issue size and project pipeline;
- currency fit and acceptable hedging cost;
- credible EU Taxonomy or ICMA positioning;
- manageable legal and assurance costs;
- acceptable rating and refinancing effects.

### Gulf

Assess each jurisdiction separately for:

- issuer and listing eligibility;
- local green or sustainable-bond designation;
- tax, FX and repatriation;
- local disclosure;
- transition-technology treatment;
- investor requirements;
- Shariah review, only if the instrument or marketing requires it.

### Asia

Assess each selected country separately for:

- taxonomy and exchange rules;
- ASEAN applicability;
- transition-finance guidance;
- local-language and verification requirements;
- investor exclusions;
- currency and hedging;
- whether an international EUR/USD issue is preferable to a local bond.

Classify markets as launch, follow-on, opportunistic or no-go.

## 10. Implementation roadmap

### First 30 days

- Appoint executive sponsor and Committee.
- Appoint global and local counsel.
- Select framework coordinator and reviewers.
- Inventory projects, debt, capex and currencies.
- Establish preliminary green, transition and excluded pools.
- Identify launch and no-go markets.
- Issue a group-wide claims-control instruction.

### Days 30–90

- Classify plant data A–D.
- Approve the emissions-accounting manual.
- Complete taxonomy, additionality and lock-in assessments.
- Set eligible-expenditure, refinancing and Class B rules.
- Design the proceeds ledger and emissions-data architecture.
- Establish project-readiness and market-selection scorecards.
- Conduct investor, bank and rating-agency sounding.
- Approve the implementation budget.

### Days 90–150

- Finalize the Framework and market supplements.
- Complete the current-law refresh.
- Obtain framework and project-pool reviews.
- Confirm EuGB feasibility.
- Complete local legal, tax, FX, exchange and intercompany analysis.
- Implement data retention, cybersecurity and continuity controls.
- Prepare the investor data room and claims register.
- Obtain Board approval.

### Issuance

- **Rapid ICMA route:** approximately 5–9 months where systems, data and legal readiness are mature.
- **Full-control route:** approximately 9–15 months where EuGB analysis, multiple jurisdictions or significant data remediation is required.

After issuance, monitor allocation monthly, report at least annually, provide event-driven updates for material issues, obtain assurance under the approved matrix and review the Framework before each issuance and at least annually.

---

## 11. Final go/no-go conditions

Do not authorize public marketing or issuance until:

1. each initial project has a documented eligibility conclusion;
2. no Class C or D project is in the core-green pool;
3. Class B use is within approved quantitative limits;
4. baselines and financing classifications are documented;
5. absolute and intensity impacts are separated;
6. Scope boundaries are clear;
7. lock-in risk is assessed;
8. proceeds controls are operational;
9. material metrics have an assurance plan;
10. the selected label is legally and technically supportable;
11. projects are ready to spend and report;
12. the issuer-wide transition narrative is consistent;
13. credit, covenant, tax, FX and intercompany gates are passed;
14. the implementation budget is approved;
15. current local counsel and reviewer confirmations are complete; and
16. the Board approves the Framework, limitations and communications.

---

## Assumptions and matters requiring current confirmation

This strategy assumes the Group can issue at parent level, transfer proceeds across borders and obtain project-level emissions and expenditure evidence. Those assumptions require confirmation.

Before launch, obtain current advice on:

- EuGB scope, criteria, allocation threshold, flexibility, non-EU projects and reviewer requirements;
- EU Taxonomy and sustainability-reporting changes;
- local Gulf and Asian taxonomies, exchanges and disclosure rules;
- eligible expenditure and refinancing treatment;
- local borrowing, guarantees, intercompany loans, remittance and withholding tax;
- FX and convertibility;
- environmental, labour, community, water, biodiversity and pollution obligations;
- PPAs, RECs, offsets and environmental-attribute ownership;
- data protection, confidentiality and cybersecurity;
- listing, settlement, trustee and paying-agent requirements;
- assurance and verification standards;
- bond-document consequences of material errors or loss of eligibility.

**Bottom line:** issue a high-confidence green bond only where the evidence supports the label; maintain a separate transition channel for broader industrial decarbonization; and expand into Gulf and Asian markets only when current law, economics, investor demand, measurement quality and assurance capability support the transaction.
