# SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

## Strategic Overview

This pharmaceutical cold-chain distribution strategy optimizes vaccine delivery across 12 West and East African corridors, balancing equipment investment, operational reliability, and spoilage risk reduction. The design differentiates equipment, routing, and contingencies by corridor tier, accounting for infrastructure variability (paved roads 35–95%), electricity reliability (30–96% uptime), customs dwell times (12–72 hours), and last-mile formalization (40–90% facility coverage).

## Key Strategic Decisions

### **Tier A Corridors (Dakar–Saint-Louis, Accra–Tema–Kumasi, Lagos–Onitsha, Abidjan–Ouagadougou)**
- **Equipment:** 4 × Active Refrigerated Vehicles (ARVs, 2.5 kW compressor, ±0.5°C precision) + 12 × powered cold boxes (hub staging backup)
- **Strategy:** ARV-only primary; powered coolers mitigate customs dwell bottlenecks
- **Expected Spoilage Reduction:** 3–4% baseline → 0.5–1% target (85% improvement)
- **Investment:** $220,000 ARV CAPEX + $50,000 powered cooler infrastructure; $12,000/year operating
- **ROI:** Positive; $1,200–1,600/year spoilage savings per ARV justifies capital cost within 18 months

### **Tier B Corridors (Accra–Tamale–Bolgatanga, secondary Ghana loops)**
- **Equipment:** 2 × passive insulated vehicles + 2 × -20°C freezers (hub gel pack recycling) + 6 × powered coolers (hub staging)
- **Strategy:** Hybrid passive transport + powered hub staging + solar-powered mobile clinic coolers
- **Expected Spoilage Reduction:** 6–10% baseline → 2–3% target (70% improvement)
- **Investment:** $35,900 CAPEX + $1,200/year operating (significantly lower than Tier A; passive vehicle adequate for 1.5 runs/week)
- **ROI:** Moderate; 7–8 year payback through spoilage reduction + capacity expansion to underserved districts

### **Tier C Corridors (Conakry–Kindia–Mamou, Sierra Leone, Liberia interior)**
- **Equipment:** 4 × 1.5m³ deployable passive coolers (district-level pre-positioned) + NGO partnership (MSF, IRC)
- **Strategy:** Eliminate road transport (Jun–Sep monsoon); replace with asynchronous inventory draw from pre-positioned coolers
- **Expected Spoilage Reduction:** 12–18% baseline → 5–8% target (55% improvement)
- **Investment:** $20,000 CAPEX (coolers) + $1,200/year operating (ice supply) + NGO partnership cost-sharing
- **ROI:** Long-term (geographic access expansion enables future revenue growth); strategic value exceeds financial ROI

   - Equipment: 500L chest freezer; -20°C ±2°C; requires 220V grid or solar array backup

   - Eliminates 60–75% of hub delay spoilage (target: hub time <45 min total)
   - Cost: $30,000 CAPEX; amortized over 5-year lifecycle

4. **Solar-Powered Cooler Deployment (Mobile Clinics):**
   - 8 × 40L solar coolers: 2 per region (Accra, Tamale, Kumasi, + 1 additional region)
   - Function: Deploy to immunization campaign sites (Q2–Q4); maintains 2–8°C during afternoon peak (14:00–18:00, sun intensity maximum)
   - SOP: CHW collects vaccine in 20L passive box from hub; solar cooler deployed to clinic site; vaccine transferred mid-day
   - Cost: $1,000–1,200/unit × 8 = $8,000–9,600 CAPEX
   - Effectiveness: Reduces CHW hand-carry passive box temperature spike 50% (eliminates most vulnerable last-mile segment)

5. **Shade Canopy Installation (Warehouse Staging):**
   - 2 × 30m² UV-resistant tents: Tamale hub + Kumasi hub
   - Function: Reduces ambient temperature over vehicle staging area from 35–38°C to 25–28°C
   - SOP: Vehicle parks under canopy during documentation processing; delays reduced 15–30 min via temperature management
   - Cost: $800–1,200 per unit × 2 = $1,600–2,400 CAPEX; reusable, 10-year lifespan
