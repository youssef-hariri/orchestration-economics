# Pan-African Banking Acquisition Integration Strategy  
## Nigeria, Kenya and Morocco | 2026 onward

## Executive thesis

The group should pursue **one control framework, one reusable digital architecture, and three locally governed execution models**.

The objective is not to create three identical banks. It is to capture group-scale benefits in cybersecurity, data governance, fraud intelligence, finance, procurement, architecture, and digital-product development while preserving the local capabilities that protect regulatory confidence, customer trust, payment continuity, inclusion, and commercial performance.

The governing sequence is:

> **Stabilize → align people and regulators → establish common controls → decouple channels from local cores → unify selected digital journeys → migrate selectively → rationalize only after evidence.**

The group should not begin with a single pan-African core, legal-entity mergers, immediate rebranding, common pricing, or identical credit processes. A local core, brand, management structure, or operating process may remain permanently where consolidation would destroy more value than it creates.

### Country posture

- **Nigeria:** scale and resilience-led modernization; retain the local core initially and prioritize payments, fraud, liquidity, USSD, agents, and cash continuity.
- **Kenya:** digital-product reference market; prioritize real-time transaction performance, mobile-money interoperability, agent resilience, partnerships, and controlled experimentation.
- **Morocco:** distinct regulatory, data, language, labor, and relationship-banking program; preserve local execution and likely the local core for longer.

# 1. Target operating model

## 1.1 Group-wide standards

The following should be mandatory across all three entities:

- Enterprise risk appetite and minimum control standards.
- Capital planning, liquidity methodology, and stress testing.
- Cybersecurity architecture, security operations, identity, privileged-access, and incident management.
- Secure software development, vulnerability management, observability, and resilience standards.
- Data classification, ownership, quality, lineage, privacy, retention, and access controls.
- AML/KYC minimum standards, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, and case management.
- Third-party risk, outsourcing, cloud governance, concentration monitoring, audit rights, and exit planning.
- API and integration standards.
- Finance consolidation, management information, and group accounting principles.
- Internal-audit methodology, operational-risk taxonomy, model governance, and business-continuity testing.
- Common digital design patterns and reusable software components.
- Leadership, conduct, succession, and balanced-incentive standards.
- Procurement frameworks and approved-vendor governance.

## 1.2 Locally adaptable capabilities

The following should remain country-owned, subject to group minimum standards:

- Legal entities, boards, licenses, and supervisory relationships.
- Local prudential, tax, accounting, FX, payment, and conduct reporting.
- Payment clearing, switching, settlement, card, mobile-money, telecom, and agent relationships.
- Core ledgers and country-specific accounting or regulatory modules.
- KYC identity integrations and customer-verification requirements.
- Product pricing, eligibility, credit approval, collections, and recovery.
- Branches, ATMs, agents, USSD, cash, and assisted digital channels.
- Customer communications, complaints, remediation, language, and vulnerable-customer support.
- Employment, labor consultation, and workforce practices.
- Government, regulator, payment-network, telecom, and strategic-partner relationships.
- Corporate, SME, affluent, and relationship banking.

Any local variation from a group standard must document:

- Accountable owner.
- Legal or regulatory rationale.
- Risk assessment.
- Compensating control.
- Review date.
- Permanent or expiry decision.

# 2. Governance and decision rights

## 2.1 Integration Management Office

Establish an interim Group Integration Management Office within 10 business days of closing. It should operate for at least the first 36 months and include:

- Group Integration Steering Committee.
- Three Country Integration Committees chaired by the country CEOs.
- Regulatory and legal-entity workstream.
- Technology, data, cyber, and resilience workstreams.
- Risk, AML/KYC, and consumer-protection workstreams.
- Customer continuity and communications office.
- People and culture workstream.
- Finance, treasury, and benefits office.
- Payments and commercial-consolidation workstreams.
- Independent assurance reporting to the Group CRO and Audit Committee.

## 2.2 Governance milestones

| Timing | Required output |
|---|---|
| Day 10 | Interim IMO, accountable executives, critical-service protection, change-control policy |
| Day 30 | Country charters, regulatory register, decision-rights charter, critical-service maps, initial baselines |
| Day 90 | Funded workplans, contingency reserves, architecture principles, data assessments, vendor-risk plans, benefits baseline |
| Day 180 | Operating-model charters, workforce plans, core options analysis, regulatory submission schedule, numerical thresholds, Phase 2 investment cases |

## 2.3 Decision rights

| Decision | Accountable owner |
|---|---|
| Country P&L, workforce, customer continuity, local crisis response | Country CEO |
| Local regulatory engagement | Country CEO and country regulatory head |
| Group minimum controls | Group CRO/CIO or relevant function head |
| Local pricing and credit policy | Country CEO/CRO within group limits |
| Country product launch | Country product owner and CEO |
| Shared-service migration | Group function head with country CEO concurrence |
| Material outsourcing or cloud arrangement | Country board/CEO with group and regulatory review |
| Core or ledger migration | Country board and Group Steering Committee |
| Brand change | Group Board/CEO with country-board and regulatory review |

Approval, consultation, veto, and information rights must be separate. Low-risk releases should be delegated to country product committees under pre-approved standards.

Every material workstream must have a formal RACI covering:

- Executive sponsor.
- Single accountable owner.
- Country owner.
- Delivery lead.
- Budget owner.
- Benefits owner.
- Regulatory owner.
- Control-function approver.
- Independent assurance reviewer.
- Escalation route.
- Decision deadline.

# 3. Strategic roadmap

## Phase 1: Protect and align  
### Q2–Q4 2026

### Objectives

Protect customers, liquidity, licenses, critical services, key people, and regulatory confidence.

### Priorities

- Confirm legal entities, licenses, boards, ownership permissions, and key-person requirements.
- Map supervisory commitments, audit findings, remediation plans, and reporting obligations.
- Reconcile capital, liquidity, provisions, NPLs, collateral, exposures, settlement balances, FX positions, and acquisition-accounting adjustments.
- Inventory systems, interfaces, data stores, vendors, manual workarounds, and critical payment dependencies.
- Maintain branches, ATMs, agents, USSD, cash, cards, apps, call centers, and payment access.
- Retain critical technology, risk, treasury, payments, operations, compliance, and relationship-management staff.
- Establish customer continuity, complaints, remediation, and anti-phishing plans.
- Establish competition and merger-control review.
- Agree country operating-model charters and decision rights.

### Exit criteria

- Every critical service has an accountable owner and tested fallback.
- Major capital, liquidity, data, KYC, technology, vendor, and customer risks have baselines.
- No critical control gap lacks funded remediation.
- Regulatory approval paths are documented.
- Customer communications and remediation processes are operational.
- Local management accountability is clear.

### 2027

### Objectives

Improve control maturity and resilience without forcing local-core or payment-system consolidation.

### Priority investments

1. Cybersecurity monitoring and privileged-access management.
2. Payment, ledger, and settlement reconciliation.
3. Data-quality and customer-master governance.
4. API management and integration inventory.
5. Identity and authentication standards.
6. Fraud intelligence and transaction monitoring.
7. Regulatory-reporting data lineage.
8. Finance consolidation and management reporting.
9. Vendor, cloud, and third-party-risk controls.
10. Local-language content and customer-service capability.
11. Recovery, resolution, separability, and data-portability capability.
12. Critical-role training and knowledge transfer.

### Country focus

- **Nigeria:** payments, settlement, fraud, USSD, agent, cash, and high-volume resilience.
- **Kenya:** mobile-money, real-time transaction performance, consent, partner resilience, and API modernization.
- **Morocco:** bilingual content, local reporting, local records, data controls, and supervisory interfaces.

### Exit criteria

- Service performance is at or above pre-acquisition levels.
- No material integration-driven increase in fraud, complaints, or failed payments.
- Shared layers have owners, costs, service levels, access controls, and exit or permanence decisions.
- Regulatory feedback has been incorporated.

## Phase 3: Controlled product convergence  
### 2028

### Objectives

Deliver common customer experiences and reusable components with country-specific rules.

### Initial product sequence

1. Authentication and security.
2. Notifications and service status.
3. Card controls and replacement.
4. Complaints and service requests.
5. Simple onboarding where local KYC is supported.
6. Payments and transfers.
7. Savings and SME services.
8. Lending, FX, trade finance, and other complex products.

- Security and authentication.
- Accessibility principles.
- Pricing transparency.
- Complaint and remediation standards.
- Common design patterns.
- Reusable APIs and notification services.

**Reusable components**

- Customer profile.
- Account dashboard.
- Card controls.
- Notifications.
- Service requests.
- Complaints and case management.
- Transfer orchestration.
- Savings goals.
- SME collections and payments.

**Country-specific elements**

- KYC and identity.
- Payment rails.
- Currency and FX.
- Fees and taxes.
- Credit eligibility and decisioning.
- Disclosures and legal language.
- Agent, branch, USSD, and cash processes.
- Local reporting and consent requirements.

### Product release gate

Each material release requires:

- Country product owner.
- Local legal, conduct, privacy, AML, fraud, and technology review.
- Language and accessibility testing.
- Payment and settlement dependency assessment.
- Customer testing.
- Pilot population.
- Fallback or containment procedure.
- Customer-remediation owner.
- Reviews at 30, 90, and 180 days.

## Phase 4: Migration decisions and pilots  
### 2028–2029

Core migration is not a group-wide milestone. Each country must decide whether to migrate, defer, or retain its existing core.

### Migration options

1. Wrap and retain the existing core.
2. Migrate individual products.
3. Migrate defined customer cohorts.
4. Run controlled parallel operations.
5. Use a local or regional core instance.
6. Replace the full core only where independently justified.

### Country sequencing

- **Kenya:** first candidate for broader modular migration if the incumbent core constrains real-time services, digital scale, or product agility.
- **Nigeria:** selective product or cohort migration after extensive payment, fraud, liquidity, and volume testing.
- **Morocco:** independent migration or continued local-core operation based on regulatory, data, language, reporting, FX, payment, labor, and commercial evidence.

### Migration gate

No migration may proceed unless:

- Regulatory approval, notification, or non-objection is complete.
- Local payment, FX, tax, accounting, credit, reporting, and product functionality is proven.
- Critical balances meet both value-based and record-level accuracy standards.
- Total deposits, loans, securities, capital, settlement balances, and regulatory records have zero unexplained differences.
- Two successful dress rehearsals are complete.
- Independent reconciliation passes.
- Interest, fees, day-count conventions, tax, accruals, repayment schedules, collateral, limits, mandates, beneficiaries, credit-bureau reporting, dormancy, and complaints are equivalence-tested.
- Peak, payroll, month-end, year-end, partner-outage, degraded-network, and fraud-load scenarios pass.
- Disaster recovery works at realistic volumes.
- Customer, branch, agent, USSD, and call-center fallbacks are ready.
- Critical staff and vendors are trained and available.
- Remediation and compensation funding is reserved.
- The business case remains positive after dual running, localization, fraud, severance, regulatory, remediation, and revenue-loss costs.

“Rollback” must be defined operationally as:

- Pre-activation abort.
- Legacy fallback.
- Compensating ledger entries.
- Manual recovery.
- Customer remediation.
- Regulatory notification.
- Independent investigation.

---

## Phase 5: Rationalize and scale  
### 2029 onward

Rationalization is permitted only after stable post-change performance and verified benefits.

Potential areas include:

- Duplicate middleware and infrastructure.
- Vendors and procurement.
- Finance and HR platforms.
- Shared cyber and fraud operations.
- Selected branches, agents, or channels.
- Brands.
- Legal entities.

No rationalization should proceed solely because a group platform exists. The group must demonstrate that the local cost, risk, and customer functionality being removed have actually been replaced.

# 4. Country execution plans

## Nigeria: scale and resilience-led modernization

### Strategic posture

Use Nigeria as an early digital and control pilot, but not as the first full-core replacement.

### Priorities

- Payment and settlement resilience.
- Fraud and account-takeover reduction.
- USSD, agent, branch, ATM, and cash continuity.
- FX and liquidity protection.
- Local KYC and identity.
- API-led account opening and transfers.
- Correspondent, remittance, cash-logistics, and settlement dependencies.

### Technology

Retain the local core initially. Build APIs, authentication, fraud monitoring, notifications, observability, and reconciliation around it. Migrate only tested products or cohorts.

### Management

Retain strong local commercial leadership. Strengthen group oversight of risk, treasury, technology, cybersecurity, payments, finance, and internal control without displacing local expertise.

### Required controls

- Transaction-level reconciliation between ledger, switch, processor, settlement account, and customer record.
- Conservative cutover limits.
- Enhanced fraud monitoring for at least 90 days after major releases.
- Defined remediation for SIM-swap, account takeover, social engineering, and migration errors.
- Local approval for cross-border data access.
- Language support determined by segment and geography.
- No reduction in branch, agent, USSD, or cash access without customer, inclusion, and regulatory assessment.

---

## Kenya: digital-product reference market

### Strategic posture

Use Kenya as the reference market for modular digital journeys, subject to a high service-quality threshold.

### Priorities

- Mobile-money and telecom interoperability.
- Real-time transaction success.
- Agent liquidity.
- USSD fallback.
- Partner and ecosystem resilience.
- Consent-controlled alternative-data use.
- English and Swahili service.

### Technology

Retain the existing core initially. Consider modular migration only if the current platform constrains real-time services, scale, or product agility.

### Management

Preserve local autonomy in digital partnerships, product management, agent distribution, fintech relationships, and customer propositions. Use delegated authority with defined group-approval turnaround times.

### Required controls

- Minimum-service mode during app, API, telecom, or partner outages.
- Real-time monitoring of failed, delayed, reversed, and duplicate transactions.
- Partner concentration monitoring.
- Defined partner liability for reversals, disputes, outages, and data incidents.
- Bias, explainability, adverse-action, consent, model-drift, override, and kill-switch controls for alternative-data credit.
- Customer and agent testing in English and Swahili.
- Contractual service levels, audit rights, data obligations, and exit arrangements for critical partners.

---

## Morocco: locally governed, bilingual, regulator-led integration

### Strategic posture

Treat Morocco as a distinct regulatory, data, language, labor, and relationship-banking program.

### Priorities

- Bank Al-Maghrib and data-protection alignment.
- Local reporting, FX, tax, and payment continuity.
- French and Arabic execution.
- Relationship-manager retention.
- Local data-processing controls.
- Branch-assisted digital access.

### Technology

Retain the local core and reporting stack until fit with local payments, FX, tax, reporting, data, language, and supervisory requirements is proven. A separate Moroccan or regional core instance may be preferable.

### Management

Retain the local CEO and senior commercial team initially. Use bilingual or French-speaking group leaders and formal local consultation processes.

### Required controls

- Mandatory Arabic right-to-left testing.
- Controlled French-Arabic terminology and disclosure library.
- Named owner for bilingual legal, collections, complaints, and customer-service content.
- Assessment of Amazigh needs by segment and geography.
- Preservation of corporate and affluent relationship coverage.
- Local reporting, FX, tax, payment, and records capabilities.
- Local retention, retrieval, and audit controls.
- No customer release until language, legal, reporting, data, and supervisory sign-off is complete.

# 5. Regulatory and legal-entity engagement

## 5.1 Legal-entity position

Initially preserve:

- Each legal entity and license.
- Local boards and senior-management accountability.
- Customer contracts and account arrangements.
- Payment memberships.
- Local books, records, and regulatory data.
- Local reporting sign-off.

Any legal-entity merger should be a separate decision based on:

- Prudential and licensing implications.
- Capital and liquidity effects.
- Tax and FX consequences.
- Customer contracts.
- Employment and consultation.
- Data and records.
- Payment memberships.
- Competition and market-conduct implications.
- Recovery and resolution.

## 5.2 Supervisory package

Before material technology, outsourcing, data, brand, channel, or legal-entity changes, provide the relevant supervisor with:

- Ownership and control structure.
- Board and management arrangements.
- Capital, liquidity, and risk impact.
- Critical-service and outsourcing maps.
- Target architecture and local overlays.
- Cloud, data-location, and cross-border-access model.
- Cybersecurity and resilience assessment.
- AML/KYC and consumer-protection impact.
- Migration testing, reconciliation, fallback, and remediation.
- Customer and financial-inclusion impact.
- Recovery, resolution, separability, and data-portability arrangements.
- Post-implementation monitoring and independent assurance.

The regulatory register must identify the authority, applicable requirement, approval route, evidence, accountable executive, submission date, response period, dependency, consequence of delay, and closure evidence.

## 5.3 Country regulatory tracks

- **Nigeria:** engage the Central Bank of Nigeria and relevant payments and data-protection authorities on ownership, governance, core and payment changes, outsourcing, cloud, cyber, fraud, consumer protection, AML/KYC, FX, liquidity, settlement, and cross-border data.
- **Kenya:** engage the Central Bank of Kenya, Office of the Data Protection Commissioner, and relevant payment and ecosystem stakeholders on mobile money, telecoms, agents, cloud, outsourcing, consent, alternative data, digital channels, core changes, reversals, disputes, and resilience.
- **Morocco:** maintain a formal calendar with Bank Al-Maghrib, CNDP, and relevant tax, FX, payments, labor, and consumer authorities on governance, outsourcing, data transfers, local records, reporting, bilingual documentation, labor, FX, and continuity.

## 5.4 Competition and market conduct

Establish a separate workstream covering:

- Merger-control and concentration.
- Customer choice and switching.
- Pricing and fee harmonization.
- Branch, agent, ATM, and payment-network rationalization.
- Information-sharing restrictions between separate entities.
- Exclusivity and interoperability.
- Partner access.
- Effects on SMEs, consumers, and financially excluded customers.

The group must not coordinate pricing, customer allocation, or competitively sensitive information between legally separate entities before legally permitted.

# 6. Brand and customer continuity

## 6.1 Brand sequence

### 2026–2027: local continuity

- Retain local brands prominently.
- Add “member of [Group]” endorsement.
- Maintain existing legal-entity names unless properly changed.
- Explain clearly what ownership change means.
- Monitor fake apps, domains, SMS, social-media accounts, and impersonation.
- State that the bank will not request PINs, passwords, or one-time codes.

### 2027–2029: association

Increase group visibility on:

- Cards.
- Digital platforms.
- Cross-border services.
- SME and corporate products.
- Employer branding.
- Group campaigns.

### 2029 onward: selective consolidation

Rebrand only where:

- Customer comprehension and acceptance are positive.
- Service and complaint performance are stable.
- Regulatory, trademark, and licensing clearance is complete.
- Local-language readiness is proven.
- Fraud and impersonation controls are operating.
- The financial case is positive after cards, signage, applications, forms, communications, and remediation.
- No simultaneous core migration or legal-entity merger is planned.

Planning windows:

- Nigeria: local brand through stabilization.
- Kenya: endorsed or dual brand for approximately 18–24 months.
- Morocco: local-first endorsed brand for at least 24–36 months, potentially longer.

## 6.2 Customer continuity commitments

- Preserve account access, balances, cards, mandates, beneficiaries, payment instructions, and complaint history.
- Provide advance notice of changes to fees, terms, account numbers, privacy practices, or contracting entities.
- Maintain assisted channels during digital migration.
- Provide multilingual and accessible communications.
- Establish priority support for vulnerable customers, rural customers, SMEs, payroll clients, corporates, and affluent customers.
- Correct erroneous, duplicated, delayed, or failed transactions promptly.
- Provide compensation or fee relief for qualifying integration-caused harm.
- Maintain daily monitoring during cutovers and 30-, 60-, and 90-day post-change reviews.

# 12. Success measures

Country baselines and numerical thresholds must be approved by the country boards and Group Risk Committee by Q4 2026.

## Customer

- Segment-specific attrition within approved thresholds.
- Failed, delayed, reversed, and duplicate transactions within defined limits.
- Critical-service availability at or above pre-acquisition levels.
- Maximum time to restore access to funds.
- Complaint resolution and recurrence within defined limits.
- Customer trust recovery within six months of major releases.
- No material reduction in rural, vulnerable, or financially excluded access.
- Compensation and remediation completed within defined deadlines.

## Technology and resilience

- 100% inventory of critical systems, data stores, interfaces, and vendors.
- Zero overdue critical vulnerabilities.
- Successful recovery testing for critical services.
- Zero unexplained balance-sheet or settlement differences after migration.
- Reconciliation exceptions within defined aging limits.
- API latency and error rates within thresholds.
- End-to-end transaction success within target.
- Declining duplicate platforms and interfaces.
- Tested separability of critical shared services.

## Risk and regulatory

- No material integration-caused regulatory breach.
- Accurate and timely regulatory submissions.
- KYC remediation within approved thresholds.
- AML and sanctions cases within target aging.
- Fraud and account-takeover losses within risk appetite.
- Capital and liquidity buffers above local requirements.
- Material outsourcing, cloud, data, and payment arrangements approved or documented.
- Shared fraud and credit models independently validated, monitored, and disableable.
- No high-risk audit or supervisory issue without funded remediation.

## Financial

- Benefits validated net of transition costs.
- Revenue retention across priority segments.
- Stable or improving country cost-to-income.
- Positive risk-adjusted return on major migration investments.
- Legacy systems retired only when their full run cost is removed.
- No benefit counted where cost or risk is transferred to a subsidiary.
- Commercial growth achieved without competition, conduct, or inclusion breaches.

## People

- Retention of critical technology, risk, treasury, payments, AML, operations, and relationship staff.
- Succession coverage for key roles.
- Knowledge-transfer completion.
- Employee engagement recovery within 12–18 months.
- Declining decision-rights disputes.
- Completion of cyber, conduct, product, and integration training.
- Workforce consultation completed within local legal requirements.

## Assurance

Every material release or migration receives independent reviews at:

- **30 days:** incidents, customer harm, reconciliation, fraud, and service stability.
- **90 days:** control effectiveness, complaints, remediation, staff readiness, and benefits.
- **180 days:** sustained performance, platform-retirement decision, regulatory commitments, and lessons learned.

---

# Final recommendation

Approve the integration program only on a **country-specific, stage-gated basis**, subject to:

1. Management alignment and decision rights being implemented during 2026.
2. Country-specific regulatory, data, competition, labor, legal-entity, and payment assessments.
3. Funded business cases with contingency and failure/remediation reserves.
4. Independent validation of customer, data, payment, liquidity, AML/KYC, and migration readiness.
5. Benefits quantified net of dual running, localization, remediation, fraud, severance, and lost revenue.
6. Recovery, resolution, separability, and data-portability plans for critical shared services.
7. No simultaneous core migration, legal-entity merger, and major rebrand.
8. Numerical customer-protection, resilience, inclusion, and financial thresholds.
9. Formal post-implementation reviews at 30, 90, and 180 days.
10. Explicit acceptance that local cores, brands, management structures, and operating processes may remain permanently where they are the risk-adjusted optimum.

The strategic rule is:

> **Standardize controls and reusable capabilities; preserve local accountability, payment ecosystems, languages, data arrangements, customer access, and relationship models; and consolidate only when customer, supervisory, resilience, competition, and economic evidence is positive.**
