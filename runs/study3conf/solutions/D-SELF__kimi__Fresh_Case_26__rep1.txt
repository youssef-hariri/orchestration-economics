**THE ROOTS FRAMEWORK**
**Global Cultural & Faith-Based Campaign Governance**
*Respect · Ownership · Observation · Trust · Stewardship*

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The ROOTS Framework replaces top-down global campaign templates with a **co-creation-with-veto** system. It embeds cultural, sacred, and legal authority directly into the approval chain—not as an external check, but as a structural power center.

The objective is not risk avoidance through bland universalism, but **deep resonance through localized truth**, managed within a rapid-response architecture built for cross-border social velocity. This framework governs all seasonal campaigns tied to Ramadan, Lunar New Year, Diwali, and related occasions across Gulf, North African, Southeast Asian, South Asian, Sub-Saharan African, East Asian, Indo-Caribbean, and diaspora markets.

Key structural commitments:
*   **75% local budget autonomy** and hard creative veto for local agencies
*   **Five-tier approval gates** including faith authorities, cultural integrity leads, and community partners with disavowal rights
*   **72-hour “Wartime Footing”** and platform-specific crisis protocols for cross-border scrutiny
*   **Mandatory IP co-ownership** and supply-chain equity with community partners

Cultural credibility is treated as a balance sheet built across fiscal years, not a seasonal line item.

## 1. CORE PRINCIPLES

These principles are non-negotiable and supersede standard brand guidelines during cultural campaign development.

### 1.1 Guest Positioning & Cultural Humility
[Brand] enters cultural and sacred moments as a guest, not a host. We earn the right to participate by adding value to community practice.

*   **No Theological Centrism:** The brand never explains the “meaning” of Ramadan, Diwali, or Lunar New Year to the communities that live them. We reflect practice; we do not annotate it.
*   **Product Subordination:** The food product is a companion to the occasion, never the hero. The narrative center belongs to the family, the ritual, and the faith practice.
*   **Acknowledge Power Asymmetry:** A multinational brand with supply-chain leverage is never a powerless guest. We actively counter extraction through fair IP sharing, supply-chain equity, and community economic benefit.

### 1.2 Sacred Sensitivity Spectrum
Rather than imposing a false sacred/secular binary, we recognize a spectrum of sacred intensity.

*   **Ramadan** is a theological obligation and demands the highest sacred sensitivity. It is also profoundly cultural, shaped by national cuisine, sonic identity, and civic ritual. Campaigns must respect Islamic ethics without reducing diverse Muslim life to a single Arab or jurisprudential template.
*   **Lunar New Year and Diwali** are not “merely cultural.” For many, they are deeply religious (Tết as ancestor veneration; Diwali as *Lakshmi puja* or, for Sikhs, *Bandi Chhor Divas*; for Jains, Mahavira’s nirvana; for Tibetan Buddhists, Losar as spiritually distinct). They require religious-literacy review to avoid commodifying sacred symbols.

### 1.3 Co-Creation Over Consultation
Consultation invites feedback; co-creation shares power, budget, and intellectual property. Local cultural practitioners, theologians, chefs, and everyday families must be in the room at **concept genesis**, not final review, and compensated at creative-partner rates.

*   **Community Creative Partners:** Every campaign credits named community partners (chefs, artisans, faith leaders, historians) in visible brand assets.
*   **Right of Disavowal:** If the brand overrides a partner’s material cultural objection, the partner may withdraw their name from credits and issue a public disavowal without contractual penalty.
*   **Independent Cultural Review (ICR):** If two or more Community Creative Circle (CCC) members dissent, they may jointly trigger binding mediation by an external cultural ombudsperson, who delivers a written determination within 72 hours.

### 1.4 Hyper-Localism Over Pan-Regionalism
There is no single “Muslim world,” “Asian market,” or “Diwali aesthetic.”

*   **Sub-national disaggregation is mandatory** where internal diversity is high: Indonesia (Javanese, Minangkabau, Acehnese, Bugis); Nigeria (Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo); India (region, caste, class).
*   Lunar New Year creative for Mandarin-speaking communities will fail in Vietnam (Tết) if color palettes, floral motifs, or zodiac interpretations are transposed without fundamental rewrite.
*   Diwali in Mumbai, Kuala Lumpur, Leicester, and Trinidad operate in distinct sonic, visual, culinary, and post-colonial registers.

### 1.5 Year-Round Stewardship
Cultural credibility is a balance sheet, not a quarterly line item. Communities must see [Brand] invested in local food systems, religious charities, and cultural preservation **outside** commercial peaks.

### 1.6 Sacred Semiotics, Acoustics, and Aniconism
*   **Acoustic Sanctity:** Never use the *adhan*, Quranic recitation, temple bells, *mantras*, or Buddhist sutras as sonic branding or transition texture.
*   **Aniconism:** Never depict prophets, divine names, or mosque interiors as set dressing. Deity imagery (*murti*) never appears on packaging or casual creative without explicit authorization.
*   **Script Integrity:** Sacred/cultural scripts must not be used as aesthetic texture by non-readers. All script requires native copywriter approval.

### 1.7 Calendar Humility
Ramadan, Eid, and Lunar New Year dates depend on lunar observation. Campaigns must use **modular asset architecture** with flexible greetings and never assert a specific date before official market moonsighting.

### 1.8 Structural Humility & Economic Equity
*   **Fair IP & Recipe Protection:** Co-ownership or licensed moral-rights protections are mandatory for community partners contributing recipes, designs, or narratives. Flat-fee buyouts are prohibited.
*   **Supply-Chain Visibility:** Campaigns celebrating a cultural cuisine must verify that sourcing benefits communities in that cuisine’s region of origin.

### 1.9 Red Line Non-Participation
Default non-participation applies where commercial engagement cannot safely navigate political or spiritual risk:
*   **Tibet (Losar):** Participation requires Board-level exception and independent human-rights due diligence.
*   Any market under active genocide designation or where religious expression is criminalized and brand presence would legitimize state narrative.

## 2. REGIONAL ADAPTATION & AGENCY GOVERNANCE

### 2.1 The “Reverse Brief” Model
Global provides brand guardrails (tone, nutritional claims, visual identity, legal requirements). **Local agencies write the cultural brief and hold the right of first refusal.** If a global concept cannot be executed authentically in a region, the region may reject the global chassis entirely and originate locally—without budget penalty.

### 2.2 Regional Agency Sovereignty Charter

| Global Role | Local Agency Role | Veto Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Brand coherence, nutritional/legal compliance, media buying scale | Cultural strategy, creative concept, casting, production, local media nuance | **Local agency holds creative veto on cultural execution.** Global may not override without joint sign-off from CMO + Regional President + Global Chief Culture & Ethics Officer (GCCEO). |

*   **Indigenous Creative Leadership:** Local agency creative leadership must include practitioners from the target culture, or it must subcontract verified community creative partners.
*   **Budget Autonomy:** Minimum 75% of seasonal campaign production budget is controlled locally.
*   **No Visual Frankensteining:** Global cannot splice local footage into pan-regional edits without re-approval by the originating local agency and its Regional Cultural Integrity Lead (RCIL).
*   **Matrix Row ≠ Brief:** The reference matrices below are starting points only. Every market requires a standalone, disaggregated cultural brief approved by the RCIL.

### 2.5 Transcreation & Linguistic Standards
*   **Script Lock:** Taglines must be written in-market by native copywriters. English-to-local translation as a starting point is prohibited.
*   **Dialect Precision:** Gulf Arabic (*Khaliji*), Egyptian, Maghrebi Darija, Turkish, Farsi, Urdu, Swahili, Somali, Wolof, Hausa, Tamil, and Bengali each require distinct authorship. Indonesian and Malay are not interchangeable.
*   **Color & Symbol Audit:** Mandatory semiotics review for every visual element. Gold signals prosperity in Chinese contexts but mourning in some Asian funerary traditions; green is sacred in Islam but carries political valence in South Asian minority contexts.

### 2.6 Talent & Casting Protocols
*   **Local Casting Mandate:** No pan-ethnic substitution (e.g., Levantine models for Maghrebi roles, East Asian models for Southeast Asian roles, North Indians for Tamil Malaysian roles).
*   **Muslim Identity Is Not Racial:** Casting must reflect local racial diversity (Black African, Southeast Asian, white/European Muslim communities) and avoid equating “Muslim” with a single Middle Eastern phenotype.
*   **Real Families, Not Archetypes:** Prioritize real community members over actors to capture micro-interaction authenticity.

## 3. MULTI-TIER APPROVAL SAFEGUARD STRUCTURE

### 3.1 Governance Architecture
The traditional “global approves local” model is replaced by a **pentagonal check system**, distributing authority to those with cultural, sacred, and legal proximity. The GCCEO sits outside the commercial P&L chain to adjudicate escalations.

### 3.2 The Five-Tier Checkpoint System

| Tier | Role | Authority | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TIER 1** | **Community Creative Circle (CCC):** 3–5 external community members per market (cooks, historians, practitioners) | Advisory with teeth; names on credits; Right of Disavowal; ICR trigger | Week 2 (concept), Week 6 (script), Week 10 (final cut) |
| **TIER 2** | **Regional Cultural Integrity Lead (RCIL):** Senior in-market employee from the target community; reports dotted-line to GCCEO, not commercial P&L | **Hard veto** on cultural grounds; override requires Regional President + CMO + GCCEO | All checkpoints |
| **TIER 3** | **Faith & Sacred Review Board (FSRB):** Contracted scholars per jurisdiction (Hanafi, Ja‘fari, Maliki, Shafi‘i, Hanbali, Alevi/Sufi; plus Hindu, Sikh, Jain, Buddhist, ancestor-veneration specialists) | **Theological veto** on faith-based errors; binding decisions | Week 6 (script/storyboard), Week 10 (final cut) |
| **TIER 4** | **Local Agency Sovereignty Signatory (LASS):** Creative director of retained local agency | **Execution veto** if global “brand consistency” edits erase cultural nuance | Week 8 (pre-production), Week 12 (final delivery) |
| **TIER 5** | **Global Brand Guardian (GBG):** Global VP Brand + **Regional Legal Counsel (RLC)** | GBG: brand safety/consistency only; cannot override Tiers 1–4 on cultural grounds. RLC: independent legal/regulatory block on local advertising law, religious expression statutes, and cross-border liability | Week 12 (final checkpoint only) |

**RCIL Selection Protocol:** An external accreditation body or established community council provides a shortlist; final appointment is made by the GCCEO plus an independent ethics advisor. Term is three years, renewable once, with annual conflict-of-interest disclosure.

**FSRB Appeals Pathway:** If a theological veto is disputed, a second FSRB member provides a written counter-argument to the GCCEO, who renders a final binding decision.

### 3.3 Approval Checkpoints & Deliverables

| Checkpoint | Timing | Required Sign-Offs | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cultural Brief Gate** | Week 2 | RCIL + CCC + LASS | Confirm cultural legibility before creative begins |
| **Concept Greenlight** | Week 4 | RCIL + LASS + CCC + **Red Team** | Validate resonance with lived community truth |
| **Script/Semiotics Lock** | Week 6 | RCIL + FSRB (if faith-based) + LASS + Native Copywriter | Audit language, symbols, and casting descriptions |
| **Pre-Production Clearance** | Week 8 | RCIL + LASS + Casting Director | Lock casting, locations, and wardrobe |
| **Rough Cut Review** | Week 10 | RCIL + CCC + FSRB (if faith-based) + LASS | Check pacing, sound design, and micro-gestures |
| **Red Team Stress Test** | Week 11 | RCIL + Crisis Comms Lead + Diaspora Liaison | Simulate cross-border backlash; finalize holding statements |
| **Final Release Authority** | Week 12 | RCIL + LASS + GBG + **Regional Legal Counsel** | Final legal, brand, and cultural sign-off |

### 3.4 Concept-Stage Red Teaming
At **Week 4 (Concept Greenlight)**, a Red Team of diaspora members from non-target markets reviews the creative platform to anticipate cross-border misreadings **before production spend is committed**. A second, lighter Red Team reviews the rough cut at Week 11 for platform virality and subtitle/meme risks.

### 3.5 Escalation Path Matrix

| Scenario | First Escalation | Final Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Local agency disputes global brand edit | LASS → RCIL → GCCEO mediation | CMO + Regional President + GCCEO |
| FSRB theological veto disputed | Second FSRB opinion → GCCEO | GCCEO (binding) |
| CCC triggers ICR | Ombudsperson (72 hours) | GCCEO |
| RLC legal block | RLC + Regional President | CEO (if commercial/legal conflict) |
| Level 3+ social crisis | Crisis Comms Lead + RCIL | CEO + Board (for Level 4 / Red Withdraw) |

### 3.6 IP & Community Partner Protection
*   **ROOTS IP Co-Ownership Template:** Mandates revenue-sharing or licensing with moral-rights protections. Flat-fee buyouts are prohibited.
*   **Credit Visibility:** Partners named in final assets and press materials.
*   **Withdrawal Rights:** Partners may withdraw name and publicly disavow if material objections are overridden.

## 4. CROSS-BORDER SOCIAL MEDIA SCRUTINY & RAPID BACKLASH PROTOCOLS

Social media collapses geographic borders. A campaign running only in Malaysia will be screenshotted, subtitled, and critiqued in Pakistan, Turkey, and London within hours. This protocol assumes scrutiny is inevitable and prepares for velocity.

### 4.1 The 72-Hour Critical Window
All cultural campaigns enter **“Wartime Footing”** for the first 72 hours post-launch. Pre-authorized response teams act within defined thresholds without waiting for standard approval hierarchies.

### 4.2 Cross-Border Sentiment Monitoring: The “Cultural Radar”

**War Room Composition**
*   Global crisis lead + regional social leads + **native-language, native-speaker moderators** (not translation software): Gulf/Maghreb Arabic, Egyptian Arabic, Levantine Arabic, Farsi, Turkish, Bahasa Indonesia/Malay, Mandarin, Vietnamese, Korean, Mongolian, Hindi, Tamil, Bengali, Wolof, Hausa, Somali, Swahili, and English.

**Platform-Specific Playbooks**
*   **TikTok / Instagram / Reels:** Visual meme analysis and stitch/duplication tracking. Priority: influencer narrative formation.
*   **X/Twitter:** Diaspora amplification tracking and hashtag co-occurrence. Priority: journalist and activist pickup.
*   **WhatsApp / Telegram:** Public channel and large-group forwarding monitoring via third-party civic-tech partnerships. Priority: encrypted virality in African, South Asian, and Southeast Asian markets.
*   **WeChat (PRC):** Monitor official account commentary; flag state-mediated suppression versus organic criticism. Do not treat silence as approval.
*   **YouTube:** Comment-section theological critique and subtitle misinterpretation.

**Shift System:** 24/7 coverage for 72 hours; 12-hour coverage for Days 4–14.

### 4.3 Backlash Severity Matrix

| Level | Indicator | Code | Response Clock | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Level 1: Micro-Critique** | Single posts, low engagement, niche commentary | **AMBER WATCH** | Monitor; log for learning | No corporate action. Log in cultural learning repository. |
| **Level 2: Trending Negative** | Hashtag formation, engagement >10k, regional influencer critique | **AMBER ACTIVE** | Acknowledge within 6h; contextual response within 24h | Authorized community managers use **ELVS** tone: Empathy, Listening, Validation, Specificity. No corporate statement. |
| **Level 3: Viral Backlash** | Mainstream media pickup, boycott hashtags, cross-border diaspora amplification | **RED PAUSE** | Campaign paused/regionally darkened within 2h; substantive response within 6h | Local MD or RCIL issues local-language video/message. Explain local source; commit to FSRB/CCC review within 48h. |
| **Level 4: Theological/Deep Offense** | Faith authorities issue condemnations; political figures engage; physical protest risk | **RED WITHDRAW** | Immediate global takedown; CMO statement within 4h; restorative action within 48h | Global takedown. Active-voice CMO apology. Community investment/restitution vetted by offended leaders. |

### 4.4 Manufactured Backlash & Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior (CIB)
The Cultural Radar must distinguish authentic community pain from coordinated disinformation.

*   **Detection Signals:** Sudden hashtag uniformity from accounts with no prior cultural engagement; identical phrasing across languages within minutes; bot-network amplification; state-affiliated media pickup originating outside the target market.
*   **Response:** Do not engage publicly with bad-faith accounts. Activate Diaspora Liaisons to issue contextual clarification through trusted community channels. Issue a single factual holding statement correcting misinformation. Escalate to platform trust-and-safety teams for network investigation.

### 4.5 Response Playbook by Severity

**Level 1–2: Contextual Engagement**
*   Authorized local community managers respond individually.
*   Example: *“We hear you. Our Gulf campaign was shaped by [Named Local Partner], but we recognize that Muslim practice is beautifully diverse. We’re listening to how this landed with you.”*

**Level 3: Coordinated Pause & Explain**
*   Pause regional paid media; halt global organic posting for 24 hours to prevent noise.
*   Local Managing Director or RCIL (not global CEO) issues video/message in local language.
*   Do not defend. Explain the local cultural source and commit to review.

**Level 4: Global Withdrawal & Restorative Accountability**
*   Immediate global takedown of all assets.
*   Global CMO issues statement in active voice: *“We caused offense by [specific action].”*
*   Beyond apology, announce tangible community investment (e.g., food security NGO funding, scholarships for cultural practitioners) vetted by offended community leaders so it does not read as “hush money.”

### 4.6 Regional Voice & Global Coordination
*   **No Global Translation of Local Apologies:** If the offense occurred in Indonesia, the apology is released in Bahasa Indonesia first, by the Indonesia MD. It is not translated into Arabic or English for efficiency. Other regions may acknowledge: *“We see the conversation happening in Indonesia and are supporting their review.”*
*   **Diaspora Liaisons:** Maintain relationships with diaspora community leaders in the UK, US, Canada, Australia, and Gulf hubs. When a campaign targets Jakarta but offends London, a pre-briefed London leader receives a private explanatory call before public escalation.

### 4.7 Resource Scaling & Surge Logic
Overlapping seasonal windows may strain war-room capacity.
*   **Tiered Staffing:** Maintain a core Cultural Radar team of 4 FTEs. Surge to 12 FTEs during active campaign windows via contracted native-speaker moderators.
*   **Market Prioritization:** If concurrent launches occur, prioritize 24/7 coverage by sacred intensity and market size. Ramadan faith-based campaigns receive first call on Arabic, Farsi, and Bahasa moderators; Tết receives Mandarin/Vietnamese/Korean; Diwali receives Hindi/Tamil/Bengali.
*   **Shared Services:** Pool English-language diaspora monitoring across campaigns to avoid redundant UK/US coverage.

### 4.8 Post-Crisis Learning Loop
Within 14 days of any Level 3+ event:
1.  **Community Listening Session:** Closed-door session with critics (not fans), moderated externally.
2.  **Public After-Action Review:** Publish anonymized learnings to brand channels.
3.  **Framework Amendment:** Update the ROOTS Framework with the specific failure mode.
4.  **Accountability Log:** Non-anonymized internal record maintained by the GCCEO tracking veto overrides and responsible executives.

### 5.3 Success Metrics

|---|---|---|

| **Backlash Recovery Time** | Hours from Level 3 trigger to substantive brand response | < 6 hours |

## CLOSING PROTOCOL NOTE

This framework intentionally redistributes power away from global marketing headquarters toward local cultural agents. It will feel slower and more expensive than centralized production. That friction is a feature, not a bug—it is the cost of entry into communities whose trust, once lost to shallow representation, cannot be rebuilt in a single fiscal year.

**Prepared for:** C-Suite Leadership, Regional Marketing Leads, Global Chief Culture & Ethics Officer  
**Next Step:** Legal, theological, and market-level operational refinement of IP templates and FSRB contracts.
