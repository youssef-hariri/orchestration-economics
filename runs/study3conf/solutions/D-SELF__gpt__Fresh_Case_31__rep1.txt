# Global Compensation Policy  
## Decision-Ready Framework for a 30-Country Remote Workforce

## Executive decision

Adopt a **globally consistent, locally adaptable compensation framework** that:

- Prices the job and level first, with geography as a bounded labor-market factor;
- Uses three to five geographic labor-market zones rather than ad hoc city-by-city pricing;
- Relies on current, role-matched, confidence-rated market data;
- Establishes consistent rules for ranges, hiring, promotions, mobility, exceptions, and appeals;
- Provides meaningful transparency while protecting personal data;
- Reviews pay equity, compression, market competitiveness, and legal compliance regularly;
- Prohibits automatic salary reductions solely because an employee relocates;
- Requires country-specific legal, payroll, tax, privacy, immigration, and employee-representative validation before implementation.

The company will not equalize salaries globally. It will ensure that geographic differences are evidence-based, bounded, explainable, consistently applied, and reviewable.

**Mandatory local law, employment contracts, collective agreements, consultation requirements, and employee rights take precedence over this global policy.**

---

# 1. Policy hierarchy

Compensation will be administered through four layers:

### Global standards

Consistent company-wide principles covering:

- Job architecture and leveling;
- Compensation philosophy;
- Market-data governance;
- Geographic methodology;
- Range design and placement;
- Hiring, promotion, and mobility principles;
- Exception and appeal controls;
- Pay-equity and compression monitoring;
- Anti-retaliation;
- Privacy and records management.

### Geographic-zone rules

Rules covering:

- Zone definitions and boundaries;
- Market-positioning targets;
- Geographic-spread limits;
- Compensation-location rules;
- Boundary cases and reclassification.

### Country addenda

Local requirements covering:

- Pay-range disclosure;
- Employee information and comparator rights;
- Pay-gap reporting;
- Consultation or bargaining;
- Wage-payment and contract rules;
- Statutory benefits;
- Privacy and data transfers;
- Equity, payroll, tax, immigration, and social security.

### Individual exceptions

Approved departures from the framework for documented business, legal, or equity reasons. Exceptions may not become an informal alternative pay system.

A maintained decision matrix will specify which matters are global, zone-based, country-specific, or individually approvable.

# 2. Compensation principles

## 2.1 Pay for the job first, location second

Pay is primarily determined by:

- Job family;
- Career level;
- Scope and accountability;
- Relevant skills and experience;
- Sustained performance;
- Scarcity or strategic importance.

Geography is a legitimate but bounded factor. It must not be used as a proxy for nationality, race, ethnicity, gender, immigration status, or another protected characteristic.

## 2.2 Comparable work should receive comparable pay

Global levels support consistency but do not conclusively determine legal comparability. Actual duties, responsibility, qualifications, working conditions, local classifications, and collective agreements must also be considered.

## 2.3 Use cost of labor, not household cost of living

The company will separately govern:

| Objective | Measure |
|---|---|
| Hiring and retention | Relevant local labor-market compensation |
| Purchasing-power support | Separate allowance or benefit, if approved |
| Currency stability | Exchange-rate methodology |
| General economic movement | Inflation and local wage data |

Cost-of-living data will not be embedded silently into base salary ranges.

## 2.4 Transparency must be meaningful and privacy-preserving

Employees and candidates should understand:

- How roles are leveled;
- How ranges are set;
- How location affects pay;
- How pay progresses;
- How concerns can be raised.

The company will not routinely disclose individual employee salaries or identifiable comparator information.

## 2.5 Market data is an input, not an automatic answer

Market data must be balanced against:

- Internal comparators;
- Pay equity;
- Compression and inversion;
- Data confidence;
- Hiring and retention outcomes;
- Affordability;
- Legal and collective requirements.

## 2.6 No retaliation

Employees may raise pay concerns, discuss compensation where legally protected, request explanations, participate in audits or consultations, and contact authorized representatives or regulators without retaliation.

# 3. Job architecture and leveling

The company will maintain global job families, including engineering, data, security, product, design, sales, marketing, customer success, finance, legal, people, operations, and executive leadership.

Global levels may include:

- Individual contributor: IC1–IC7;
- Management: M1–M6;
- Executive: E1–E5.

Local titles may differ, but level expectations will be globally defined. Each level profile will address:

- Scope;
- Complexity;
- Autonomy;
- Decision-making;
- Technical or functional expectations;
- Leadership expectations;
- Promotion criteria.

Every employee and open role must have:

- A job family;
- A level;
- A documented role profile;
- An employing entity;
- A compensation location;
- An approved range.

Managers may not create informal levels, use title changes to bypass ranges, or materially alter role scope without Compensation review.

# 4. Geographic compensation model

## 4.1 Compensation location

The default compensation location is the employee’s **legally authorized primary work and payroll location**, subject to local law and contract terms.

The following do not automatically change compensation location:

- Short-term travel;
- A manager’s location;
- Nationality or hiring origin;
- A second residence;
- Informal work from another country.

Cross-border commuting, multiple residences, employer-of-record arrangements, and long-term temporary work require review by HR, Compensation, Payroll, Tax, Immigration, and Legal.

Employees may not artificially select a location or arrangement solely to obtain a more favorable range.

## 4.2 Geographic zones

The company will use three to five labor-market zones, such as:

- **Zone A:** Premium technology labor markets;
- **Zone B:** Established, highly competitive technology markets;
- **Zone C:** Competitive distributed markets;
- **Zone D:** Emerging or lower-cost technology markets.

A country will generally have one national range. City-level differentiation requires reliable, role-specific evidence and central approval.

Zone boundaries will be documented and reviewed using:

- Technology labor-market data;
- Talent availability;
- Recruiting and retention evidence;
- Neighboring-market comparisons;
- Internal comparators;
- Administrative feasibility.

Zone reclassification requires advance notice, impact analysis, and a transition plan.

## 4.3 Pricing method

The primary method is:

> **Local range midpoint = Matched local-market benchmark × approved market-positioning factor**

A global reference currency may be used for reporting and analysis, but it is a notional analytical reference, not an automatic salary-conversion formula or employee entitlement.

## 4.4 Market positioning

Initial targets are:

| Population | Target positioning |
|---|---|
| General corporate roles | Approximately local market median |
| Core engineering, infrastructure, and security | Median to approximately 60th percentile |
| Highly scarce or strategic roles | Higher positioning with documented approval |
| Early-career roles | Competitive median with development opportunity |
| Executives | Benchmark-based total compensation under executive governance |

The company must define whether each benchmark reflects base salary, total cash, total compensation, incumbent pay, or new-hire pay.

Statutory benefits and employer contributions must be shown separately and may not be used to conceal base-pay inequity.

## 4.5 Geographic spread guardrail

As an initial governance standard, the ordinary base-salary midpoint difference between zones for the same job family and level should not exceed **25%**, measured as:

> **(Higher midpoint − lower midpoint) ÷ lower midpoint**

A wider spread requires:

- Current, role-specific market evidence;
- Internal comparator analysis;
- Pay-equity assessment;
- Hiring and retention evidence;
- Affordability analysis;
- Legal review where relevant;
- Compensation Governance Committee approval;
- A defined review date.

The Committee must validate the 25% threshold through modeling of the relevant markets, job families, levels, recruitment outcomes, payroll cost, compression, and employee sentiment. The threshold is a governance starting point, not an economic or legal conclusion.

# 5. Market-data governance

## Approved sources

The company may use:

- Technology-specific salary surveys;
- Country and regional benchmarks;
- Internal accepted-offer data;
- Retention and attrition evidence;
- Recruiting intelligence;
- Competitor postings, used cautiously;
- Local wage and labor-market indicators.

Public salary forums, recruiter claims, and job postings may identify review signals but may not determine an individual employee’s pay or be entered into an employee’s compensation record unless independently verified, lawfully obtained, and relevant.

## Benchmark standards

Each benchmark must record:

- Source and provider;
- Survey and effective dates;
- Geographic coverage;
- Target employer population;
- Job-match quality;
- Sample size;
- Percentile basis;
- Currency and exchange-rate convention;
- Incumbent versus new-hire population;
- Treatment of bonus, equity, and benefits;
- Known limitations;
- Confidence rating.

Data should generally be no more than 18 months old for ordinary range-setting, but age will be assessed with market volatility, source quality, sample size, and job-match quality. Older data may be used with a lower confidence rating and documented approval.

Where data is sparse, conflicting, or dominated by contractors, the company will use broader ranges, fewer geographic distinctions, greater central oversight, and a data-improvement plan.

Ranges will be reviewed annually and sooner if triggered by:

- Sustained market movement;
- Material hiring failure;
- Significant regretted attrition;
- Currency or inflation disruption;
- Major geographic expansion;
- New legal requirements;
- Repeated exceptions;
- Material business-strategy change.

# 6. Ranges and pay decisions

## 6.1 Range structure

Each range must specify:

- Minimum;
- Midpoint;
- Maximum;
- Local currency;
- Effective date;
- Job family and level;
- Country or zone;
- Market-positioning target;
- Confidence rating;
- Treatment of employees outside the range.

Ranges must be sufficiently broad to recognize legitimate differences but narrow enough to limit unexplained discretion.

## 6.2 Range placement

General placement guidance:

- **Lower portion:** Developing capability, new to level, or limited relevant experience;
- **Middle:** Fully proficient and consistently performing at level;
- **Upper portion:** Deep expertise, broad scope, sustained exceptional contribution, or scarce skills.

Managers must use approved rationale codes. “Culture fit,” “market fit,” or manager judgment alone is not sufficient justification for a material difference.

## 6.3 Hiring

Before an offer is approved, the record must include:

1. Job family and level;
2. Compensation location;
3. Applicable range and range version;
4. Proposed range position;
5. Relevant experience and skills;
6. Internal comparators;
7. Scarcity evidence;
8. Bonus, equity, and sign-on treatment;
9. Required candidate disclosures;
10. Any exception request.

Approval is required for:

- Offers above the ordinary hiring zone;
- Offers materially above comparable incumbents;
- Scarcity premiums;
- Out-of-range offers;
- Material sign-on or retention awards.

Scarcity premiums must have a review or sunset date.

As a global company standard, salary history will not be requested or used, subject to local implementation review.

## 6.4 Promotions

Promotion decisions must be based on demonstrated scope and contribution, not tenure alone.

A promotion should generally:

- Place the employee within the new range;
- Meet the new range minimum unless an approved exception applies;
- Follow a standard placement or increase guideline;
- Be coordinated explicitly with the annual merit cycle.

The company will use a recommended promotion outcome of placement at the new range minimum or a meaningful increase appropriate to the level and range position. An 8% increase may be used as a planning guideline, not an absolute entitlement, and remains subject to local law, collective agreements, and range position.

Employees already above the new range may receive a base-pay hold, lump sum, equity adjustment, or other approved treatment.

# 7. Currency, inflation, and total rewards

## Currency

For reporting:

- Use one approved exchange-rate source;
- Define spot, annual-average, or effective-date conventions;
- Report both local-currency and converted values;
- Do not mix conventions within an analysis.

For employee pay:

- Pay in the legally required or contractually specified currency;
- Do not automatically adjust base pay for short-term exchange-rate movement;
- Review material sustained currency disruption quarterly.

## Inflation and local wage movement

Inflation, general wage growth, technology-market movement, and currency changes will be analyzed separately. High-inflation markets may receive a range review, temporary allowance, or targeted adjustment. Automatic inflation indexing will not be promised unless approved, funded, and legally permissible.

## Total rewards

The company will maintain separate definitions for:

- Base salary;
- Target and actual bonus;
- Equity grant value;
- Current equity value;
- Employer contributions;
- Statutory benefits;
- Discretionary benefits;
- Paid leave;
- Pension;
- Health coverage;
- Cash allowances;
- Sales commissions.

Employee, contractor, EOR, intern, part-time, and contingent-worker compensation will not be compared without normalizing legal status, taxes, benefits, working time, and other material differences.

---

# 8. Mobility and relocation

Separate rules apply to:

- Employer-requested relocation;
- Employee-requested permanent relocation;
- Temporary work from another country;
- Business travel;
- Cross-border commuting;
- In-country moves;
- International moves.

## Employer-requested relocation

The company will generally preserve base pay and provide required relocation support, subject to local law, contracts, tax, payroll, immigration, and employee-representative requirements.

## Employee-requested relocation

A move may result in prospective reassignment to a different market range only after:

- Legal and contractual review;
- Payroll and tax review;
- Immigration confirmation;
- Advance notice;
- Consideration of a transition period;
- Written explanation.

As a global company standard, the company will not automatically reduce base salary following relocation. Any proposed reduction requires prior review by HR, Compensation, Legal, Payroll, Tax, and applicable representatives. Local law or contract terms may require consent, consultation, or amendment.

A reduction must never be retaliatory or triggered by a pay complaint.

## Temporary cross-border work

Employees must obtain approval before working from another country. Review must cover immigration, tax, payroll, social security, permanent-establishment risk, employment law, benefits, and data security.

# 9. Exceptions and appeals

## Permitted exceptions

Exceptions may be considered for:

- Critical hiring;
- Retention risk;
- Scarce or strategic skills;
- Employer-requested relocation;
- Legal or collective requirements;
- Temporary market disruption;
- Pay-equity or compression remediation.

## Exception controls

Every exception must record:

- Employee or role;
- Job family and level;
- Compensation location and range version;
- Proposed amount and total-rewards treatment;
- Business rationale;
- Internal comparator analysis;
- Pay-equity and compression impact;
- Duration or review date;
- Legal review where required;
- Approvals.

Delegated approval thresholds will apply to routine exceptions. Material, unusual, repeated, or high-risk exceptions require Committee approval.

## Employee review and appeal

Employees may request review of:

- Job family or level;
- Compensation location;
- Range assignment;
- Range placement;
- Promotion outcome;
- Relocation treatment;
- Suspected pay inequity;
- Inaccurate source data.

Global service targets are:

- Acknowledgment within five business days;
- Initial review within 30 calendar days;
- Written outcome where legally and operationally appropriate.

Local law may require different procedures or timelines. Review should be independent of the original decision-maker where feasible.

The process does not replace statutory complaints, union rights, works-council processes, whistleblower channels, or regulatory remedies.

# 10. Pay equity, compression, and remediation

At least annually, and more frequently where required, the company will assess:

- Base salary;
- Total cash;
- Bonus opportunity;
- Equity;
- Range position;
- Starting offers;
- Promotions;
- Merit increases;
- Performance ratings;
- Attrition;
- Time to promotion;
- Exceptions;
- Relocation outcomes.

Analyses will control for legitimate factors such as job family, level, actual scope, work location, relevant experience, tenure, performance, and employment status. Protected-characteristic analysis will occur only where lawful and appropriate.

## Compression triggers

A targeted review is required where:

- A new hire is materially above comparable incumbents;
- An incumbent is below a revised range minimum;
- A promotion produces little or no meaningful pay change;
- A scarcity premium creates persistent inversion;
- A geographic-factor change materially worsens an incumbent’s position;
- A revised range reveals a material historical gap.

## Remediation priorities

1. Statutory or contractual noncompliance;
2. Unexplained protected-class disparities;
3. Employees below range minimum;
4. Severe new-hire inversion;
5. Material compression;
6. Critical retention populations;
7. Broader market alignment.

The company will maintain a first-year remediation reserve. Affordability may affect sequencing but may not justify indefinite deferral of unlawful or unexplained discriminatory disparities.

# 11. Transparency and communication

## Employee standard

Following a pre-disclosure audit, employees will receive:

- Job family and level;
- Applicable country or zone;
- Salary range minimum, midpoint, and maximum;
- Range effective date;
- Placement factors;
- Progression and promotion rules;
- Review timing;
- Relocation principles;
- Pay-review and appeal process.

The global standard will not include exact range position. Approximate position or additional information may be provided where legally required, operationally reliable, and approved by Compensation, Legal, and Privacy.

Employee communications will explain:

> Pay is determined first by role and level, then adjusted for the labor market in the employee’s legal work location. The adjustment reflects the market cost of hiring and retaining comparable talent. It is not a judgment about the employee’s contribution, nationality, or household cost of living.

Current pay, bonus, equity, and benefits should be presented separately where included in compensation statements, with clear definitions of target versus actual amounts and grant-date versus current equity value.

## Candidate disclosures

A country matrix will identify:

- Whether a range must be posted;
- When it must be disclosed;
- Required content, format, and language;
- Treatment of bonus, equity, benefits, and allowances;
- Salary-history restrictions;
- Internal-transfer and promotion requirements;
- Recordkeeping obligations.

Every posting and candidate disclosure must be traceable to the approved range version in effect at the relevant date.

## Public salary comparisons

The company will:

1. Acknowledge concerns without dismissing them;
2. Avoid confirming or denying individual compensation;
3. Refer the issue to HR and Compensation;
4. Escalate to Legal and Communications where appropriate;
5. Determine whether a broader methodology, compression, or equity review is required;
6. Publish aggregated findings only after privacy, legal, data-quality, and representative review.

Managers will receive a consistent response script and may not characterize a disputed pay difference as lawful or unlawful before review.

# 12. Privacy, automated tools, and records

The company will:

- Limit manager access to necessary information;
- Restrict demographic and protected-characteristic data;
- Use aggregation and small-cell suppression;
- Assess re-identification risk;
- Maintain lawful processing bases and employee notices;
- Review cross-border transfers;
- Apply local retention requirements;
- Preserve audit logs and historical versions.

Before new compensation analytics are launched, the responsible Privacy/Data Protection function must approve an assessment covering:

- Purpose and lawful basis;
- Data minimization;
- Protected-characteristic data;
- Vendors and subprocessors;
- International transfers;
- Retention;
- Employee rights;
- Residual risk and risk acceptance.

If automated tools are used to assign zones, recommend pay, identify gaps, or prioritize remediation:

- Human review is mandatory;
- Sole reliance on automated recommendations is prohibited;
- Bias and adverse-impact testing is required;
- Models must be documented and explainable;
- Changes require approval and testing;
- Employees must be able to correct inaccurate underlying data.

The system of record must preserve historical:

- Ranges;
- Zone factors;
- Market data;
- Exchange-rate assumptions;
- Offers;
- Employee communications;
- Approvals;
- Remediation decisions.

# 14. Country implementation safeguards

Each country must have an approved addendum addressing:

- Equal-pay and discrimination rules;
- Local comparable-work standards;
- Range disclosure;
- Employee explanation and comparator rights;
- Pay-gap reporting;
- Works-council, union, or representative processes;
- Protected-data rules;
- Cross-border transfers;
- Record retention;
- Salary changes and contract amendments;
- Currency and wage-payment rules;
- Statutory bonuses and benefits;
- Equity and variable pay;
- Payroll and social security;
- Immigration and tax;
- Permanent-establishment risk;
- Local remedies, penalties, and deadlines.

No country may go live until:

1. Local ranges and currency are approved;
2. Required disclosures are configured;
3. Required consultation, bargaining, or consent is completed;
4. Privacy and transfer assessments are complete;
5. Payroll and statutory compensation are validated;
6. Historical records and effective dates are preserved;
7. Managers, recruiters, HR, payroll, and representatives are trained;
8. Employee review channels are operational;
9. Reporting ownership and deadlines are documented;
10. Local counsel confirms material assumptions.

“Consultation scheduled” is insufficient where prior consultation, bargaining, or consent is required.

Legal will maintain a change register with owners, deadlines, interim controls, and escalation procedures. Affected postings, offers, disclosures, or pay changes may be paused if compliance cannot be assured.

# 15. Implementation plan

## Phase 1 — Diagnose: 0–90 days

- Inventory countries, entities, worker populations, contracts, currencies, payroll providers, and systems;
- Map roles to the global architecture;
- Analyze pay, offers, promotions, equity, benefits, and exceptions;
- Identify pay gaps, compression, inversion, and below-range employees;
- Assess market-data quality;
- Build legal, privacy, payroll, tax, immigration, and consultation matrices;
- Establish employee-sentiment and recruiting baselines;
- Approve the RACI, decision matrix, spread threshold, and remediation principles.

## Phase 2 — Design: 90–180 days

- Finalize levels and role profiles;
- Establish zones and boundary rules;
- Define market positioning and affordability rules;
- Define range placement, promotion, relocation, exception, and appeal rules;
- Define compensation-component and benefits-normalization rules;
- Create reporting calendars and data dictionaries;
- Configure system access, retention, and version control;
- Draft employee, candidate, manager, recruiter, and public-response materials.

## Phase 3 — Pilot and calibrate: 180–270 days

Pilot in representative markets, including:

- A premium technology market;
- A lower-cost market;
- A significant transparency jurisdiction;
- A consultation-heavy jurisdiction;
- A currency- or inflation-sensitive market.

Test:

- Role matching;
- Zone assignment;
- Payroll and HRIS synchronization;
- Disclosures;
- Privacy controls;
- Appeals;
- Administrative workload;
- Compression and remediation processes.

## Phase 4 — Remediate and launch: 270–365 days

- Complete country readiness gates;
- Correct statutory issues, data errors, unexplained disparities, compression, and below-range pay;
- Complete required consultations;
- Train managers, recruiters, HR, payroll, and representatives;
- Publish the policy and FAQs;
- Provide approved employee information;
- Implement candidate disclosures;
- Activate exception and appeal workflows;
- Begin the first annual cycle.

Countries launch only when ready; elapsed time does not override a failed readiness gate.

## Phase 5 — Ongoing governance

- Quarterly exception and compression reviews;
- Quarterly legal and disclosure updates;
- Semiannual employee-understanding surveys;
- Annual market and range review;
- Annual pay-equity audit;
- Annual privacy and access review;
- Annual payroll and systems audit;
- Independent control testing at least every two years;
- Geographic-zone review every 12–24 months;
- Formal policy review and reapproval at least every two years or after a material legal, business, or operating change.

# 16. Required response to Warsaw, Lisbon, Berlin, and Toronto

HR and Compensation must complete an evidence-based review within **90 days of policy approval**. The deadline is for analysis and recommendation; implementation remains subject to local law and consultation.

An interim employee communication must:

- Acknowledge the concern;
- Explain that a structured review is underway;
- Avoid confirming individual compensation;
- Reaffirm non-retaliation;
- Provide a channel for affected employees to submit information;
- State the expected completion date.

The review must use a defined evidence set covering:

- Comparable roles, duties, scope, and levels;
- Base pay, bonus, equity, benefits, and statutory compensation separately;
- Legal work location and employing entity;
- Range and zone in effect at each decision date;
- Market-data quality, age, and confidence;
- New-hire versus incumbent pay;
- Promotions, merit increases, and exceptions;
- Protected-characteristic effects where lawful;
- Disclosure and representative requirements;
- Cost, retention, and remediation implications.

Each material difference will be classified as:

- Supported by current labor-market evidence;
- Job or level classification error;
- Data error;
- Compression or inversion;
- Unexplained disparity;
- Potentially discriminatory pattern;
- Local-law or consultation issue; or
- Insufficient evidence.

Required actions:

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Supported difference | Preserve, explain, and monitor |
| Data or classification error | Correct records and pay where required |
| Compression or inversion | Conduct targeted incumbent remediation |
| Unexplained disparity | Investigate and remediate |
| Potential discrimination | Escalate to Legal and conduct protected review |
| Low-confidence evidence | Use broader ranges and improve data |
| Excessive spread | Narrow at the next effective date or sooner if material |
| Legal or consultation issue | Pause affected implementation until resolved |

The Committee must approve the written outcome, remediation budget, communication plan, external-response strategy, owners, effective dates, and six- and twelve-month follow-up measures.

# 18. Explicit local-counsel caveat

Before implementation in any country, local employment counsel and relevant specialists must validate:

- Whether geographic pay differences are lawful and adequately supported;
- Definitions of equal, equivalent, or comparable work;
- Range-disclosure requirements and timing;
- Employee explanation and comparator rights;
- Salary-history restrictions;
- Pay-gap reporting and remediation;
- Works-council, union, consultation, bargaining, or consent obligations;
- Collection and analysis of protected characteristics;
- Cross-border data transfers and localization;
- Record-retention periods;
- Salary reductions, relocation, and contract amendments;
- Currency, wage-payment, and payslip requirements;
- Statutory bonuses, benefits, and social contributions;
- Equity, bonus, and variable-pay requirements;
- Immigration, tax, payroll, and permanent-establishment implications;
- Local remedies, penalties, deadlines, and employee protections;
- Whether audits can be structured for legal advice or privilege.

These are local-counsel dependencies, not conclusions established by this global policy. Legal review must be refreshed when legislation, guidance, collective agreements, employing entities, or operating countries change.

## Governing standard

> **Location may affect the market price of labor, but it cannot excuse unexplained, inconsistent, retaliatory, or discriminatory pay differences.**
