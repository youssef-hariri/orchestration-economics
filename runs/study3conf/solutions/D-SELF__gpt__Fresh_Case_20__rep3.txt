# Global Monetization and Product-Governance Strategy — 2026

## 1. Executive recommendation

Operate **one global game with a modular, jurisdiction-aware commerce layer**.

The shared game should preserve:

- Core gameplay, balance, characters, maps, modes, and live-service systems.
- Account identity, progression, inventory, collections, guilds, friends, chat, and social reputation.
- Matchmaking, ranked rules, anti-cheat, esports eligibility, and tournament standards.
- Earnable progression and meaningful free access to essential gameplay.
- Common safety, accessibility, support, refund, and error-remediation principles.

Only the commerce and compliance layer should vary by jurisdiction, platform, age category, and publishing territory. Regional differences may affect:

- Paid-randomized products.
- Catalog contents, prices, taxes, currencies, and payment methods.
- Probability and purchase disclosures.
- Gifting and gift cards.
- Age, parental, spending, and self-exclusion controls.
- Advertising, scarcity language, influencer activity, and event calendars.
- Features requiring local publishing or regulatory approval.

The commercial model should be **deterministic-first**: direct cosmetics, choice-based collections, battle passes, subscriptions, premium content, known-value gifting, and creator/community content should become the primary revenue pillars.

The publisher should:

1. Operate deterministic commerce in Belgium and the Netherlands under the current 2026 operating assumption, pending current local legal confirmation.
2. Permit paid randomization in Korea only after mechanic-specific legal, platform, and governance approval, with prominent disclosure and auditable records.
3. Run China through a separate publishing, operating, data, payment, and compliance program.
4. Prohibit paid competitive power globally.
5. Reduce dependence on paid randomization over 24–36 months, with retirement decisions based on legal risk, player-protection outcomes, and validated financial performance.
6. Treat profitability as a hypothesis to validate through pilots and scenario modeling—not as a guaranteed outcome.

This approach preserves network effects and one global player community without assuming that one commerce model is lawful or culturally appropriate everywhere.

## 2. Legal operating model and caveats

This strategy is a company operating framework, **not legal advice or a legal opinion**. Local counsel, regulatory specialists, platform teams, privacy professionals, and regional operating entities must approve each market and material monetization change.

### 2.1 Source-backed obligations register

Legal should maintain a jurisdiction-by-product register containing:

- Applicable statute, regulation, regulator guidance, court decision, platform rule, or publishing condition.
- Source reference and link.
- Effective date and next review date.
- Product mechanics covered.
- Required disclosure, approval, recordkeeping, or control.
- Accountable legal and business owner.
- Evidence required to demonstrate compliance.
- Escalation route for uncertainty or regulatory change.

The register should be reviewed at least quarterly and whenever a material legal, platform, or publishing development occurs.

### 2.2 Mandatory review areas

Before launch or material change, the publisher must obtain appropriate local review of:

- Gambling and games-of-chance classification.
- Consumer protection, dark patterns, and digital-content rights.
- Probability and reward disclosure.
- Minors, age assurance, guardian consent, and child-directed advertising.
- Privacy, profiling, automated decisions, and cross-border data transfers.
- Tax, VAT, invoicing, currency, and all-in pricing.
- Subscriptions, renewals, cancellation, refunds, and chargebacks.
- App-store, platform, payment-provider, and web-store rules.
- Advertising, influencer, livestream, and creator obligations.
- Accessibility.
- Gifting, account transfers, virtual currency, and resale.
- Creator intellectual property, tax, and revenue sharing.
- Fraud, sanctions, and restricted payments where relevant.

### 2.3 Company standards

The following are global company policies, not claims of universal legal obligation:

- No paid competitive power.
- Deterministic alternatives to high-value random items.
- Prominent price, contents, odds, guarantee, duplicate, expiry, and expected- or maximum-spend disclosures where relevant.
- Spending limits, cooling-off, refunds, and self-exclusion.
- No monetization targeting based on inferred vulnerability.
- Independent audits and transparency reporting.
- Progressive reduction of paid-randomization dependence.

A global charter or independent audit does not replace local law, regulatory approval, platform policy, or publishing requirements.

## 3. One game, regional commerce modes

### 3.1 Globally shared experience

The following must remain materially equivalent across regions:

- Combat values, balance, hitboxes, characters, maps, and gameplay systems.
- Ranked eligibility, matchmaking, ratings, anti-cheat, and tournament rules.
- Earnable progression and essential competitive access.
- Account identity, collections, achievements, guilds, friends, chat, moderation, and reporting.
- Core gameplay events and narrative, except where local publishing or cultural requirements necessitate changes.
- Accessibility, safety, support, refund, and error-remediation standards.

A player in Belgium may see a different shop, but should not have inferior matchmaking, slower access to competitive content, weaker social functionality, or materially slower earnable progression.

### 3.2 Regionalized commerce

The entitlement service may control:

- Paid randomized rewards.
- Catalog contents, prices, currencies, taxes, and payment methods.
- Gifting and gift cards.
- Battle-pass terms where local rules require differences.
- Promotions, scarcity claims, and advertising.
- Age gates, guardian consent, spending limits, and self-exclusion.
- Locally approved content and live-service features.
- Invoicing, refunds, complaints, and support routing.

Regional commerce must not change combat balance, hitboxes, ranked access, matchmaking rules, or tournament outcomes.

### 3.3 Parity standard

Before launch, Product, Data, Finance, and Fairness teams must set numerical thresholds for:

- Payer/nonpayer time to competitive readiness.
- Regional earnable currency and reward volume.
- Access delay for essential gameplay.
- Ranked and tournament eligibility.
- Character and team-composition access.
- Queue times and match quality.
- Win-rate and rank-distribution variance.
- Nonpayer retention and competitive participation.
- Usability of purchased entitlements after lawful relocation.

If a regional product is unavailable, provide a comparable deterministic product, earnable route, or substitute reward with comparable gameplay and collection value where feasible.

Repeated or severe parity breaches require redesign, compensation, product suspension, or executive escalation.

## 4. Commerce entitlement and jurisdiction controls

Deploy a server-authoritative **Commerce Entitlement Service**. The client must never determine its own legal commerce mode.

### 4.1 Decision hierarchy

Using only data necessary and legally permitted, the service should generally prioritize:

1. Verified legal residence.
2. App-store or platform territory.
3. Payment country.
4. Account history.
5. Fraud or circumvention indicators as review triggers, not sole proof of residence.

IP address alone is insufficient. Payment country may support fraud detection but should not automatically establish legal residence.

When signals conflict, the default should be the safer commerce catalog while preserving gameplay and social access.

### 4.2 Privacy and automated-decision controls

For every signal, maintain a data dictionary specifying:

- Source.
- Purpose.
- Lawful basis.
- Access permissions.
- Retention period.
- Correction and deletion process.
- Whether the signal can restrict a transaction, catalog, or account.

Before deployment, complete:

- Data mapping and purpose limitation.
- Privacy impact assessment where required.
- Cross-border transfer assessment.
- Vendor, payment-processor, and security review.
- Automated-decision and appeal assessment.
- Bias and false-positive testing.
- Player-facing explanations.

Precise location, device, payment, and region-switching information must not be retained indefinitely.

### 4.3 Travel, relocation, and appeals

The default should be to:

- Preserve existing lawful entitlements during travel and relocation.
- Restrict new purchases rather than invalidate owned content.
- Preserve gameplay and social access.
- Provide a human review route and explanation.
- Resolve standard appeals within a defined service-level target.
- Handle expatriate accounts, dual residence, family accounts, account recovery, and payment-provider errors explicitly.

Region changes should have a documented cooling-off period. Rules must address:

- Paid and earned currency.
- Battle-pass ownership.
- Region-exclusive cosmetics.
- Cross-border gifts.
- Subscriptions.
- Tax and refund rights.
- Movement into more or less restrictive jurisdictions.
- Discontinued products.

### 4.4 Anti-circumvention

The publisher will:

- Block prohibited products in official mobile and web stores.
- Coordinate restrictions across mobile, web, linked platforms, subscriptions, gifting, and support.
- Monitor mismatches among residence, storefront, account, payment, and platform data.
- Restrict transactions before suspending accounts where possible.
- Prohibit marketing that encourages VPNs, foreign accounts, third-party sellers, foreign gift cards, or other workarounds.
- Maintain audit logs for catalog decisions and entitlement outcomes.

## 5. Revenue model

### 5.1 Direct cosmetics

Offer:

- Character skins and effects.
- Emotes, finishers, mounts, banners, and profile items.
- Housing and guild decoration.
- Voice, narrative, commemorative, and social content.
- Prestige cosmetics and collection displays.
- Approved creator-designed items.

Requirements:

- Final local-currency price and contents are clear before purchase.
- Ownership and availability terms are clear.
- “Limited,” “exclusive,” and discount claims are accurate.
- Rotating products have a reasonable return path unless permanent exclusivity is clearly disclosed.
- Cosmetic rarity does not imply competitive or financial value.
- The lowest price tier provides meaningful value.
- Forced residual currency balances are minimized.

### 5.2 Battle passes

Each pass should provide:

- A meaningful free track.
- A paid track with complete reward listings.
- No competitive power.
- Completion forecasts and catch-up mechanics.
- No loss of earned rewards after a season ends.
- Transparent level-skip pricing without coercive messaging.
- Clear renewal, cancellation, and refund terms.
- Reasonable currency recirculation for active players.

### 5.3 Subscriptions

Subscriptions may provide:

- Cosmetic stipends.
- Monthly known-content items.
- Replay, statistics, customization, or social tools.
- Convenience features with no competitive effect.

They may not provide combat stats, exclusive ranked access, or material acceleration toward ranked power.

### 5.4 Premium content

Offer:

- Story chapters.
- Cooperative campaigns.
- New environments.
- Noncompetitive modes.
- Housing and social expansions.
- Premium creator or community content.

Essential competitive gameplay must remain viable without these purchases. Premium content must not make nonpurchasers socially or competitively irrelevant.

### 5.5 Deterministic collections

Use:

- Direct-purchase catalogs.
- Choice packs listing every choice.
- Selected-item progress meters.
- Deterministic crafting.
- Complete-content bundles.
- Earnable event currencies with published acquisition rates.
- Maximum-cost or direct-acquisition paths for featured items.

Do not use “free” random draws as a disguised paid-randomization funnel where the surrounding economy is designed to induce paid-currency purchases.

### 5.6 Gifting

Initially permit only known-content gifts:

- Direct cosmetics.
- Known-value bundles.
- Gift cards where legally and operationally permitted.

Controls should include:

- Sender and recipient age safeguards.
- Minor-to-minor gifting disabled by default.
- Recipient acceptance settings.
- Gift limits, history, and receipts.
- Fraud, chargeback, and resale monitoring.
- No anonymous high-pressure gifting.
- No random gifts or competitive power.
- No gifting-based regional circumvention.

### 5.7 Creator and community economy

Subject to local law and platform approval:

- Sell approved creator and user-generated content.
- Publish revenue-share, payment, tax, and moderation terms.
- Protect intellectual-property rights.
- Prohibit creator-run gambling, random-reward markets, resale, or cash-out schemes.
- Apply influencer, livestream, and child-marketing disclosure rules.

## 6. Competitive-integrity standard

### 6.1 Prohibited globally

The following are prohibited:

- Paid-only combat stats, abilities, characters, or enhancement outcomes.
- Paid random power or paid enhancement failure.
- Paid rerolls for competitive attributes.
- Paid consumable advantages in ranked modes.
- Exclusive ranked or tournament advantages.
- Paid access to essential anti-cheat, accessibility, safety, or competitive functions.
- Spending leaderboards that confer gameplay benefits.
- Social or guild requirements that effectively require paid power.
- Randomized crafting for competitive items.
- Temporary paid advantages during competitive events.

### 6.2 Product classification

Every monetized feature receives a classification:

| Tier | Definition | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Cosmetic or social, with no gameplay effect | Global default |
| 1 | Convenience, with no material competitive impact | Routine review |
| 2 | Progression acceleration that may affect ranked readiness | Enhanced review |
| 3 | Competitive power affecting combat, rank, team composition, or meaningful competitive opportunity | Prohibited |

“Eventually obtainable for free” is not sufficient. Payment must not materially alter time to competitive readiness, ranked opportunity, or team viability.

### 6.3 Testing

Measure by region, payer status, age category, and cohort:

- Time to competitive readiness.
- Win rate and rank distribution.
- Ranked participation.
- Character and team-composition access.
- Queue times and match quality.
- Retention and churn.
- Player-perceived fairness.
- Tournament integrity.

New gameplay-affecting content must be tested before ranked eligibility. Tournaments may use normalized inventories or standardized accounts.

## 7. Regional operating treatment

| Market | Operating treatment | Localization and compliance focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Belgium** | Deterministic commerce pending current legal confirmation | Disable paid draws, random enhancement, uncertain-reward entries, random paid bundles, random paid-currency crafting, and random gifts. Review earned currency, transferability, gifting, web stores, and inducement to purchase. |
| **Netherlands** | Deterministic commerce as the conservative 2026 default | Maintain a separate legal analysis. Review gambling classification, economic value, transferability, minors, dark patterns, web stores, gifting, and platform policy. Do not describe Dutch law as identical to Belgian law. |
| **Korea** | Paid randomization only after mechanic-specific approval | Provide Korean-language, pre-purchase disclosure of odds, pools, duplicates, pity, guarantees, resets, event duration, expected attempts, and direct alternatives. Retain the rendered disclosure, price, odds version, guarantee state, timestamp, and delivery record. |
| **China** | Separate publishing and operating model | Obtain local approval for publishing, real-name and minor controls, virtual currency, payments, data, content, advertising, livestreams, influencers, support, reporting, and live-service changes. Default to direct and deterministic monetization. |
| **Japan** | Premium, collection-rich, deterministic-first | Emphasize character, voice, story, housing, gifting, select systems, ceilings, direct acquisition, and reruns. Prohibit complete-collection structures requiring multiple randomized acquisitions to unlock a further reward. |
| **Gulf markets** | Country-specific review | Review Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, and Oman separately. Use known-value bundles, prestige cosmetics, family-safe social features, Arabic support, and controlled gifting. Review religious, content, payment, advertising, and data issues. |
| **Brazil** | Affordable, simple, deterministic-first | Use localized price ladders, simple currencies, low-price entry products, direct cosmetics, free event tracks, Portuguese support, and compliant local payments. Monitor affordability, failed payments, residual balances, refunds, and regret. |
| **United States** | Transparency above the legal minimum | Monitor federal and state law, platforms, consumer protection, children’s privacy, subscriptions, dark patterns, advertising, litigation, and advocacy. Use all-in pricing, clear currency conversion, parental controls, and direct alternatives. |

### Regional cultural principles

- Treat each country as a distinct legal and cultural market where appropriate.
- Use native-language UX, support, advertising, and cultural review.
- Keep prestige cosmetic or social, never combat-based.
- Support gifting without coercion, anonymity, competitive power, or minor exploitation.
- Do not use religious, family, or community observances to create exploitative urgency.
- Review sexualized, religiously sensitive, violent, and culturally inappropriate content locally.
- Use predictable event reruns and communicate availability accurately.
- Base regional pricing on affordability, taxes, platform fees, and operating costs—not inferred vulnerability.
- Assume regional differences will become public and design them to be explainable.

## 8. Player-protection standards

Publish and enforce a **Global Fair Monetization Charter** requiring:

- Age-appropriate commerce defaults.
- Tiered age assurance and guardian controls.
- Purchase confirmation and accessible transaction history.
- Daily, weekly, and monthly spending limits.
- Spend dashboards and attempt counters.
- Cooling-off periods.
- Account-wide commerce freeze.
- Publisher-controlled self-exclusion across mobile, web, gifting, subscriptions, currencies, and linked accounts where technically and legally feasible.
- Clear refunds, cancellation, and complaint routes.
- No targeting based on financial distress, grief, loneliness, addiction, debt, or inferred vulnerability.
- No loss-chasing prompts, near-miss animations, false scarcity, fake discounts, misleading countdowns, or permanent “last chance” claims.
- No minor-directed paid randomization.
- No minor-to-minor gifting by default.
- No monetization of essential accessibility or safety functions.
- No meaningful competitive advantage sold for money.

### Protective use of risk signals

Rapid spending, repeated failed attempts, unusual purchase velocity, repeated limit increases, or chargeback patterns may trigger only neutral protections:

- Spend reminders.
- Limit suggestions.
- Cooling-off options.
- Self-exclusion information.
- Support access.
- Temporary transaction review where lawful.

These signals must be technically segregated from commercial targeting. They must not trigger aggressive offers, personalized scarcity, higher prices, loss-recovery messaging, win-back campaigns, or reactivation during exclusion.

### Refunds and compensation

Maintain a compensation matrix for:

- Incorrect odds.
- Omitted rewards.
- Incorrect prices.
- Wrong regional catalog.
- Missing guarantee progress.
- Unavailable disclosure.
- Incorrect duplicate conversion.
- Failed or delayed delivery.

Legitimate refunds must not be obstructed or automatically punished. Material errors require correction, refund, replacement, or equivalent remediation according to applicable law and company policy.

## 9. Governance and accountability

Create a **Global Monetization and Player Protection Council** with authority to approve, reject, pause, or disable commerce features.

### Accountable roles

- **Chief Product/Monetization Officer:** accountable for commercial and player-experience outcomes.
- **Global Legal/Regulatory Lead:** owns jurisdiction classifications and approvals.
- **Privacy Lead/DPO:** owns data, profiling, age, retention, and transfer reviews.
- **Trust and Safety Lead:** owns minors, vulnerable players, limits, and self-exclusion.
- **Platform Compliance Lead:** owns app stores, payment providers, advertising, and web commerce.
- **Economy/Fairness Lead:** owns odds, inflation, power access, and parity.
- **Finance:** owns scenarios, tax, accounting, refunds, and regional contribution margin.
- **Live-Ops:** executes approved configurations only.
- **Regional GMs and publishing entities:** own local execution and counsel coordination.
- **Customer Support:** owns complaints, refunds, compensation, and escalation.
- **Independent advisors:** audit and challenge the program.
- **Executive Risk Committee or Board:** receives quarterly reporting and unresolved escalations.

### Decision rights

Maintain a RACI and decision-rights matrix covering:

- New monetization mechanics.
- Probability, reward-pool, price, and targeting changes.
- Regional catalog decisions.
- Age and vulnerability controls.
- Experiments.
- Refunds and compensation.
- Incident response.
- Policy exceptions.

Legal, Privacy, Trust and Safety, and Platform Compliance have domain vetoes. No veto may be overridden where it concerns a confirmed legal prohibition or publishing condition. Other overrides require:

- Written rationale.
- Named executive risk acceptance.
- Defined mitigation.
- Expiry date.
- Executive Risk Committee visibility.

No operator may change odds, reward pools, prices, targeting, or regional eligibility without versioned approval, dual authorization, audit logging, impact assessment, and rollback capability.

Maintain:

- A policy-exception register.
- Audit-finding deadlines.
- Whistleblower protections.
- Annual governance-effectiveness reviews.
- Documented consequences for bypassing controls or missing remediation deadlines.

Independent advisors should have a written mandate, appropriate budget, conflict-of-interest rules, evidence access, protected independence, and a route to escalate unresolved concerns.

## 10. Disclosure, experimentation, and incident response

### 10.1 Disclosure standard

Where paid randomization remains, disclose before money or paid currency is committed:

- Final local-currency price.
- Effective price after currency conversion.
- Full reward pool.
- Required item or category odds.
- Pity, guarantee, step-up, and reset rules.
- Duplicate treatment.
- Event duration and expiry.
- Expected attempts.
- Maximum or direct-acquisition cost where meaningful.
- Configuration version.
- Refund and remediation terms.

The disclosure must be:

- Adjacent to the purchase decision.
- Readable on mobile.
- Accessible.
- Locally translated and reviewed.
- Understandable without specialist knowledge.
- Preserved in the transaction record.

Representative-player comprehension testing must confirm that users can identify price, outcomes, odds, guarantees, duplicate treatment, and expected or maximum cost. The Council must approve a minimum pass threshold.

### 10.2 Experiment restrictions

Do not A/B test:

- Odds or pity rules.
- Probability disclosures.
- Parental safeguards.
- Refund friction.
- Self-exclusion.
- Minor protections.
- Access to deterministic alternatives.
- Different treatment of vulnerability cohorts.

Approved commercial experiments must include harm stop conditions and measure long-term retention, fairness, affordability, regret, refunds, support demand, and spend velocity—not only short-term revenue.

### 10.3 Incident levels

**Severity 1:** prohibited sale in a restricted market, material odds error, systemic minor-control failure, unauthorized pay-to-win item, or multi-channel self-exclusion failure.

**Severity 2:** regional catalog error, material disclosure defect, major gifting or refund failure, material pricing error, or significant parity breach.

**Severity 3:** isolated delivery, localization, or support issue.

For Severity 1 and 2 incidents:

1. Freeze the affected product or configuration.
2. Preserve logs, disclosures, targeting records, and transaction evidence.
3. Identify affected jurisdictions, ages, transactions, and cohorts.
4. Assess regulator, platform, privacy, publishing, and notification duties.
5. Refund, compensate, replace, or correct as appropriate.
6. Notify affected players clearly.
7. Complete an independent root-cause review.
8. Track remediation to closure.
9. Escalate missed deadlines to the Executive Risk Committee.

## 11. KPIs

Every KPI must have a baseline, owner, population definition, reporting frequency, tolerance, data-quality check, threshold, and mandatory response.

### Commercial

- Revenue per MAU and payer.
- Payer conversion.
- Renewal and churn.
- Regional contribution margin after content, platform, tax, support, compliance, audit, refund, and fraud costs.
- Revenue mix by product.
- Product cannibalization.
- Content and live-ops cost.
- Spend distribution across the top 0.1%, 1%, and 5%.
- Median, 95th-percentile, and 99th-percentile spend.
- Spend velocity.
- Unused currency balances.

Planning objectives—not guarantees—may include:

- Revenue per MAU within 90–105% of matched pre-transition baseline by month 18.
- Direct-purchase share of at least 45% by month 12.
- Pass, subscription, and expansion share of at least 30% by month 18.
- Paid-randomization share below 15% globally by month 12 and below 5% by month 24.
- D90 retention no worse than 95% of matched baseline.

### Fairness and parity

- Payer/nonpayer time to competitive readiness.
- Earnable reward volume.
- Maximum cost to obtain featured content.
- Ranked participation.
- Win rate and rank distribution.
- Character and team-composition access.
- Queue times and match quality.
- Nonpayer retention.
- Purchase comprehension.
- Perceived fairness.
- Regional substitution effectiveness.

### Player protection

- Blocked underage purchase attempts.
- Guardian interventions.
- Cooling-off activations.
- Self-exclusion bypass attempts.
- Rapid-spend incidents.
- Limit increases.
- Refund and chargeback rates.
- Complaints per transaction.
- Regret reports within 24 and 72 hours.
- Minor exposure to monetization advertising.
- Use of protection signals for protective rather than commercial purposes.
- Compensation completion time.

### Compliance and operations

- Prohibited-sale incidents.
- Regional-decision accuracy.
- False-positive rate.
- Appeal resolution time.
- Odds/disclosure mismatch.
- Disclosure-comprehension pass rate.
- Entitlement failure rate.
- Time to disable defective products.
- Rollback time.
- Overdue audit findings.
- Incident-remediation completion.
- Platform or publishing violations.

Threshold breaches trigger review by the responsible lead and Council. Repeated or severe breaches require product suspension, redesign, compensation, or Executive Risk Committee escalation.

## 12. Advocacy and transparency

Treat coordinated player-protection organizations as stakeholders.

The publisher will:

- Publish the Global Fair Monetization Charter.
- Maintain a public archive of major catalog and odds versions where appropriate.
- Publish annual aggregate data on monetization mix, refunds, complaints, minor controls, odds errors, self-exclusion, and spend concentration.
- Report regional differences using a consistent comparison methodology.
- Hold quarterly briefings with credible advocacy groups.
- Invite review of disclosures, dark patterns, youth safeguards, and self-exclusion.
- Maintain a regulator, platform, and advocacy response playbook.
- Prohibit moving withdrawn high-risk mechanics into less-regulated markets.
- Publish material corrections and remediation outcomes.

Material complaints receive:

- A named owner.
- An evidence-review deadline.
- A response deadline.
- A documented outcome.
- Council escalation when substantiated or systemic.

Missing a public commitment triggers automatic Council review, corrective action, and public disclosure where material.

Transparency, advisory participation, and voluntary codes are not legal safe harbors.

## 13. Financial validation

Before binding revenue commitments, Finance and Product must approve three cases:

1. Conservative transition.
2. Base case.
3. Upside case.

Each case must include:

- Revenue per MAU and payer.
- Conversion, renewal, retention, and churn.
- Average, median, and high-percentile spend.
- Product cannibalization.
- Content-production and live-ops costs.
- Platform, payment, tax, support, moderation, audit, privacy, and compliance costs.
- Refunds, chargebacks, fraud, and resale losses.
- Regional pricing effects.
- Creator revenue shares.
- Migration and entitlement costs.
- Player-trust and harm indicators.
- Sensitivity to lower conversion, higher refunds, slower content production, and price changes.

A product must be redesigned or suspended if improved revenue depends on increased regret, harmful spending velocity, materially higher complaints, or greater reliance on vulnerable players.

The commercial objective is to preserve viability while reducing regulatory and player-harm exposure, not to guarantee revenue neutrality.

## 14. Implementation roadmap

### Phase 0 — Stabilize: 0–90 days

- Freeze new high-risk paid-random mechanics.
- Operate deterministic catalogs in Belgium and the Netherlands pending counsel confirmation.
- Inventory randomization, currencies, bundles, gifts, advertising, targeting, enhancement, and live-event systems.
- Build the source-backed obligations register.
- Confirm current legal, platform, payment, and publishing requirements.
- Establish the China program and named local owner.
- Form the Council and approve the Competitive Fairness Standard.
- Establish incident severity, emergency disablement, and player communications.
- Begin privacy, age-assurance, refund, financial, and platform assessments.
- Set budget, staffing, vendor, and content-capacity requirements.

### Phase 1 — Foundation: 3–6 months

- Deploy the privacy-reviewed entitlement service.
- Launch deterministic Belgium and Netherlands catalogs.
- Add direct cosmetics, choice packs, purchase history, spending dashboards, limits, guardian controls, cooling-off, self-exclusion, and refund workflows.
- Implement rendered disclosure archival and transaction-linked odds records for Korea and China.
- Define travel, relocation, migration, currency, gifting, and entitlement rules.
- Establish the compensation matrix.
- Test false positives, appeals, linked platforms, web stores, family accounts, and rollback.
- Obtain local counsel, platform, privacy, and publishing approvals.

### Phase 2 — Commercial transition: 6–12 months

- Expand direct cosmetics, passes, subscriptions, expansions, deterministic crafting, and known-value gifting.
- Simplify currency layers.
- Localize prices, payments, language, support, advertising, and event calendars.
- Run approved pilots with matched cohorts and harm stop conditions.
- Begin independent odds, privacy, player-protection, and parity audits.
- Publish the first transparency report.
- Validate conservative, base, and upside financial scenarios.

### Phase 3 — Optimization: 12–18 months

- Improve cosmetic discovery and collection tools.
- Expand creator and community commerce.
- Tune prices using affordability, retention, fairness, refund, and support data.
- Add social and prestige monetization without competitive power.
- Reassess regional parity, player sentiment, advocacy feedback, and financial performance.
- Evaluate retirement of additional paid-random systems.

### Phase 4 — Durable model: 18–36 months

- Make deterministic monetization the global default.
- Retain paid randomization only where lawful, transparent, low-risk, and commercially justified.
- Conduct annual retirement reviews based on legal exposure, harm metrics, player comprehension, and financial contribution.
- Institutionalize annual independent audits and quarterly Executive Risk Committee reporting.
- Refresh regional configurations as law, platform policy, and player expectations change.

### Mandatory launch gates

No regional launch or material monetization change proceeds without:

1. **Legal and platform readiness:** no unresolved high-severity legal, publishing, privacy, payment, or platform issue.
2. **Technical integrity:** tested regional decisions, entitlements, refunds, travel, relocation, gifting, audit logging, and rollback.
3. **Parity validation:** approved thresholds for time-to-power, earnable progression, ranked access, match quality, and entitlement continuity.
4. **Player-protection readiness:** tested age assurance, guardian controls, limits, cooling-off, self-exclusion, refunds, disclosures, support, and incident response.
5. **Disclosure readiness:** comprehension threshold met and rendered evidence retained.
6. **Commercial validation:** pilot evidence, financial scenario approval, and funded downside plan.
7. **China readiness:** publishing, operating, local payment, data, content, and minor-control approvals.
8. **Incident readiness:** completed Severity 1 and Severity 2 response exercises.
9. **Resource readiness:** dedicated engineering, legal, privacy, trust-and-safety, support, finance, regional, audit, and content capacity.

A big-bang migration is prohibited. Use staged rollout, feature flags, matched cohorts, controlled pilots, and tested rollback.

## 15. Final recommendation

Approve the strategy as a **gated transformation program**, subject to current local legal, privacy, platform, publishing, and financial validation.

The durable boundary is:

- **One shared game** for gameplay, identity, progression, social systems, matchmaking, and competition.
- **Multiple governed commerce modes** for regional legal requirements, cultural norms, prices, disclosures, protections, and publishing constraints.

Profitability should come from the breadth and durability of player value—collection, expression, gifting, seasonal participation, social prestige, subscriptions, and premium content—rather than escalating uncertainty or pressure on a small number of high-spending players.

This allows one global title to remain commercially viable without creating separate regional games or irreparable player-experience fragmentation.
