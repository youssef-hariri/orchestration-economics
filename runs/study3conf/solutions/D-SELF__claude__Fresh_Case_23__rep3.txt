## PART 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### 1.1 Strategic Vision & Core Challenge

The pan-African banking group has acquired three operationally independent banks with **incompatible core systems, distinct regulatory supervisors, and entrenched local management cultures**. The integration mandate requires:

- **Unified digital products** (single mobile app, integrated APIs, consolidated customer experience)
- **Group operational coherence** (shared risk frameworks, standardized reporting, consolidated profitability)
- **Local regulatory compliance** (CBN, CBK, BAM each require subsidiary-level decision authority and continuity of local relationships)
- **Customer confidence preservation** (avoid brand-shock disruption; maintain local banking relationships)

**Strategic Tension:** These objectives are **partially in conflict**. Aggressive centralization (forced brand unification, rapid technology cutover, immediate redundancies) generates organizational resistance and regulatory friction, triggering **estimated 25–35% voluntary departure of senior staff** in Months 3–9 and **6–12 month timeline delays** across regulatory approval cycles.

**Recommended Path:** **"Respect Autonomy, Embed Accountability" model**—preserve local market autonomy in P&L, brand, and customer relationships while centralizing digital platform standards, risk frameworks, and regulatory submissions through matrix accountability (local CROs report to both Market MD and Group CRO; Group CDO sets technology roadmap with Market MD consensus, not override authority).

---

### 1.2 Three-Country Integration Vision (Phased, Not Simultaneous)

**Phase 1: Kenya (Months 4–10 Go-Live; Months 10–16 Stabilization)**
- **Rationale:** Smallest customer base (1.8M); least regulatory friction (CBK is most progressive); best technology foundation (Fiserv DNA already cloud-hybrid, APIs mature). Establishes playbook.
- **Outcome:** Unified Temenos platform operational; digital app launched; customer migration achieved with <1% attrition.
- **Regulatory Gate:** CBK Mpesa integration certification by Month 9; CBK formal approval by Month 8.

**Phase 2: Morocco (Months 8–18 Go-Live; Months 18–27 Stabilization)**
- **Rationale:** Mid-complexity customer base (0.9M); front-loaded Sharia re-certification (12–16 week critical path) manages BAM relationship. Validates playbook extensibility to relationship-driven regulators.
- **Critical Dependency:** BAM Sharia Board re-certification must complete by Month 18 (non-negotiable gate). Requires elevated Sharia Officer (Dr. Abdel Aziz) to governance council; pre-engagement with BAM Islamic Finance Unit Months 8–10.
- **Outcome:** Temenos platform with bespoke Islamic finance modules operational; customer migration achieved; BAM confidence in group's risk management established.
- **Regulatory Gate:** BAM Sharia certification by Month 18; BAM formal approval by Month 17.

**Phase 3: Nigeria (Months 15–30 Go-Live; Months 30–36 Stabilization)**
- **Rationale:** Largest complexity (4.2M customers, $2.8B assets, highest regulatory scrutiny). Phases 1–2 learnings directly embedded. Extended parallel run (16 weeks vs. standard 12 weeks) for CBN RTGS certification (non-negotiable).
- **Critical Dependency:** CBN RTGS certification must complete by Month 28 (Months 24–28 parallel run, not compressible). Requires proactive CBN engagement Months 1–3 to lock testing windows; cannot be regulator-constrained.
- **Outcome:** Largest market successfully transitioned; CBN confidence in group's technology governance established; foundation for future pan-African digital platform.
- **Regulatory Gate:** CBN RTGS certification by Month 28; CBN formal approval by Month 27; cybersecurity penetration testing clearance by Month 27.

**Overall Group Timeline:** 36 months baseline; 9 months embedded contingency (2 months Kenya, 3 months Morocco, 4 months Nigeria); effective 45-month envelope.

### 1.3 Technology Architecture Recommendation

**Platform:** Temenos SaaS (cloud-native, multi-tenant, API-first)

**Deployment Model:**
- **Data Residency:** **Cloud-hybrid approach (Option B recommended)**—Temenos primary deployment on AWS Africa region (Cape Town or Johannesburg); local on-premise backup/gateway in each market (Lagos for Nigeria, Nairobi for Kenya, Casablanca for Morocco) for regulatory comfort and settlement finality redundancy. Cost: $1–2M capex per country; timeline impact: +2–3 months facility setup.
- **Fallback if Regulators Demand On-Premise:** Be prepared to pivot to **Option A (full on-premise)** for Nigeria/Morocco if CBN/BAM formally reject cloud. Cost: $3–5M capex Nigeria, $1.5–2M Morocco; timeline impact: +6 months Nigeria, +4 months Morocco. Decision gate at Month 2 (after formal regulatory meetings).

**API Architecture:**
- **Hybrid Connectivity Model:** Cannot achieve "pure API-first" globally due to regulatory constraints. Must support:
  - **Kenya:** Modern REST APIs to Mpesa (CBK approved approach)
  - **Nigeria:** Legacy SWIFT protocol to RTGS and NIBSS (CBN requirement; cannot use internet-based APIs for settlement)
  - **Morocco:** Legacy SWIFT to CIE (Moroccan payment switch); API modernization dependent on external CIE evolution (not group-controlled)
- **Impact:** Temenos architecture must embed protocol bridges and legacy connectors; increases system complexity +15–20% vs. pure cloud-native model; accepted as necessary for regulatory compliance.

**Sharia Module (Morocco-Critical):**
- **Requirement:** Temenos must support Islamic finance products (Murabaha, Ijara, Musharaka, Sukuk) with BAM Sharia Board certification. Sopra's proprietary Islamic finance codebase cannot be ported to Temenos; complete re-engineering required.
- **Recommendation:** Hire **external Islamic Finance SME (Subject Matter Expert)** consultant to validate Temenos Islamic finance capability and design custom modules. Cost: $200–300K; timeline: Months 8–14 (parallel to Kenya go-live, enabling Morocco Phase 2 Sharia design). **Non-negotiable risk mitigation** (Sharia module is critical path; single point of failure if Temenos capability insufficient).
- **BAM Engagement:** Pre-submit Sharia module design to BAM Sharia Advisory Board for informal feedback by Month 10 (Months 8–10 design; Month 11–12 pre-review; Months 13–15 formal submission; Months 16–18 certification). Reduces rework risk.

### 1.4 Brand Strategy Recommendation: Federated Model (Option B)

**Decision:** Retain local bank names (First Trust Bank Nigeria, KCB Kenya, Banque du Devenir Morocco) with subtle group co-branding.

**Rationale:**
- **Customer Retention:** Local brand equity 20–30 years established (especially Nigeria); rebranding triggers 3–5% customer attrition risk (competitor poaching during transition).
- **Regulatory Acceptability:** CBN/CBK/BAM expect "local continuity"; unified brand rebranding seen as operational risk during migration.
- **Organizational Resistance Minimized:** Local management retains identity autonomy; reduces voluntary departure risk (~30% reduction in flight risk vs. unified brand approach).
- **Timeline Impact:** Minimal (brand strategy does not drive technology schedule); versus unified brand adds 6–8 weeks (logo/UI redesign delays system go-live).

**Implementation:**
- **Backend Unification:** Single Temenos platform for all three markets (unified customer master, consolidated reporting, shared risk frameworks).
- **Frontend Differentiation:** Three distinct mobile apps (per local brand) + three distinct web portals, but **unified API layer** reduces development overhead. Cost: +25–30% product development vs. single global app (acceptable tradeoff).
- **Marketing Narrative:** "Local bank you trust, pan-African scale and digital capabilities" (combines local autonomy messaging with group credibility).

**Go-to-Market Timeline:**
- **Month 4 (Kenya):** Announce "KCB Digital Upgrade—faster banking, same trusted bank"
- **Month 8 (Morocco):** Announce "Banque du Devenir modernization—same Islamic finance values, enhanced technology"
- **Month 15 (Nigeria):** Announce "First Trust Nigeria digital transformation—deeper local service, group backing"

### 1.5 Management & Organizational Structure: Respect Autonomy + Embed Accountability

**Core Principle:** Market MDs retain P&L ownership, customer relationship accountability, and regulatory liaison authority. Group embeds accountability through **matrix reporting** for risk/compliance functions and **milestone-based authority** for technology decisions (Group CDO has timeline authority, Market MD has veto only if regulator objects).

**Governance Structure:**

```
GROUP CEO

├─ Group CRO (Risk framework, capital adequacy)
├─ Group CDO (Technology platform, digital products)
├─ Group Head Regulatory Affairs (Regulatory coordination)
└─ Group CMO (Brand strategy, communications)
    |
    ├─ NIGERIA MARKET MD (Kunle Okafor — RETAINED)
    │   ├─ CFO Nigeria (reports to Kunle + Group CFO—dual reporting)
    │   ├─ CRO Nigeria (reports to Kunle + Group CRO—matrix accountability)
    │   ├─ Head of Regulatory Affairs—Nigeria (reports to Kunle + Group Head Regulatory)
    │   └─ [Technology & Digital report to Group CDO for migration milestones]
    │
    ├─ KENYA MARKET MD (Jane Kipchoge — RETAINED)
    │   ├─ CFO Kenya (reports to Jane + Group CFO)
    │   ├─ CRO Kenya (reports to Jane + Group CRO)
    │   ├─ Head of Regulatory Affairs—Kenya (reports to Jane + Group Head Regulatory)
    │   └─ [CTO (Raj promoted to Group CDO; interim replacement for Kenya CTO)]
    │
    └─ MOROCCO MARKET MD (Hassan El Kadi — RETAINED)
        ├─ CFO Morocco (reports to Hassan + Group CFO)
        ├─ CRO Morocco (reports to Hassan + Group CRO)
        ├─ Chief Sharia Officer (Dr. Abdel Aziz — elevated to group governance council)
        ├─ Head of Regulatory Affairs—Morocco (reports to Hassan + Group Head Regulatory)
        └─ [Technology & Digital report to Group CDO for migration milestones]
```

**Key Design Decisions:**
1. **All three MDs retained** (signals stability; preserves regulatory relationships; reduces organizational resistance).
2. **Dual reporting for CROs:** Local accountability to Market MD + Group CRO (ensures local risk ownership AND group risk standardization; conflict resolution via escalation, not override).
3. **Group CDO (new role):** Recommend **internal promotion of Raj (Kenya CTO) to Group CDO** (preserves Kenya cultural fit; fast onboarding; technical credibility with local IT teams; avoids 8–12 week external hire delay). **Interim Kenya CTO** required (external hire or internal promotion from Kenya IT team).
4. **Dr. Abdel Aziz elevated:** Morocco Chief Sharia Officer joins group governance council (seats on Risk Committee, Technology Committee); signals group respect for Islamic finance preservation; ensures Sharia expertise embedded in all technology decisions (not sidelined as "compliance function").
5. **No redundancy announcements until Month 11+** (post-Kenya go-live success confirmed). Early redundancy announcements trigger departures of 25–35% of senior staff Months 3–6; organizational capacity reduced exactly when needed for Phase 1 execution.

**Talent Retention Strategy:**
- **MD Retention Agreements:** Kunle, Jane, Hassan sign 2–3 year employment contracts with 40–50% annual bonus tied to (a) phase go-live success, (b) regulatory incident avoidance, (c) customer satisfaction maintenance.
- **High-Flight-Risk Talent:** Lock retention of Chisom (Nigeria digital), Raj (Kenya CTO → Group CDO), Sarah (Kenya head of digital), Dr. Abdel Aziz (Morocco Sharia officer) with **40–50% retention bonuses** + **"severance protection" clause** (if made redundant, severance ≥12 months salary). Budget: $5–8M over 2 years.
- **Backfill Hiring Delayed:** HR does not hire replacements for departures until Month 6+ (absorb turnover via temporary cross-training; preserves integration budget for migration capex vs. recruitment costs).
- **Knowledge Transfer Protocol:** Before any senior departure, 4-week formal knowledge transfer (successor shadowing + documentation) mandatory; enforced via severance clause (severance withheld if knowledge transfer incomplete).
