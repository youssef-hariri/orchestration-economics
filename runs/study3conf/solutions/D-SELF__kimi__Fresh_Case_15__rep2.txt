## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The Asymmetric Crisis.** Fixed USD-denominated royalties now consume **22–41% of franchisee operating cash flow (OCF)** in Turkey, Egypt, and Nigeria—triple the sustainable threshold. In Indonesia and Brazil, moderate depreciation and manageable inflation have not yet triggered acute insolvency, but franchisee associations are mobilizing for preemptive concessions. Left unaddressed, we project a **12–18% net unit decline** in distressed markets within 18 months, system-wide quality degradation, regulatory hostility, and collective litigation that would destroy **>$180 million in NPV** over the five-year planning horizon.

**The Thesis.** Permanent royalty concessions are structurally irreversible and value-destructive. The correct strategy is a **tiered, temporary, and legally enforceable recalibration** of revenue extraction—the **Emerging Market Adaptive Royalty (EMAR)** architecture—differentiated between **Crisis Markets** (Turkey, Egypt, Nigeria) and **Stabilization Markets** (Indonesia, Brazil). This is paired with an **$85 million captive financing facility**, supply-chain monetization, and sharply differentiated negotiation playbooks.

**The Financial Outcome.** The EMAR program preserves **~$166 million in NPV** at an estimated restructuring cost of **<$17 million NPV**, net of recaptured supply-chain margins, deferred-receivable yields, and liquidity-facility returns. Corporate stores in hard-currency jurisdictions will fund the liquidity facility; they remain operationally ring-fenced and accretive.

**The Board Ask.**
1. **Authorize** the **$85 million Emerging Market Franchise Liquidity Facility (EMFLF)** ($50 million internal treasury reallocation as subordinated capital; $35 million senior revolving credit facility secured by hard-currency market receivables).
2. **Delegate** authority to the CEO and General Counsel to execute jurisdiction-specific EMAR addenda within the parameters of this framework, subject to local enforceability opinions.
3. **Adopt** the macroeconomic graduation triggers, MFN firewall policy, and legal compliance protocols as binding franchise system policy.

## II. THE EMAR ARCHITECTURE: A TIERED APPROACH

A one-size-fits-all chassis invites cross-border precedent risk and over-concedes in moderate-distress markets. We deploy two tiers:

| Feature | **EMAR-Full** (Crisis Markets) | **EMAR-Selective** (Stabilization Markets) |
|---------|-------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| **Markets** | Turkey, Egypt, Nigeria | Indonesia, Brazil |
| **BLF** | 4.0% of Gross Revenue, LCY | 4.0% of Gross Revenue, LCY |
| **BSF** | 1.5% of Gross Revenue, USD-benchmarked | 1.5% of Gross Revenue, USD-benchmarked |
| **FX Absorption Trigger** | >20% depreciation from baseline | >30% depreciation from baseline |
| **Hardship Cap** | 10% of OCF; auto-activated | 12% of OCF; activated upon audited application |
| **CPI Indexation** | Monthly (Turkey) / Quarterly (Egypt, Nigeria) | Quarterly |
| **Monthly CPI Cap** | 8% (Turkey only) | N/A |
| **Energy/Base Exclusions** | Yes (Nigeria diesel) | No |
| **Sunset** | 36 months | 24 months |
| **Marketing Fund** | 4.0%, local escrow (Turkey, Egypt) / cooperative (Nigeria) | 4.0%; bilateral governance committee (Indonesia) / standard remittance (Brazil) |

### Core Component Mechanics

**A. Base License Fee (BLF) — 4.0% of Gross Revenue, payable in LCY**
Adjusted by the greater of (i) local CPI or (ii) F&B Producer Price Index, at the frequency specified above. Caps prevent runaway indexation. BLF eliminates the FX mismatch on the core intellectual property fee.

**B. Brand Services Fee (BSF) — 1.5% of Gross Revenue, USD-benchmarked**
Payable in LCY at the contract baseline FX rate. Subject to the **FX Absorption Band**: if LCY depreciation exceeds the tier trigger, the franchisor absorbs 50% of the excess FX move via a BSF reduction, **never below the floor** (0.75% in Full; 1.0% in Selective). This preserves hard-currency economics within a tolerable band while sharing tail risk.

**C. Marketing Fund — 4.0% of Gross Revenue, LCY**
Held in a locally governed account or remitted to a cooperative. Non-negotiable and ring-fenced from restructuring to protect consumer traffic and brand equity.

**D. Hardship Cap**
Total royalties (A+B+C) cannot exceed the OCF ceiling defined for the tier. If exceeded, Component B scales down to its statutory floor. **OCF is defined strictly as:** Gross Revenue less COGS, less Labor, less Occupancy, less Utilities, and less Local Taxes, *before* any royalty, brand, or marketing fees. OCF shall be calculated without deduction for (i) discretionary management fees paid to franchisee affiliates exceeding 3% of Gross Revenue, (ii) non-market-rate leases with affiliated entities, or (iii) distributions to equity holders. OCF is subject to verification by an independent auditor.

**E. Sunset & Graduation**
All relief provisions expire automatically upon the earlier of the tier sunset period or the satisfaction of macroeconomic graduation triggers (Section VIII). No extensions without Board approval.

## III. MARKET-SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATIONS

### TURKEY — EMAR-Full: The Formulaic Anchor
*Economic Core:* Hyperinflation and chronic lira depreciation. USD royalties are consuming franchisee cash flow and inviting UFRAD-led litigation.

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| **BLF** | 4.0% of Gross Revenue, TRY. Adjusted **monthly** by the greater of prior-month CPI or F&B PPI. **Hard cap:** 8% per month cumulative increase. |
| **BSF** | 1.5%, USD-benchmarked at contract baseline. FX Absorption Band at >20% depreciation; floor at **0.75%**. |
| **Marketing Fund** | 4.0%, held in a **franchisor-controlled local escrow** until CBRT FX availability permits repatriation or natural hedging. |
| **Hardship Cap** | Total royalties ≤ **10% of OCF**. If triggered, BSF floor drops to **0.5%**. |
| **Repayment / Deferral Yield** | Deferred BSF accrues a **Liquidity Support Fee** at TR CB policy rate + 400 bps (structured as a deferred service charge to avoid usury recharacterization). Repaid via (i) 1.25% royalty uplift for 24 months post-sunset, or (ii) a **System Continuity Fee** equal to 5% of the franchise transfer value if sold within 5 years. |

**Regulatory & Repatriation Nuance.** CBRT FX rationing prevents immediate USD repatriation. BSF collections will be deposited into a **franchisor-controlled local FX account**. If repatriation is unavailable for >60 days, trapped TRY may be converted into FX-protected lira instruments (e.g., KKM-linked deposits) or applied against local supplier payables via intra-company netting. The addendum explicitly defines the FX rate as the franchisor’s designated correspondent bank buy rate on the payment due date.

**Negotiation Protocol.** Engage UFRAD’s Technical Committee and the five largest franchisees (controlling 45% of units). Frame EMAR as a **mathematical necessity** to prevent system collapse, not a concession. Appoint a Turkish national as co-spokesperson. **Red line:** No negotiation on monthly CPI caps or brand standards. **Lever:** Establish a **Turkey Franchisee Innovation Council** with formal input on menu localization. **BATNA:** Proceed with individual EMAR opt-ins, bypassing UFRAD; convert the weakest 10% of non-compliant units to corporate-managed stores under existing default provisions.

### EGYPT — EMAR-Full: The Elite Pilot
*Economic Core:* Managed float with persistent official/parallel-market divergence (15–25% gap). Repatriation risk for USD royalties. Master franchisees dominate the landscape.

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| **BLF** | 4.0% of Gross Revenue, EGP. Quarterly CPI adjustment, capped at **6% per quarter**. |
| **BSF** | 1.5%. The applicable FX rate is the **franchisor’s local correspondent bank buy rate** (not the CBE official rate if a material gap exists). |
| **USD Retention Account** | If repatriation is blocked, franchisees may deposit BSF into a **local USD retention account** held by the franchisor’s Egyptian subsidiary. Balances accrue interest at CBE corridor rate + 300 bps, settled via new-store development credits or intra-company service offsets. |
| **Hardship Cap** | Total royalties ≤ **10% of OCF**. If triggered, BSF converts to a **12-month non-interest-bearing note** (not forgiven). |
| **Development Kicker** | Franchisees accepting EMAR commit to **2 new-store development rights** within 18 months post-sunset, at a reduced $15,000 franchise fee (vs. $25,000 standard). |

**Regulatory & Repatriation Nuance.** To mitigate trapped-capital risk, the franchisor will seek **priority-sector exporter status** for its Egyptian subsidiary, which may improve queue position for FX repatriation. BSF notes held in the retention account are structured as **subordinated intra-company obligations**, not equity, to avoid foreign-ownership restriction complexities under Investment Law.

**Negotiation Protocol.** Private, closed-door sessions with the FECC Franchise Committee and dominant master franchisees (typically 2 groups control 60% of units). Frame EMAR as **protecting their sunk equity**. Grant the largest master franchisee **90-day “Pilot Partner” exclusivity** to socialize the model. **Red line:** Zero tolerance for split-rate gaming; the FX definition is unambiguous. **BATNA:** Trigger technical default under existing master franchise agreements for FX-gaming or development shortfalls; offer an **equity-cure joint venture** (franchisor takes 15% of the master entity) only as an alternative to termination, subject to legal permissibility. **Walk-away:** If master franchisees exploit the official/parallel spread, suspend central-kitchen and import-license support, freezing new product rollouts.

### NIGERIA — EMAR-Full: The MUO-Led Restructuring
*Economic Core:* Naira hyper-depreciation, diesel/energy shocks, and FX illiquidity. Formal associations lack cohesion; real power resides with 2–3 multi-unit operators (MUOs).

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| **BLF** | **Revenue-band schedule:** 4.5% on first ₦150M annual revenue; 3.5% on revenue above ₦150M; 2.5% above ₦400M. Protects high-throughput MUOs with thin margins. |
| **Energy Exclusion** | Diesel, generator, and grid-power costs are **excluded from the royalty base** (treated as pass-through below-the-line). |
| **BSF** | **1.0%** (reduced from 1.5%) with no FX absorption band due to extreme volatility. Payable into a **LCY collection account** converted to USD monthly by the franchisor via the I&E window or, if inaccessible, invested in short-term CBN OMO bills pending FX clearance. |
| **Hardship Cap** | **8% of OCF** (tighter than other markets due to higher operating risk). |
| **Supply Co-op Rebate** | MUOs importing through the franchisor’s SCC facility receive an additional **0.5% BLF rebate**. |

**Regulatory & Repatriation Nuance.** CBN FX policy is unpredictable. To avoid trapped cash, the franchisor will minimize local BSF accumulation by (i) netting BSF against local supplier payables, and (ii) utilizing **pre-export financing structures** where local USD collections collateralize import letters of credit for the SCC program. All intra-group flows must comply with FCCPC transfer-pricing guidelines.

**Negotiation Protocol.** Target the **top 3 MUOs** directly (35% of units, 50% of system revenue). One-on-one CEO meetings. Present a **“system collapse”** scenario: without EMAR, 40% of single-unit operators fail, concentrating market power with informal street food. Offer **Franchisee Health Bridge Loans** (Section IV) **exclusively to MUOs for 90 days** before broader market access. Condition association-wide rollout on written MUO backing. **Red line:** No concessions for single-unit operators who refuse mandatory centralized digital POS (cash-leakage prevention). **BATNA:** Permit single-unit failures; consolidate territories to MUOs or corporate via distressed asset purchases at 0.4x revenue.

### INDONESIA — EMAR-Selective: The Co-Creation Model
*Economic Core:* Moderate rupiah depreciation and manageable inflation. Distress is real but not existential; regulatory nationalism and AFI’s consensus culture are the dominant strategic risks.

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| **BLF** | 4.0% of Gross Revenue, IDR. Quarterly CPI adjustment, capped at **3% per quarter**. |
| **BSF** | 1.5%, standard USD benchmark. FX Absorption Band activates only if IDR depreciates >**30%** from baseline. |
| **Efficiency Rebate** | If system same-store sales (SSS) growth exceeds BI inflation + 200 bps in a calendar year, BSF is reduced by **0.25%** for the following year, funded by supply-chain savings. |
| **Joint Governance** | Marketing escrow managed by a **bilateral franchisor-AFI committee**. |
| **Hardship Cap** | **12% of OCF**; activated only upon audited application with Big 4 or equivalent verification. |

**Regulatory & Repatriation Nuance.** Bank Indonesia maintains credible FX oversight; repatriation risk is low. The primary risk is regulatory nationalism if the franchisor is perceived as extractive. The bilateral governance structure and joint white paper (see Negotiation Protocol) provide political insulation.

**Negotiation Protocol.** Engage AFI (Asosiasi Franchise Indonesia) through a formal **6-month *musyawarah* (consultative) task force** co-chaired by the franchisor Regional President and AFI Chairman. Commit to publishing a **joint white paper**—“Sustainable Franchising in ASEAN”—positioning Indonesia as the regional benchmark. **Red line:** Marketing fund escrow is non-negotiable (protects against localization pressure). **BATNA:** Unilateral EMAR implementation is commercially viable given moderate distress; the *musyawarah* is preferred but not strictly necessary. **Fallback:** If AFI demands deeper cuts, extend the **efficiency rebate to 0.5%** (funded by expanded local procurement rebates) but require a **5-year contract renewal** and a local franchisee training academy co-investment. **Walk-away:** If negotiations stall beyond 6 months, implement EMAR unilaterally and redirect relationship investment to the Ministry of Cooperatives and SMEs.

### BRAZIL — EMAR-Selective: The Contractual Firewall
*Economic Core:* Cyclical real weakness but deep hedging markets and highly sophisticated legal culture. Risk is judicialization and “most favored nation” (MFN) spillover across Latin America.

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| **BLF** | 4.0% of Gross Revenue, BRL. Quarterly CPI adjustment, capped at **4% per quarter**. |
| **BSF** | 1.5%, standard USD benchmark. FX Absorption Band activates only if BRL depreciates >**30%** from baseline; floor at **1.0%**. |
| **Hardship Cap** | **12% of OCF**, activated only upon submission of audited financials by a **Big 4 or equivalent firm** (BDO Brasil, RSM, etc.). |
| **MFN Firewall** | Addendum explicitly states Brazilian terms are **market-specific**, driven by unique macroeconomic conditions, and create **no rights or precedent** for franchisees in any other jurisdiction. |
| **Audit Rights** | Franchisor and ABF Technical Committee receive full **P&L audit rights** to verify OCF calculations annually. |

**Regulatory & Repatriation Nuance.** Brazil’s deep financial markets and BCB credibility minimize repatriation risk. The dominant legal risk is class-action aggregation under CDC principles. The MFN Firewall and binding arbitration clauses are the primary defenses.

**Negotiation Protocol.** Engage the ABF Technical Committee and individual franchisee legal representatives. Benchmark EMAR against **publicly disclosed or industry-rumored restructuring terms** of competing QSRs in Brazil. Present the model as the **most transparent and auditable** in the market. Hire an independent accounting firm to validate the Hardship Cap methodology and distribute the methodology paper to all franchisees before negotiations begin. **Red line:** Strict MFN Firewall; no arbitration forum shopping. **BATNA:** Invoke individual arbitration under **CAM-CCBC** rules (existing in most Brazilian franchise agreements) to prevent class-action aggregation. Highlight that CDC class-action exposure could exceed **R$200 million** under a negative precedent. **Fallback:** If ABF demands broader concessions, offer to **extend the hardship cap lookback period from quarterly to monthly** (more relief) in exchange for **binding arbitration of all EMAR disputes** and a strict no-strike/no-boycott pledge. **Walk-away:** If franchisees challenge the MFN Firewall or attempt to forum-shop across Latin America, litigate aggressively in São Paulo courts and suspend new-store incentives for the entire market until a favorable declaratory judgment is obtained.

## IV. FRANCHISE-FINANCING & LIQUIDITY ARCHITECTURE

Royalty relief alone is palliative. To preserve unit economics, the franchisor must inject **liquidity and working-capital structure**.

### 1. The Emerging Market Franchise Liquidity Facility (EMFLF)
Capitalize an **$85 million captive facility** ($50 million internal treasury reallocation as subordinated capital; $35 million senior revolving credit facility secured by hard-currency market receivables) to provide:

- **LCY Working Capital Guarantees:** The franchisor guarantees 25% of local bank lines extended to qualified franchisees, cutting local interest rates by 300–500 bps.
- **FX Forward Aggregation:** The facility aggregates franchisee USD needs (for imported inputs) and negotiates 90-day forwards at institutional rates, with the franchisor subsidizing the premium in Year 1.

### 2. Royalty Deferral with Yield (RDY)
Franchisees may defer up to **50% of Components A and B for up to 12 months**.
- Deferred amounts accrue a **Liquidity Support Fee** at **local central bank policy rate + 400 bps**.
- Repayment via **1.25% permanent royalty uplift** for 24 months post-deferral, or via a **System Continuity Fee** giving the franchisor a 5–8% option on franchisee entity equity (where legally permissible).
- *This converts a haircut into a high-yield receivable or equity-like upside.*

### 3. Supply Chain Capitalization (SCC)
Where legally viable, the franchisor centralizes procurement of core proteins, packaging, and kitchen equipment in USD, then **on-sells to franchisees on 90-day LCY terms**.
- Franchisor captures a **supply-chain margin** (15–25% on imported goods) that partially offsets lower BSF collections.
- Franchisees avoid black-market FX premia and improve cash conversion cycles.

### 4. CAPEX Leasing SPV
Establish a Dubai- or Singapore-based SPV to own kitchen equipment and POS technology.
- Franchisees pay **monthly LCY leases** (inflation-linked) instead of upfront USD capex.
- Removes FX mismatch on store remodels and reduces barriers to mandatory image upgrades.

### 5. Franchisee Health Bridge Loans (FHBL)
For markets facing acute distress (Nigeria, Egypt in Phase 1), offer **emergency 6-month bridge loans** in LCY, collateralized by:
- Personal/corporate guarantees of the franchisee;
- A lien on the franchise agreement (transfer restrictions);
- Assignment of insurance policies.

## V. RISK MITIGATION, LEGAL GUARDRAILS & BRAND PRESERVATION

### Operational & Brand Non-Negotiability
- **Quality Gates:** Any royalty relief requires a **current Operations Excellence Score (OES) ≥ 85%**. Relief is suspended if OES drops below 80% for two consecutive quarters.
- **Mandatory Reinvestment:** Franchisees receiving EMAR concessions must commit to **remodel/tech upgrades within 18 months**, funded via the CAPEX Leasing SPV. Relief cannot be used to dividend cash to owners.

### Economic & Legal Guardrails
- **Clawback & Cross-Default:** If a franchisee defaults on local tax, supplier, or landlord obligations while under EMAR, all deferred royalties and concessions accelerate immediately.
- **No Cross-Border Precedent (MFN Firewall):** Every EMAR addendum contains explicit language that terms are **market-specific** and create no rights for franchisees in other countries.
- **Transfer & Exit Restrictions:** Franchisees under hardship relief may not sell their units without franchisor approval and repayment of deferred amounts.
- **Franchisor NPV Protection:** To offset reduced royalty yield in distressed markets:
  - Increase approved-supplier rebates by 200 bps temporarily;
  - Monetize data/analytics services to franchisees in stable markets;
  - Maintain corporate store profitability in hard-currency markets as a Treasury backstop.

### Macroeconomic & Repatriation Risk
- **Local Currency Collection Accounts:** In Egypt, Nigeria, and Turkey, franchisees pay into franchisor-controlled local accounts. USD repatriation occurs when FX markets allow, or via **intra-company netting** with local supplier payables.
- **Political Risk Insurance:** For the EMFLF Facility and receivables, secure **OPIC/MIGA-style political risk coverage** (or private market equivalent) for expropriation and transfer restriction in Egypt and Nigeria.

## VI. PHASED IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP & DECISION TRIGGERS

### Phase 1: Stabilize & Diagnose (Months 1–3)
**Actions:**
- Activate **FHBL emergency liquidity** in Nigeria and Egypt (acute distress).
- Launch “listening tours” led by Regional Presidents in all five markets.
- Conduct franchisee-level P&L stress tests to calibrate Hardship Cap thresholds.

**Decision Triggers to Advance:**
- >60% of MUOs in Nigeria/Egypt accept emergency bridge terms; **OR**
- Association leadership in Turkey/Brazil/Indonesia agrees to formal working groups.

### Phase 2: Framework Design & Pilot (Months 4–8)
**Actions:**
- Finalize EMAR legal addenda with local counsel.
- **Pilot in Brazil and Turkey:** Brazil because of ABF sophistication (clean data); Turkey because of high volume and UFRAD institutional discipline.
- Launch CAPEX Leasing SPV and begin supplier negotiations for SCC.

**Decision Triggers to Advance:**
- >70% pilot adoption with **zero** brand-standard OES degradation; **AND**
- FX/Inflation triggers remain active (see Section VIII).

### Phase 3: Scaled Rollout (Months 9–15)
**Actions:**
- Deploy EMAR in **Indonesia, Egypt, and Nigeria**.
- Activate EMFLF local-currency guarantees and FX forward programs.
- Transition Nigeria/Egypt from FHBL to full EMAR.

**Decision Triggers to Advance:**
- Association sign-off or legal opinion confirming no class-action injunction risk; **AND**
- Central bank FX policy clarity (e.g., Egypt repatriation queue, Nigeria I&E window stability).

### Phase 4: Optimization & Exit (Months 16–24)
**Actions:**
- Monitor macro indicators for **graduation** from Hardship Cap provisions.
- Monetize deferred-receivable yields and supply-chain spreads.
- Publish case studies with AFI/ABF/UFRAD to lock in reputational gains.

**Graduation Triggers (Concessions Sunset):**
- **FX Stability:** LCY/USD spot rate remains within **15% of the EMAR baseline for 6 consecutive months**; **OR**
- **Inflation Stability:** Local CPI prints below **15% annualized for 2 consecutive quarters** (market-specific threshold: 25% for Turkey, 20% for Nigeria/Egypt).

### Master Macro Triggers for Reactivation
If, post-graduation, any market experiences:
- **FX:** >25% depreciation in 90 days; **OR**
- **Inflation:** CPI exceeds 20% annualized for 2 consecutive quarters,

EMAR-Full or EMAR-Selective provisions automatically reactivate for that jurisdiction, subject to a 30-day Board notification.

## VII. THE BOARD DECISION

To execute Project Bridge, the Board is asked to resolve as follows:

1. **Facility Authorization.** Approve the **$85 million Emerging Market Franchise Liquidity Facility (EMFLF)**, comprising $50 million in subordinated treasury reallocation and $35 million in a senior revolving credit facility secured by hard-currency market receivables.
2. **Delegated Authority.** Authorize the Chief Executive Officer and General Counsel, acting jointly, to negotiate and execute jurisdiction-specific EMAR addenda consistent with the tiered parameters, component floors, and sunset clauses set forth in this document, provided that local counsel delivers an enforceability opinion prior to execution in each market.
3. **Policy Adoption.** Ratify the macroeconomic graduation triggers, reactivation thresholds, and MFN firewall policy as binding franchise system standards, effective immediately.

**Time is a material term.** Every quarter of delay erodes approximately **$12 million in recoverable NPV** through accelerated franchisee insolvency and brand degradation. We recommend Board action at the present session.
