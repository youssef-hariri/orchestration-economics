# International Product Strategy, 2026–2029

## 1. Executive summary

### Strategic decision

Expand with **one globally shared adaptive-learning platform and four locally governed market modules**. Do not translate and relaunch the US product unchanged.

Standardize the technology that creates learning value:

- Adaptive sequencing, diagnostics, mastery estimation, remediation, and spaced practice
- Curriculum and content-authoring infrastructure
- Identity, permissions, analytics, reporting, and synchronization
- Security, consent, safeguarding, audit, and accessibility primitives
- Teacher assignment, override, and recommendation-explanation tools
- Experimentation and learning-outcome measurement

Localize the elements that determine educational legitimacy, safety, and adoption:

- Curriculum, textbook, examination, and competency mappings
- Instructional language, terminology, typography, audio, and examples
- Pedagogy, teacher workflows, parent controls, and screen-time defaults
- Content rights, cultural review, safeguarding, and data governance
- Hosting, integrations, devices, connectivity, payments, procurement, pricing, and support
- Partnerships, implementation, training, and government relations

### Portfolio roles

| Market | Strategic role | Initial proposition | Recommended entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| France | Trust and pedagogy reference | Transparent, teacher-controlled differentiation | Private schools, tutoring, inclusion programs |
| Vietnam | Supplementary-learning growth market | Curriculum-aligned mathematics, English, and exam support | Private schools, tutoring centers, selective DTC |
| Kenya | Inclusive-delivery innovation market | Affordable, offline, mobile-first, shared-device learning | One tightly scoped school or NGO model |
| Saudi Arabia | Institutional value market | Secure, Arabic-first, partner-led adaptive learning | Qualified private-school or institutional partner |

### Recommendation

Proceed as a **stage-gated portfolio**, not four simultaneous full launches.

- **2026:** validate target segments, buyers, legal requirements, content rights, economics, and platform foundations.
- **2027:** operate France and Vietnam as primary pilots; run one bounded Kenya pilot; prepare Saudi Arabia through partner and compliance work.
- **2028:** scale the strongest France and Vietnam segments; expand Kenya only where funding and delivery economics are proven; launch a narrow Saudi pilot.
- **2029:** pursue selective government and multi-school expansion, renewals, and portfolio decisions.

The company should operate no more than **two fully active launch markets and one preparatory market at a time**, unless additional staffing and capital are approved.

# 2. Global platform principles

## 2.1 Shared capabilities versus local elements

| Capability | Globally shared | Locally configurable or localized |
|---|---|---|
| Adaptive engine | Skill graph framework, diagnostics, sequencing, mastery estimation, remediation | Country calibration, mastery thresholds, approved learning paths |
| Curriculum | Standards registry, mapping tools, versioning | National standards, textbooks, examination crosswalks, pacing rules |
| Content | Authoring tools, item schemas, review workflow, accessibility metadata | Native authoring, translation, examples, terminology, approval status |
| Assessment | Analytics infrastructure and common measurement definitions | Local item formats, grading conventions, assessment validation |
| Teacher experience | Roles, assignments, overrides, recommendation explanations | Degree of autonomy, reporting, pacing, intervention workflows |
| Parent experience | Accounts, consent, controls, progress reporting | Language, visibility, messaging, payment, screen-time defaults |
| Infrastructure | Identity, APIs, synchronization, monitoring, security primitives | Hosting, integrations, device requirements, offline deployment |
| Accessibility | Core components and testing framework | Language/script testing, local devices, print and offline adaptations |
| Commercial platform | Packaging architecture, entitlements, billing framework | Price, payment methods, contract structure, support SLAs |
| Governance | Audit, consent, safeguarding, incident management | Local legal requirements, reviewers, escalation routes |

## 2.2 Adaptive-learning architecture

Build a curriculum-agnostic engine with:

- Versioned skill graphs and prerequisite models
- Diagnostic assessment
- Mastery estimation with uncertainty indicators
- Personalized sequencing and remediation
- Difficulty adjustment and spaced practice
- Teacher intervention recommendations
- Teacher assignment and override controls
- Explainable recommendation logs
- Model and content version tracking

The engine may recommend only from **locally approved, curriculum-mapped content**. It must not independently impose US grade sequences, mastery thresholds, or high-stakes decisions.

A global mastery model should be reused only where calibration demonstrates comparable performance. The platform must support country-specific calibration, thresholds, and—in some cases—separate models.

## 2.3 Curriculum and content infrastructure

Build once, configure locally:

- Standards and curriculum registry
- Textbook and examination crosswalks
- Local sequencing and pacing rules
- Native-authoring and translation workflows
- Reviewer attribution and approval status
- Effective dates, version history, and rollback
- Accessibility metadata
- Content-defect, correction, and takedown tracking
- Rights and license records

“Mapped,” “reviewed,” and “officially approved” must remain distinct claims.

## 2.4 Institutional and family editions

### Institutional edition

- Learner, parent, teacher, school, district, county, ministry, and partner roles
- Bulk provisioning and rostering
- Hierarchical administration
- Teacher assignments and intervention workflows
- Identity, LMS, and reporting integrations
- Multi-school dashboards
- Configurable hosting and deployment
- SLAs and incident response
- Training and implementation workflows
- Contract-end data export and deletion

### Family edition

- Parent consent and controls
- Family accounts
- Local payment methods
- Monthly and annual plans
- Local-language onboarding and reports
- Short-session defaults and break reminders
- Trials or limited free access
- Cancellation, refund, and consumer-support capability

# 3. Global product, policy, and trust principles

## Curriculum authority

“Curriculum aligned” requires:

- Grade, subject, and competency mappings
- Local prerequisite and sequencing rules
- Textbook and examination crosswalks
- Approved terminology
- Reviewer records and version history
- Teacher-visible explanations

Provide two operating modes:

1. **Coverage mode:** supports required content, pacing, and assessment windows.
2. **Mastery-support mode:** provides remediation and extension within approved boundaries.

Teachers and institutions should be able to set mandatory units, exam calendars, pacing constraints, and maximum remediation time.

## Content governance and rights

Every child-facing lesson or assessment must pass:

1. Subject-matter authoring or translation
2. Independent linguistic review
3. Local pedagogical and cultural review
4. Classroom comprehension testing
5. Safeguarding and accessibility review
6. Versioning and post-launch monitoring

Maintain a rights inventory covering curriculum documents, textbooks, examination or exam-like items, publisher content, illustrations, audio, translations, and adaptations.

The company must confirm commercial rights before launch. Local expert review does not itself constitute ministry approval or licensing authorization.

## Assessment

Keep separate:

1. **Diagnostic assessment:** identifies instructional need.
2. **Formative practice:** adapts learning and feedback.
3. **Summative or high-stakes assessment:** locally validated and controlled.

Platform scores must not automatically determine promotion, admissions, discipline, or permanent learner labels.

During outcome studies, freeze model and content versions except for safety fixes.

## Screen time

Global defaults should include:

- Short, age-appropriate sessions
- Break reminders
- Parent and teacher controls
- Offline, printable, group, and teacher-led alternatives
- Limits on notifications, streaks, and late-night use
- Reporting focused on mastery and next steps rather than minutes

Commercial engagement metrics must not override healthy-use safeguards.

## Privacy, safeguarding, and algorithmic governance

The global baseline includes:

- Data minimization and encryption
- Role-based access and audit logs
- Consent and assent records
- Retention, deletion, export, and contract-end destruction
- No advertising based on child data
- Moderated or disabled child communication by default
- Human review for consequential decisions
- Safeguarding reporting and escalation
- Fairness and calibration testing
- Separate language-proficiency and subject-mastery measures
- Teacher, parent, and learner correction or appeal mechanisms

The default should be **no pooling of identifiable learner data across countries**. Any use of aggregated or de-identified data for global model improvement requires separate legal, contractual, privacy, and fairness review.

# 4. Country strategies

## 4.1 France

### Strategic objective

Prove that adaptive learning can be transparent, privacy-preserving, accessible, and controlled by teachers.

### Initial segment and buyers

Prioritize one primary segment initially:

- Private schools or tutoring providers; or
- Inclusion and differentiated-learning programs.

Likely buyers include school owners, group academic directors, principals, tutoring operators, and local authorities. A private-school pilot should not be treated as evidence of public-sector demand.

### Curriculum and language

Initial scope:

- French national curriculum
- Socle commun competencies
- French mathematics and literacy
- Remediation and differentiated learning
- Selected collège, lycée, and examination pathways
- French assessment terminology and formats

Use French teachers and curriculum specialists for precise mappings. Native pedagogical authoring may be required where US content cannot be adapted without changing instructional assumptions.

### Product and pedagogy

Required:

- Teacher-controlled assignment and pacing
- Inspectable and editable recommendations
- Formative analytics rather than rankings
- No public leaderboards
- Short, purposeful sessions
- Teacher-directed home practice
- Reports focused on mastery and next steps
- European hosting options where required
- Identity and learning-management integrations selected with target buyers

Positioning:

> Transparent, teacher-controlled differentiation that supports professional judgment.

### Trust and governance

Before scale:

- French/EU pedagogical and privacy advisory group
- Data-flow documentation and DPIA materials
- Clear explanation of profiling, recommendations, retention, and access
- Teacher correction and override mechanisms
- Accessibility conformance evidence
- French-language support
- Confirmed rights for publisher, textbook, and assessment-derived content

### Channels and partnerships

Prioritize:

- Private schools and school groups
- Tutoring providers
- Inclusion organizations
- French publishers and curriculum specialists
- Teacher-training organizations
- European identity and LMS providers
- Independent research institutions

### Pricing

- Annual contracts aligned with the academic year
- Per-establishment or per-active-learner licensing
- Paid implementation and professional development
- Family subscriptions for supplementary use
- No premium gate on basic privacy, accessibility, or teacher controls

### Rollout

- **2026:** buyer, privacy, interoperability, content-rights, and segment discovery.
- **2027:** mathematics and literacy pilots.
- **2028:** expansion through school groups and selected institutional partners.
- **2029:** selective local-authority or public procurement.

### KPIs

- At least 80% of pilot teachers complete core workflows after training
- Fewer than 10% report unacceptable administrative burden
- 90%+ of launch content mapped and reviewed, weighted by learner exposure
- Pre-specified learning improvement on French-aligned measures
- Teacher trust and explainability above agreed baseline
- Pilot-to-paid conversion and renewal meet the financial plan
- Accessibility acceptance criteria achieved
- Zero unresolved critical privacy, safety, or accessibility findings

### Principal risks

- Teacher skepticism or concern about profiling
- Privacy and procurement delays
- Interoperability costs
- Perception of commercialization
- Insufficient evidence that private-school success transfers to public-sector demand

- Exam and revision modes
- Parent reports on mastery and next steps

> Curriculum-mapped adaptive practice that improves classroom teaching, tutoring, and home study.

- Conceptual mastery
- Curriculum practice
- Examination preparation

- Local teacher and publisher review
- Textbook and examination crosswalks
- Vietnamese customer support
- Local names, currency, geography, and daily contexts
- Explicit teacher-support positioning
- Rights clearance for publisher and assessment content
- Deployment-specific data and consent review

### Channels and partnerships

Prioritize:

- Private school groups
- Tutoring and English-center networks
- Curriculum publishers
- Teacher-training institutions
- Telecom and device providers
- Provincial education departments
- Local implementation and support providers

### Pricing

- Affordable monthly family plans
- Annual school-year plans
- School licenses by seat or active learner

- Telecom or sponsored bundles

### Rollout

- **2026:** validate segment, price, content rights, curriculum requirements, and support model.

- **2028:** expand through school networks after adoption, quality, and renewal gates.

### KPIs

- Competition with tutoring and local applications
- Curriculum or examination mismatch
- Procurement delays
- Provincial connectivity variation
- Insufficient evidence of incremental value over existing tutoring

## 4.3 Kenya

### Strategic objective

Prove meaningful adaptive learning under intermittent connectivity, shared devices, limited electricity, and constrained purchasing power.

### Initial segment and buyers

Select one primary pilot model after field validation:

1. Low-cost private schools using Android devices; or
2. An NGO or teacher-training program using offline and shared-device modes.

Potential buyers include school proprietors, NGOs, donors, county authorities, teacher-training institutions, and telecom/device partners.

Every pilot contract must identify:

- Pilot funder
- Implementer
- Device owner
- Charging, repair, and replacement owner
- Support owner
- Post-pilot payer
- Renewal evidence
- Safeguarding responsibilities

### Curriculum and language

Initial scope:

- Kenya Competency-Based Curriculum
- Foundational mathematics, literacy, and science
- English and Kiswahili where required
- Grade- and competency-level mappings
- Kenyan assessment relationships
- Local names, money, geography, and daily contexts

Language requirements must be validated by grade, subject, location, and school type. English and Kiswahili should not be assumed sufficient for every foundational-learning context.

### Product and pedagogy

Provide:

- Offline lessons and assessments
- Shared-device and teacher-led group modes
- Printable worksheets linked to digital records
- Audio for early literacy
- Low-data synchronization
- Battery-efficient use
- Teacher dashboards requiring minimal preparation
- Offline content updates and conflict-resolution mechanisms

Positioning:

> Measurable competency learning that works with limited connectivity, shared devices, and teacher mediation.

### Trust and governance

Use local teachers, teacher-training institutions, NGOs, and community organizations in review. Monitor outcomes by device mode, connectivity, gender, geography, language, and disability where lawful and appropriate.

### Assessment

Validate separately in:

- Individual connected use
- Shared-device classrooms
- Offline use
- Teacher-mediated group instruction

Measure both CBC competencies and examination-relevant outcomes where appropriate.

### Channels and partnerships

Prioritize:

- NGOs and foundations
- County education authorities
- Teacher-training institutions
- Low-cost private-school networks
- Mobile operators
- Device distributors
- Assessment and research organizations
- Community-based organizations

Contracts must address devices, charging, theft, repair, replacement, field support, content updates, data protection, and safeguarding.

### Pricing

- Low-cost school licenses
- Mobile-money payments
- Flexible family plans
- Sponsored and donor-funded access
- Telecom bundles
- Device-plus-content packages
- Outcome-based NGO or government contracts

Offline, shared-device, accessibility, and teacher support must be included in the base institutional offer.

### Rollout

- **2026:** field discovery, device and connectivity testing, language selection, and payer validation.
- **2027:** one fully staffed pilot; a second model requires separate funding and capacity.
- **2028:** scale only where synchronization, learning, unit economics, and post-pilot funding are proven.
- **2029:** expand selectively through counties, donors, telecoms, or sustainable school networks.

### KPIs

- Offline lesson completion rate
- Synchronization success above 98% in tested environments
- Data consumed per completed lesson
- Device/session success rate
- Teacher training completion and classroom utilization
- Learning gain versus comparable classrooms
- Post-pilot renewal or funding commitment
- Cost per active learner within approved range
- English/Kiswahili comprehension
- Support cost per learner
- No material exclusion of disabled or low-connectivity learners

### Principal risks

- Affordability and irregular payment
- Device access, charging, theft, and repair
- Teacher turnover and workload
- Donor discontinuity
- Language variation
- Field-support costs
- Failure to convert pilots into recurring funding

# 5. Channel and procurement strategy

Institutional sales require a separate product and operating model, including:

- Local contracting capability
- Curriculum and assessment evidence
- Security, privacy, accessibility, and safeguarding dossiers
- Implementation and teacher-training plans
- Local support and SLAs
- Multi-school reporting
- Independent evaluation
- Data export and deletion commitments
- Multi-year pricing and invoicing
- Clear post-pilot renewal terms

For every opportunity, document:

1. Curriculum approver
2. Budget holder
3. Security approver
4. Procurement authority
5. Contract signer
6. Implementer
7. Teacher decision-maker
8. Parent or community influence
9. Post-pilot payer

A ministry sponsor, donor, or school principal may support a pilot without controlling the eventual purchase.

## 5.2 School-led and partner distribution

Schools and partners can provide:

- Local credibility
- Teacher onboarding
- Family access
- Implementation and training
- Payment collection
- Usage support
- Evidence for institutional procurement

Partner due diligence should assess:

- Curriculum credibility
- Government or school access
- Implementation capacity
- Security and data competence
- Financial health
- Conflicts with competing platforms
- Support and renewal capability
- Exclusivity demands

Contracts should include implementation milestones, data-processing terms, safeguarding obligations, audit rights, customer ownership, transition assistance, and restrictions on unauthorized regulatory claims.

## 5.3 Direct-to-consumer channel

Use DTC to validate:

- Parent demand
- Pricing
- Language quality
- Product usability
- Trust messaging
- Retention and renewal

DTC is most relevant initially in Vietnam and France and may support selected urban or private-school segments in Saudi Arabia and Kenya.

DTC evidence must not be generalized to institutional demand. DTC requires:

- Local payment methods
- Parent consent
- Consumer cancellation and refund processes
- Responsible marketing
- Local support
- Churn and retention analysis
- No child-directed profiling

# 8. Decision gates

A market may move from pilot to scale only when it passes all applicable gates.

| Gate | Provisional requirement |
|---|---|
| Curriculum | At least 90% of launch content mapped and reviewed, weighted by learner exposure; no critical mismatch |
| Language | Two qualified local reviewers approve launch content; critical defects below agreed threshold |
| Rights | All launch content has documented commercial rights |
| Legal and policy | No unresolved critical privacy, safeguarding, accessibility, hosting, or procurement issue |
| Teacher adoption | At least 80% of pilot teachers complete core workflows after training |
| Workflow burden | Fewer than 10% report unacceptable administrative burden |
| Learning | Pre-specified improvement versus a local baseline or comparison |
| Trust | Teacher, parent, and institutional trust meets target |
| Operations | Local support, training, content correction, and incident response are functional |
| Kenya delivery | Offline synchronization exceeds 98% in target environments |
| Commercial | Renewal intent, post-pilot funding, and contribution-margin path meet the approved plan |
| Equity | No material exclusion of low-connectivity or disabled learners |
| Safety | Zero unresolved critical safeguarding incidents |

Thresholds should be finalized before each pilot and recorded in the pilot plan.

## Pause or exit triggers

Pause or exit if:

- Legal clearance is not obtained by the agreed date.
- No credible post-pilot payer is identified.
- Content defects remain above threshold after remediation.
- Learning outcomes are absent after the pre-specified evaluation period.
- A partner fails implementation, data, or safeguarding milestones.
- Localization or support costs exceed the approved ceiling.
- The product materially disadvantages multilingual, low-connectivity, or disabled learners.
- The company cannot staff the required support and governance model.
- The market fails the approved contribution-margin or investment-loss limit.

# 10. Evidence and algorithmic governance

Each pilot must define before launch:

- Primary learning outcome
- Secondary outcomes
- Baseline instrument
- Comparison design
- Minimum detectable effect
- Sample size and duration
- Exposure definition
- Attrition threshold
- Subgroup analysis
- Model and content version
- Independent evaluator
- Rules for interpreting null or mixed results

Algorithm governance must include:

- Calibration by country and language
- Fairness testing by lawful and relevant subgroups
- Separate language-proficiency and subject-mastery variables
- Confidence thresholds before pathway changes
- Teacher, parent, and learner correction workflows
- Periodic model review
- Human approval for consequential recommendations
- No automated high-stakes decisions
- Investigation of material disparities in error or override rates

Do not treat engagement, NPS, recommendation acceptance, or time-on-platform as substitutes for learning evidence.

# 11. Risks and mitigations

| Curriculum mismatch | All | Versioned curriculum maps, local review, teacher-visible crosswalks, curriculum-change monitoring |

| Screen-time backlash | All | Short sessions, controls, break reminders, offline/print alternatives, healthy-use metrics |

# 13. Assumptions requiring validation

The following remain hypotheses rather than established facts:

## Demand and pricing

- Vietnamese families will pay for an additional subscription.
- French private-school success will translate into broader institutional demand.
- Saudi private schools will purchase before government endorsement.
- Kenyan pilots will convert into recurring school, donor, county, or NGO funding.
- Institutional buyers will accept per-active-learner or proposed annual pricing.
- Sponsorship and telecom bundles will produce sustainable economics.

## Curriculum and content

- Existing US content can be adapted economically rather than substantially rewritten.
- Local curriculum maps can be maintained at sustainable cost.
- English and Kiswahili are sufficient for the selected Kenyan segments.
- Arabic content can meet required quality within the planned production capacity.
- Local schools will accept platform diagnostics alongside existing assessments.
- Required textbook, examination, and publisher rights can be obtained economically.

- Identified partners can implement, support, and renew—not merely introduce leads.
- Public procurement cycles fit the proposed timeline.
- A ministry or institutional sponsor can identify a viable contracting route.
- Private schools can purchase without additional approvals.
- Donor-funded pilots can convert into recurring contracts.

## Technology and operations

- Offline synchronization is reliable in target Kenyan environments.
- Target learners have adequate device access.
- Institutional buyers will accept the proposed cloud, regional-hosting, or deployment model.
- French schools can support required identity and LMS integrations.
- Local-language support can meet the required SLA.
- The startup can staff all required content, legal, support, field, and partner functions.

## Trust and outcomes

- Parents will view short educational sessions as productive screen time.
- Teachers will use dashboards without mandates or excessive training.
- Learning gains will appear within a commercially viable pilot period.
- Shared mastery metrics will remain valid across curricula and languages after calibration.
- Explainability and teacher control will materially improve trust.
- Engagement metrics will not conflict with healthy-use goals.

Every assumption must have:

- Owner

# Final recommendation

Approve the expansion direction as a **stage-gated, capacity-constrained portfolio**.

Retain:

- **France** as the reference market for privacy, transparency, accessibility, and teacher autonomy;
- **Vietnam** as the market for curriculum-aligned supplementary learning and family demand;
- **Kenya** as a bounded experiment in affordable, offline, shared-device delivery;
- **Saudi Arabia** as a partner-led institutional opportunity requiring Arabic, security, cultural, and procurement readiness.

The company’s defensible advantage should be the combination of:

1. A reusable adaptive-learning and governance platform;
2. A disciplined curriculum, content-rights, and localization operating system;
3. Local language, pedagogy, trust, and implementation capabilities;
4. Deployment models suited to each country’s infrastructure and procurement;
5. Evidence that learning outcomes, safety, equity, and economics hold locally.

Scale any market only when curriculum legitimacy, content rights, learning evidence, buyer commitment, operational readiness, sustainable economics, and harm-prevention controls are demonstrated together.
