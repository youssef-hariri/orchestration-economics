# Supplier Integration Playbook  
## Mexico, Morocco, and Vietnam

## 1. Purpose and scope

This playbook governs the qualification, production transfer, launch, stabilization, requalification, suspension, and exit of suppliers added during tariff realignment and expanded export controls.

It applies to:

- Parts, assemblies, tooling, software, firmware, and technical data
- Manufacturing sites and approved subcontractors
- Logistics lanes, brokers, and freight providers
- Relevant sub-tier suppliers
- Customer, regulatory, cybersecurity, and responsible-sourcing activities

### Governing principle

> Replace assumptions with evidence: controlled specifications, demonstrated understanding, validated processes, confirmed commitments, lawful transactions, traceable records, clear authority, and measured performance.

Geography may affect regulation, logistics, language, and working calendars. It must not be used to stereotype individuals or replace evidence-based qualification.

---

# 2. Operating model

## 2.1 Mandatory global controls

Every supplier and site must have:

### Product and process controls

- One controlled specification package and revision history.
- Defined document precedence for drawings, CAD, BOMs, software, firmware, tests, packaging, and purchase orders.
- Objective acceptance criteria, test methods, measurement conditions, and defect classifications.
- Approved manufacturing site, materials, tooling, equipment, software, firmware, and sub-tier sources.
- Written deviation and engineering-change approval.
- Risk-appropriate traceability and lot genealogy.
- Calibrated measurement and test equipment.
- Applicable process flow, PFMEA, control plan, work instructions, and reaction plan.
- Production-like process validation before volume release.
- Operator competency verification.
- Nonconformance containment and corrective-action procedures.

### Delivery controls

- Standard definitions for forecast, firm order, frozen period, ship date, arrival date, receipt date, delivery window, partial shipment, and force majeure.
- Written purchase-order acknowledgment.
- Confirmed quantity, dates, capacity, materials, tooling, logistics assumptions, and sub-tier dependencies.
- Milestone-based schedule monitoring.
- Advance notification of material, capacity, quality, compliance, and logistics risks.
- One OTIF formula with documented tolerances, exclusions, and delay attribution.

### Compliance controls

- Sanctions and denied-party screening.
- Export-control classification and authorization review.
- Controlled access to technical data, software, firmware, source code, keys, and test tools.
- Country-of-origin and customs-record accuracy.
- Product-safety, environmental, labor, anti-bribery, and responsible-sourcing controls.
- Subcontractor and sub-tier disclosure.
- Intellectual-property protection and cybersecurity controls.
- Applicable customer, market, and regulatory approvals.
- Defined retention, audit, evidence, and incident-reporting requirements.

### Governance controls

- Named primary and backup contacts.
- Documented decision rights and no-go authorities.
- Central issue, assumption, deviation, change, decision, and approval logs.
- Severity-based escalation.
- Defined production-release, suspension, requalification, and exit criteria.
- Supplier and manufacturer performance scorecards.
- Fact-based dispute resolution.

## 2.2 Locally adaptable practices

The following may vary by site without weakening global controls:

- Working language and translation format
- Meeting times and communication channels
- Shop-floor language and training methods
- Holiday and shutdown planning
- Local brokers, carriers, ports, crossings, and inland routes
- Audit frequency and supplier-development support
- PPAP, equivalent quality dossier, or proportionate submission format
- Local payment mechanics, subject to contract and applicable law

# 3. Risk classification and qualification tracks

## 3.1 Risk factors

Rate each part, supplier, and site for:

- Safety, regulatory, and customer impact
- Electrical, battery, firmware, software, and cybersecurity sensitivity
- Export-control and technical-data sensitivity
- Technical complexity and process maturity
- Failure-consequence severity
- Single-source exposure
- Tooling and qualification lead time
- Sub-tier dependency
- Country-of-origin and tariff importance
- Border, port, and transport exposure
- Ramp volume, staffing, overtime, and temporary-labor requirements
- Financial and business-continuity risk

## 3.2 Qualification tracks

| Track | Typical application | Minimum approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Full qualification** | Batteries, PCBA, complex electronics, connected products, safety-critical parts, export-controlled items, single-source or high-consequence parts | Full due diligence, on-site audit, capacity and bottleneck validation, process qualification, production-like pilot, customer/regulatory approval, logistics rehearsal, cybersecurity review, continuity assessment, enhanced launch controls |
| **Proportionate qualification** | Low-risk packaging, standard mechanical parts, commodities, readily substitutable items | Desktop due diligence, targeted audit, first article, proportionate process documentation, enhanced initial inspection, and defined performance observation |

A part may use the proportionate commercial track but still require full controls for product safety, batteries, cybersecurity, export controls, technical data, or critical sub-tiers.

## 3.3 Minimum qualification file

Before production release, the file must contain, as applicable:

- Legal-entity and beneficial-ownership review
- Sanctions and export-control screening
- Product and market regulatory applicability assessment
- Customer-approval determination
- Quality and process audit
- Capacity, staffing, and bottleneck evidence
- Sub-tier map
- Controlled specification package
- Compliance matrix and assumptions log
- Process flow, PFMEA, control plan, and work instructions
- Measurement-system and capability evidence
- First-article or equivalent submission
- Pilot-lot results
- Traceability demonstration
- Packaging and logistics validation
- Cybersecurity and technical-data approval
- Business-continuity assessment
- Accepted residual-risk record

Any waiver must state the reason, risk assessment, compensating control, owner, expiry date, and approval authority.

### Supplier representatives

- Executive sponsor
- Program manager
- Quality manager
- Engineering lead
- Production manager
- Planning and logistics lead
- Compliance contact
- Cybersecurity/data-security contact
- Regulatory contact, where applicable
- Primary and backup contacts for each critical role

## 4.2 Decision rights

The RACI must identify who may:

- Release or revise specifications
- Approve technical translations
- Commit capacity
- Confirm delivery dates
- Approve deviations
- Authorize shipment of suspect product
- Approve material, tooling, process, site, software, firmware, or sub-tier changes
- Approve customer and regulatory submissions
- Release production
- Accept residual risk
- Suspend, requalify, or exit a supplier

## 4.3 No-go authorities

Commercial urgency may not override these stops:

| Risk | No-go authority |
|---|---|
| Product safety or regulatory noncompliance | Quality or regulatory authority |
| Export-control or sanctions concern | Trade-compliance/legal authority |
| Cybersecurity or controlled-data concern | Security/privacy authority |
| Unclear product requirements | Engineering authority |
| Unvalidated process or critical defect | Quality authority |
| Critical continuity risk | Operations executive |
| Contract, liability, anti-bribery, or privacy concern | Legal and authorized functional authority |

# 5. Phase-gate plan

## Phase 0 — Stabilize and define  
**Timing: Weeks 0–4**

### Objectives

Prevent uncontrolled production, clarify ownership, and protect continuity while qualification proceeds.

### Activities

1. Create the transition charter and part/supplier register.
2. Freeze the approved specification baseline.
3. Identify affected parts, tooling, software, firmware, technical data, sites, and sub-tiers.
4. Assign risk ratings and qualification tracks.
5. Screen entities, ownership, intermediaries, destinations, end uses, and technical-data transfers.
6. Review tariff classification, origin, and preferential-trade eligibility.
7. Identify customer, regulatory, safety, environmental, and market approvals.
8. Identify long-lead and single-source components.
9. Establish interim inventory, alternate-route, and contingency plans.
10. Name owners, backups, no-go authorities, and on-call personnel.
11. Establish secure document, issue, deviation, change, and escalation systems.
12. Confirm internal engineering, quality, legal, regulatory, cybersecurity, translation, and logistics capacity.

### Deliverables

- Transition charter
- Risk register
- Approved-source and approved-process list
- Specification baseline
- Compliance-screening record
- Tariff and origin analysis
- Regulatory applicability matrix
- Capacity and lead-time model
- RACI and contact list
- Escalation matrix
- Shared holiday and shutdown calendar
- Resource and service-level plan
- Phase dependency map

### Exit criteria

- Every part has an owner, risk rating, qualification track, and release target.
- No production may begin against obsolete or uncontrolled documents.
- Compliance has cleared the transaction and technical-data structure.
- Required customer and regulatory approvals are identified.
- Critical parts have interim continuity protection.
- Hard gates and parallel workstreams are documented.

## Phase 1 — Due diligence and prequalification  
**Timing: Weeks 2–8**

### Review areas

#### Legal, compliance, and responsible sourcing

Assess:

- Legal entity, beneficial ownership, and control
- Sanctions and denied-party exposure
- Export-control classification and licensing requirements
- Anti-bribery, gifts, hospitality, agents, and conflicts of interest
- Forced labor, recruitment fees, wages, working hours, and grievance mechanisms
- Environmental permits, hazardous materials, waste, water, and emissions
- Product-safety obligations
- Data protection, cybersecurity, and IP controls
- Conflict-minerals and relevant battery-material due diligence
- Subcontractors and sub-tier suppliers
- Financial condition, insurance, and continuity planning

#### Manufacturing and quality

Verify:

- Actual good-output capacity at bottleneck processes
- Equipment condition, maintenance, uptime, and backup capability
- Tooling ownership and maintenance
- Test coverage and calibration
- ESD, moisture, temperature, cleanliness, and storage controls
- Staffing, training, overtime, and temporary labor
- Material availability
- Outsourced-process and sub-tier dependency
- Corrective-action history and effectiveness
- Traceability, rework, scrap, and change control

#### Logistics

Verify:

- Export documentation
- Customs-broker capability
- Importer/exporter responsibilities
- Incoterms and named place
- Packaging and labeling
- Transit-time variability
- Border, port, and inland alternatives
- Carrier and freight-forwarder contingency plans
- Dangerous-goods capability for batteries and regulated materials

### Exit criteria

Critical findings must be closed or formally accepted. Conditional approval must specify:

- Action
- Owner
- Due date
- Interim control
- Expiry date
- Suspension or disqualification trigger

## Phase 2 — Specification and requirements alignment  
**Timing: Weeks 4–10**

This phase is the primary control against specification misunderstandings.

### Controlled requirements package

Each part package must contain, as applicable:

1. Current drawing and revision
2. Designated governing CAD model
3. Bill of materials
4. Approved materials and sources
5. Critical-to-quality characteristics
6. Key product and process characteristics
7. Tolerances, datums, and measurement conditions
8. Test methods and acceptance limits
9. Appearance and cosmetic standards
10. Golden sample or approved reference unit
11. Packaging, labeling, and shipping requirements
12. Traceability requirements
13. Reliability and environmental tests
14. Regulatory and certification requirements
15. Software or firmware version
16. Inspection and control-plan requirements
17. Deviation and nonconformance rules
18. Engineering-change requirements
19. Forecast, order, and delivery rules
20. Approved contacts and escalation routes

### Same-understanding review

The supplier must provide:

- Clause-by-clause compliance matrix
- Marked-up drawing
- Questions, assumptions, and exclusions
- Process flow
- Outsourced-process list
- Measurement and test plan
- Capacity and lead-time assumptions
- Proposed control plan
- Approved translation, where applicable

The supplier must demonstrate understanding by:

- Explaining critical characteristics
- Demonstrating measurement methods
- Showing workstation instructions
- Running an inspection or test
- Explaining reaction plans

A signed acknowledgment or verbal “understood” is not sufficient.

### Measurement and translation controls

For critical characteristics:

- Compare manufacturer and supplier results using the same samples, conditions, datums, and methods.
- Define acceptable measurement disagreement based on tolerance, uncertainty, gauge resolution, and product risk.
- Use an approved technical glossary.
- Require engineering review of critical translations.
- Retain the governing-language version.
- Back-check critical translated work instructions.
- Confirm shop-floor comprehension.
- Use approved secure translation and file-transfer tools.

### Exit criteria

- No unresolved critical assumptions
- Compliance matrix accepted
- Critical characteristics linked to process controls
- Measurement methods correlated
- Translations approved
- Delivery definitions accepted
- Document precedence confirmed

**Timing: Weeks 8–18**

### Required evidence

For full qualification:

- Process flow
- PFMEA
- Control plan
- Work instructions
- Measurement-system analysis
- Capability studies where statistically appropriate
- Inspection and test plan
- Traceability scheme
- Reaction plan
- Packaging validation
- Maintenance and calibration records

- First article, PPAP-style, or equivalent submission

- Normal operators and shifts
- Actual materials
- Production tooling
- Planned inspection and test equipment
- Standard work instructions
- Standard packaging and labels
- Actual or representative logistics routes

Where applicable, verify:

- Approved software and firmware versions

- Software bill of materials

- Secure-development responsibilities

- Build reproducibility
- Signing-key custody
- Factory-programming controls

- Device identity or certificate provisioning

## Phase 5 — Controlled ramp-up and launch  
**Timing: Weeks 16–28**

### Ramp sequence

1. Engineering or validation lot
2. Limited production release
3. Increased volume after stable results
4. Dual-source or alternate-route coverage where practical
5. Full production after product-specific release criteria are achieved

### Launch controls

- Enhanced incoming inspection
- Source inspection for high-risk parts
- Lot-level traceability
- Pre-shipment document review
- Confirmed logistics bookings
- Temporary safety stock
- Daily material, capacity, quality, delivery, and compliance dashboard
- Immediate escalation of critical events

### Production-release criteria

The product-specific validation plan must define pass/fail criteria. Typical requirements include:

- Accepted production-like lots or an approved statistical alternative
- No unresolved critical defects
- Stable yield
- Acceptable capability where applicable
- Completed reliability testing or approved interim controls
- Complete traceability
- Validated packaging and logistics
- Required customer and regulatory approvals
- Effective corrective actions
- Successful customs and import transactions
- No unresolved critical compliance or cybersecurity findings

Three accepted lots may be adequate for simple, stable parts but are not a universal rule for batteries, low-volume high-consequence parts, software-enabled products, or long-cycle reliability programs.

## Phase 6 — Stabilization and continuous improvement  
**Timing: Months 7–12 and ongoing**

Reduce controls only after evidence supports the change. Possible reductions include:

- Lower incoming-inspection levels
- Less frequent launch meetings
- Removal of temporary buffers
- Dock-to-stock authorization
- Normal scorecard governance

Continue:

- Layered process audits
- 8D or A3 problem solving
- Yield and scrap improvement
- Test-coverage improvement
- Packaging and freight optimization
- Forecast-accuracy improvement
- Sub-tier risk reduction
- Continuity exercises
- Joint engineering projects
- Supplier and manufacturer performance reviews

# 6. Regional execution guidance

These are operating considerations to validate with each site, not assumptions about individuals.

## Mexico

- Define whether delivery means ex-factory, border arrival, or plant receipt.
- Validate customs brokers, tariff classifications, origin records, importer/exporter responsibilities, and applicable trade programs.
- Review USMCA or other preferential-treatment eligibility; do not assume qualification.
- Confirm IMMEX or other program applicability where relevant.
- Maintain alternate crossings, carriers, and inland routes.
- Review trucking security and route-continuity risks.
- Verify overtime, temporary labor, and subcontracting assumptions.
- Use English-Spanish materials where appropriate.
- Schedule escalation across North American time zones.

## Morocco

- Confirm working language by function; French, Arabic, English, or a combination may apply.
- Have engineering approve technical translations.
- Pre-check certificates, labels, customs records, and export documentation.
- Confirm applicable Moroccan conformity, labeling, and market requirements.
- Plan against the actual site calendar, including national and religious holidays.
- Do not assume productivity impacts during Ramadan or other periods; use confirmed staffing and shift data.
- Include local logistics and compliance expertise.
- Allow time for formal administrative approvals.
- Control government-facing interactions through anti-bribery procedures.

## Vietnam

- Use plain English, visual aids, written question logs, and teach-back.
- Identify the actual authority for capacity, deviations, process changes, and subcontracting.
- Conduct factory-floor interviews and layered audits.
- Verify staffing, overtime, temporary labor, and sub-tier assumptions.
- Plan materials, capacity, and inventory well before Tet.
- Confirm customs, export-document, conformity, labeling, and data requirements.
- Have engineering approve translated drawings and work instructions.
- Use relationship-building to improve communication, not to replace formal approvals, anti-bribery controls, or written commitments.

# 7. Communication and escalation

## 7.1 Communication controls

- Agree the working language by function.
- Use a controlled technical glossary.
- Use concise text, diagrams, photographs, and annotated samples.
- Send agendas before meetings.
- Record decisions, owners, due dates, assumptions, and unresolved issues.
- Use read-back or teach-back for critical requirements.
- Do not treat silence as agreement.
- Do not rely on verbal approval for changes, deviations, or delivery commitments.
- Use approved secure collaboration and file-transfer tools.
- Prohibit personal messaging applications for controlled technical information.
- Maintain primary and backup contacts.
- Provide after-hours coverage.
- Test escalation contacts periodically.
- Track manufacturer response times as well as supplier response times.

## 7.2 Routine service levels

Initial targets should be adjusted to product risk and internal capacity:

| Activity | Initial target |
|---|---:|
| Critical technical question | 4 working hours |
| Standard technical question | 2 business days |
| Deviation blocking production | 1 business day |
| Drawing clarification | 1 business day |
| Regulatory question | 2 business days |
| Critical customer-approval issue | Same business day |
| Pilot-lot disposition after complete evidence | 2 business days |
| Production-release decision after complete evidence | 3 business days |
| Payment-dispute acknowledgment | 2 business days |

## 7.3 Severity levels

| Level | Example | Notification | Initial action |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Minor documentation error or isolated minor defect | 1 business day | Correct and track |
| 2 | Repeated defect, forecast risk, or late shipment with recovery plan | Same business day | Contain and issue recovery plan |
| 3 | Major defect, critical missed shipment, capacity shortfall, or unauthorized change | 4 hours | Hold or stop as appropriate |
| 4 | Safety, regulatory, export-control, sanctions, cybersecurity, or major continuity event | Immediate; no later than 1 hour | Secure product/data, stop affected activity, activate crisis team |

Every notification must state:

- What happened and when
- Affected parts, lots, or orders
- Quantity potentially affected
- Immediate containment
- Customer and production impact
- Known facts versus assumptions
- Named owner
- Next update time

There must be no retaliation for early escalation.

# 8. Change and configuration control

Written approval is required before changing:

- Manufacturing location
- Subcontractor or sub-tier source
- Material or formulation
- Tooling
- Test method
- Software or firmware
- Critical process sequence or equipment
- Packaging or labeling
- Country-of-origin information
- Inspection method
- Critical-process capacity plan

Each request must state:

- Affected parts and lots
- Reason for change
- Technical and regulatory impact
- Risk assessment
- Required validation
- Customer or regulatory approval
- Effective date
- Inventory disposition
- Temporary-deviation expiration
- Approval authority

Emergency containment may be authorized verbally only to protect people, product, or continuity. It must be documented and formally regularized promptly.

# 9. Quality, suspension, requalification, and disputes

## 9.1 Nonconformance process

1. Identify affected lots, shipments, and inventory.
2. Stop shipment or production where justified.
3. Contain supplier, in-transit, receiving, and production stock.
4. Notify affected functions and customers where required.
5. Perform sorting or temporary screening.
6. Establish root cause using evidence.
7. Implement permanent corrective action.
8. Validate effectiveness.
9. Close only after recurrence risk is reduced.

“Operator error” is not a sufficient root cause unless the underlying system weakness is corrected.

## 9.2 Suspension triggers

Consider suspension or shipment hold for:

- Critical safety or regulatory defect
- Repeated major defects
- Unauthorized site, process, material, software, firmware, or sub-tier change
- False or materially inaccurate records
- Sanctions, export-control, customs, or origin concern
- Cybersecurity incident
- Uncontrolled subcontracting
- Persistent capacity shortfall
- Failure to raise known risks
- Severe responsible-sourcing violation
- Financial distress
- Failure to close critical corrective actions

## 9.3 Requalification triggers

Require requalification after:

- Significant process, site, material, tooling, software, or sub-tier change
- Extended production inactivity
- Major quality or cybersecurity incident
- Ownership or legal-entity change
- Loss of required certification or approval
- Significant capacity expansion
- Material change in export-control or sanctions status

## 9.4 Dispute process

1. **Contain:** Protect people, product, customer supply, data, and evidence.
2. **Establish facts:** Review specifications, revisions, purchase orders, dates, inspection data, changes, deviations, Incoterms, and communications.
3. **Assign interim responsibility:** Agree who performs or funds sorting, rework, replacement, testing, premium freight, or customer containment.
4. **Resolve:** Use 8D or equivalent for quality issues and contract analysis for commercial issues.
5. **Escalate:** Working level, functional management, commercial/legal review, executive settlement or mediation, then contractual arbitration or court proceedings.

Interim cost allocation does not determine final liability. Responsibility must be attributed using evidence, including manufacturer-caused design changes, late approvals, inaccurate forecasts, supplier nonconformance, logistics failures, government action, and force majeure.

| Topic | Required review | Evidence | Release authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Export controls | Classification, destination, end user, end use, personnel access, re-export | Classification and authorization record | Trade compliance |
| Sanctions | Entity, ownership, intermediaries, logistics parties, transaction triggers | Screening record | Compliance/legal |
| Origin and tariffs | Substantial transformation, documentation, preference eligibility | Origin analysis and supplier records | Customs/trade lead |
| Product approval | Safety, EMC, radio, battery, environmental, labeling, and market requirements | Certificates, reports, declarations | Regulatory affairs |
| Cybersecurity | Data access, firmware, secure programming, vulnerabilities, incident response | Security assessment and approval | Security authority |
| Privacy | Personal-data access, storage, transfer, retention | Privacy assessment and transfer mechanism | Privacy/legal |
| Responsible sourcing | Labor, forced labor, recruitment, environment, minerals, remediation | Audit and corrective-action evidence | Responsible-sourcing authority |
| Contract enforceability | Governing law, liability, audit, data, labor, and disputes | Legal review | Legal |

## Screening triggers

Re-screen at:

- Supplier nomination
- Contract award
- Purchase-order release
- Shipment release
- Ownership change
- Addition of an intermediary or freight forwarder
- New end user or end use
- New technical-data transfer
- Material geopolitical or corporate change

Potential sanctions or export-control matches must trigger an automatic hold until cleared by the authorized function. No shipment, purchase-order release, production activity, or technical-data transfer may proceed for the affected transaction while the hold remains unresolved.

## Data controls

Classify and control:

- Personal data
- Employee records
- Customer data
- Controlled technical data
- Source code
- Firmware
- Signing keys
- Security logs
- Product telemetry
- Test tools and programming software

For each class, define permitted users, storage location, transfer mechanism, encryption, access logging, retention, deletion, incident notification, and approval authority.

# 11. Inventory and continuity

For each critical part, define:

- Minimum and maximum buffer
- Buffer location
- Funding owner
- Replenishment trigger
- Maximum inventory age
- Emergency allocation authority
- Alternate-route activation trigger
- Alternate-source qualification lead time
- Recovery-time objective
- Continuity-test frequency

Buffer levels must consider demand variability, supplier and transit lead time, border and port variability, alternate-source qualification time, customer service requirements, part criticality, shelf life, battery-storage limits, and obsolescence exposure.

Test continuity plans for:

- Border or port disruption
- Carrier failure
- Supplier-site outage
- Critical sub-tier failure
- Cybersecurity incident
- Export-control or sanctions hold
- Natural disaster
- Labor disruption
- Major quality containment
- Loss of supplier systems or traceability data

Each test must define the scenario, owner, maximum response time, required communication, minimum supply restored, data-recovery requirement, lessons-learned owner, corrective-action deadline, and retest requirement.

During constrained supply, use a preapproved allocation protocol covering safety-critical products, customer commitments, launches, service parts, strategic markets, existing inventory, and alternate-source availability.

# 13. KPIs and performance management

Every KPI must have a formula, source system, owner, reporting frequency, baseline, target, escalation threshold, corrective-action trigger, attribution rule, and data-quality control.

| KPI | Formula | Typical source | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| OTIF | Quantity received at destination within agreed window ÷ quantity due | ERP/WMS | Planning |
| Order-confirmation accuracy | Orders fulfilled as acknowledged ÷ confirmed orders | ERP | Supplier planning |
| Major defect rate | Major defects ÷ units inspected | QMS | Supplier quality |

## Suggested control targets

- Zero critical defects
- Zero unauthorized changes
- Zero unresolved critical assumptions at production release
- 100% required training completed with competency evidence
- 100% critical changes approved before implementation
- At least 95% corrective actions closed by due date
- No recurrence of closed critical issues
- 100% qualifying risk notifications within SLA
- Launch OTIF target set from baseline and lane risk
- Mature OTIF target set by commodity and demonstrated capability
- Cpk/Ppk ≥ 1.33 only where statistically appropriate

Track leading indicators including:

- Critical requirements with approved visual standards
- Open assumptions older than the defined limit
- Pilot operators fully trained
- Material availability before pilot
- Tooling readiness before pilot
- Critical sub-tiers audited
- Milestones at risk
- Days of advance warning for late delivery
- Unresolved document conflicts
- Customer approvals completed before production release
- Manufacturer approval delays
- Suppliers reporting risks before production impact

Scorecards must distinguish supplier-caused, manufacturer-caused, logistics-caused, government-caused, and force-majeure events.

# 14. First 90 days

## Days 0–15 — Stabilize

- Establish the control tower.
- Freeze specifications and identify obsolete documents.
- Rank parts and suppliers.
- Complete initial sanctions, export-control, origin, and tariff screening.
- Identify customer and regulatory approvals.
- Define delivery terms, no-go authorities, and escalation rules.
- Create assumptions and open-issues logs.
- Establish interim inventory and logistics protections.
- Confirm internal resource capacity and systems of record.

## Days 16–45 — Align

- Complete audits and capacity studies.
- Conduct requirement-by-requirement reviews.
- Approve translations and visual standards.
- Validate staffing, tooling, materials, bottlenecks, and subcontracting.
- Sign quality, commercial, cybersecurity, data, responsible-sourcing, and change-control agreements.
- Validate brokers, routes, Incoterms, labels, origin records, and regulatory submissions.
- Confirm customer-approval requirements.

## Days 46–75 — Prove

- Complete process documentation, MSA, capability studies, and training.
- Conduct production-like pilots.
- Run a logistics rehearsal using actual documents and labels.
- Demonstrate traceability, secure programming where applicable, and change control.
- Complete customer and regulatory submissions.
- Test escalation contacts and continuity procedures.
- Close or formally accept critical risks.

## Days 76–90 — Launch under control

- Release limited production.
- Use enhanced inspection and daily launch reviews.
- Track yield, demonstrated capacity, delivery adherence, milestone risk, compliance, and manufacturer approval delays.
- Apply formal containment and dispute procedures.
- Increase volume only after product-specific release criteria are achieved.

## Definition of success

A supplier is fully integrated when it can demonstrate that it:

- Uses the current approved requirements at the workstation.
- Produces conforming product under normal production conditions.
- Maintains validated capacity and realistic delivery commitments.
- Preserves traceability and follows change control.
- Meets agreed quality, delivery, regulatory, cybersecurity, and responsible-sourcing requirements.
- Escalates risks early and accurately.
- Protects technical data and complies with trade controls.
- Operates without hidden subcontracting or unsustainable emergency practices.
- Supports customer, warranty, recall, service-part, and reverse-logistics obligations.
- Participates in fair dispute resolution and measurable improvement.

> **Replace assumptions with evidence: controlled documents, demonstrated understanding, validated processes, confirmed commitments, lawful transactions, traceable records, clear authority, and measured performance.**
