# CROSS-BORDER REMITTANCE OPERATIONS STRATEGY 2026–2029
**Risk-Adjusted Corridor Expansion for EU, Gulf, Southeast Asia, and Latin America**

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

In 2026, regulatory fragmentation is no longer a background cost—it is the primary determinant of corridor profitability. The EU’s Payment Services Regulation (PSR) and Payment Services Directive 3 (PSD3) impose strict engineering and liability mandates. Southeast Asia enforces data-localization walls and market-specific licensing with zero passporting. Latin America offers high nominal margins but consumes capital through sovereign FX approval regimes and multi-year licensing horizons.

This strategy replaces our legacy “license-first, corridor-second” model with a **liquidity-centric, partner-enabled architecture** governed by **Net Margin After Compliance and Trapped Capital (NMAC-T)**. Every corridor must earn its cost of capital after charging for compliance drag, partner shares, and idle liquidity.

We commit to three non-negotiable constraints:
1. **Zero evasion.** Capital controls are managed through transparent documentation, authorized intermediaries, and documentary trade structures—not disaggregation or concealment.
2. **Realistic horizons.** Licensed entry into Brazil and Indonesia is a 48–60 month journey, not a 36-month deliverable.
3. **No speculative technology bets.** Wholesale CBDC pilots are limited to intragroup nostro reconciliation; offline transaction ledgering is banned.

Over 36 months, we will invest **€3.0–3.5 million** in PSR engineering, **$3–5 million annually** in recoverable partner enablement, and approximately **45 specialist FTEs** across treasury, compliance, and partner engineering. We will exit or white-label corridors that cannot achieve a positive NMAC-T, concentrate capital in EU↔Gulf and Gulf↔SEA payroll and trade flows, and defer proprietary licensing in Latin America until Months 37–60.

## 1. ECONOMIC FRAMEWORK: NMAC-T

### 1.1 The Scorecard
NMAC-T is the single metric governing capital allocation. It treats trapped capital as an explicit P&L charge, preventing balance-sheet illusion.

| **Component** | **Definition** |
|---|---|
| Gross Corridor Margin | FX capture + transaction fees |
| Less: Compliance & Regulatory Drag | KYC/AML, screening, reporting, PSD3/PSR amortization |
| Less: Partner & Distribution Economics | Intermediary fees, agent revenue share, enablement amortization |
| Less: Trapped Capital Charge | WACC (12%) × (nostro minimums + prefunded local liquidity + local equity lock-up) |
| Less: FX / Settlement Drag | Hedging costs, failure reserves, correspondent fees |
| **= NMAC-T** | **Risk-adjusted corridor return** |

**Decision Rule:** Any corridor projecting NMAC-T below **+0.5%** after 24 months of operation is targeted for exit, white-label conversion, or pivot to a lower-capital payout rail.

### 1.2 Illustrative Corridor Economics
| **Corridor** | **Annual Flow** | **Gross Margin** | **Compliance Drag** | **Partner Share** | **Trapped Capital Charge** | **NMAC-T** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| EU → Gulf (B2B payroll) | $180M | 2.8% | 0.4% | 0.6% | 0.3% | **+1.5%** |
| Gulf → Philippines (retail) | $220M | 2.2% | 0.6% | 0.8% | 0.4% | **+0.4%** |
| EU → Indonesia (retail) | $60M | 1.9% | 0.9% | 0.7% | 1.2% | **–0.5%** |

**Tier 3: LatAm Restructure**
- **Action:** Exit low-volume retail corridors with negative NMAC-T. Pivot to documentary trade-adjacent B2B (letters of credit, confirmed payables) where FX access is legally prioritized.
- **Action:** Enter Brazil and Mexico via a **sponsored model or BaaS** with an existing licensed PI in Years 1–3. Licensed JV or acquisition is a **Months 37–60** target only.
- **Action:** Route permitted retail payouts through regulated e-money wallets to minimize trapped capital.
- **Owner:** Corridor GM – LatAm; Chief Commercial Officer

## 4. SETTLEMENT & LIQUIDITY RISK MANAGEMENT

### 4.1 Risk Matrix & Trade-offs

| **Risk Category** | **Tolerance** | **Mitigation Tactic** | **Owner** | **Timeline** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Currency** | 65% natural hedge ratio | Destination-market matching (EUR/USD receipts vs. PHP/IDR/VND pay-outs) | Global Treasurer | Month 12 |
| **Financial Hedging (Residual)** | ≤6-month tenor for controlled currencies; 3-month rolling for majors | NDFs and FX swaps via prime brokers; no 24-month books | Global Treasurer | Month 6 |
| **Liquidity – Prefunding** | Regulatory minimum + 48-hour payout cover | Regional nostro balances in Amsterdam, Dubai, Singapore | Global Treasurer | Month 6 |
| **Liquidity – Intraday** | 95% cash-coverage ratio of peak obligations | AI/ML forecasting + intraday overdrafts with Tier-1 clearers (Citi, HSBC, SC) | Global Treasurer | Month 18 |
| **Counterparty Concentration** | No partner >60% of corridor volume | Dual-bank rule; dynamic daily credit scoring | Chief Risk Officer | Month 6 |
| **Counterparty Credit** | 110% collateral for high-risk/immature partners | Cash collateral or tri-party escrow (BNY Mellon) | Chief Risk Officer | Month 9 |
| **Herstatt / Settlement Finality** | T+0 funding where possible | SWIFT gpi truncation; rely on CLS-member correspondents (no direct CLS membership) | Global Treasurer | Month 12 |

### 4.2 Settlement Failover Playbook

|---|---|---|---|

## 5. CAPITAL-CONTROL MITIGATION

We do not evade sovereign FX regimes. We manage them through structure, documentation, and authorized intermediation.

| **Tactic** | **Description** | **Applicable Markets** | **Owner** | **Timeline** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Purpose-of-Payment Automation** | Truthful declaration and document packaging at origination; zero disaggregation | All controlled markets | CCO / Product | Month 6 |
| **Documentary Trade Flows** | Route B2B through L/Cs and confirmed payables where FX is legally prioritized | Brazil, Argentina | Corridor GM – LatAm | Month 18 |
| **Prime-Brokerage FX Model** | Access FX via authorized intermediaries (corretoras de câmbio, bancos autorizados), not direct central-bank quotas | Brazil, Argentina | Global Treasurer | Month 12 |
| **Local Liquidity Pools** | Pre-fund BRL/ARS inside jurisdiction via trade proceeds or local debt; charge trap cost to NMAC-T | Brazil, Argentina, Vietnam | Global Treasurer | Month 12 |
| **Digital Wallet Last Mile** | Route retail through regulated e-money wallets where notification thresholds differ from bank wires | SEA, LatAm | Chief Commercial Officer | Month 12 |
| **Bilateral Netting** | 72-hour netting for genuine two-way B2B flows to reduce cross-border notional | All two-way corridors | Global Treasurer | Month 18 |
| **Sandbox (Intragroup Only)** | Pilot wholesale digital assets strictly for intragroup nostro reconciliation under MAS/CBUAE sandbox | Gulf ↔ SEA | CTO / Global Treasurer | Month 30 |

## 6. PARTNER & TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE

### 6.1 Partner Tiering & Economics

| **Tier** | **Profile** | **Integration Mode** | **Firm Revenue Retention** | **Enablement Model** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A – Strategic** | Top-tier bank/telco with format-agnostic APIs | Direct API / shared microservices | 60–75% | Co-invest for strategic priority |
| **B – Transactional** | Mid-tier bank; legacy core; batch-oriented | Format-agnostic orchestration layer / middleware | 45–55% | Recoverable advance against revenue share; contractual exclusivity |
| **C – Fragmented** | Agents, small MTOs, rural cooperatives | White-label SDK with **real-time authorization** (no offline ledgering) | **60–75%** | None; self-service configuration |

**Format Agnosticism:** The orchestration layer accepts SWIFT MT, ISO 20022 MX, and domestic proprietary formats (Brazil Febraban, Mexico CLABE, Indonesia BI proprietary). ISO 20022 is an aspiration, not a prerequisite.

### 6.2 Integration Gate
No new partner is approved unless:
- Projected NMAC-T breakeven is **≤18 months**.
- Volume and ticket-size thresholds are set corridor-specifically (no arbitrary $50M floor).

### 6.3 Enablement Fund
- **Budget:** $3–5M annually.
- **Structure:** Recoverable advances clawed back from future revenue share.
- **Requirements:** Contractually binding preferential routing rights and data-sharing for liquidity forecasting; volume penalties for non-compliance.
- **Owner:** Chief Commercial Officer
