# SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (1–2 PAGES)

## Strategic Overview

**Objective:** Distribute temperature-sensitive vaccines across 11 priority corridors in West and East Africa, balancing infrastructure fragmentation, seasonal impassability, and institutional capacity constraints.

**Key Findings:**
- Road quality varies 72-point PCI spread (Mombasa 82 → Livingstone 30); seasonal degradation adds 30–65% transit delays Jun–Oct
- Customs dwell times range 8–72 hours; passive containers fail thermal integrity beyond 1–2 hours without gel-pack rotation
- Electricity reliability spans 30% (rural Mali) to 92% (Dakar); solar panels degrade 0.5–3% annually depending on dust/humidity
- Ministry of Health partnerships strong (Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda) to weak (Nigeria, Mali, Niger); last-mile cold-storage capacity critical bottleneck
- Contingency trigger points (power loss, customs delays, road washouts) require embedded real-time monitoring and pre-positioned backup supplies

**Strategic Architecture:**

| Element | Specification | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| **Equipment Model** | Hybrid: grid cool-rooms (hubs) + solar 250–300L (regional) + passive PCM 100L (final-mile) | No single-tech approach viable across infrastructure diversity; powered units justified only where electricity reliable; passive primary for high-dwell checkpoints requires dry-ice contingency |
| **Deployment Phasing** | Phase 1 (Q1–Q2 dry): 5 corridors, 2–3 shipments/week; Phase 2 (Q3–Q4 rainy): selective 3–4 corridors, contingency protocols active | Seasonal windows critical; rainy-season deployment for high-risk corridors (Clusters B, E) unviable without major equipment/cost escalation |
| **Capital Requirement** | USD 185,000–235,000 (equipment, installation, training, contingency reserve) | Original builder estimate USD 153k–192k understated; installation labor, integration, UPS/generator hookup, training add USD 30k–50k |
| **Operating Budget** | USD 176,500–240,000 annually (consumables, labor, contingency, insurance) | Gel-packs, dry-ice, technician maintenance, data logging, hub electricity/fuel dominate; contingency reserve (USD 25,000–40,000) non-negotiable given rainy-season risk |
| **Expected Spoilage Rate** | 3–5% (optimistic, high-infrastructure corridors); 8–15% (realistic, multi-corridor average); 15–25% (acceptable maximum, wet season) | Achievable via contingency activation; exceeds 25% signals protocol failure requiring operational halt and remediation |

## Spoilage Risk Mitigation & Contingency Triggers

### Embedded Decision Architecture

**Power Loss at Hub (Trigger: Grid outage >4 hrs)**
- Level 1 (Automatic): UPS bridges 60–90 min; diesel generator auto-engages
- Level 2 (Manual): Hub staff rotate ice packs, prepare vaccine triage by shelf-life
- Level 3 (Escalation): Quarantine affected batch; initiate spoilage claim if temp >10°C for >2 hrs

**Customs Delays >12 hrs (Trigger: Dwell-time forecast exceeds 12 hrs)**
- Pre-positioned dry-ice cache at 5 checkpoints (Kayes, Kano, Bolgatanga, Iringa, Livingstone)
- Backup passive container deployed from regional hub (2–4 hrs transit)
- Level 3: Alternate routing or air-freight segment if dwell >18 hrs

**Road Damage & Unscheduled Stops (Trigger: Vehicle halted >4 hrs)**
- Real-time road-condition network (WhatsApp + radio) flags hazards 2–4 hrs in advance
- Alternate routing database (pre-planned 1–2 bypass routes per corridor)
- Mobile refrigeration unit deployed (Nairobi, Accra, Dar) for emergency vehicle support

# SECTION 2: CORRIDOR-BY-CORRIDOR PLAYBOOKS

## PLAYBOOK 1: DAKAR–BAMAKO CORRIDOR (Senegal–Mali)
**Distance:** 1,300 km | **Transit Time:** 14–18 days (dry); 20–26 days (rainy) | **Road Quality:** PCI 72 | **Customs:** Kayes checkpoint, 8–12 hrs typical

### Infrastructure Constraints & Solutions

| Constraint | Impact | Mitigation Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **Kayes Checkpoint Dwell 8–12 hrs** | Passive container temp rises 3–4°C per hour if vehicle stops; after 6 hrs, exceeds 12°C → spoilage risk 40% | Pre-position gel-pack cache at Kayes; rotate packs at 4–6 hr mark; deploy backup passive container if first container exceeds 8°C |
| **Laterite road section (Kayes–Bamako, 40% of route)** | Rainy-season ruts, potholes 0.3–0.6 m deep; 3–4 hr delays common Jun–Aug | Use powered vehicle (diesel truck, not air-cooled van); reduce speed 15 km/h; coordinate convoy timing (leave Kayes 6 AM to avoid midday heat) |
| **No electricity beyond Dakar hub until Bamako** | Cannot re-cool gel packs at checkpoints; manual rotation only | Pre-freeze 16 gel packs before dispatch (double-stock); rotating every 4 hrs requires crew rotation (2-driver team); each driver carries 2 coolers for quick swap |
| **Checkpoint customs scrutiny of medical goods** | Repeated container opening (gel rotation) invites security/tariff inspection; delays +2–4 hrs | Prepare documentation: WHO waybill, health ministry letter, customs pre-clearance forms (Senegal–Mali transit agreement); designate single liaison at checkpoint for all rotations |

### Equipment & Protocols

**Hub-Level (Dakar Port):**
- 1× 20 m³ grid cool-room (2–4 kW, UPS 8 kW standby for power outages)
- Capacity: 600 vaccine doses per batch; 24–48 hr dwell before dispatch
- Temperature logging: continuous digital recorder (1–6 hr resolution)
- Procedures:
  1. Incoming vaccine batch: 4 hrs in cool-room for thermal stabilization (ensure internal temp = 4°C before container dispatch)
  2. Gel-pack pre-freezing: freeze 16 packs overnight (−18°C minimum)
  3. Container pre-cooling: pre-cool 100L PCM container to −5°C in cool-room (2–3 hrs)
  4. Final handoff: load vaccine, seal container, attach data logger, assign driver team
  5. Departure protocol: 6 AM dispatch (cooler morning temps delay thermal rise)

**In-Transit (Dakar → Kayes → Bamako):**
- **Container:** 100–150L PCM with 8× glycol gel packs + 1 kg PCM material (melting point 24°C)
- **Driver rotation:** 2-driver team; 4 hrs driving, then handoff to co-driver (maintains alertness, allows 1 driver to monitor container temperature every 1 hr)
- **Gel-pack rotation protocol:**
  - Hour 0: Depart Dakar, 6 AM; all gel packs frozen −18°C; container internal temp 2–4°C
  - Hour 8: Morning stop (Kaolack, 280 km); check container temp (should be 3–6°C). If 6–8°C: swap 2 gel packs (remove warm, insert fresh from backup cooler). If >8°C: ESCALATE (see contingency)
  - Hour 16: Overnight stop (Tambacounda, 550 km); container in shaded area. Swap 2 gel packs if needed. Freeze replacement packs using town generator/hotel fridge overnight
  - Hour 20: Depart Tambacounda; container temp check (should be 4–6°C)
  - Hour 28: Kayes checkpoint (1,100 km); **CRITICAL DWELL: 8–12 hrs anticipated**
    - Stop immediately upon arrival; assess temperature (if already >9°C, initiate Level 2 contingency — see below)
    - Access checkpoint freezer (pre-arranged); rotate 4 gel packs immediately (remove warmest, replace with checkpoint-frozen packs)
    - Repeat rotation every 4 hrs for dwell duration
    - Vaccine inspected by customs; prepare documentation (MOH letter + WHO waybill) to avoid repeated container openings
  - Hour 40–44: Depart Kayes; final 200 km to Bamako, ~8 hrs driving
  - **Total Transit: 14–18 days (including 2–3 overnight stops for crew rest + potential checkpoint dwell delays)**

**Endpoint (Bamako District Hub):**
- Handoff to Mali Ministry of Health EPI coordinator
- Retrieve data logger; review 2-hour resolution temperature log
- If max temp ≤8°C, entire batch → Grade A (approve for distribution)
- If max temp 8–10°C for <2 hrs, → Grade B (approve, flag priority; distribute within 24 hrs)
- If max temp >10°C or >8°C for >2 hrs → Grade C (quarantine; initiate spoilage claim)

### Seasonal Adjustments

**Dry Season (Dec–May): Standard Protocol Above**
- Expect 14–18 day transit, 95% on-time arrival, <5% spoilage

**Rainy Season (Jun–Nov): Modified Operations**
- **Jun–Aug (Heavy Rain):** Laterite section impassable 5–10% of days; add 4–6 day contingency buffer in planning
  - Monitor road reports daily via WFP Logistics Cluster (contact Dakar coordinator)
  - If major washout reported, defer shipment 3–5 days OR divert via alternate route (add 2–4 days, sandier tracks = higher heat load on container, increase gel-pack stock to 12 packs)
  - Reduce dispatch frequency: 1 shipment/week (vs. 2–3 dry season)
- **Sep–Nov (Light Rain):** Road conditions improve; return to 2 shipments/week by Oct
- **Cost Impact:** Contingency gel-pack stock (+USD 1,200/month), reduced throughput (−USD 8,000–12,000/month revenue), increased insurance/spoilage claims (+USD 500–2,000/month)

### Ministry Coordination & Last-Mile Handoff

**Pre-Deployment (4–6 weeks before first shipment):**
1. MOH Senegal (Dakar EPI directorate) + MOH Mali (Bamako EPI directorate): sign cold-chain partnership MOU
   - Designate national cold-chain focal points (typically EPI director or deputy)
   - Confirm Dakar port cool-room facility sharing (coordinate with port authority)
   - Confirm Bamako hub location (typically MOH central warehouse or regional hospital)
   - Agree on customs pre-clearance procedure (streamlined if possible via Senegal–Mali trade agreement)

2. Checkpoint liaison training (Kayes):
   - Meet with Kayes regional health officer + customs officer
   - Brief on gel-pack rotation protocols, data logger procedures, emergency dry-ice use
   - Verify freezer capacity (hospital, clinic, or ice supplier)
   - Establish WhatsApp group for real-time communication (temperature alerts, dwell-time forecasts)

3. Insurance & documentation:
   - Confirm cold-chain liability coverage with insurance broker
   - Draft WHO vaccine transit waybills (required for cross-border movement)
   - Pre-arrange customs pre-clearance forms (streamline border delays)

**Per-Shipment Coordination (48 hrs before dispatch):**
1. Dakar hub: notify Bamako EPI coordinator of shipment departure (batch codes, expected arrival date, carrier details)
2. Kayes checkpoint: notify 24 hrs before arrival; confirm freezer readiness & officer availability
3. Driver briefing: confirm route, checkpoint contact phone numbers, emergency procedures

**Upon Arrival (Bamako Hub):**
1. EPI coordinator receives shipment; verifies data logger (temperature log reviewed)
2. If Grade A or B: release for onward distribution to districts
3. If Grade C: quarantine; MOH + logistics firm conduct joint assessment; if spoilage confirmed, initiate insurance claim

## PLAYBOOK 2: MOMBASA–NAIROBI–KISII CORRIDOR (Kenya)
**Distance:** 750 km (Mombasa–Nairobi: 500 km; Nairobi–Kisii: 250 km) | **Transit Time:** 6–9 days (dry); 7–11 days (rainy) | **Road Quality:** PCI 82 (A104 trunk, excellent) | **Customs:** Mombasa port 1–2 hrs; minimal inland delays

### Infrastructure Strengths & Operating Model

**Advantages Over West Africa Corridors:**
- **Road Quality:** A104 modern 4-lane highway, 98% paved; minimal potholes (1–2 per km vs. 10+ in Sahel)
- **Electricity:** Mombasa, Nairobi, Kisii all grid-connected (90%+ uptime); no generator dependency
- **Customs:** Digital Single Window (JICA-funded 2022); typical Mombasa port clearance 1–2 hrs (vs. 8–24 hrs West African ports)
- **NIP Maturity:** Kenya EPI strongest in region; real-time DHIS2 cold-chain tracking; EPI staff trained to WHO standards
- **Minimal Checkpoints:** No security checkpoints between Mombasa–Nairobi (unlike Nigeria–Niger); single Kenya–Uganda border (if extending to Uganda)

**Key Differentiator:** This corridor **justifies powered solar refrigeration** because:
1. Transit time 6–9 days well within solar battery capacity (150Ah, daily sun ~8 hrs)
2. Road quality minimizes unscheduled stops (risk of thermal excursion <5%)
3. Hub handoff at Nairobi (midpoint) allows battery recharge + vaccine transfer
4. NIP partnership strong enough to manage powered equipment maintenance

---

### Equipment & Protocol (Optimized for Mombasa–Nairobi–Kisii)

**Hub-Level (Mombasa Port):**
- 1× 25 m³ grid cool-room (3 kW, UPS 10 kW + backup diesel generator 15 kW)
  - Capacity: 1,000 vaccine doses; 2–4 day dwell
  - Redundancy: Two 10L backup ice storage units (manual reserve if compressor fails)
- Procedures:
  1. Port intake: vaccine received, temperature validated (should be 2–8°C after sea transit)
  2. Batch staging: separate by destination (Nairobi distribution, Kisii final-mile, Uganda extension if applicable)
  3. Pre-cooling: 4–6 hrs in cool-room to stabilize internal temperature
  4. Solar unit preparation: **charge solar battery to 100% (2–3 hrs in sunny area adjacent to port) before vaccine loading**
  5. Vaccine loading: 50–100 doses per deployment (scaled to route frequency)
  6. Departure: any time (road quality permits day or night transit); target early morning (cooler ambient) if possible

**In-Transit (Mombasa → Nairobi via A104, 500 km, ~9–11 hrs driving):**
- **Container:** 300L solar-powered refrigerated unit (60W solar, 100Ah Li battery, thermostat 2–8°C, ±1°C accuracy)
- **Monitoring:** Integrated GSM modem; real-time temperature alerts every 30 min (sent to Nairobi hub dispatcher)
- **Driver Protocol:**
  - Single driver, 9–11 hr journey (A104 well-maintained; coffee stop at Mtito Andei, ~5 hrs; fuel stop Voi, ~3 hrs)
  - Monitor solar unit dashboard (battery voltage, internal temp) every 1–2 hrs
  - If battery voltage drops below 50% state-of-charge (unlikely in daylight), reduce AC load (thermostat 8°C instead of 2°C) to extend range
  - No contingency gels packs needed (powered unit autonomous); backup data logger onboard for validation
  - Maintain vehicle speed 80–100 km/h (faster = hotter vehicle bed, but A104 speed limit 100 km/h anyway)

**Regional Hub (Nairobi):**
- 1× 20 m³ grid cool-room (2–3 kW, grid reliable 90%+; no UPS needed but recommended for institutional credibility)
  - Capacity: 800 doses; 2–3 day dwell
- **Critical Function:** Battery recharge + vaccine transfer
  - Upon arrival Mombasa vehicle: dock solar unit in cool-room or adjacent shaded area with 240V grid hookup
  - Battery charge cycle: 3–4 hrs to reach 90% capacity (not 100%, to preserve Li battery longevity)
  - Vaccine transfer: open powered unit, retrieve 50–100 dose batch, transfer to cold-room staging area
  - **Vaccine split:**
    - 30% doses: immediate re-dispatch to Kisii (final-mile distribution)
    - 70% doses: held 1–2 days in Nairobi cool-room for consolidation with other shipments (optimize vehicle utilization)
- Staffing: 1× EPI cold-chain technician (oversee unit docking, battery charge, vaccine transfers)

**Final-Mile (Nairobi → Kisii via A104/B1, 250 km, ~12–15 hrs):**
- **Container:** Transition to passive PCM 100L (powered unit remains at Nairobi for next batch)
- **Rationale:** Kisii 250 km leg, 12–15 hrs, manageable for passive with 1 gel-pack rotation
- **Protocol:**
  1. At Nairobi hub: transfer 50 doses into pre-cooled 100L passive PCM container (4–6 hrs pre-cooling before transfer)
  2. Load with 8× gel packs, data logger, attach MOH documentation
  3. Dispatch early morning (6–7 AM) to Kisii (estimated arrival ~6–8 PM same day if no delays)
  4. Intermediate stop (Nairobi City exit, 1 hr): brief checkpoint at Nairobi-Kisii boundary (minimal delay, routine inspection)
  5. Container temp check at midpoint (Nakuru, ~3 hrs from Nairobi): should be 4–6°C; if 6–8°C, swap 2 gel packs
  6. Arrival Kisii district hospital (~5–7 PM, 12–15 hrs total): hand off to district EPI officer

**Endpoint (Kisii District):**
- District hospital receiving: Kisii EPI officer retrieves data logger, validates temperature log
- District cold-storage assessment: **Critical bottleneck** — Kisii district has 1× 200L WHO-prequalified fridge (shared with patient vaccines); typical patient vaccine occupancy 120L → only ~80L available for incoming stock (50 doses occupy ~40L)
- **Problem if unprepared:** 50-dose shipment may exceed available space; risks cross-contamination or temperature fluctuation if squeezed into already-full fridge
- **Solution (built into strategy):** Pre-coordinate with Kisii district MOH 2 weeks before first shipment to clear patient vaccine space or identify secondary refrigeration (clinic fridge 50 km away). Establish SOP: incoming vaccines → prioritize oldest stock for immediate distribution to clinics (2–4 day turnaround), freeing space for new batch.

**Dry Season (Jun–Oct): Standard Protocol Above**
- Expect 6–9 day transit, 96% on-time, <3% spoilage
- Deployment frequency: 3–4 shipments/week (maximum throughput)

**Rainy Season (Nov–May, particularly Mar–May): Minor Adaptations**

- Transit time increases slightly (7–11 days due to slower speeds on wet roads, single driving delays 1–2 hrs)
- Battery capacity check: if cloudy 3+ days, ensure solar unit reaches Nairobi with >60% charge (may need to reduce dispatch frequency if consecutive cloudy days forecast)
- Expected spoilage rate increases marginally: 3–5% (vs. <3% dry season)
- Deployment frequency: maintain 2–3 shipments/week (reduce if multi-day rainy forecast)
