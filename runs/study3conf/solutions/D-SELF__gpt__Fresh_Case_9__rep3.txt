# Global Brand Framework  
## Responding to Geopolitically Driven Consumer Activism  
**For global consumer brands, employees, franchisees, and leaders | 2025–2026**

---

## Executive standard

When geopolitical activism creates opposing demands across markets:

> **Apply one global standard to safety, dignity, non-discrimination, truthful communication, human-rights due diligence, remedy, non-retaliation, data protection, and lawful conduct. Adapt the language, channel, timing, stakeholder engagement, and operational response to local conditions.**

The company should:

1. Protect people and locations first.
2. Verify the company’s actual connection to the issue.
3. Distinguish online visibility from customer behavior and real-world risk.
4. Address serious human-rights or safety concerns even when avoiding partisan political endorsement.
5. Give employees and franchisees practical guidance.
6. Communicate only verified, useful information.
7. Explain regional differences through objective criteria—not different values.
8. Document decisions, review dates, and exit criteria.
9. Avoid retaliating against or unnecessarily exposing lawful critics.
10. Measure safety, trust, continuity, governance, and remedy—not just sentiment or sales.

This framework is not jurisdiction-specific legal advice. Local counsel and relevant specialists must review live incidents, particularly where sanctions, anti-boycott rules, labor rights, privacy, political activity, human rights, advertising, disclosure, or public-order laws are implicated.

---

# 1. Purpose and operating premise

Geopolitically motivated consumer activism may involve several issues at once:

- customer trust and commercial performance;
- employee expression and workplace relations;
- franchisee and licensee governance;
- physical safety;
- misinformation, impersonation, or deepfakes;
- human-rights impacts;
- sanctions, export controls, and anti-boycott restrictions;
- supply-chain continuity;
- community legitimacy;
- investor, regulator, or government pressure.

It should therefore be treated as a **people, rights, legal, operational, information-integrity, and communications issue**, not simply a public-relations problem.

The goal is not to satisfy every political demand or eliminate criticism. The goal is to make decisions that are principled, lawful, evidence-based, locally informed, proportionate, operationally feasible, and explainable across markets.

---

# 2. Principles and decision hierarchy

## 2.1 One global standard, locally adapted execution

Global standards should remain consistent. Regional teams may adapt:

- language and tone;
- channels and timing;
- spokespersons;
- customer-service scripts;
- community engagement;
- security measures;
- employee support;
- temporary marketing, event, or operating decisions.

Localization must not weaken or selectively apply global standards.

## 2.2 Decision hierarchy

When considerations conflict, use this order:

1. **Protect life and physical safety.**
2. **Prevent or mitigate serious human-rights harm through lawful means.**
3. **Comply with applicable law and mandatory disclosure obligations.**
4. **Prevent discrimination, retaliation, and harassment.**
5. **Maintain responsible business continuity and partner relationships.**
6. **Protect long-term trust and brand coherence.**
7. **Manage short-term sentiment and commercial performance.**

No business unit may knowingly violate applicable law to address a rights concern. If local law prevents adequate mitigation or protection, escalate to the general counsel and responsible human-rights executive, and consider lawful alternatives, operational redesign, suspension, or withdrawal.

## 2.3 Protect people before positioning

Prioritize:

- employees;
- customers;
- franchisees and licensees;
- suppliers and delivery personnel;
- local communities;
- people directly affected by a humanitarian crisis.

Do not expose people or locations unnecessarily through public statements, imagery, personal data, or operational disclosures.

## 2.4 Facts before advocacy

Verify claims about:

- ownership, control, royalties, donations, investments, and political contributions;
- franchise and licensing structures;
- suppliers, distributors, banks, and charitable partners;
- executive statements;
- alleged human-rights impacts;
- the authenticity, timing, and context of digital content.

Use internal confidence labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Verified | Supported by reliable evidence |
| Probable | Credible but incomplete |
| Disputed | Materially conflicting evidence |
| Not substantiated on current evidence | Insufficient support; not necessarily disproven |
| Disproven | Reliable evidence contradicts the claim |

## 2.5 Consistency without false equivalence

Use consistent criteria globally, even when local actions differ. Do not imply that people in one market deserve less safety, dignity, or concern.

Do not create false equivalence. A credible threat, discriminatory incident, or substantiated rights impact requires more than online criticism or an unverified allegation.

## 2.6 Neutrality has limits

The company may decline to endorse a government, military, party, or movement. It should not invoke neutrality to avoid addressing:

- violence or threats;
- discrimination;
- employee or customer safety;
- credible human-rights concerns;
- unlawful conduct;
- forced labor or corruption;
- serious supply-chain risks;
- the company’s own conduct.

Before using “neutrality” or “non-involvement” language, prepare a **corporate-impact inventory** covering operations, taxes, royalties, ownership, investments, donations, suppliers, executive statements, and recent operating changes.

## 2.7 Proportionality, reversibility, and remedy

Use the least escalatory communication that is truthful and protective. Take whatever operational or remedial measures are necessary to prevent or address serious harm.

Every significant action must have:

- scope;
- owner;
- rationale;
- duration;
- review date;
- exit, reopening, or remediation criteria.

A public statement or apology is not itself a remedy. Where the company caused or contributed to harm, remedy may include correction, compensation, reinstatement, operational change, supplier remediation, independent investigation, or engagement with affected stakeholders.

# 3. Global commitments and local boundaries

## 3.1 Non-negotiable global commitments

The company should maintain a short global policy committing to:

- safety and dignity;
- non-discrimination and respectful treatment;
- opposition to threats, harassment, violence, and retaliation;
- truthful and substantiated communications;
- compliance with applicable law;
- proportionate human-rights due diligence and remedy;
- responsible sourcing and business conduct;
- personal-data protection;
- needs-based, vetted humanitarian assistance;
- non-retaliation for good-faith employee concerns;
- appropriate standards for franchisees, licensees, suppliers, and partners.

Minimum partner standards should address safety, non-discrimination, truthful brand representation, forced labor, anti-bribery, cooperation with investigations, grievance channels, non-retaliation, and data protection.

## 3.2 Matters requiring approval

Regional teams and franchisees should not independently:

- endorse or condemn a political actor on behalf of the company;
- announce donations, divestments, boycotts, market exits, or supplier suspensions;
- characterize unverified allegations as fact;
- use company data to identify or target lawful critics;
- disclose sensitive information about people, investigations, or security;
- threaten activists or customers;
- alter global safety, rights, or anti-discrimination standards;
- imply that a local operator’s position represents the global company.

# 4. Decision tree

## Step 1: Is there an immediate threat?

### If yes: act operationally first

- Activate security and business continuity.
- Protect people and locations.
- Consider additional security, schedule changes, evacuation, or temporary closure.
- Contact authorities where appropriate.
- Preserve evidence.
- Communicate safety instructions only if useful and safe.

A threat does not automatically require public disclosure. Consider credibility, privacy, law-enforcement guidance, and amplification risk.

### If no: proceed.

## Step 2: Is the concern credible or material?

### If no: listen and monitor

- Record the claim, source, reach, and confidence.
- Verify relevant facts.
- Prepare FAQs and holding language.
- Do not repeat a fringe allegation merely to rebut it.

### If yes or plausibly credible: proceed.

## Step 3: What is the company’s connection?

Classify internally as:

- **Direct:** the company caused or materially contributed to the impact.
- **Linked:** a business relationship is associated with the impact.
- **Perceived:** an allegation exists but is not verified.
- **Not substantiated on current evidence.**

For direct or linked connections, assess:

- severity and scale;
- urgency and reversibility;
- affected-group vulnerability;
- proximity to company conduct;
- company leverage;
- ability to mitigate;
- consequences of disengagement.

A linked connection does not automatically require withdrawal or a public statement.

## Step 4: Is material misinformation causing harm?

If yes, issue a narrow correction only when:

- the claim is material;
- the facts are verified;
- correction is likely to reduce harm;
- amplification risk is acceptable;
- legal and safety reviews are complete.

Escalate threats, doxxing, fraud, impersonation, and manipulated media to security, legal, cybersecurity, and relevant platforms.

## Step 5: Is a global commitment implicated?

If yes, communicate a values-based position and take practical action. The company need not endorse a broader political position to address safety, dignity, discrimination, retaliation, or serious rights concerns.

## Step 6: Would public comment reduce harm?

### Communicate publicly when:

- company conduct is materially implicated;
- silence would reasonably appear deceptive or dismissive;
- a credible safety or rights issue requires reassurance;
- verified misinformation is materially harmful;
- disclosure is legally or contractually required;
- the company has a concrete action to explain.

### Remain publicly silent while acting privately when:

- facts are unverified;
- the company has no material connection;
- comment would amplify a fringe claim;
- public attention could increase danger;
- the issue is a private dispute;
- the company has no useful action or information;
- local law restricts the proposed content.

Silence requires a documented rationale, named approvers, private actions, review time, and triggers for future communication.

# 5. Market playbooks

Assess each market across four dimensions:

1. stakeholder sensitivity;
2. business and operational exposure;
3. legal and regulatory severity;
4. human-rights severity.

Add a vulnerable-group overlay for risks affecting particular ethnic, religious, nationality, political, or other protected groups. A credible safety threat overrides the commercial assessment.

## Low sensitivity / low exposure

**Default:** Monitor and prepare.

- Monitor local-language sources.
- Verify claims.
- Prepare FAQs.
- Brief managers and franchisees.
- Maintain normal operations.
- Avoid public engagement unless the issue becomes material.

## High sensitivity / low exposure

**Default:** Listen actively and communicate selectively.

- Engage employees, franchisees, and communities privately.
- Review marketing, imagery, events, and partnerships.
- Localize customer-service guidance.
- Avoid political endorsement.
- Monitor for escalation into safety or commercial effects.

## Low sensitivity / high exposure

**Default:** Act operationally and communicate practical facts.

- Protect continuity.
- Assess alternative suppliers and distribution.
- Coordinate franchisees.
- Brief material investors and partners.
- Use targeted factual clarification rather than broad political messaging.

## High sensitivity / high exposure

**Default:** Listen, act, and communicate in parallel.

- Activate the expanded response team.
- Maintain one shared, version-controlled fact base.
- Conduct legal, human-rights, sanctions, security, and supply-chain review.
- Provide employee and franchisee support.
- Use compatible, locally adapted communications.
- Define temporary measures and exit criteria.
- Reassess at least twice daily.

## Safety or rights override

**Default:** Act immediately.

- Activate crisis command.
- Protect people and preserve evidence.
- Engage authorities and specialist counsel where appropriate.
- Mitigate, suspend, or redesign implicated activities.
- Provide remedy where warranted.
- Communicate only safe, verified, useful information.

# 6. Guidance for employees

Employees should receive:

- timely information about what is known, unknown, and being done;
- safety instructions and reporting channels;
- non-retaliation assurances;
- manager guidance;
- confidential, language-accessible support;
- clarity that personal views are not automatically company views.

Protect lawful expression and collective activity to the extent required by local law. Separate such activity from:

- threats;
- harassment;
- discrimination;
- unsafe conduct;
- unauthorized representation;
- misuse of company resources;
- material operational disruption.

Before discipline involving activism, political expression, collective activity, whistleblowing, or protected complaints, require HR, employment counsel, and employee-relations review for legality, viewpoint neutrality, consistency, proportionality, evidence quality, and retaliation risk.

Consult unions, works councils, employee representatives, or affected employees where required or appropriate, particularly for closures, major schedule changes, monitoring, discipline rules, and safety measures.

**Manager script**

> “Colleagues may hold different personal views. Our workplace standard is consistent: no threats, harassment, discrimination, or retaliation. Please report safety or conduct concerns and direct media inquiries to authorized spokespeople.”

Use anonymized, need-to-know communications. Apply minimum-group and access controls to employee dashboards. Do not create dossiers on employees or lawful activists.

---

# 7. Guidance for franchisees and licensees

## Franchisee requirements

Provide:

- approved local FAQs and scripts;
- authority boundaries;
- incident-reporting deadlines;
- safety and closure guidance;
- customer and protester protocols;
- emergency contacts;
- local-language materials;
- closure and reopening support.

A local statement may be reputationally global if it uses:

- brand assets;
- official accounts;
- uniforms or signage;
- company facilities;
- centrally supplied advertising;
- language implying corporate authorization.

**Approved local language**

> “We are a local operator of [brand]. We follow the company’s standards on safety, respect, and lawful conduct. Questions about broader corporate matters should be directed to the designated company channel.”

Central controls must have a documented basis in brand attribution, consumer safety, non-discrimination, legal compliance, truthful representation, security, or valid contractual standards. Local counsel should review restrictions involving political speech, off-duty conduct, labor practices, agency, joint-employer, franchise, or competition issues.

## Enforcement ladder

1. Education and correction.
2. Written warning.
3. Removal of unauthorized content.
4. Retraining.
5. Monitored corrective communication.
6. Contractual remedies.
7. Suspension or termination for serious or repeated breaches.

Interim measures may include supervised operations, temporary suspension of implicated activity, removal of brand assets, customer-protection measures, or expedited investigation.

Do not publicly distance the company from a franchisee until authorization, attribution, contract, agency, and oversight responsibilities are assessed.

# 8. Human-rights, legal, and ethical controls

## Human-rights assessment

For a credible allegation:

1. Identify the alleged harm and affected people.
2. Determine whether the company caused, contributed to, is linked to, or is not substantiated by current evidence.
3. Assess severity, scale, urgency, reversibility, vulnerability, leverage, and disengagement consequences.
4. Preserve evidence.
5. Consult affected stakeholders where safe and feasible.
6. Mitigate, remediate, suspend, redesign, or disengage as warranted.
7. Assign a remedy owner and deadline.
8. Verify effectiveness.
9. Provide grievance or appeal routes.
10. Record unresolved harm and escalation.

Use an independent investigation where allegations involve senior leaders, severe impacts, repeated failures, contested evidence, conflicts of interest, regulators, or courts. The general counsel and human-rights executive should jointly approve the investigator, terms of reference, budget, conflict checks, evidence access, and reporting route.

## Legal review

Review, as applicable:

- sanctions and export controls;
- anti-boycott laws;
- anti-bribery and corruption;
- political contributions and charitable-giving rules;
- advertising and consumer protection;
- labor, collective-action, and expression rights;
- privacy and monitoring;
- defamation and media law;
- securities disclosure;
- franchise, agency, competition, and joint-employer issues;
- product and supply-chain disclosure.

For conflicts of law:

1. identify conflicting requirements;
2. assess exemptions, licenses, and alternative structures;
3. escalate to the general counsel and human-rights executive;
4. document the selected position;
5. preserve minimum internal protections;
6. consider redesign, suspension, or withdrawal if no lawful and ethical solution exists.

Also review insurance notification, litigation holds, evidence preservation, privilege, cyber response, and claims management for Level 2 and Level 3 incidents.

## Humanitarian assistance

Require:

- verified local need;
- sanctions and beneficial-ownership screening;
- anti-corruption controls;
- qualified implementing partners;
- restricted-purpose documentation;
- monitoring and reporting;
- beneficiary-data protection;
- separation from sales promotion.

# 9. Communication playbook

Use five elements where appropriate:

1. **Principle**
2. **Fact boundary**
3. **Action**
4. **Limit**
5. **Review point**

Every material message should include a concrete fact, action, or next review time unless disclosure would create a documented safety, legal, privacy, or investigative risk.

## General values statement

> “We believe every person deserves dignity, safety, and respectful treatment. We reject threats, harassment, and discrimination against anyone. We are supporting our teams, serving communities responsibly, and reviewing verified concerns through the appropriate process.”

## Humanitarian concern

> “We are deeply concerned about the human impact of the situation. We are assessing whether our own operations, partners, or supply chains are connected to relevant impacts. Any assistance we provide is based on verified need and delivered through appropriately vetted channels. This does not determine broader political questions, but it does not limit our responsibility to address impacts connected to our business.”

## Factual correction

> “For clarity, the claim that [specific claim] is not accurate. The verified position is [fact]. Information about our ownership, operations, and current policies is available at [link].”

## Operational adjustment

> “For the safety of our employees and customers, we have temporarily adjusted operations at certain locations. This is a safety measure, not a political statement. We will review it as conditions change and provide updates through local channels.”

## Holding statement

> “We are aware of the concerns being raised and are reviewing the information through our established processes. Our immediate priority is the safety and dignity of employees, customers, and partners. We will share verified information and material actions through our official channels.”

## Corrective apology

> “We issued inaccurate information about [issue]. That was our error, and it undermined trust. We apologize to the people and communities affected. We have corrected the information and are reviewing our approval process. We will report on the resulting changes by [date].”

An apology should identify affected stakeholders, corrective action, and how effectiveness will be verified.

## Explaining regional differences

> “Our global standards are consistent across markets. The measures differ because local legal, operating, human-rights, and safety conditions differ. We apply the same decision criteria to each market and review actions as conditions change.”

# 10. Worked hypothetical: opposing regional demands

## Situation

A global food-and-beverage brand is targeted by an online campaign alleging that its global parent indirectly supports a contested political actor through royalties and local operations.

- In **Market A**, advocacy groups and some customers demand a public condemnation and temporary suspension of local marketing.
- In **Market B**, customers and franchisees accuse the company of abandoning national interests if it makes that statement.
- A local franchisee in Market A publishes an unauthorized post suggesting the company will “stand with” one side.
- Employees in both markets ask the company to take a clear position.
- A manipulated image appears to show the CEO announcing a political donation.
- Two stores receive online threats, but no physical incident has occurred.

## Initial assessment

### Verified

- The brand operates through independent franchisees in both markets.
- The company has not authorized the franchisee’s political statement.
- The alleged CEO image is manipulated.
- No political donation matching the claim is identified in the company’s records.
- Threats name two store locations but provide limited specificity.

### Probable or under review

- The campaign may be coordinated across platforms.
- Some customers in Market A may be changing purchasing behavior.
- Local royalties and tax payments may be interpreted differently by stakeholders.

### Not substantiated on current evidence

- The claim that the company made the alleged political donation.
- The claim that all franchisees support the same position.
- The claim that online engagement represents the majority of customers in either market.

## Decision

- **Activation:** Level 2, with a safety override for the named locations.
- **Global position:** No partisan endorsement.
- **Immediate actions:** Security assessment, franchisee correction process, fact-base governance, legal review of royalties and anti-boycott exposure, employee briefing.
- **Communication:** One global factual correction and compatible regional messages.
- **Commercial response:** Review nonessential marketing in Market A; no permanent withdrawal or boycott commitment without further evidence and analysis.

## Global communication

> “A manipulated image circulating online falsely suggests that our CEO announced a political donation. We have found no record of such a donation. Our company-wide standards remain focused on safety, dignity, non-discrimination, truthful communication, and lawful conduct. We are reviewing the other claims being raised and will share verified material updates.”

## Market A message

> “We recognize the serious concerns being expressed in our community. We are reviewing the specific claims about our operations and relationships, and we have temporarily adjusted selected marketing activity while that review continues. Our local teams are receiving additional support, and we will provide an update by [date].”

## Market B message

> “Our global standards are consistent across markets. We are not endorsing a political actor. We are continuing lawful operations while reviewing verified concerns and supporting our local employees, customers, and franchise partners. Local measures may differ because safety, legal, and operating conditions differ.”

## Franchisee action

- Contact the franchisee privately.
- Require removal or correction of the unauthorized statement where lawful and contractually appropriate.
- Provide approved language.
- Assess whether the post used brand assets or implied corporate authorization.
- Review whether additional oversight or training is required.

## Employee guidance

> “The company recognizes that colleagues may hold different personal views. Employees may raise concerns through established channels without retaliation. No one should be pressured to express a political position, and threats, harassment, discrimination, and unauthorized company representation are not permitted.”

## Review and exit criteria

Review within 48 hours, then at least daily while threats remain active. Reopen normal marketing when:

- security assesses the locations as manageable;
- no new credible threats emerge;
- the factual review is complete or has a documented next phase;
- franchisee guidance is implemented;
- local communications are no longer likely to increase risk.

## Metrics

Track:

- threat credibility and response time;
- customer behavior in Market A compared with matched markets;
- correction reach and persistence;
- employee and franchisee confidence;
- unauthorized franchisee content;
- completion of legal and human-rights reviews;
- whether regional messages remain factually compatible.

The company does not adopt contradictory political positions. It uses one global standard, provides market-specific operating measures, and explains differences through evidence, law, safety, and local conditions.

# 11. Governance and decision rights

## Response structure

### Core rapid team

- security;
- legal;
- regional operations;
- communications;
- HR.

### Expanded team

- human rights and sustainability;
- procurement and supply chain;
- franchise operations;
- privacy and cybersecurity;
- finance and investor relations;
- relevant regional leaders.

### Executive team

- CEO or delegate;
- general counsel;
- enterprise-risk leadership;
- responsible human-rights executive;
- board liaison where appropriate.

## Activation levels

| Level | Description | Review cadence |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Limited discussion; no credible material risk | Weekly or as needed |
| 1 | Localized material pressure | Daily |
| 2 | Multi-market escalation or measurable operating effect | At least twice daily |
| 3 | Threats, violence, serious rights concerns, regulatory intervention, or major disruption | Continuous or shift-based |

The incident lead may immediately increase an activation level after a credible safety, legal, rights, or operational trigger. De-escalation requires documented evidence and approval from relevant security, legal, human-rights, and operations owners.

## Level 3 authority

Crisis command controls:

- global factual claims;
- brand-wide messaging;
- cross-border operating changes;
- corporate-policy statements.

Regional leaders retain authority for immediate, locally lawful safety actions. Crisis command should not delay or reverse such action without a documented safety basis. Disputes go to the executive incident lead, with general-counsel escalation where necessary.

## Decision log

Record:

- source and time of information;
- confidence level;
- affected people and locations;
- company connection;
- legal advice;
- alternatives considered;
- safety and rights assessment;
- commercial considerations and their influence;
- decision owner;
- regional rationale;
- message issued;
- review date and exit criteria.

Prior cases should inform, but not mechanically determine, future decisions. Maintain a precedent and comparability register.

# 12. Business continuity checklist

For Level 2 or Level 3 incidents, assess:

## Locations

- opening, closure, evacuation, and reopening criteria;
- staffing and transportation;
- security and facility access;
- customer refunds and complaints;
- signage and local communications.

## Supply chain

- alternate suppliers and routes;
- inventory allocation;
- supplier personnel safety;
- sanctions and export-control review;
- consequences of suspension or disengagement.

## Employees

- payroll and benefits continuity;
- safe commuting and travel;
- temporary reassignment;
- emergency leave or relocation;
- consultation obligations.

## Technology and information

- account security;
- backup communication channels;
- impersonation response;
- evidence preservation;
- cyber insurance and notification review.

## Finance and governance

- emergency spending authority;
- insurance notification;
- litigation hold;
- investor disclosure;
- post-event approval and documentation.

# 13. Metrics and accountability

Use a metric register with definition, baseline, target, owner, data source, frequency, escalation action, and reporting forum.

| Metric | Illustrative target | Owner | Escalation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level 3 safety report acknowledgement | Acknowledge within 30 minutes where systems and local conditions permit | Security | Immediate security escalation |
| Initial claim assessment | Confidence rating and owner within two hours | Incident lead | Governance escalation if delayed |
| Unauthorized franchise statement | Operator contacted within four hours | Franchise operations | Regional executive review |
| Affected-manager briefing | 95% within 24 hours | HR | Regional HR escalation |
| Temporary closure review | Daily during active incident | Operations | Executive review |
| Human-rights review plan | Owner and timetable within five business days | Human-rights lead | Executive committee review |
| Material correction | Decision after evidence, legal, safety, and amplification review | Communications/legal | Incident lead review |
| Remedy | Milestone completion plus effectiveness check | Responsible function | Human-rights escalation |

Track:

- threat severity and response time;
- injuries, harassment, discrimination, and retaliation;
- employee confidence and reporting-channel awareness;
- closures, disruptions, supply continuity, and recovery time;
- franchisee compliance and confidence;
- claim-verification quality;
- local-language and cultural-review completion;
- correction effectiveness;
- stakeholder understanding;
- legal and human-rights review completion;
- remediation effectiveness and recurrence.

Safeguards:

- minimum-group thresholds;
- aggregation and cell suppression;
- restricted access;
- retention limits;
- no political profiling;
- no incentives based solely on low incident counts;
- periodic audit of severity classifications;
- independent review of Level 3 incidents.

Report Level 3 incidents to the board or risk committee with:

- people and safety impacts;
- legal and regulatory exposure;
- human-rights findings;
- commercial and continuity effects;
- remediation;
- unresolved risks;
- control changes;
- lessons learned.

# 14. Implementation roadmap

## First 30 days

- appoint global, regional, and franchise response owners;
- approve global commitments and decision rights;
- map franchise and license authorities;
- establish escalation contacts and privacy controls;
- create multilingual holding statements and FAQs;
- complete legal, human-rights, and vulnerable-group risk mapping.

## Days 31–60

- issue employee, manager, franchisee, supplier, and continuity playbooks;
- create country legal checklists;
- build a version-controlled claims library;
- establish platform, community, authority, and emergency contacts;
- identify vetted humanitarian partners;
- define thresholds, closure criteria, and review intervals;
- run a cross-market simulation.

## Days 61–90

- test dashboards and reporting;
- conduct tabletop exercises;
- audit local implementation;
- review cultural and translation controls;
- integrate lessons into contracts, training, enterprise risk, insurance, and executive reporting.

## Ongoing

- conduct 30-day operational reviews;
- conduct 90-day governance reviews;
- perform annual cross-market audits;
- run comparability and equity reviews;
- test crisis-command and local-authority arrangements;
- update the precedent register and playbooks.

# Final executive checklist

Before approving a significant action, ask:

1. What is verified, and what remains unknown or disputed?
2. Is there an immediate safety override?
3. Who could be harmed by action, inaction, or communication?
4. What is the company’s actual connection?
5. Is the connection material?
6. What leverage, responsibility, and remedy options exist?
7. Which global commitment is implicated?
8. What legal, regulatory, cultural, and labor constraints apply?
9. Should the company listen, act, communicate, or remain publicly silent?
10. Would communication reduce harm or amplify it?
11. What concrete protection, operational action, or remedy is available?
12. What should be global, and what should be localized?
13. Is the message accurate within its stated scope?
14. Why do regional measures differ?
15. Has the decision been tested against comparable prior cases?
16. What are the review date, exit criteria, and reopening conditions?
17. Who owns the decision, and when must it be revisited?

**Default posture:**  
Listen seriously. Protect people first. Verify the facts. Address the company’s actual responsibilities. Apply one global standard. Adapt execution locally. Communicate with purpose and restraint. Remain publicly silent when comment would amplify harm without adding truth or protection.
