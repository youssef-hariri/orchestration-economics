# CBAM EMERGING-MARKET PARTNERSHIP STRATEGY
## Final Comprehensive Strategy Document for Stakeholder Delivery

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This strategy enables a European industrial buyer to support emerging-market suppliers (India, Turkey, North Africa) through EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) compliance while preserving long-standing commercial relationships and building genuine partnership despite fundamental disagreements about policy fairness.

**Core Value Proposition:**
- **For Suppliers:** Transform CBAM from existential cost burden into managed expense through transparent baseline methodology, shared cost allocation, co-investment in emissions reduction, and long-term volume security
- **For Buyer:** Preserve supply continuity, protect relationships worth €200M+ in lifetime value, and position as ethical partner (not exploiter), while creating competitive differentiation through demonstrated sustainability leadership

**Success Criteria:** Retain 70%+ of supplier base through 2028 financial phase; achieve 15-20% aggregate emissions reduction; maintain price increases <3% above baseline commodity escalation

# SECTION 1: SUPPLIER SEGMENTATION & ENGAGEMENT ROADMAP

## 1.1 Four-Quadrant Segmentation Model

**Axis 1: Technical Readiness** (emissions data systems, compliance infrastructure)
**Axis 2: CBAM Perception** (view of policy fairness and legitimacy)

```
QUADRANT 1: CHAMPIONS (15-20% of suppliers)
├─ Profile: Larger, export-focused, ISO 14001 certified, some emissions monitoring
├─ Perception: See CBAM as competitive opportunity or accept as market reality
├─ Behavior: Proactive engagement; willing to co-invest; low exit risk
├─ Strategy: Accelerate to industry leadership; co-brand as sustainability leaders
├─ Support need: Minimal (baseline audit + technology access)
└─ Cost to buyer: €30-50k/supplier (3-year total)

QUADRANT 2: PRAGMATISTS (35-40% of suppliers)
├─ Profile: Mid-sized, mixed compliance culture, some efficiency programs
├─ Perception: Resigned acceptance; view CBAM as business cost
├─ Behavior: Willing to comply if ROI clear; responsive to structured incentives
├─ Strategy: Build transparent ROI cases; provide scaffolded support
├─ Support need: Moderate (baseline audit + co-investment for efficiency projects)
└─ Cost to buyer: €100-150k/supplier (3-year total)

QUADRANT 3: RESISTERS (25-30% of suppliers)
├─ Profile: Smaller, domestic-focused, resource constraints, limited certifications
├─ Perception: CBAM as unfair protectionism; threat to survival
├─ Behavior: Reluctant engagement; high exit risk; need reassurance
├─ Strategy: High-touch relationship management; intensive cost-sharing
├─ Support need: Intensive (baseline audit + smart metering + monthly support)
└─ Cost to buyer: €150-250k/supplier (3-year total)

QUADRANT 4: DISENGAGED (10-15% of suppliers)
├─ Profile: Smaller suppliers, passive, capacity constraints, overwhelmed
├─ Perception: Aware but powerless; minimal agency
├─ Behavior: Need clear direction; responsive to structured programs
├─ Strategy: Intensive guidance; external resource provision; simplified pathways
├─ Support need: High (baseline audit + dedicated support coordinator)
└─ Cost to buyer: €80-120k/supplier (3-year total)
```

# SECTION 2: CARBON COMPLIANCE SUPPORT ARCHITECTURE

## 2.1 Three-Year Phased Emissions Reporting Rollout

### **PHASE 1: RAPID BASELINE ESTABLISHMENT (2025, Months 1-6)**

**Objective:** Establish CBAM-compliant emissions baseline for all Tier-1 suppliers by end of 2025

**Mechanism 2.1A: Simplified Baseline Audit Program**

```
STEP 1: PRE-AUDIT SELF-ASSESSMENT (Week 1-2)
├─ Supplier completes questionnaire (20 questions, 2-3 hours effort):
│  ├─ Production volumes (2020-2023 annual data)
│  ├─ Energy sources (grid %, fuel mix %, captive power %)
│  ├─ Equipment inventory (furnace types, capacity, age)
│  ├─ Existing certifications (ISO 14001, EMS status)
│  └─ Baseline data confidence (self-rated: High/Medium/Low)
├─ Buyer system scores readiness (0-100 scale)
└─ Output: Readiness level determines audit intensity (Tier A/B/C)

STEP 2: INDEPENDENT BASELINE AUDIT (Week 3-8)
├─ On-site audit (2-3 days per facility)
│  ├─ Facility walkthrough: equipment mapping, meter verification, fuel storage inspection
│  ├─ Document review: Energy bills (24 months), production records, maintenance logs
│  ├─ Scope 1 verification:
│  │  ├─ Fuel invoices: Segregate by type (coal, natural gas, HFO, biomass)
│  │  ├─ Fuel quality: Sample testing (ash %, calorific value for coal; sulfur % for HFO)
│  │  ├─ Process emissions: Document production route (integrated vs. EAF vs. DRI)
│  │  └─ Off-gas capture: Furnace efficiency, combustion rates (if applicable)
│  ├─ Scope 2 verification:
│  │  ├─ Grid connection: Capacity check, monthly consumption verification
│  │  ├─ Regional grid factor: Establish correct carbon intensity for location
│  │  └─ Captive power: Document fuel mix, efficiency, alternative use
│  └─ Compliance readiness: Data systems assessment, documentation quality
│
├─ Third-party auditor:
│  ├─ Pre-approved list: DNV GL, TÜV SÜD, Bureau Veritas (global credibility)
│  ├─ Local subcontractors: Trained on emerging-market-specific issues (informal invoices, equipment variation)
│  └─ Cost: €3,500-6,000 per supplier (higher for integrated mills)
│
├─ Cost allocation:
│  ├─ Buyer pays: 80% of audit cost
│  ├─ Supplier pays: 20% of audit cost (investment commitment signal)
│  └─ Exception: Tier 3 suppliers (Resisters/Disengaged), buyer pays 100%
│
└─ Deliverable: Baseline Report (40-60 pages)
   ├─ Emissions profile (Scope 1 + 2, normalized to production)
   ├─ Methodology documentation (which factors applied, why)
   ├─ Confidence assessment (<70% = "provisional"; requires annual re-audit)
   └─ Recommendations (quick-win efficiency opportunities)

STEP 3: BASELINE REVIEW & TIER PLACEMENT (Week 9-12)
├─ Buyer technical team reviews audit (5-10 days)
├─ Provisional tier placement (subject to supplier response)
├─ Supplier disputes baseline: Independent review (costs borne by loser)
├─ Final tier assignment (published to supplier; confidential otherwise)
└─ Timeline: Baseline finalized within 12 weeks of audit completion

TIMELINE: All major suppliers baselined by December 2025
├─ India (30-35 suppliers): Start July 2025; complete December 2025
├─ Turkey (15-20 suppliers): Start May 2025; complete October 2025
├─ North Africa (8-10 suppliers): Start September 2025; complete February 2026
```

**Cost Summary – Phase 1:**
- Audits (60 suppliers × €4.5k average × 80% buyer cost): €216,000
- Baseline Authority governance (CBAC-NA setup): €150,000
- Regional grid factor data curation: €50,000
- **Total Phase 1 budget: €416,000 (Q2-Q4 2025)**

## 2.2 Carbon Pricing & Financial Support Structure

### **Mechanism 2.2A: Tiered CBAM Liability Sharing Model (2026-2028)**

**Core Principle:** CBAM costs shared proportionally based on emissions reduction effort. Rewards efficiency; protects against windfall costs.

```
TIER 1A: "ASPIRATIONAL LEADERS" (≥15% below sectoral baseline)
├─ Profile: E.g., EAF mini-mills, integrated mills with modern efficiency, renewable power PPAs
├─ Buyer cost-sharing: 85% of CBAM liability
├─ Supplier cost-share: 15% of CBAM liability
├─ Example (Indian integrated mill):
│  ├─ Baseline intensity: 2.1 tCO2/ton
│  ├─ Supplier actual: 1.75 tCO2/ton (17% reduction)
│  ├─ CBAM price: €30/ton CO2
│  ├─ CBAM liability (5,000 tons): €30 × 1.75 × 5,000 = €262,500
│  ├─ Supplier pays: 15% × €262,500 = €39,375 (~€8/ton or 2% price increase)
│  ├─ Buyer absorbs: 85% × €262,500 = €223,125
│  └─ Outcome: Supplier remains highly profitable; buyer absorbs reasonable cost
│
├─ Price adjustment mechanism: €5-15/ton premium (vs. pre-CBAM baseline)
├─ Duration: Perpetual (if emissions remain >15% below baseline)
├─ Requirements:
│  ├─ Monthly verified emissions reporting (via platform)
│  ├─ Annual third-party audit
│  ├─ Documented ongoing emissions reduction plan (min. 2.5% annually)
│  ├─ Participation in peer learning network (share best practices)
│  └─ Commitment to data transparency (auditors can visit anytime)
│
└─ Benefits beyond cost-sharing:
   ├─ Marketing: Buyer highlights Tier 1A suppliers as "green leaders"
   ├─ Volume security: Preferred allocation in case of supply constraints
   ├─ Technology access: First access to buyer-funded efficiency technologies
   └─ Relationship: Annual strategic summit with buyer CEO

TIER 1B: "ROUTE CHAMPIONS" (5-15% below baseline)
├─ Profile: Efficient suppliers within their technology class (e.g., best EAF mill in region)
├─ Buyer cost-sharing: 80% of CBAM liability
├─ Supplier cost-share: 20%
├─ Price adjustment: €3-10/ton premium
├─ Duration: 2026-2028 (reviewed in 2029 financial phase transition)
├─ Requirements: Same as Tier 1A (reporting, audit, reduction commitment)
└─ Benefits: Recognized as "efficient supplier"; future co-investment priority for further reductions

TIER 2: "COMPLIANT BASELINE" (±5% of baseline)
├─ Profile: Suppliers meeting baseline standards; competitive but not leaders
├─ Buyer cost-sharing: 50% of CBAM liability
├─ Supplier cost-share: 50%
├─ Example:
│  ├─ Supplier at 2.15 tCO2/ton (baseline 2.1) = 2% above baseline
│  ├─ CBAM liability: €30 × 2.15 × 5,000 = €322,500
│  ├─ Supplier pays: €161,250 (50%); requires ~€32/ton price increase (8%)
│  ├─ Buyer absorbs: €161,250
│  └─ Outcome: Manageable price increase; supplier remains stable
│
├─ Price adjustment: €15-25/ton (3-5% increase needed)
├─ Duration: 2026-2028
├─ Requirements:
│  ├─ Quarterly verified emissions reporting
│  ├─ Biennial third-party audit
│  ├─ Basic emissions reduction roadmap (optional; encouraged)
│  └─ Annual relationship review
│
└─ Upgrade path: Achieve Tier 1B by 2028 (if efficiency investments succeed)

TIER 3A: "CATCH-UP PERIOD" (5-15% above baseline)
├─ Profile: Smaller/older suppliers; fixable efficiency gaps
├─ Buyer cost-sharing: 30% of CBAM liability ONLY IF supplier meets support milestones
├─ Supplier cost-share: 70% (unless co-investment conditions met)
├─ Example:
│  ├─ Supplier at 2.45 tCO2/ton (baseline 2.1) = 17% above baseline (OUTSIDE Tier 3A range, but illustrative)
│  ├─ CBAM liability: €30 × 2.45 × 5,000 = €367,500
│  ├─ Base supplier share: 70% × €367,500 = €257,250 (€51.45/ton = 13% price increase—unsustainable)
│  ├─ BUT: With co-investment of €120k reducing emissions 12% → 2.15 tCO2/ton
│  ├─ New CBAM liability: €322,500 (€64.5k savings from efficiency)
│  ├─ Buyer absorbs 30% of new liability: €96,750
│  ├─ Supplier pays: €225,750 (still high, but partially offset by efficiency savings)
│  └─ Outcome: Viable if co-investment delivers results
│
├─ Price adjustment: €20-30/ton (higher if no co-investment; lower if investment delivered)
├─ Duration: 2026-2028; extended to 2029 only if clear progress
├─ Requirements (MANDATORY):
│  ├─ Participation in Buyer-funded technical program
│  ├─ Baseline audit within 6 months of entry to Tier 3A
│  ├─ Monthly emissions reporting (close monitoring)
│  ├─ Co-investment commitment: Supplier contributes 20% of project cost (cash or in-kind labor)
│  ├─ 12-month emissions reduction roadmap (documented; auditable)
│  └─ Quarterly progress reviews (with Buyer technical team)
│
├─ Co-investment allocation (see Mechanism 2.2C):
│  ├─ Smart metering: €10-15k (Buyer 100%)
│  ├─ Efficiency projects: €60-100k (Buyer 60-80%; Supplier 20-40%)
│  └─ Training/audit: €5-15k (Buyer 100%)
│
└─ Graduation path: Achieve Tier 2 by 2028 via efficiency improvements

TIER 3B: "URGENT INTERVENTION" (>15% above baseline)
├─ Profile: Critically inefficient; at risk of non-viability
├─ Buyer cost-sharing: 10% of CBAM liability (minimal; contingent on action)
├─ Supplier cost-share: 90% (severe penalty to incentivize change)
├─ CBAM liability nearly unsustainable without radical intervention
├─ Requirements (MANDATORY):
│  ├─ Immediate facility audit (within 8 weeks)
│  ├─ Monthly emissions monitoring
│  ├─ Intensive buyer technical support (weekly calls minimum)
│  ├─ Major co-investment: €150-250k Buyer funding (nearly 100%)
│  ├─ Supplier contributes 10-15% in-kind (labor, premises, etc.)
│  ├─ Documented 24-month transformation roadmap (aggressive targets)
│  └─ Performance milestones (quarterly; miss one → re-evaluate partnership)
│
├─ Co-investment strategy: Prioritize "quick wins" + major efficiency retrofit
│  ├─ Examples: Boiler replacement (€80-150k), furnace upgrade (€60-120k), renewable PPA facilitation (€100-200k)
│  └─ Goal: Move to Tier 3A by end of Year 1; Tier 2 by end of Year 2
│
├─ Exit contingency:
│  ├─ If supplier rejects co-investment: Automatic exit process begins (6-month wind-down)
│  ├─ If co-investment underdelivers (efficiency <8% by month 12): Partnership review
│  └─ Buyer continues CBAM absorption for 6-month exit window (Mechanism 2.2A, Tier 4)
│
└─ Strategic messaging: "We're investing heavily to save your business. This is our strong signal of long-term partnership commitment. But you must engage."

TIER 4: "EXIT SUPPORT" (Voluntary Withdrawal)
├─ Mechanism: Suppliers choosing to exit supply relationship
├─ Buyer coverage: 100% of CBAM costs on final 18 months of supply
├─ Conditions:
│  ├─ Minimum 6-month notice required (orderly transition planning)
│  ├─ Supplier co-investment repayment: If received co-investment within 24 months, repay 50%
│  ├─ Purchase commitment: Buyer commits to volume for 18-month runway (no cancellation)
│  └─ Orderly exit: No abrupt quality deterioration or supply cuts
│
├─ Duration: Maximum 2 years' worth of supply at historical volumes
├─ Example:
│  ├─ Supplier exports 4,000 tons/year to buyer
│  ├─ 18 months runway = 6,000 tons total CBAM coverage
│  ├─ CBAM cost at 2.5 tCO2/ton, €30/ton: €450k buyer absorption
│  └─ Buyer commits to purchase at market price (no premium needed during exit)
│
└─ Rationale: Provides suppliers honorable exit; maintains relationship goodwill (door open to re-entry); reduces likelihood of supply disruption

TRANSITION RULE (2029 Financial Phase):
├─ All tiers transition to new CBAM Phase 2 rules (if EU changes regulations)
├─ Buyer signals commitment: "We will re-evaluate cost-sharing under new rules; committed to maintaining partnership"
├─ Default assumption: Cost-sharing percentages decrease 20-30% post-2028 (budget constraints)
├─ But: Suppliers who achieve efficiency gains may maintain higher tiers (incentive for ongoing improvement)
└─ Communication NOW: Publish 2029+ outlook (reduce surprise/betrayal risk)
```

### **Mechanism 2.2B: CBAM Reserve Fund & Volatility Hedge (2026-2030)**

**Objective:** Protect suppliers from CBAM price volatility; create collective risk-sharing pool

```
STRUCTURE: Commodity-Style Price Hedging Fund

CAPITALIZATION:
├─ Buyer annual contribution: €1,500,000 (covers ~15-20% of projected CBAM costs)
├─ Supplier contributions: Risk-based
│  ├─ Tier 1 suppliers: 0.5% of annual CBAM liability (minimal)
│  ├─ Tier 2 suppliers: 1.0% of annual CBAM liability
│  ├─ Tier 3 suppliers: 1.5% of annual CBAM liability
│  └─ Maximum per supplier: €50,000/year (protects small suppliers from excessive contribution)
│
└─ Fund management:
   ├─ Independent trustee (e.g., major investment bank)
   ├─ Quarterly reporting (transparent fund position, payouts)
   ├─ Annual audit (external verification of fund integrity)
   └─ 7-year duration (2026-2032; reviewed annually for renewal)

PAYOUT MECHANISM:
├─ Baseline calculation: Expected annual CBAM cost at contract price (e.g., €30/ton)
├─ Quarterly true-up:
│  ├─ If actual CBAM price >125% of baseline (e.g., €37.50/ton vs. €30 expected):
│  │  └─ Fund pays 40% of excess to suppliers
│  │  └─ Example: (€37.50 - €30) × 40% = €3/ton reimbursement to supplier
│  │
│  ├─ If actual CBAM price 75-125% of baseline (normal range):
│  │  └─ No payout; fund accumulates
│  │
│  └─ If actual CBAM price <75% of baseline (price drop):
│     └─ Suppliers contribute 20% of underage to fund (sustainability mechanism)
│     └─ Used to fund new efficiency programs (perpetual improvement)
│
├─ Individual supplier cap:
│  ├─ Maximum annual payout: 5% of supplier's gross margin (doesn't create windfall)
│  ├─ Example: 8% margin supplier earning €320k on €4M revenue
│  │  └─ Max fund payout: €16k/year (5% × €320k)
│  └─ Excess returns: Accumulated in fund (benefits all suppliers over time)
│
└─ Fund depletion scenario:
   ├─ If fund depleted before year-end (rare): Buyer tops up from capex budget
   └─ If fund surplus at year-end: Carry forward or distribute as "efficiency dividend" to Tier 1 suppliers
