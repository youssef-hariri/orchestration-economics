# Global Flexible-Work Policy Package

**Status:** Draft for approval and country-level validation  
**Policy owner:** Global People/HR  
**Applies to:** Employees of [Company] in twelve country offices  
**Review cycle:** At least annually, or earlier where law, contracts, collective arrangements, operations, or employee experience change

> **Important:** This package is a global framework, not country-specific legal advice. It must be implemented through approved country schedules and, where applicable, collective agreements, works-council processes, employee consultation, and individual arrangements.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

[Company] will provide a globally consistent framework for flexible work while allowing lawful and evidence-based local adaptation.

The policy supports:

- Standard, flexible-hour, hybrid, remote, part-time, reduced-hours, and compressed-week arrangements;
- Four-day-week pilots where legally permissible and operationally viable;
- Sustainable performance, rest, and a right to disconnect;
- Fair access to promotion, development, recognition, and high-impact work;
- Customer coverage, security, safety, and business continuity;
- Cultural differences in visibility, office presence, hierarchy, and communication.

The policy does **not** create a universal entitlement to remote work, hybrid work, a four-day week, reduced hours, or a particular schedule. Eligibility depends on applicable law, contracts, collective arrangements, role requirements, operational needs, security, safety, client commitments, and approved processes.

The central commitment is:

> Commitment will be assessed through dependable results, quality, collaboration, customer care, integrity, and sustainable performance—not unnecessary physical presence, constant online availability, or unpaid work.

---

# 2. Policy Architecture and Precedence

The policy operates at four levels:

| Level | Purpose | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Global policy** | Establishes mandatory minimum safeguards and principles | Applies globally, subject to more protective local requirements |
| **Country schedule** | Specifies locally validated legal, contractual, payroll, privacy, and employee-representation rules | Mandatory for employees in that country |
| **Team operating agreement** | Defines collaboration, coverage, meetings, handoffs, and office-use practices | Must comply with the global policy and country schedule |
| **Individual arrangement** | Records an employee’s approved schedule, location, hours, and conditions | Must comply with all higher-level requirements |

### 2.1 Precedence

The following apply in order of legal authority:

1. Applicable mandatory law and legally binding regulatory requirements;
2. Collective agreements, works-council agreements, and other binding employee-representation arrangements;
3. Individual employment contracts and formally agreed arrangements;
4. This global policy and approved country schedules;
5. Team operating agreements and managerial instructions.

Collective agreements and contracts may provide more protective rights. No policy, team rule, or individual arrangement may reduce a mandatory legal entitlement unless applicable law expressly permits it.

Tax, immigration, payroll, social-security, privacy, information-security, health-and-safety, and accessibility requirements must be assessed before implementation where relevant.

This policy does not itself amend:

- Employment contracts;
- Collective agreements;
- Statutory entitlements;
- Established working arrangements;
- Approved accommodations.

# 3. Global Principles

The following are **mandatory global rules**, subject to more protective local requirements.

## 3.1 Lawful, documented implementation

No flexible-work model, monitoring practice, contractual change, or cross-border arrangement may be implemented until required legal, employee-representative, payroll, privacy, security, health-and-safety, and operational validations are complete.

A documented interim process may be used only where Local Legal approves it, defines its safeguards and duration, and identifies the responsible owner.

## 3.2 Role- and evidence-based decisions

Decisions must be based on documented:

- Role requirements;
- Customer or service coverage;
- Security and confidentiality;
- Health and safety;
- Equipment or facility needs;
- Working-time requirements;
- Team capacity and handoffs;
- Applicable law and collective arrangements.

Manager preference, cultural stereotypes, seniority alone, perceived loyalty, or a general belief that office presence demonstrates commitment is not sufficient.

## 3.3 Equal dignity and non-discrimination

The company prohibits discrimination or retaliation based on, among other factors:

- Work arrangement;
- Office presence;
- Nationality, culture, race, or ethnicity;
- Gender, pregnancy, or family status;
- Disability or health;
- Religion;
- Age;
- Caregiving;
- Part-time or reduced-hours status;
- Perceived ambition or commitment.

Employees must not be disadvantaged for:

- Requesting or using approved flexibility;
- Choosing or requiring a conventional schedule;
- Disconnecting during protected non-working time;
- Declining unlawful or unsafe out-of-hours work;
- Raising a good-faith concern;
- Requesting an accommodation;
- Participating in an investigation or fairness review.

## 3.4 Outcome-based performance

Performance must be assessed primarily through:

- Delivery against agreed objectives;
- Quality, reliability, and security;
- Customer or user impact;
- Collaboration and knowledge sharing;
- Problem-solving and initiative;
- Leadership and development of others;
- Professional conduct;
- Sustainable execution.

The following must not be used as standalone evidence of performance, commitment, or promotion readiness:

- Physical presence;
- Badge swipes or attendance alone;
- Online status;
- Camera use;
- Immediate responses outside working hours;
- Number of messages or meetings;
- Long workdays;
- Optional social-event attendance;
- Keystrokes, screenshots, or continuous activity monitoring.

## 3.5 Accurate working time and sustainable workload

Employees must record working time through the applicable local process wherever legally required. Elsewhere, employees and managers must use the designated process to report material additional hours, on-call work, or work during protected rest periods.

Managers must not request, encourage, reward, conceal, or knowingly permit unpaid or unrecorded work. They must take reasonable steps to identify and correct:

- Recurring excess hours;
- Missed breaks or rest;
- Work on non-working days;
- Excessive meeting burdens;
- Repeated late or early meetings;
- Persistent out-of-hours communication.

Unpaid work must not be described as flexibility, ownership, loyalty, or commitment.

## 3.6 Fair career access

Employees using remote, hybrid, part-time, reduced-hours, compressed, or conventional schedules must have equitable access to:

- High-impact assignments;
- Client and leadership exposure;
- Promotion processes;
- Mentoring and sponsorship;
- Training and conferences;
- Stretch opportunities;
- Recognition and professional networks.

Managers must provide meaningful remote-equivalent access, not merely a meeting link. This may include advance materials, decision records, follow-up access, deliberate sponsorship, and rotation of presentation opportunities.

## 3.7 Purposeful office attendance

Office attendance may be required for legitimate, documented purposes, such as:

- Onboarding or training;
- Workshops and planning;
- Customer meetings;
- Secure equipment or facility access;
- Laboratory or physical work;
- Safety, regulatory, or incident requirements;
- Relationship-building activities that cannot reasonably be delivered remotely.

Attendance rules must be proportionate, communicated in advance, legally validated, and applied consistently. Optional office social activities must not create career advantages.

## 3.8 Privacy and proportionate monitoring

Monitoring must be lawful, transparent, necessary, proportionate, purpose-limited, and locally validated.

Continuous keystroke, screen, webcam, or activity monitoring is prohibited as a default productivity measure. Exceptional monitoring requires prior Privacy and Legal approval, any required employee-representative involvement, appropriate notice, an expiry or review date, and safeguards against secondary use.

Attendance, badge, online-status, and location data may be used only for documented security, facilities, safety, payroll, or approved fairness purposes. They must not be used as proxies for productivity, commitment, ambition, loyalty, or promotion readiness.

# 4. Locally Adaptable Provisions

The following require **country-level legal, HR, payroll, privacy, employee-representative, and operational review** before implementation:

- Maximum daily and weekly hours;
- Rest and break requirements;
- Overtime eligibility, approval, payment, and recording;
- Flexible-work request rights and deadlines;
- Compressed-week structures;
- Part-time and reduced-hours pay and benefits;
- Leave and public-holiday treatment;
- Right-to-disconnect procedures and exceptions;
- Remote-work expenses, equipment, insurance, and safety;
- Contract and work-location changes;
- Cross-border work, tax, payroll, immigration, and social security;
- Monitoring and employee-data practices;
- Accommodation procedures;
- Employee-representation requirements;
- Treatment of contractors, agency workers, interns, and apprentices;
- Local language, notices, filings, records, and appeal procedures.

Each departure from the global framework must be classified as:

1. Mandatory legal or regulatory variation;
2. Collective-agreement or contractual variation;
3. Employee-representation requirement;
4. Documented operational, security, or safety variation;
5. More protective company standard;
6. Temporary emergency measure.

Cultural preference or internal tradition alone is not a sufficient basis for restricting flexibility.

# 5. Operating Standards

## 5.1 Flexible-work models

Subject to local validation and role suitability, the company may offer:

### Standard schedule

The normal contractual schedule and location for the role, with ordinary flexibility under local procedures.

### Flexible daily hours

Variable start and finish times within approved limits, while meeting contractual hours, rest, collaboration, coverage, time-recording, and overtime requirements.

### Hybrid work

Work from both an approved company workplace and an approved remote location. The written arrangement must state:

- Approved locations;
- Office-attendance expectations and purposes;
- Working days and hours;
- Collaboration window;
- Coverage and handoffs;
- Equipment, expense, security, and review terms.

### Primarily remote work

Work performed primarily away from a company workplace from an approved location. Working from another country, relocating the primary work location, or working from an unapproved location requires prior written approval and Local HR/Legal review.

### Part-time or reduced-hours work

Work performed for reduced contractual hours with proportionate treatment of objectives, workload, meetings, on-call duties, pay, benefits, leave, and development.

### Temporary flexibility

Time-limited arrangements for health, recovery, caregiving, education, emergencies, temporary relocation, business continuity, recruitment, or retention.

Requests potentially connected with disability, pregnancy, health, religion, or another protected status must be referred to the confidential accommodation process.

---

## 5.2 Compressed workweeks and four-day arrangements

A four-day arrangement may be:

1. A compressed full-time schedule;
2. A reduced-hours four-day schedule;
3. A fixed-term team pilot;
4. A staggered coverage model.

A compressed arrangement may be used only where the country schedule confirms the permitted structure and the written arrangement states the lawful daily, weekly, and, where relevant, reference-period hours.

Every arrangement must specify:

- Contractual hours;
- Daily schedule and non-working day;
- Pay, benefits, bonus, equity, and leave treatment;
- Meetings and collaboration expectations;
- Coverage and escalation;
- On-call obligations;
- Workload and objective adjustments;
- Time recording and overtime;
- Non-working-day contact rules;
- Review, modification, extension, and ending provisions.

A four-day week must not be created by transferring a five-day workload into unsafe, unlawful, or unpaid hours. Routine work and communications must not be shifted to the non-working day.

Payroll and Benefits review is mandatory before implementation.

---

## 5.3 Collaboration windows

Each team must publish a written operating agreement covering:

- Collaboration window;
- Core service hours;
- Response-time expectations;
- Meeting norms;
- Meeting-free periods;
- Handoffs and documentation;
- Incident and escalation coverage;
- Office-attendance purposes;
- Time-zone rotation;
- Treatment of non-working days;
- Absence and holiday backup.

A collaboration window is the minimum period needed for routine synchronous work. It is not continuous availability.

Recurring cross-time-zone meetings must be rotated where practicable. If rotation is not feasible, the reason and alternative safeguard must be documented.

---

## 5.4 Client and operational coverage

Before approving a flexible arrangement, the team must complete a coverage assessment addressing:

- Customer-facing hours;
- Service-level obligations;
- Support and incident response;
- Releases and deployment windows;
- Public holidays and closures;
- Absence backup;
- Time-zone handoffs;
- Escalation contacts;
- Security and facility access.

Restrictions may be justified by:

1. Binding legal or contractual requirements;
2. Documented service-level or customer requirements;
3. Demonstrable operational, security, or safety necessity.

A customer preference, cultural expectation, or generalized assumption about local availability is not sufficient by itself.

The assessment must explain why alternatives such as staggered schedules, rotations, handoffs, asynchronous work, additional staffing, or revised service levels are insufficient.

Burdensome early, late, weekend, or holiday coverage must be rotated where feasible. If rotation is not feasible, the team must document why, provide required compensation or rest, set reasonable frequency limits, and review the arrangement periodically.

---

## 5.5 Working hours and availability

Employees are expected to be reachable during agreed working hours, collaboration windows, published client coverage periods, scheduled on-call periods, or genuine emergencies.

Availability categories must remain distinct:

| Category | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Working availability** | Reachability during scheduled working time |
| **On-call availability** | Separately scheduled and governed obligation |
| **Emergency contact** | Narrow, unexpected event requiring immediate action |
| **Visibility of contribution** | Evidence of results, impact, and collaboration |

Online status, calendar visibility, or immediate response must not be treated as universal performance requirements.

---

## 5.6 Right to disconnect

Subject to local law and documented on-call arrangements:

- Routine communications outside working hours do not require a response;
- Managers should avoid sending non-urgent messages outside recipients’ working hours or use delayed delivery;
- Routine meetings must not be scheduled during protected rest or agreed non-working time;
- Employees must not suffer retaliation or career disadvantage for disconnecting;
- Voluntary out-of-hours work must not be requested, encouraged, rewarded, or converted into an informal expectation.

### Permitted exceptions

1. **Routine communication:** No response expected.
2. **Scheduled on-call or standby:** Documented in advance with notice, frequency, compensation or time treatment, escalation, and rest rules.
3. **Genuine emergency:** An unexpected event requiring immediate action to protect safety, security, critical service continuity, or a material customer obligation.

A predictable deadline, staffing shortage, or poor planning is not a genuine emergency. Repeated emergency contact must be recorded, reviewed, and addressed through staffing, process, or service-level changes.

No performance, promotion, compensation, recognition, client-exposure, or assignment decision may favor routine out-of-hours availability.

---

## 5.7 Mandatory meetings, training, travel, and incidents

Mandatory meetings and training should:

- Occur within scheduled working time where practicable;
- Be repeated, recorded, or supported by asynchronous alternatives;
- Be treated as working time;
- Not routinely fall on a compressed-schedule non-working day;
- Comply with overtime, compensation, and rest rules.

Country schedules must address:

- Business travel during non-working time;
- Cross-time-zone travel;
- Rest after travel;
- Travel required for office attendance;
- Travel expenses, insurance, and liability;
- Whether travel time is working time.

On-call and incident-response arrangements must define:

- Designation authority;
- Notice;
- Frequency limits;
- Compensation or time off;
- Recovery time;
- Escalation;
- Treatment of non-working-day interruptions;
- Post-incident workload review.

---

## 5.8 Remote-work location, equipment, and security

Employees may work only from approved locations and must:

- Use approved devices, networks, and security controls;
- Protect company and client information;
- Maintain a safe and suitable workspace;
- Report incidents, injuries, and equipment failures;
- Follow privacy and records requirements.

Country schedules must define rules for:

- Equipment and return;
- Internet, furniture, and other expenses;
- Insurance and liability;
- Home-workplace assessments;
- Ergonomics;
- Remote-work injuries;
- Shared homes, co-working spaces, and public locations;
- Confidential conversations and documents.

Security requirements must be risk-based and must not assume that caregiving, shared housing, or public workspaces automatically make remote work unsuitable.

# 6. Fairness and Career Safeguards

## 6.1 Promotion and talent decisions

Promotion, compensation, talent-review, recognition, and assignment decisions must be supported by role-relevant evidence.

Where a decision appears to rely on physical presence, out-of-hours responsiveness, or informal visibility, HR must review the decision through the applicable calibration process and correct it where appropriate.

Managers must provide multiple ways to demonstrate impact, including:

- Written goals and progress;
- Documented decisions;
- Demonstrations and presentations;
- Customer or stakeholder outcomes;
- Collaborator feedback;
- Knowledge sharing;
- Leadership and development of others.

## 6.2 Conventional-schedule fairness

Employees must not be disadvantaged for using an approved flexible arrangement or for choosing or requiring a conventional working pattern where that pattern is available and lawful.

## 6.3 Proximity-bias reviews

At least twice yearly, where lawful and privacy-protective, HR should review outcomes by work arrangement, including:

- Performance ratings;
- Promotions;
- Compensation;
- High-impact assignments;
- Client and leadership exposure;
- Training and sponsorship;
- Attrition;
- Complaints and appeals.

Reports should be aggregated and subject to local privacy requirements. Material disparities must trigger investigation and remediation.

## 6.4 Inclusion and accommodations

The company will provide legally required reasonable accommodations and assess additional accessibility measures through the applicable local process. Operational limitations must be reviewed through that process and must not be assumed by the manager.

The company will support, as legally required or operationally appropriate:

- Accessible collaboration technology;
- Captioning and transcripts;
- Screen-reader-compatible documents;
- Ergonomic and assistive equipment;
- Flexible breaks and start times;
- Accessible offices;
- Alternatives to travel;
- Religious-observance scheduling;
- Quiet or sensory-sensitive workspaces;
- Caregiving-related requests.

Health, disability, family, and religious information must be handled confidentially. Managers must not request unnecessary sensitive information.

# 8. Request, Approval, and Appeal Standards

## 8.1 Employee request

Requests should use the global form or approved local equivalent and identify:

- Proposed schedule and location;
- Working hours and non-working days;
- Start date and duration;
- Collaboration and response expectations;
- Coverage and handoffs;
- Equipment or accessibility needs;
- Review date.

Sensitive medical, disability, family, or religious information must be submitted through the confidential process.

## 8.2 Manager assessment

The manager must document:

1. Role requirements;
2. Coverage and client impact;
3. Security and safety;
4. Working-time and rest implications;
5. Workload and objective adjustments;
6. Comparable cases;
7. Alternatives considered;
8. Equality and accessibility impact;
9. Required HR, Legal, Payroll, Privacy, or Security review;
10. Decision and review date.

A standardized decision matrix must be used.

## 8.3 Approval categories

A request may be:

- Approved;
- Approved with modifications;
- Approved temporarily as a pilot;
- Declined with written reasons;
- Referred to the accommodation or legal process.

A refusal must identify the specific evidence-based reason and a feasible alternative where one exists.

Where local law does not set a deadline, the company target is **20 business days** after receiving information reasonably necessary to assess the request, unless the employee agrees to an extension. Urgent health, safety, or accommodation matters require expedited handling.

## 8.4 HR oversight

HR review is mandatory for:

- All refusals;
- All exceptions;
- Compressed or reduced-hours arrangements;
- Cross-border work;
- On-call arrangements;
- Monitoring;
- Accommodations;
- Material changes to established arrangements.

HR must also periodically sample routine approvals and review patterns by manager, team, role, level, and country.

## 8.5 Appeals

Each country schedule must specify:

- Appeal deadline;
- Reviewer or panel;
- Response deadline;
- Interim treatment;
- Representative participation;
- Confidentiality;
- Available remedies;
- Corrective-action process.

The original decision-maker must not be the sole appeal reviewer.

# 9. Changes, Exceptions, and Established Arrangements

## 9.1 Definitions

A **material change** includes a change to:

- Contractual hours;
- Primary work location;
- Non-working day;
- Required office attendance;
- On-call duties;
- Pay or benefits;
- Response expectations;
- Monitoring;
- Duration or termination conditions.

An **established arrangement** is a continuing arrangement that may have contractual, statutory, collective, or other legal significance.

A **genuine emergency** is an unexpected event requiring immediate action to protect safety, security, critical service continuity, or a material customer obligation. It excludes routine staffing shortages and predictable deadlines.

## 9.2 Ending or modifying arrangements

Except in a genuine emergency, changes to an established arrangement require:

- A documented reason;
- Compliance with law, contract, and collective arrangements;
- Required consultation;
- Reasonable notice as defined locally;
- Consideration of employee and accommodation impact;
- A transition period where practicable;
- Appeal or reconsideration;
- No retaliation.

An established flexible-work arrangement may not be changed solely because this policy or a country schedule has been introduced.

## 9.3 Exceptions register

All exceptions must record:

- Reason;
- Legal, contractual, operational, or safety basis;
- Affected employee or group;
- Duration;
- Safeguards;
- Coverage plan;
- Approver;
- Fairness assessment;
- Expiry or review date.

Repeated exceptions must trigger review of the underlying policy or operating model.

The Council will include representatives from:

- Privacy;
- Information Security;
- Payroll and Benefits;

- Accessibility or occupational health;

- Review cross-country fairness;
- Approve high-risk models;
- Monitor metrics;
- Review exceptions;
- Issue templates and manager guidance;
- Coordinate annual review.

## 10.2 Responsibilities

### Global HR

Owns the global framework, templates, training, metrics, governance, and annual review.

### Local HR and Legal

Validate country schedules, advise managers, manage local requests and appeals, and complete required consultations.

### Business leaders

Define genuine role and coverage requirements and prevent informal availability norms.

### Managers

Set proportionate objectives, assess requests consistently, protect rest, plan coverage, document decisions, and ensure equitable development access.

### Employees

Meet agreed objectives and hours, record time where required, follow security and safety requirements, respect colleagues’ non-working time, and raise concerns promptly.

### Employee representatives

Are consulted or involved where required by law, collective arrangements, or established company processes.

# 11. Metrics and Review

Metrics must be lawful, proportionate, aggregated where necessary, and used to identify systemic issues—not to rank countries, teams, or employees by commitment.

## 11.1 Business and customer metrics

- Delivery predictability;
- Quality and defect rates;
- Customer satisfaction;
- Service-level attainment;
- Incident response and resolution;
- Security and compliance outcomes;
- Handoff failures;
- Coverage gaps.

## 11.2 Employee and sustainability metrics

- Engagement and inclusion;
- Workload and well-being;
- Actual additional hours and overtime;
- Meeting burden;
- Out-of-hours communications;
- Disconnect experience;
- Absence and regretted attrition;
- Satisfaction with flexible work.

## 11.3 Fairness metrics

- Approval, refusal, and appeal rates;
- Access by role, level, office, and lawful demographic categories;
- Performance ratings;
- Promotions and compensation;
- High-impact assignments;
- Client and leadership exposure;
- Training, mentoring, and sponsorship;
- Attrition and complaints.

Fairness analytics must have defined access controls, retention limits, minimum group sizes, privacy notices, and remediation procedures. They must not be used for automatic employment decisions.

# 12. Implementation Roadmap

## Phase 1: Global design and governance

1. Approve the global policy principles.
2. Establish the Global Flexible Work Council.
3. Identify the twelve employing entities and country owners.
4. Approve standard templates for:
   - Country schedules;
   - Requests;
   - Manager decisions;
   - Team agreements;
   - Individual arrangements;
   - Four-day pilots;
   - Appeals;
   - Exceptions.
5. Define global records, privacy, and reporting standards.

## Phase 2: Country validation

For each office:

1. Complete the localization checklist below.
2. Draft the country schedule.
3. Complete equality, accessibility, and operational-impact assessments.
4. Consult employee representatives where required.
5. Configure payroll, benefits, leave, and timekeeping.
6. Approve privacy, security, and health-and-safety controls.
7. Complete the country validation certificate.
8. Publish the schedule in required accessible formats and languages.

## Phase 3: Role and team design

1. Classify roles by flexibility suitability.
2. Document genuine location, security, client, safety, and coverage requirements.
3. Create team operating agreements.
4. Map time-zone burdens and coverage rotations.
5. Establish remote-equivalent access to career opportunities.
6. Review attendance requirements for indirect discrimination.

## Phase 4: Launch

Before accepting requests:

1. Train all decision-makers.
2. Activate the integrated HR request workflow.
3. Publish the policy, country schedules, FAQs, and forms.
4. Activate accommodation, appeal, privacy, and grievance channels.
5. Confirm payroll, timekeeping, security, and leave-system readiness.
6. Communicate that no lawful work pattern is a measure of loyalty or ambition.

## Phase 5: Review and improvement

- Managers review individual arrangements on the documented review date.
- Teams review operating agreements at least twice yearly.
- HR reviews fairness and workload indicators at least twice yearly where lawful.
- The Council reviews the framework at least annually.
- Material legal, operational, contractual, or employee-representation changes trigger an earlier review.

## Final Policy Statement

[Company] recognizes that lawful and effective work may be organized in different ways across countries, roles, teams, and individuals. The company will accommodate those differences without allowing cultural assumptions, office visibility, constant availability, or managerial preference to determine commitment or career opportunity.

Local adaptation is permitted where supported by law, collective arrangements, documented client or operational requirements, security, safety, or accessibility needs. Every adaptation must be proportionate, transparent, reviewable, and assessed for workload and career impact.

This policy becomes effective only after approval by Global People/HR, completion of required country-level validation, consultation with employee representatives where applicable, and confirmation of operational readiness.
