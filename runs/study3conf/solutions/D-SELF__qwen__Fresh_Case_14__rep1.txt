**CONFIDENTIAL EXECUTIVE MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors and Executive Committee  
**FROM:** Head of Corporate Finance & ESG Strategy  
**DATE:** October 2025  
**SUBJECT:** 2026 Multi-Jurisdictional Green and Transition Bond Financing Strategy  

---

### **Executive Summary**
In 2026, the Group must finance critical decarbonization retrofits across Europe, North Africa (NA), and Southeast Asia (SEA). However, we face a highly fragmented capital markets landscape: strict EU Taxonomy requirements contrast sharply with the transition-heavy frameworks in the Gulf and ASEAN. Compounding this, inconsistent internal emissions data and conflicting guidance from global ESG rating agencies versus regional banks threaten our cost of capital and corporate reputation.

To secure necessary funding without triggering greenwashing claims or alienating regional investors, a monolithic bond framework is unviable. Instead, this strategy proposes a **"Modular Multi-Asset Framework."** By strictly ring-fencing our use of proceeds by taxonomy, financing our own data-infrastructure upgrades, and bifurcating our engagement strategy for global raters versus regional banks, we will optimize our weighted average cost of capital (WACC), satisfy divergent regulatory regimes, and fiercely protect our corporate credibility.

---

### **Pillar 1: Structuring Issuance Across Divergent Taxonomies**

**The Challenge:** A single framework attempting to encompass both strict EU "green" and regional "transition" assets risks taxonomy contamination. This would alienate European investors and invite severe greenwashing scrutiny.

**The Strategy: The "Modular Multi-Asset Framework" Model**  
We will abandon the concept of a single "Global Green" framework. Instead, we will establish a modular program with strictly ring-fenced tranches, each governed by its specific regional taxonomy and labeled with absolute precision.

*   **Tranche A: The "Eurobond" (Strict Green)**
    *   **Target Market:** European institutional investors and EU Green Bond Standard (EuGBS) mandates.
    *   **Taxonomy Alignment:** 100% aligned with the EU Taxonomy, strictly meeting "Do No Significant Harm" (DNSH) and Technical Screening Criteria.
    *   **Use of Proceeds (UoP):** Ring-fenced exclusively for Capex in European plants. 
    *   **Disclosure:** Full compliance with CSRD/ESRS and EuGBS.
*   **Tranche B: The "MENA Transition & Sukuk" (Transition)**
    *   **Target Market:** Gulf sovereign wealth funds, regional banks, and Islamic investors.
    *   **Taxonomy Alignment:** Aligned with UAE/Saudi Sustainable Finance Frameworks and AAOIFI standards.
    *   **UoP:** Finances retrofits in NA and the Middle East. Explicitly utilizes "transition" categories for hard-to-abate sectors that do not yet pass EU DNSH tests but align with regional net-zero pathways.
*   **Tranche C: The "ASEAN Transition Bond" (Transition)**
    *   **Target Market:** Singaporean, Malaysian, and Indonesian investors.
    *   **Taxonomy Alignment:** Aligned with the ASEAN Taxonomy (utilizing the "Amber/Transition" category) and the Singapore-Asia Taxonomy.
    *   **UoP:** Finances SEA plant retrofits, acknowledging these assets are transitioning rather than fully "green."

**Harmonized Baseline:** To prevent reporting fatigue, the overarching program will adopt **IFRS S2 (ISSB)** as the global baseline for climate disclosures, applying EU-specific ESRS overlays *only* for Tranche A.

---

### **Pillar 2: Managing Internal Emissions Measurement-Quality Risk**

**The Challenge:** Inconsistent measurement quality across NA and SEA plants risks post-issuance target misses. Relying solely on Capex allocation without credible impact estimates will deter sophisticated investors, while introducing operational performance covenants into a Use-of-Proceeds (UoP) bond is structurally inappropriate and legally complex.

**The Strategy: "Ex-Ante Engineering Validation" and "Data-Infrastructure Capex"**  
We must bridge the gap between poor historical baseline data and investor demand for credible impact reporting, while maintaining clean legal documentation appropriate for a UoP bond.

1.  **Independent Technical Review (ITR) of Ex-Ante Models:**
    *   Rather than relying on flawed historical baselines, we will commission an independent technical consultant (e.g., DNV, TÜV, or WSP) to validate the **ex-ante engineering models** for each retrofit project. 
    *   Impact reporting will be based on these ITR-validated engineering estimates, shifting the burden of proof from historical operational data to forward-looking, audited technical design.
2.  **Financing the "Measurement Infrastructure" Itself:**
    *   We will explicitly include the deployment of Continuous Emissions Monitoring Systems (CEMS), IoT sensors, and automated data pipelines in NA and SEA as eligible UoP. 
    *   Under both the ASEAN and Gulf frameworks, digitalization and measurement infrastructure qualify as "enabling activities." This turns a core data vulnerability into a bond-eligible investment, directly resolving the measurement-quality gap over the bond's lifecycle without requiring complex operational covenants.

---

### **Pillar 3: Navigating Conflicting Guidance from Rating Agencies and Banks**

**The Challenge:** Global ESG rating agencies (e.g., S&P, MSCI) penalize transition assets if lumped into the overall corporate profile, while regional banks offer favorable pricing specifically for local transition alignment.

**The Strategy: Bifurcated Narrative and "Ring-Fenced" Reporting**  
We will separate our engagement with global credit/ESG raters from our engagement with regional bond investors, ensuring no single stakeholder's conflicting demands derail the issuance.

1.  **Protecting the Corporate ESG Credit Rating:**
    *   Global ESG raters assess the *corporate entity*. To prevent our regional transition assets from diluting our corporate ESG score, we will report our EU-aligned green Capex and performance separately in our S&P/MSCI submissions. 
    *   We will provide a rigorously managed "Shadow EU Taxonomy" alignment report at the corporate level. This demonstrates to global raters that the core of our business remains strictly aligned with the world's most rigorous standard, shielding our credit rating.
2.  **Dual Second-Party Opinion (SPO) Strategy for the Bonds:**
    *   Engage a top-tier global SPO provider (e.g., CICERO or Sustainalytics) *strictly* for Tranche A to satisfy EU investors.
    *   Engage regional specialists (e.g., an Islamic finance scholar for the Sukuk, or an ASEAN-focused advisor) for Tranches B and C to satisfy regional banks and local regulators without compromising the EU tranche's integrity.
3.  **Syndicate Structuring:**
    *   Appoint Global Systemically Important Banks (G-SIBs) as Joint Global Coordinators for Tranche A.
    *   Appoint dominant regional banks (e.g., First Abu Dhabi Bank, DBS) as Joint Lead Managers for Tranches B and C, leveraging their balance sheets and local regulatory capital relief incentives to secure favorable pricing.

---

### **Pillar 4: Protecting Credibility with Investors (Anti-Greenwashing)**

**The Challenge:** Issuing "transition" bonds under a "green" umbrella, or utilizing inconsistent data, invites severe scrutiny from NGOs, the financial press, and sophisticated investors.

**The Strategy: Radical Transparency, Accurate Labeling, and Robust Governance**  
Credibility in 2026 is not about claiming perfection; it is about demonstrating rigorous governance and transparency regarding imperfections.

1.  **Accurate and Defensible Labeling:**
    *   We will strictly avoid the term "Green" for Tranches B and C. Tranche A is a **Green Bond** (EuGBS aligned); Tranche B is a **Transition Sukuk**; Tranche C is a **Transition Bond**. Accurate labeling immediately neutralizes the primary vector for greenwashing accusations.
2.  **Tiered Independent Assurance:**
    *   For Tranche A, we will commission **ISAE 3000 reasonable assurance** on allocation and impact, strictly meeting EuGBS mandatory requirements.
    *   For Tranches B and C, we will commission **ISAE 3000 limited assurance**. This tiered approach optimizes audit costs while maintaining high trust across all tranches.
3.  **Robust Governance with External Advisory Oversight:**
    *   Establish an internal **Green Finance Allocation Committee** (comprising the CFO, CSO, and Regional Heads) with final decision-making authority.
    *   Augment this committee with an **Independent External ESG Advisory Council** (comprising two external experts). While the external members will not hold legal veto power (to avoid fiduciary complications), the committee’s charter will mandate a "comply or explain" mechanism: any decision overruling the Advisory Council’s recommendation must be publicly disclosed and justified in the annual allocation report. This ensures external accountability without creating legal gridlock.
4.  **Holistic, Localized Impact Reporting:**
    *   To satisfy regional investors, impact reporting for Tranches B and C will include localized co-benefits mapped to regional priorities (e.g., local job retention and community health in NA; water efficiency and grid resilience in SEA).

### **Conclusion and Next Steps**

By executing this modular strategy, the Group will successfully raise the required capital for decarbonization. We avoid a "lowest common denominator" approach that would alienate European investors, and a "highest common denominator" approach that would price us out of Gulf and Asian markets. Most importantly, by ring-fencing our taxonomies and upgrading our data infrastructure via the bond proceeds, we turn our current vulnerabilities into a demonstration of strategic maturity.

**Immediate Next Steps (Q4 2025):**
1.  **Governance:** Formally charter the Green Finance Allocation Committee and recruit the Independent External ESG Advisory Council.
2.  **Technical Validation:** Issue the RFP for the Independent Technical Consultant (ITR) to validate ex-ante engineering models for the retrofit projects.
3.  **Data Infrastructure:** Initiate the "Data Gap Analysis" across all NA and SEA plants to quantify the exact Capex required for CEMS and IoT infrastructure to be included in the UoP.
4.  **Market Sounding:** Begin preliminary, confidential soundings with the proposed Global and Regional Joint Lead Managers to test the modular framework structure and gauge investor appetite.
