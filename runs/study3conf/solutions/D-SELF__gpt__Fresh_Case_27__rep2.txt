# Korean-Content Localization and Marketing Strategy  
## 2026 Executive Decision Document

---

## 1. Executive summary

### Decision

Adopt a **subtitle-first, selective-premium-dubbing strategy** managed at the **title × language × territory** level.

This model is designed to maximize reach within a limited budget while protecting Korean cultural meaning and platform quality.

### Strategic principles

1. **Subtitles are the universal access layer.** Launch high-quality subtitles broadly and on time.
2. **Dubbing is a growth investment, not a default.** Fund it where it demonstrably improves starts, completion, family co-viewing, acquisition, retention, or franchise value.
3. **Prioritize titles, not language coverage alone.** Broad romance, family, thriller, survival, fantasy, comedy, and action titles generally offer more dubbing upside than niche or highly Korea-specific content.
4. **Preserve Korean meaning.** Honorifics, hierarchy, relationships, history, religion, identity, consent, and political context must not be flattened or silently altered.
5. **Use global campaigns with local execution.** Coordinate around a common proposition while adapting titles, creative, creators, release timing, and moderation by market.
6. **Hold back capital.** Maintain a protected emergency reserve and an unallocated growth reserve until launch evidence supports expansion.
7. **Measure incrementality.** Evaluate additional viewing and business value caused by localization—not merely total views or social engagement.

### Initial priority languages

- **Premium dubbing candidates:** Latin American Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, French, German, Arabic, Indonesian.
- **Selective dubbing candidates:** European Spanish, Italian, Thai, Vietnamese, Turkish, Polish, Malay, Filipino/Tagalog.
- **Subtitle- and metadata-first:** English-language markets, Nordics, Netherlands, smaller European and Southeast Asian markets, and additional Arabic dialects.

### Recommended first-year portfolio

- **3–5 global anchor titles:** full premium localization.
- **8–15 regional growth titles:** subtitles broadly; dubbing in 2–4 best-fit languages.
- Remaining slate: subtitle-led, with title-specific cultural review and catalog testing.
- Separate priority track for **family and children’s content**, where dubbing may have greater co-viewing and accessibility value.

# 2. Market segmentation

| Segment | Representative markets | Viewer characteristics | Recommended approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fandom-led, subtitle-tolerant | Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines, Malaysia, Singapore; selected Latin American and European audiences | High Korean-content familiarity, strong social sharing, greater tolerance for Korean audio and subtitles | High-quality subtitles, mobile optimization, fandom activation, selective dubbing |
| Mainstream, dubbing-growth | Brazil, Mexico, France, Germany, Spain, Italy, Poland | Dubbing can expand household reach, casual viewing, and completion | Premium dubbing for anchor and family/mainstream titles |
| Context-sensitive | Gulf markets, Egypt, North Africa, Turkey | Greater sensitivity to romance, sexuality, religion, gender, alcohol, politics, and history | Country-level review, carefully selected dubbing, alternate creative |
| Fragmented premium Europe | France, Germany, Spain, Italy, Poland, UK/Ireland, Nordics, Netherlands | Strong but varied language conventions and quality expectations | Concentrated dubbing in core markets; subtitle-led discovery elsewhere |

### Universal principles

Apply across all markets:

- Subtitles must be translated from Korean, not an English intermediary.
- Maintain consistent terminology, names, relationships, titles, and pronunciation.
- Separate translation from compliance editing.
- Document every material adaptation or territory edit.
- Review dialogue, signs, lyrics, captions, graphics, trailers, thumbnails, and creator scripts.
- Correct material errors quickly and transparently.
- Protect accessibility, child safety, and content warnings.

### Region-specific tactics

- **Southeast Asia:** prioritize mobile, social conversation, fan communities, and Indonesian dubbing.
- **Latin America:** prioritize Brazilian Portuguese and Latin American Spanish dubbing, actor-led campaigns, and country-cluster creative.
- **Europe:** prioritize premium dubbing in France, Germany, Spain, Italy, and Poland; use critics and festivals for prestige titles.
- **MENA:** prioritize Arabic subtitles and selected dubbing, with Gulf/Egypt/North Africa segmentation and country-level compliance.

# 3. Localization and dubbing policy

## 3.1 Subtitle policy

Subtitles are both the broad-access layer and the cultural-fidelity reference.

For Tier A and high-risk titles:

- translate directly from the Korean master;
- create a Korean terminology and pronunciation glossary;
- create a character relationship and register matrix;
- preserve meaningful honorific, age, rank, kinship, and status changes;
- localize important signs, text messages, lyrics, and on-screen information;
- apply defined reading-speed, line-break, and timing standards;
- test on mobile, connected TV, low-bandwidth devices, and accessibility interfaces.

## 3.2 Dubbing policy

Commission dubbing when there is a credible case for:

- incremental starts;
- higher completion;
- lower first-episode abandonment;
- family or household co-viewing;
- subscriber acquisition or retention;
- catalog or multi-season value;
- strategic learning with executive approval.

Dubbing should remain subtitle-led when:

- the title is niche or prestige-oriented;
- Korean performance and linguistic nuance are central;
- demand is uncertain;
- adaptation risks changing character relationships or historical meaning;
- expected incremental value does not cover full costs.

### Dubbing formats

Use the least expensive format that preserves the viewing experience:

- full character dubbing for narrative series;
- voice-over or narrator tracks for selected unscripted content;
- partial dubbing where appropriate;
- premium full dubbing for major family, comedy, romance, and mainstream titles.

Adult subtitle releases should not be delayed for an incomplete dub. Simultaneous dubbing may be required for major children’s and family launches.

## 3.3 Language-specific rules

### Spanish

- Use one Latin American Spanish dub where it is natural and economically efficient.
- Adapt titles, metadata, humor, thumbnails, and marketing by major country cluster.
- Consider separate dialogue versions for children’s content, comedy, or titles where testing demonstrates material regional preference.
- Report Spain separately within Europe.

### Arabic

- Use Modern Standard Arabic for broad metadata and subtitles where appropriate.
- Use conversational pan-Arabic for suitable mainstream drama.
- Test dialect variants for comedy, youth, and highly conversational titles only when expected lift exceeds incremental cost.
- Do not use dialect to introduce new class, ethnic, political, or moral meaning.

### English

English localization must have explicit ownership, whether funded through this program or the core platform budget. It supports global fandom, press, multilingual households, Singapore, the Philippines, the UK, and Ireland.

# 4. Cultural-risk principles

## 4.1 Adaptation framework

### Must preserve

- names and identity;
- honorifics, age, rank, and kinship;
- relationship progression;
- historical and political meaning;
- colonial and wartime context;
- religion and ritual;
- consent, coercion, and sexual meaning;
- plot-critical threats, slurs, and discrimination;
- culturally important practices and objects.

### May adapt for function

- puns and wordplay;
- comedic timing;
- workplace, school, military, or bureaucratic jargon;
- terms of endearment;
- idioms and poetic imagery;
- unfamiliar institutional references.

Adapt the dramatic function, social force, and emotional temperature—not merely the literal words.

### May edit only with authorization

- legally restricted sexual or violent content;
- prohibited political or religious material;
- required ratings or platform-policy edits;
- rights-restricted music, lyrics, or visuals.

Every material change must have a reason code: law, platform policy, rights, or documented market-access decision.

## 4.2 Honorifics and relationships

Each priority title requires a relationship matrix covering:

- speaker and addressee;
- age and rank;
- formal or informal speech;
- kinship terms;
- workplace, military, and institutional titles;
- first-name use;
- affection, sarcasm, contempt, or coercion;
- relationship changes across episodes.

Do not automatically translate *oppa*, *unnie*, *hyung*, or *noona* as romantic. Do not flatten all dialogue into first names or generic politeness.

## 4.3 Sensitive content

Specialist review is mandatory for titles involving:

- colonialism, war, sexual violence, or contested history;
- religious conflict or ritual;
- political claims or national identity;
- LGBTQ+ identity;
- race, ethnicity, migration, or diaspora;
- disability;
- self-harm, suicide, drugs, consent, or coercion;
- slurs or humor based on status, class, dialect, or identity.

Historical titles require a Korean historical-context memo distinguishing documented, fictionalized, contested, and anachronistic elements. Marketing must not present dramatized history as neutral fact or reduce trauma to spectacle.

## 4.4 Decision authority

- **Legal/policy:** lawful availability and mandatory edits.
- **Korean editorial:** source meaning and historical intent.
- **Regional cultural review:** target-market interpretation and sensitivity.
- **Localization leadership:** production consistency and vendor decisions.
- **Executive content governance:** exceptional release, delay, restriction, or strategic override.

Disagreements must document the evidence, competing interpretations, final decision, rationale, and residual risk.

# 5. Title and language prioritization

## Title tiers

| Tier | Annual target | Profile | Localization |
|---|---:|---|---|
| A: Global anchors | 3–5 | Major cast, clear premise, high fandom and acquisition potential | Subtitles broadly; premium dubbing in approximately 5–7 languages; full marketing and QA |
| B: Regional growth | 8–15 | Strong genre or regional appeal, moderate risk | Subtitles broadly; dubbing in 2–4 languages; targeted campaigns |
| C: Fandom, prestige, catalog | Remainder | Niche, auteur, idol-centered, experimental, or catalog content | Subtitle-led; community and editorial marketing; selective catalog dubbing |
| D: High-risk/Korea-specific | Title-dependent | Dense comedy, contested history, political or identity-sensitive content | Subtitle-first pilot, specialist review, country-level release decision |

## Priority genres

### Highest initial dubbing potential

- family and children’s content;
- broad romance and melodrama;
- youth titles;
- mainstream thrillers and crime;
- survival and action;
- accessible fantasy;
- comedy, only with specialist dialogue adaptation.

### Subtitle-led until validated

- auteur and prestige dramas;
- linguistically dense comedy;
- highly Korea-specific institutional stories;
- historically contested titles;
- titles with substantial regulatory or cultural risk.

## Scoring model

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Addressable platform audience | 15% |
| Existing Korean-content demand | 15% |
| Expected incremental localization lift | 20% |
| Genre and title portability | 10% |
| Acquisition and retention value | 15% |
| Fandom and organic amplification | 8% |
| Localization unit cost | 7% |
| Rights and distribution feasibility | 5% |
| Cultural and regulatory risk | 5% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

# 7. Marketing and fandom plan

## Global campaign architecture

Use a common global proposition—romance, mystery, revenge, survival, fantasy, comedy, or family conflict—while adapting:

- title and logline;
- thumbnail and key art;
- trailer;
- captions and voiceover;
- creator selection;
- release timing;
- cultural context;
- moderation language.

Do not market fictionalized history, stereotypes, contested political claims, or unavailable edited content as fact.

## Regional tactics

### Southeast Asia

- mobile-first vertical clips;
- real-time episode discussions;
- watch parties;
- fan edits and reaction formats;
- actor greetings;
- K-content specialists and local genre creators.

### Latin America

- actor- and romance-led campaigns;
- couple and character discussion;
- soundtrack, beauty, and fashion partnerships;
- Mexican and country-cluster copy;
- independent Brazilian Portuguese campaigns.

### Europe

- critics, festivals, genre creators, and premium interviews;
- country-specific press kits and ratings;
- historical explainers;
- subtitle-led discovery in the UK, Nordics, Netherlands, and similar markets.

### MENA

- Arabic creators;
- country-level creative review;
- family and emotional hooks where appropriate;
- separate Gulf, Egypt, and North Africa planning;
- careful treatment of romance, sexuality, alcohol, religion, gender, and politics.

## Community operations

Each priority market must define:

- monitored platforms;
- supported languages;
- staffing and operating hours;
- moderation service-level agreement (SLA), meaning the response-time commitment;
- spoiler controls;
- harassment, brigading, and doxxing procedures;
- escalation owner;
- crisis budget;
- creator-selection criteria.

### Fandom trust policy

Provide official reporting channels for:

- mistranslations;
- inconsistent names;
- honorific errors;
- missing signs or lyrics;
- offensive dubbing;
- historical or religious inaccuracies.

Classify reports as factual error, reasonable translation choice, adaptation choice, territory edit, or policy issue. Verified material errors should be corrected promptly and acknowledged when audience impact is substantial.

Fan communities provide feedback but do not replace professional translation, cultural review, or editorial authority.

# 8. Implementation timeline

## Phase 0: Preparation

| Package | Minimum lead time |
|---|---:|
| Standard subtitle package | 6–8 weeks |
| Standard single-language dub | 8–12 weeks |
| Tier A multi-language dub | 14–20 weeks |
| Comedy or linguistically dense title | 16–22 weeks |
| Historical or political title | 16–24 weeks |
| Children’s/family package | 14–20 weeks |
| Territory-specific alternate versions | Add 3–6 weeks |

The timeline begins when the final Korean master, script, music materials, and on-screen text inventory are available.

Preparation includes rights confirmation, title scoring, cultural-risk review, glossary creation, campaign testing, vendor booking, creator recruitment, and crisis planning.

## Phase 1: Anchor launch

- Release subtitles simultaneously in approved priority markets.
- Release ready, fully QA’d dubs simultaneously.
- Do not delay adult titles for incomplete dubbing.
- Launch localized landing pages, trailers, recaps, recommendation rows, and social assets.
- Coordinate global cast material with local versions.

## Phase 2: Evidence-based expansion — weeks 2–6

Add languages, dubbing, marketing, or territory coverage where:

- starts exceed forecast;
- subtitle viewers show weak completion;
- audio-language demand is visible;
- creator activity generates incremental viewing;
- quality and compliance remain acceptable.

## Phase 3: Catalog and franchise — weeks 6–16

- connect new titles to earlier seasons and related content;
- add catalog dubs where franchise value supports them;
- create actor, genre, soundtrack, and webtoon collections;
- use explainers, behind-the-scenes content, watch parties, and fan assets;
- release growth-reserve funds only after review.

# 9. KPIs and experimentation

## Business KPIs

- incremental Korean-content starts;
- completed hours;
- completion rate;
- first-episode abandonment;
- new Korean-content viewers;
- subscriber acquisition and retention;
- cost per incremental completed hour;
- cost per attributable subscriber;
- catalog and franchise lift.

## Localization KPIs

- subtitle versus dub starts;
- audio-language selection and switching;
- completion by audio language;
- voice naturalness and satisfaction;
- critical defects per 10,000 viewing hours;
- correction and redubbing rate;
- time from master delivery to localized availability;
- device and accessibility failure rate.

## Initial directional targets

Reset after baseline analysis:

- 15–25% increase in Korean-content starts in priority markets;
- 8–12% completion improvement for anchor titles;
- 10% reduction in first-episode abandonment where premium dubbing is added;
- 20% reduction in critical localization defects;
- at least three titles meeting a predefined breakout threshold;
- positive incremental return on tested creator campaigns.

A **breakout** must be defined before launch as a specified completed-hours, local ranking, or subscriber-impact threshold achieved across at least three regions.

## Experiment standards

Every pilot must pre-register:

- hypothesis;
- treatment and control;
- target audience;
- primary metric;
- minimum detectable effect;
- sample size and statistical power;
- measurement window;
- attribution method;
- confidence threshold;
- decision owner;
- stop-loss rule;
- expansion or cancellation rule.

### Priority tests

1. Subtitle-only versus subtitle-plus-dub.
2. Existing fans versus casual viewers.
3. Family households versus individual viewers.
4. Creator-led versus paid-media acquisition.
5. Pan-Arabic versus market-specific creative.
6. Shared Spanish dub versus country-specific campaign adaptation.
7. New-release versus catalog dubbing.
8. Standard versus contextualized historical marketing.

Use user-level randomized exposure where feasible. Otherwise use matched markets or titles while controlling for placement, pricing, title strength, device, audience affinity, marketing, release timing, and rights.

# 10. Quality, vendor, and accessibility standards

## Vendor model

Use a primary and qualified backup vendor for every major language where feasible.

Assess vendors on:

- Korean-source competence;
- dialogue adaptation;
- cultural and historical review;
- casting and direction;
- lip synchronization;
- pronunciation;
- continuity across seasons;
- accessibility;
- security;
- delivery reliability;
- correction turnaround;
- cost per finished minute;
- labor and union compliance.

## Quality gates

Tier A and high-risk titles require:

1. Korean editorial briefing.
2. Independent translation.
3. Cultural and historical review.
4. Separate subtitle and dubbing adaptation.
5. Table read or sample recording.
6. Full production.
7. In-language linguistic QA.
8. Technical mix and lip-sync QA.
9. Marketing-asset review.
10. Device and accessibility check.

### Launch standards

- zero unresolved critical defects;
- no plot, identity, consent, relationship, or historical-meaning errors;
- named-entity and pronunciation accuracy above 98%;
- major defects below a pre-agreed per-episode limit;
- minimum voice-naturalness score;
- accessibility and device pass before release.

### Correction SLA

- Critical issue acknowledged within 24 hours.
- Severity assessed within 48 hours.
- Correction plan issued within 72 hours.
- Deployment scheduled according to severity and production complexity.
- Immediate suspension for legal, safety, or materially misleading content.

Mandatory redubbing applies when an error changes plot, identity, consent, relationship, or historical meaning.

Accessibility standards should include SDH for priority titles and audio description where feasible, with separate QA rather than assuming ordinary subtitles meet accessibility needs. Children’s titles require age-appropriate language, warnings, parental controls, and specialist review.

# 11. Risk mitigation and contingency actions

| Risk | Contingency action |
|---|---|
| Dubbing quality fails | Pause campaign assets, deploy corrected track, activate backup vendor, use protected reserve |
| Repeated subtitle errors | Audit the vendor, freeze related deliveries, update glossary and relationship matrix |
| Historical or religious controversy | Convene Korean editorial, regional cultural, legal, policy, and communications teams; review before removal |
| Regulatory change | Use alternate territory version, revised ratings, alternate creative, or temporary restriction |
| Vendor capacity failure | Shift work to backup vendor; prioritize anchor episodes and subtitle launch |
| Poor dubbing lift | Stop expansion; redirect funds to subtitles, discovery, QA, or fandom |
| Strong unexpected demand | Release growth reserve after financial and quality review; prioritize catalog and franchise dubs |
| Creator misconduct | Suspend creator, remove or replace assets, activate brand-safety and communications protocol |
| Fan backlash over translation | Verify issue, distinguish factual error from adaptation choice, correct material errors, communicate clearly |
| Spoilers or coordinated harassment | Enforce embargoes, spoiler controls, moderation escalation, and community safety procedures |
| Technical playback failure | Disable faulty track or asset, deploy corrected version, test across affected devices |
| Child-safety or accessibility failure | Suspend affected asset if necessary, correct warnings or access features, conduct compliance review |

- launch approved anchor titles;

## Final recommendation

Proceed with a **subtitle-first, selective-premium-dubbing portfolio**.

The first investment wave should concentrate on:

1. **Latin American Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese** for scalable dubbing.
2. **French, German, European Spanish, and Italian** for selected European mainstream titles.
3. **Indonesian**, with selective Thai and Vietnamese dubbing.
4. **Arabic subtitles and conditional dubbing** supported by country-level review.
5. **Subtitle-led treatment** in highly tolerant markets.

The plan is designed to make Korean content more accessible without removing the information that makes it distinctive. Success means viewers understand not only **what characters say**, but **who they are to one another, what status and history they carry, and what emotional, cultural, moral, or political meaning their words convey**.
