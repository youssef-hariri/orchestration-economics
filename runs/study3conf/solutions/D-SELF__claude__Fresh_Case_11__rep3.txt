# FINAL DELIVERABLE: FINANCIAL-OPERATIONS STRATEGY FOR CROSS-BORDER REMITTANCE CORRIDORS
## Executive Framework for Board & Stakeholder Presentation

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (1 Page)

### STRATEGIC INTENT

A mid-size remittance payments firm manages cross-border corridors across 4 regional blocs (EU, GCC, Southeast Asia, Latin America) under fundamentally divergent licensing, KYC/AML/CFT, and settlement regimes. This strategy sustains profitable operations and selective expansion while maintaining **zero-tolerance regulatory standing** (primary objective) and defending 50–80 basis-point margins against fintech competition and capital-control tightening.

**Core Strategic Pillars:**
1. **Regional Hub Model** – Single primary license per bloc (EU PESA, GCC DFSA, Philippines BSP RPS, Mexico CNBV STP) + agent licensing for secondary countries (cost efficiency)
2. **Risk-Tiered KYC/Compliance** – Centralized decisioning with Tier-1 auto-approval (<€5K, low-risk = <1 hr turnaround) reducing compliance cost from €1.50 to €0.50 per transaction
3. **Segregated Settlement Architecture** – Dedicated nostro pools with tiered backup clearing + FX options overlay (protects against 3–5% currency moves; cost: €400–600K/year)
4. **Partner Capability Realism** – Accept Indonesia/Vietnam technical limitations; scale selectively (phase volumes up only if auto-approval rates >70%); exit Argentina (margin -62 bps) unless regulatory exemption granted
5. **Margin Defense via Non-Price Value** – Invest €2–3M in settlement speed/reliability (Pix real-time in Brazil = 168 bps margin vs. 60 bps elsewhere); prioritize highest-ROE corridors first

- **PSD2 Open Banking Deepening (2025):** XS2A (Access to Account) data-sharing expands; opportunity for premium KYC via bank-provided verification. Benefit: Reduce KYC cost -0.10 EUR/txn. Action: Negotiate with German banks for API access.
