## 2.1 MEXICO: RELATIONSHIP-CENTRIC PLAYBOOK

### Cultural Foundation

**Business Custom Profile**:
- **Decision-Making**: Hierarchical; final authority concentrates at owner/CEO; consensus occurs within hierarchy, not across peers
- **Relationship Primacy**: Personal trust precedes transactions; directness signals disrespect if relationship not yet established
- **Time Orientation**: Flexible; punctuality less critical than relationship maintenance
- **Communication Style**: High-context; verbal commitment carries moral weight exceeding written contract
- **Authority Source**: Owner's personal reputation and relationship; institutional processes secondary

### 2.1.2 Dual-Track Communication Protocol

**Principle**: Mexico culture values both formal documentation AND personal relationship. Strategy must address both channels.

#### Formal Channel (Documented, Auditable)

**Monthly Quality & Delivery Report**
- **Format**: 1-page written report (Spanish), submitted by supplier to you
- **Due Date**: 5th of following month
- **Contents**:
  - Defect rate (ppm) for month
  - On-time delivery % for month
  - Quality issues raised (if any) and resolution status
  - Production forecast (next 2 months)
  - Any concerns/risks supplier wants to flag
- **Review**: Your QA Manager reviews; filed in shared system
- **Feedback**: Brief acknowledgment from you (email or brief call); confirms receipt

**Quarterly Business Reviews (QBRs)**
- **Frequency**: Every 3 months (4/year)
- **Duration**: 90 minutes
- **Location**: Rotating (supplier facility, your facility, neutral location) to show mutual respect
- **Participants**:
  - Your VP Supply Chain + QA Manager + Account Executive (PAE)
  - Supplier Owner + Operations Director + Quality Manager
  - (Lunch component optional but recommended for relationship deepening)
- **Agenda**:
  - Quarterly scorecard review (defect rate, OTD, quality compliance)
  - Strategic discussion (upcoming production, challenges, growth)
  - Relationship health check (any concerns from either side)
  - Celebration of wins (quality improvements, delivery successes)
  - Action items (items to address before next QBR)
- **Documentation**: Formal minutes taken; action items tracked; signed by both parties
- **Tone**: Professional but warm; acknowledge supplier's partnership contribution

**Annual Contract Review**
- **Timeline**: Month 12 (or contract anniversary)
- **Format**: Legal + business review; potential contract amendments
- **Participants**: Your VP Supply Chain + Legal; Supplier Owner + Finance
- **Deliverable**: Updated contract (if needed); renewal signed for Year 2

#### Informal Channel (Relationship Maintenance)

**Bi-Weekly Phone Calls** (PAE + Supplier Owner)
- **Frequency**: Every 2 weeks (26 calls/year)
- **Duration**: 15–20 minutes
- **Format**: Conversational; no formal agenda
- **Topics**:
  - "How's business? How's family? How's the industry treating you?"
  - Quick production update: "Any issues we should know about? Anything we can help with?"
- **Tone**: Personal, interested, warm
- **Objective**: Maintain relationship visibility; catch issues early in informal setting

**Monthly Email Updates** (PAE to Supplier Owner)
- **Frequency**: Once per month
- **Format**: Brief (2–3 sentences); non-transactional
- **Examples**:
  - "Saw your company mentioned in [industry publication]—congratulations on the award!"
  - "Our customer just gave us positive feedback on your quality last week."
  - "Happy [Mexican holiday]—hope you're celebrating with family."
  - "Our new product launch is progressing; your contribution is critical to the timeline."
- **Objective**: Maintain personal attention; show you value the relationship beyond transactions

**Quarterly Informal Gatherings** (If Feasible)
- **Frequency**: 2–3 times/year
- **Format**: Lunch, industry event attendance, facility tour
- **Objective**: Relationship deepening outside formal settings
- **Note**: Frequency/format flexible; adapt to supplier owner's preferences and geography

#### Problem-Escalation Communication

**Minor Quality Issue** (Example: One shipment has 250 ppm defect rate vs. target 150 ppm)
1. **PAE's First Call** (same day issue discovered; keep personal, collaborative):
   - "Hey [Owner name], we have a situation that came up, and I wanted to discuss it with you personally rather than through formal channels."
   - Frame as partnership challenge: "Our customer is flagging quality on the last shipment. Can we work together on this?"
   - Listen to supplier's perspective; don't immediately assign blame
2. **Follow-Up Action**:
   - Written description sent to owner (documents for record)
   - CC to operations director (keeps hierarchy informed)
   - Schedule quick call with quality teams to discuss root cause
3. **Resolution Framing**:
   - "Let's solve this together. What do you think caused it? How can we prevent it?"
   - Position company as supportive: "We want you to succeed."

**Major Quality Issue** (Multiple shipments out of spec; customer return risk)
1. **Escalation to Owner** (PAE and VP Supply Chain call)
   - Frame professionally but urgently: "This is significant; we need your personal attention."
   - Owner-to-owner discussion (signals seriousness; respects hierarchy)
2. **Off-Site Problem-Solving Meeting**
   - Location: Neutral venue (not supplier facility; supplier doesn't feel trapped)
   - Participants: Your QA Manager + third-party QA consultant; Supplier Owner + Operations Director + Quality Manager
   - Third-party consultant present (mediator position; company is collaborative, not prosecutorial)
   - Focus: Process improvement, not blame
   - Outcome: Joint corrective action plan (supplier leads execution; you provide support)
3. **Public Recognition**
   - When issue resolved, acknowledge supplier's improvement efforts
   - "The quality improvements you made show your commitment to excellence."

#### Language & Tone Guidance for Account Executives

| **Situation** | **Tone** | **Language Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **Delivery Request** | Respectful, appreciative | "We're ramping up production next quarter and would appreciate if you could help us scale. Based on our growth, we're thinking 12,000 units/month. Is that workable with your capacity?" |
| **Spec Clarification** | Consultative, not prescriptive | "We've run into some challenges with surface finish tolerances on our customer side. How do you currently approach measuring this? We'd like to align our standards." |
| **Quality Issue** | Collaborative problem-solving | "We found some cosmetic scratches in the last shipment. This isn't a functionality issue, but our customer is sensitive to appearance. How do you think we can prevent this together?" |
| **Price Negotiation** | Acknowledge value, propose mutual benefit | "As our volume grows, we're hoping to discuss pricing that benefits both of us. Can we model improved economics together?" |
| **Problem Escalation** | Urgent but respectful | "I need your personal attention on something. Quality results came back higher than expected, and our customer is concerned. Can we problem-solve this together?" |

### 2.1.4 Specification & Delivery Commitment Protocol (Mexico-Specific)

#### Specification Clarity Framework

**Mechanism**: Detailed specifications with video demonstrations + joint interpretation walkthrough + operator certification

**For each critical specification** (top 15–20 by quality impact):

**Document Structure**:
```
SPECIFICATION: Surface Finish Ra ≤ 1.6 µm

1. WHAT IT IS
   ├─ Definition: Surface finish measured as roughness average (Ra)
   ├─ Units: Micrometers (µm)
   ├─ Requirement: Must be ≤ 1.6 µm (smoother surfaces acceptable)
   └─ Visual Reference: [Photos of acceptable vs. unacceptable samples]

2. WHY IT MATTERS
   ├─ Customer Impact: Consumer feels product surface; must be smooth for comfortable use
   ├─ Performance Impact: Rough surfaces can degrade optical clarity; affect tactile experience
   ├─ Quality Standard: Aligns with consumer electronics industry norms (ISO 4287)
   └─ Consequence of Non-Compliance: Customer complaints; product returns; relationship damage

3. HOW TO MEASURE
   ├─ Instrument: Profilometer (e.g., Mitutoyo SurfTest SJ-210)
   ├─ Calibration: Monthly calibration required; certificate maintained
   ├─ Measurement Points: Minimum 4 points across part surface (not just one spot)
   ├─ Measurement Process: [Step-by-step procedure with photos]
   └─ Operator Certification: All QA staff must pass training + practical test before measurement

4. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
   ├─ Pass Condition: All 4 measurement points ≤ 1.6 µm
   ├─ Fail Condition: Any point > 1.6 µm = part rejected
   ├─ No Averaging: Individual points must meet spec (not average of points)
   └─ Sample Size: 100% inspection of first 50 parts; then AQL 1.5% (ANSI Z1.4) for remaining batch

5. DOCUMENTATION REQUIRED
   ├─ Calibration certificates: Monthly; filed in quality records
   ├─ Test results: Raw measurement data (not summary); linked to part serial numbers
   ├─ Operator certification: Copy of certification for each QA staff member
   └─ Traceability: Part serial # → Batch # → Measurement results → Acceptance/Rejection
```

**Specification Interpretation Validation** (Pre-Production, Week 2 of onboarding):

**Meeting**: 2-hour video call or in-person
- **Participants**: Your QA Engineer + Supplier QA Manager + Supplier Production Supervisor + Supplier Operator (who'll perform measurement)
- **Agenda**:
  1. Walk through each critical spec point-by-point
  2. Show video demonstration of correct measurement technique
  3. Show real parts (acceptable and unacceptable examples) side-by-side
  4. Supplier demonstrates their current measurement method on sample parts
  5. Compare supplier's results to your reference measurements
  6. **If divergence >5%**: STOP; resolve methodology before production starts
  7. Document agreed methodology in meeting minutes; both parties sign

**Dual-Measurement Validation** (Post-meeting, within 1 week):
- Supplier measures 10 sample parts using their equipment/procedure
- You measure same 10 parts using your equipment/procedure
- Compare results: divergence should be <5%
- **If alignment achieved**: Proceed to operator certification
- **If divergence >5%**: Investigate discrepancy; retrain on methodology; re-test

**Operator Certification** (Before first production run):
- Supplier's operator conducts measurement on 20 test parts under your supervision
- Operator must achieve 100% agreement with your reference measurements (or 95% with re-test)
- Certification valid 6 months; recertification quarterly
- Certificate displayed at measurement station (operator reminder)
