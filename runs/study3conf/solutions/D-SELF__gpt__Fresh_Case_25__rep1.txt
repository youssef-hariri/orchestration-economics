# 2026 Latin America D2C Launch Strategy  
## Mexico, Colombia, and Brazil

## Executive summary

Launch with **one regional brand platform and three country-specific commercial playbooks**. Do not treat Latin America as one market, and do not launch Brazil as a translated extension of Mexico or Colombia.

### Recommended sequence

1. **Mexico first:** marketplace-led scale and Spanish-language learning.
2. **Colombia shortly afterward:** community, referral, and assisted-commerce pilot.
3. **Brazil after formal readiness approval:** Portuguese operations, local compliance, domestic fulfillment, payment localization, and credible product and shade validation.

### Regional proposition

> **Asian formulation expertise, locally proven for local skin tones, climates, routines, and buying habits.**

Asian origin should signal formulation expertise, sensory innovation, and discovery. It should not imply cultural superiority or replace local evidence of performance.

### Strategic priorities

- Launch a focused assortment of approximately **5–8 product families**, with country-specific shade depth.
- Treat complexion credibility as a brand-level trust issue.
- Use marketplaces for first-purchase trust, reviews, and fulfillment; use owned channels for education, CRM, loyalty, and repeat purchase.
- Build human guidance through creators, makeup artists, advisors, and WhatsApp—without recruitment commissions, inventory loading, or other MLM mechanics.
- Scale paid media and inventory only after each market passes product, operational, compliance, and contribution-margin gates.

### Country roles

| Country | Strategic role | Initial model | Positioning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mexico | Spanish-language scale and marketplace learning | Mercado Libre + creators + owned site | Innovative beauty that performs in real life |
| Colombia | Community-commerce experimentation | Creators + referrals + WhatsApp + marketplaces | Fresh, effective beauty you can discover and share |
| Brazil | Long-term growth and local credibility | Brazilian owned site + one primary marketplace + local creators | Advanced Asian formulation made credible for Brazilian skin and climate |

# 1. Regional principles

## 1.1 One operating backbone, not one campaign

### Centralize

- Brand platform and visual identity
- Product information and claims governance
- Commerce, analytics, and CRM infrastructure
- Creator contracting templates
- Affiliate attribution
- Product-quality and adverse-event procedures
- Financial reporting
- Customer-feedback taxonomy
- Core training and compliance materials

### Localize

- Language and copywriting
- Product families and shade counts
- Pricing and promotions
- Creator selection
- Marketplace mix
- Customer service
- Regulatory execution
- Fulfillment and returns
- Community and professional partnerships

## 1.2 Define D2C by customer ownership

D2C refers to a brand-controlled go-to-market model, not exclusively an owned website.

| Channel | Transaction owner | Customer-data access | Primary role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owned site | Brand | Brand-controlled, subject to consent | Education, conversion, CRM, loyalty |
| Marketplace | Platform or approved seller | Limited and platform-controlled | Trust, search, reviews, fulfillment |
| Creator/affiliate link | Brand or marketplace | Depends on destination and consent | Recommendation and acquisition |
| WhatsApp-assisted commerce | Brand or approved partner | Consent-based | Service and assisted conversion |
| Retail partner | Retailer | Usually limited | Trust, sampling, and reach |

The brand must not scrape marketplace data, buy customer lists, or send unsolicited off-platform communications.

## 1.3 Product credibility before media scale

Do not scale national demand generation until each market has:

- A legally marketable assortment
- Approved labels and claims
- Local payment methods
- Predictable delivery
- Functional returns and exchanges
- Native-language service
- Local product and shade validation
- Adequate hero-SKU and shade inventory

## 1.4 Compete with relationship-selling benefits, not MLM mechanics

Legacy direct-selling firms may retain trust through personal recommendations, demonstrations, sampling, payment relationships, and local availability.

The brand should answer those benefits through:

- Creators and makeup artists
- Beauty advisors
- WhatsApp consultations
- Discovery kits
- Referrals
- Transparent affiliates
- Local reviews
- Reliable local service

Prohibit:

- Recruitment-based compensation
- Downstream commissions
- Mandatory purchases
- Inventory loading
- Unsupported earnings claims
- Pressure selling
- Unauthorized price changes
- Off-platform customer-data retention

# 2. Positioning and messaging

## 2.1 Regional message architecture

### Core promise

**Effective, sensorial Asian formulas adapted to local skin, climate, and routines.**

### Proof pillars

1. **Performance:** finish, wear, layering, and ease of application
2. **Shade confidence:** local swatches and undertone assistance
3. **Simplicity:** short routines and multi-use products
4. **Transparency:** ingredients, instructions, limitations, and substantiated claims
5. **Local relevance:** local creators, language, payment, service, and fulfillment

### Avoid unsupported claims

Do not use claims such as:

- “For every skin tone”
- “Safe for all skin”
- “Works for everyone”
- “Dermatologically tested” without evidence
- “Non-toxic” or “clean” without a defined standard
- Guaranteed whitening, treatment, medical, or corrective outcomes

## 2.2 Content system

### Demonstration

- Natural-light shade try-ons
- Full-day wear tests
- Application over sunscreen
- Coverage and finish comparisons
- Multiple complexion depths
- “What it does and does not do”
- Climate or oil-control testing only where substantiated

### Education

- Undertone selection in plain language
- Coverage and finish guidance
- Routine sequencing
- Ingredient benefits without medical overstatement
- Climate-specific application tips
- Practical translation of Asian formulation concepts

### Social proof

- Verified customer reviews
- Creator first impressions
- Makeup-artist demonstrations
- Consistently lit before-and-after content
- Customer routines
- Troubleshooting content
- Honest mixed feedback

## 2.3 Language and cultural standards

- Mexico and Colombia require separate Spanish copywriting and creator scripts.
- Brazil requires native Brazilian Portuguese copy, service, product education, and transactional messaging.
- Review brand and shade names for pronunciation, unintended meanings, trademark availability, and cultural sensitivity.
- Avoid stereotypes about national beauty, ethnicity, or skin color.
- Do not suggest local beauty practices require correction.
- Do not use machine translation for claims, product education, or service.

# 3. Product and shade strategy

## 3.1 Assortment

Launch approximately **5–8 product families**, with country-specific shade counts.

Potential families:

1. Complexion or complexion-adjacent hero
2. Prep, hydration, or barrier product
3. Long-wear lip product
4. Blush or multi-use color product
5. Setting, oil-control, or wear product
6. Discovery set or mini routine

A complexion product may be one product family but dozens of sellable shade SKUs. Forecasting, compliance, replenishment, and working capital must be managed at the shade-SKU level.

## 3.2 Shade principles

A smaller, balanced range is preferable to a larger range concentrated in light shades.

Test and publish:

- Light through deep shades
- Neutral, warm, golden, olive, and rosy undertones
- Natural- and indoor-light swatches
- Oxidation and wear results
- Sunscreen compatibility
- Mobile-video appearance
- Two or three likely shade recommendations, without claiming perfect matching

Analyze returns and exchanges by:

- Product
- Shade
- Depth
- Undertone
- City
- Creator
- Channel
- Swatch or content exposure

## 3.3 Brazil product decision

Select one path before packaging, regulatory submissions, inventory, creator contracts, or media approval.

### Path A: Inclusive complexion launch

Requires:

- Locally tested depth and undertone range
- Brazilian creator and consumer validation
- Natural-light swatches
- Domestic inventory across priority shades
- Shade-exchange process
- Return monitoring by shade, depth, undertone, product, city, and channel

### Path B: Non-complexion pilot

Launch validated lip, cheek, skincare, prep, or other non-complexion heroes. Build reviews and trust while developing complexion locally.

Do not launch an incomplete complexion range to meet a calendar date.

# 5. Mexico playbook

## Role

Mexico is the first learning and scale market for:

- Spanish-language creative
- Marketplace operations
- Creator commerce
- Shade education
- Owned-channel retention

## Positioning

> **Innovative beauty that performs in real life.**

Test three benefit territories rather than assuming one finish preference:

- Radiant but controlled
- Natural skin-like
- Soft-matte and long-wearing

## Product priorities

- Skin tint, foundation, concealer, or cushion
- Long-wear lip
- Blush and lip-cheek products
- Setting or oil-control product
- Lightweight skincare preparation
- Routine and travel bundles

## Geographic approach

Begin in selected urban areas where:

- Marketplace and 3PL coverage is reliable
- Creator supply is strong
- Payment data can be monitored
- Customer service can meet response targets

Marketplace availability may be broader than paid-media targeting. Expand media and inventory only after geographic service data is stable.

## Channel plan

1. Mercado Libre
2. TikTok and Instagram
3. Owned site
4. Amazon and selected marketplaces
5. WhatsApp for shade assistance and service

Mercado Libre should support trust, reviews, and first purchase. The owned site should provide education, bundles, loyalty, and consent-based CRM.

## Payments and fulfillment

Evaluate:

- Cards and debit
- Mercado Pago
- SPEI
- OXXO or other cash-voucher methods where justified
- Installments or “meses sin intereses” where margin permits

For every payment method, document fees, authorization, settlement timing, fraud exposure, failed-payment recovery, and reconciliation ownership.

Use local or reliable regional fulfillment with:

- Tracking
- Transparent taxes and delivery costs
- Local returns
- Batch and expiry control
- Shade-level safety stock

## Creator plan

Use:

- A limited number of reach creators
- Mid-tier beauty educators
- Micro-creators across complexion depths and regions
- Makeup artists
- Performance affiliates with marketplace or owned-site links

Prioritize shade try-ons, wear tests, routines, and problem/solution content over reach-only imagery.

## Mexico pilot gates

Thresholds must be finalized through category benchmarks and the country P&L. Each gate requires a minimum sample, observation period, owner, and corrective action.

| Area | Gate |
|---|---|
| Product | Local shade and wear validation completed |
| Compliance | Product, label, claims, and importer approval completed |
| Commerce | Payment, checkout, marketplace, returns, and service tested |
| Fulfillment | Promised delivery and failed-delivery thresholds approved by operations |
| Economics | Hero-SKU variable contribution is positive |
| Demand | Creator and marketplace conversion meets approved test forecast |
| Retention | Early repeat and CRM opt-in data is sufficient for scale decision |

# 6. Colombia playbook

## Role

Colombia is the primary test market for:

- Community commerce
- Referrals
- WhatsApp-assisted selling
- Regional creator coverage
- Ethical affiliate and advisor economics

## Positioning

> **Fresh, effective beauty you can discover and share.**

Fresh, natural-to-polished, versatile, and easy-to-use beauty are hypotheses to validate by city and segment, not descriptions of every Colombian consumer.

## Product priorities

- Multi-use cheek and lip
- Lightweight, buildable base
- Setting and blurring products
- Brow and eye products
- Hydration and barrier products
- Fresh daily, long-day, and event-ready bundles

## Geographic testing

Test at least:

- Bogotá or another major urban center
- One warmer coastal or lower-altitude market

Compare product preferences, shade demand, delivery, payment, service, and repeat behavior.

## Channel plan

1. Creator affiliates and community commerce
2. WhatsApp-assisted selling
3. Mercado Libre and major marketplaces
4. Owned site
5. Live demonstrations where platform functionality is confirmed

Marketplaces provide trust and fulfillment support but should not be the sole commercial engine.

## Payments and fulfillment

Evaluate:

- Cards
- PSE
- Nequi
- Daviplata
- Cash/payment-network methods where justified
- Fraud and address validation

Provide regional delivery estimates, failed-delivery recovery, and local returns.

## Assisted-selling experiment

Compare:

1. Self-serve owned-site orders
2. WhatsApp-assisted orders
3. Creator affiliate orders
4. Marketplace orders

Measure:

- Conversion
- Labor minutes per order
- AOV
- Payment failure
- Cancellation
- Returns
- Contribution margin
- Customer satisfaction
- 90-day repeat

WhatsApp should scale only if higher conversion offsets advisor labor, commissions, cancellations, and returns. It may ultimately be a sales channel, premium shade service, or customer-service function.

## Affiliate and advisor controls

Prohibit:

- Required inventory purchases
- Recruitment bonuses
- Downstream commissions
- Earnings guarantees
- Unauthorized price changes
- Off-platform customer databases
- Unapproved claims
- Uncontrolled cash collection

Require:

- Written agreements
- Commercial disclosure
- Approved product education
- Transparent payouts
- Refund clawbacks
- Tax documentation
- Customer-consent rules
- Audit and termination rights

## Colombia pilot gates

| Area | Gate |
|---|---|
| Product | Local shade, climate, and product validation completed |
| Assisted commerce | Positive contribution after labor and commission |
| Payment | PSE, Nequi, Daviplata, cards, and relevant methods tested |
| Service | Response and escalation capacity operational |
| Fulfillment | Regional delivery and failed-delivery performance acceptable |
| Demand | Referral and creator cohorts show incremental demand |
| Retention | 90-day repeat meets the approved base-case forecast |

# 7. Brazil playbook

## Role

Brazil is the largest long-term opportunity but requires the greatest investment in:

- Product classification and regulatory compliance
- Brazilian Portuguese
- Domestic payment
- Domestic fulfillment
- Complexion inclusion
- Local service
- Local creator credibility

## Positioning

> **Advanced Asian formulation, made credible for Brazilian skin and climate.**

Emphasize performance, complexion inclusion, sensory quality, local validation, Portuguese education, and domestic service. Do not position the brand as teaching Brazil about beauty.

## Initial operating model

Start with:

- One Brazilian owned site
- One primary marketplace
- One fulfillment model
- One creator cohort
- One customer-service operation
- One product path: inclusive complexion or non-complexion

Defer a second marketplace, retail, and broad professional partnerships until the initial model passes its gates.

## Regulatory and tax gate

Obtain product-by-product advice covering applicable **ANVISA** requirements, including:

- Product classification
- Notification or registration pathway, as applicable
- Local responsible entity or importer
- Portuguese labeling
- Ingredient restrictions
- Claims substantiation
- Tax and invoicing
- Consumer rights and returns
- LGPD privacy compliance
- Adverse-event reporting
- Batch traceability and recall procedures

Requirements may differ for color cosmetics, skincare, sunscreen, eye products, active ingredients, and treatment-related claims.

## Channel plan

1. Brazilian owned site
2. One primary marketplace, selected after economic and fulfillment review
3. Brazilian Portuguese creators
4. WhatsApp customer service and CRM

Defer broad retail until the brand demonstrates product-market fit. Add a second marketplace or professional channel only if it creates clear incremental value.

## Payments and fulfillment

Launch only with:

- PIX
- Credit cards
- Installments
- Local fraud screening
- Applicable CPF and checkout-data requirements reviewed by counsel and payment providers
- Domestic inventory or reliable local fulfillment
- Local returns
- Portuguese transactional communication
- Portuguese customer service

Model installment costs, payment settlement, fraud, chargebacks, and refunds explicitly. Do not use uncertain cross-border delivery for the main paid-media launch.

## Creator plan

Recruit Brazilian Portuguese-speaking:

- Beauty educators
- Makeup artists
- Professional reviewers
- Complexion-diverse creators
- Live-commerce creators where functionality is verified
- Local beauty community leaders

Global K-beauty creators may support awareness but should not be the primary conversion engine.

## Brazil pilot gates

| Area | Gate |
|---|---|
| Product | Brazil Path A or Path B selected and validated |
| Compliance | ANVISA, label, claims, importer, tax, and privacy requirements completed |
| Commerce | PIX, cards, installments, checkout, returns, and service tested |
| Fulfillment | Domestic stock and reliable delivery operational |
| Product proof | Brazilian creator/customer content and reviews established |
| Economics | Positive hero-order variable contribution before paid-media scale |
| Safety | Complaint, adverse-event, hold, and recall SOPs operational |

# 8. Product safety, regulatory, privacy, and claims governance

## Country-by-product compliance register

For each country and product family, record:

- Product classification
- Applicable authority
- Notification or registration status
- Importer/responsible entity
- Ingredient review
- Label approval
- Claims evidence
- Advertising approval
- Batch traceability
- Complaint and adverse-event process
- Recall process
- Expiry and storage requirements

Relevant workstreams include:

- **Mexico:** COFEPRIS and applicable labeling, import, claims, and consumer-protection requirements
- **Colombia:** INVIMA and applicable cosmetic notification, labeling, import, claims, and post-market requirements
- **Brazil:** ANVISA and applicable classification, notification/registration, labeling, import, claims, and post-market requirements

## Claims matrix

Every claim must identify:

- Exact wording
- Country applicability
- Evidence source
- Required disclaimer
- Approved channel
- Legal owner
- Review date

Creators and affiliates may not make claims that the brand could not legally publish itself.

## Product-safety procedure

Customer service must be able to:

1. Log complaints.
2. Capture product, batch, purchase, and usage information.
3. Escalate potential adverse events immediately.
4. Notify quality and regulatory owners.
5. Place a product or batch on hold where required.
6. Coordinate medical, legal, and regulatory responses.
7. Initiate corrective action or recall.
8. Provide approved customer and creator communications.

## Privacy and consent

For each market, document:

- Data collected
- Legal basis
- Consent language
- Permitted channels
- Retention period
- Opt-out mechanism
- Data processors
- Cross-border transfer controls
- Incident-response process

Separate transactional, service, and marketing messaging. Advisor access to customer information must be role-based and auditable.

# 9. Channel, creator, and review governance

## Creator tiers

1. **Seeded experts:** product gifting; no guaranteed positive review.
2. **Paid creators:** defined deliverables, disclosure, claims rules, and usage rights.
3. **Performance affiliates:** trackable links, codes, or storefronts with transparent commissions.

## Creator contracts must cover

- Paid, gifted, and affiliate disclosure
- Claims restrictions
- Product-use instructions
- Before-and-after and comparison rules
- Usage rights
- Paid amplification and whitelisting
- Exclusivity
- Safety escalation
- Crisis procedures
- Content retention and audit
- Tax and payment documentation

## Review integrity

Report separately:

- Verified customer reviews
- Incentivized reviews
- Gifted-product reviews
- Paid creator testimonials
- Marketplace reviews

Reviews may be encouraged but never conditioned on positive sentiment. Do not suppress negative reviews; use them for service recovery, product investigation, and claims correction.

# 10. Pricing, commerce economics, and inventory

## 10.1 Country-level P&Ls

Before final inventory or media approval, build conservative, base, and upside cases by country, channel, product family, and material shade group.

Include:

- Product and packaging cost
- Freight and duties
- Local taxes
- Marketplace commissions
- Payment and installment fees
- Creator and affiliate commissions
- Fulfillment
- Returns and exchanges
- Customer-service labor
- Discounts and free shipping
- Paid media
- Regulatory and setup costs
- Local personnel or agency fees
- Inventory write-offs
- Working capital
- Cash conversion cycle
- 90- and 180-day repeat assumptions
- Break-even order volume
- Maximum pilot spend
- FX and import-delay sensitivity

### Core formulas

**Net revenue**  
Gross sales − discounts − cancellations − refunds

**Contribution margin**  
Net revenue − product cost − taxes/duties − payment fees − marketplace fees − creator/affiliate costs − fulfillment − returns − variable customer-service cost

**Fully loaded CAC**  
Variable acquisition and onboarding cost ÷ new completed customers

**90-day repeat rate**  
Customers with a second completed order within 90 days ÷ first-order customers

## 10.2 Indicative commercial allocation

Exact amounts require approved P&Ls. If resources are constrained, use this only as a starting hypothesis:

- Mexico: 40–45%
- Brazil: 30–35%, weighted toward readiness and local operations
- Colombia: 20–25%, weighted toward community and experimentation

Do not approve this split without market sizing, setup costs, and contribution economics.

## 10.3 Promotional hierarchy

Prioritize:

1. Minis and discovery sets
2. First-order bundles
3. Free-shipping thresholds
4. Creator-curated bundles
5. Loyalty benefits
6. Limited launch drops
7. Broad discounts only when economically justified

Avoid permanent deep discounting and unexplained price differences between marketplaces and owned channels.

## 10.4 Supply-chain requirements

For every market, document:

- Importer of record
- Seller of record
- Incoterms
- Customs broker
- Import route
- Warehouse
- 3PL
- Marketplace fulfillment option
- Returns destination
- Stock ownership
- Batch and expiry controls
- Minimum shelf life on arrival
- Reorder lead time
- Safety stock
- Recall and product-hold process

Inventory principles:

- Forecast by product family, shade, city, and channel.
- Use FEFO where relevant.
- Hold safety stock for hero products and priority shades.
- Do not aggressively advertise low-stock products.
- Use waitlists and back-in-stock alerts where permitted.
- Set maximum pilot inventory exposure.
- Define a slow-shade and near-expiry policy.
- Model FX and import delays.

# 11. Retention and CRM

Retention should reflect product consumption and channel consent.

## Lifecycle journeys

1. Order confirmation
2. Delivery and onboarding
3. Application and shade guidance
4. Review request
5. Shade exchange or service recovery
6. Replenishment
7. Routine cross-sell
8. Lapsed-customer reactivation
9. Referral and advocacy

### Category logic

- Skincare may support replenishment reminders or subscriptions.
- Makeup should generally use flexible reminders rather than automatic subscriptions.
- Discovery kits should trigger education and full-size conversion.
- Wrong-shade customers should receive support before cross-sell.
- Marketplace customers may receive CRM only through valid consent mechanisms.

# 12. KPIs and decision framework

## 12.1 Core KPIs

- Contribution margin after media, commissions, payment, fulfillment, and returns
- 90-day and 180-day repeat purchase
- CAC by country, SKU, and channel
- Net revenue and AOV
- Review volume and rating
- Shade exchange and return rate
- Payment completion by method
- On-time delivery
- Failed delivery
- Customer-service response and resolution time
- Hero-SKU availability and stockouts
- Creator incrementality
- CRM opt-in and incremental CRM revenue

## 12.2 Reporting dimensions

Report by:

- Country
- City or region
- Product family
- Shade and undertone
- Channel
- Creator
- New versus repeat customer
- Fulfillment mode
- Payment method
- Promotion

## 12.3 Gate governance

Every metric gate must specify:

- Formula
- Data source
- Minimum sample size
- Observation period
- Target
- Stop-loss threshold
- Owner
- Corrective action
- Decision date

Do not scale based on:

- Reach alone
- Follower count
- Last-click creator codes
- ROAS excluding returns and commissions
- Marketplace revenue without repeat or contribution analysis
- First-order conversion without service and return data

Use holdouts, matched creator tests, geo tests, or post-view studies where feasible.

# 13. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Early indicator | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Shade mismatch | Exchanges, negative swatch feedback | Local testing, better labels, local imagery, easy exchanges |
| Reach without sales | High views, weak product-page activity | Shift creator mix, improve pages, test incrementality |
| Marketplace margin loss | Negative contribution after fees | Restrict SKUs, cap ads, move bundles to owned site |
| Cross-border delivery failure | Late orders and support volume | Domestic stock, 3PL, marketplace fulfillment |
| Regulatory delay | Unresolved classification or labels | Local counsel, responsible entity, delay gate |
| Payment failure | High abandonment or declines | Local orchestration and method-level optimization |
| Privacy violation | Complaints, opt-outs, unauthorized messages | Consent controls, platform compliance, audits |
| MLM-style drift | Recruitment or inventory-pressure complaints | Code of conduct, no downstream commissions, audits |
| Counterfeits | Price anomalies or authenticity complaints | Trademark protection, authorized storefronts, takedowns |
| Supply shortage | Stockouts and cancellations | Shade forecasting, safety stock, media suppression |
| Product-safety issue | Adverse-event reports | Immediate escalation, batch traceability, hold/recall |
| Over-discounting | Weak full-price sales and repeat | Bundles, loyalty, service, limited promotions |
| Creator misconduct | Undisclosed or unsupported claims | Contracts, training, audit, suspension rights |
| Inventory overhang | Slow shades or expiry risk | Exposure limits, FEFO, transparent markdown/disposal policy |
| FX/import volatility | Landed-cost or lead-time variance | Scenario modeling, buffers, reorder review |

# 14. Launch phases

## Phase 0: Evidence and readiness

Complete before public demand generation:

- Consumer and concept testing
- Product, shade, and climate testing
- Claims and packaging approval
- Country-by-product regulatory register
- Country P&Ls and working-capital model
- Payment integration
- Marketplace onboarding
- 3PL and returns setup
- Customer-service staffing and training
- Creator contracting
- Inventory and batch plan
- Naming, trademark, and counterfeit review
- Privacy and consent review
- Adverse-event and recall procedures

## Phase 1: Controlled launch

### Mexico

Owned site and Mercado Libre in selected geographies, limited hero assortment, controlled creator cohort, and small paid-media test.

### Colombia

Creator affiliates, discovery kits, WhatsApp-assisted conversion, referral pilot, and marketplace support across controlled regions.

### Brazil

Launch only after selecting Path A or Path B and passing the Brazil readiness gate.

## Phase 2: Optimization — months 4–9

- Reallocate spend based on contribution, not reach
- Improve shade recommendations
- Correct high-return products or shades
- Increase reviews and UGC
- Expand profitable bundles
- Add loyalty and replenishment
- Improve inventory by shade and city
- Test selected professionals or retail partners

## Phase 3: Scale — months 10–18

- Expand country-specific assortment
- Introduce co-created shades or bundles
- Increase domestic inventory
- Add retail where it provides incremental trust or sampling
- Expand ethical advisor programs
- Add adjacent categories only after core repeat and contribution economics are proven

Delay launch or cap demand generation if:

# 16. Prioritized 90-day action plan

## Days 1–30: Decide and validate

### Executive decisions

1. Approve the gated sequencing: Mexico, Colombia, then Brazil.
2. Select Mexico and Colombia pilot geographies.
3. Select Brazil Path A or Path B.
4. Approve the initial product-family shortlist.
5. Define the maximum pilot budget and inventory exposure by country.
6. Assign accountable owners for every workstream.

### Evidence and economics

7. Commission local consumer and creator research by country, complexion depth, city, and use occasion.
8. Complete competitor price, assortment, shade, review, and channel mapping.
9. Build conservative, base, and upside P&Ls.
10. Calculate landed cost, payment cost, marketplace cost, creator economics, returns, and break-even volumes.
11. Define provisional gate metrics, formulas, samples, observation windows, and stop-loss rules.

### Product and compliance

12. Begin local shade, undertone, wear, oxidation, sunscreen, and climate testing.
13. Create the country-by-product regulatory register.
14. Review claims, labels, packaging, naming, trademarks, and counterfeit risks.
15. Establish product-safety, adverse-event, hold, recall, and complaint SOPs.

## Days 31–60: Build the launch system

### Commerce and operations

16. Select seller-of-record, importer-of-record, tax, and invoicing structures.
17. Select 3PL, warehouse, returns, and marketplace fulfillment models.

# Final recommendation

Proceed as a **gated regional launch program**:

- **Mexico:** first marketplace and Spanish-language scale market.
- **Colombia:** community-commerce and assisted-selling laboratory.
- **Brazil:** separate local business launch after compliance, payment, fulfillment, language, and product-fit readiness.

The brand should compete neither through foreignness nor low price. Its defensible advantage should be:

> **Locally demonstrated performance, credible shade guidance, sensorial Asian formulation expertise, transparent human assistance, and reliable local commerce.**

Approve the strategic direction now. Approve final inventory, national media, and country scale only after the country P&Ls, regulatory registers, operating designs, pilot geographies, Brazil product path, and quantified decision gates are completed and signed off.
