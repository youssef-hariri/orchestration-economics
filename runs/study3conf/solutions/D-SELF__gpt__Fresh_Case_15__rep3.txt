# Royalty and Franchise-Financing Restructuring Plan

## 1. Executive recommendation

Adopt a **single global franchise-resilience policy with country-specific parameters**. The policy should replace uncapped fixed-USD obligations for franchisees whose revenues and costs are predominantly local currency.

The recommended model combines:

1. **Local-currency, sales-based royalties** as the primary charge;
2. **One controlled secondary adjustment**—either partial inflation indexation or a capped FX-linked component;
3. **Temporary, formula-based relief** triggered by objective financial and market conditions;
4. **Local-currency financing**, leasing, supplier credit, receivables finance, and limited franchisor guarantees;
5. **Formal franchisee consultation and dispute mechanisms** consistent with local law;
6. **Relief-for-compliance covenants** covering reporting, food safety, sourcing, labor, tax, and reinvestment;
7. **Centralized governance, exposure limits, and automated monitoring** across the network;
8. **Selective company-owned conversions** only where strategic and financially justified.

The objective is to maximize **risk-adjusted realized system cash flow**, not nominal royalty accrual:

\[
\text{System Value}
=
\text{Collected Sustainable Royalties}
+
\text{Brand and Supply Value}
-
\text{Closures}
-
\text{Defaults}
-
\text{Support Losses}
-
\text{Brand Damage}
\]

An uncapped USD claim that causes closures or nonpayment is economically inferior to a lower local-currency charge that remains collectible and preserves the network.

---

## 2. Market comparisons and commercial archetypes

Country classification should reflect not only inflation, but also currency convertibility, financing conditions, purchasing power, import dependence, tax exposure, and collective-action risk.

| Archetype | Illustrative markets | Main characteristics | Default approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Severe FX and convertibility stress** | Egypt; selected Nigerian conditions | Sharp depreciation, restricted FX access, repatriation delays, import disruption, severe purchasing-power pressure | Local-currency percentage royalty; suspend hard-currency fees during stress; no fixed minimum |
| **B. High inflation with functioning finance** | Turkey | Rapid repricing, high inflation and interest rates, meaningful local banking access | Local-currency royalty; partial capped indexation; local-currency financing |
| **C. Managed volatility and financing capacity** | Indonesia and Brazil | Tradable currency, stronger payment infrastructure, more available bank, leasing, and receivables finance | Local-currency royalty; periodic indexation; limited FX exposure |
| **D. Stable-currency markets** | Stable-currency company-owned and franchised markets | More predictable margins, financing, convertibility, and consumer demand | Conventional royalty, minimums, and normal financing |

These classifications should be dynamic. A country can move between archetypes as inflation, FX access, financing, or operating conditions change.

### Country-specific application

- **Turkey:** emphasize local-currency royalties, partial indexation, quarterly caps, and explicit interest-rate and refinancing tests.
- **Egypt:** prioritize convertibility protection, blocked-funds treatment, local settlement options, and suspension of hard-currency charges when remittance is unavailable.
- **Nigeria:** include energy, fuel, security, logistics, import, and power-reliability costs in viability tests; use practically accessible FX rates.
- **Indonesia:** use regional parameters for logistics, rent, purchasing power, and distribution costs; support bank, leasing, and merchant-receipt finance.
- **Brazil:** rely primarily on local-currency economics and receivables finance, while addressing complex federal and state tax, disclosure, and litigation requirements.
- **Stable markets:** retain conventional economics and use company-owned stores as normalized benchmarks, not as automatic subsidies for stressed markets.

---

## 3. Franchisee segmentation

Market rules should be supplemented by franchisee-level classification:

### Normal

- Positive post-charge cash flow;
- current payments;
- compliant operations;
- adequate reinvestment capacity.

### Pressure

- Positive but deteriorating cash flow;
- margin compression;
- increased working-capital needs;
- declining debt-service coverage.

### Severe stress

- Post-charge cash flow is negative or insufficient to fund essential obligations;
- material arrears;
- high closure risk;
- significant FX, import, energy, or refinancing disruption.

### Nonviable

- No credible path to positive cash flow after operational restructuring and available relief.

Relief should be available to viable operators that provide reliable information and meet brand obligations. Market stress alone should not automatically qualify every franchisee.

# 4. Royalty architecture

## 4.1 Define eligible sales

Use one global definition, supplemented by country-specific tax and channel rules:

\[
E_t =
G_t - T_t - Ref_t - Chg_t - Disc_t
\]

Where:

- \(G_t\) = customer sales and receipts;
- \(T_t\) = VAT, sales tax, or similar taxes collected for government;
- \(Ref_t\) = refunds;
- \(Chg_t\) = chargebacks;
- \(Disc_t\) = approved discounts and coupons.

The contracts must address:

- delivery-platform sales;
- online sales;
- catering and institutional sales;
- gift cards;
- cash sales;
- related-party transactions;
- franchisor-funded promotions;
- unauthorized sales channels.

### Delivery-platform treatment

The default should be a gross-sales basis because it is simpler to administer. Where platform commissions materially distort store economics, an approved credit may be used:

\[
E_t^{*}
=
E_t-\min(\text{Verified Platform Commission},pE_t)
\]

The credit should be capped, independently verifiable, and applied consistently.

---

## 4.2 Core royalty

The primary charge should be a percentage of eligible local-currency sales:

\[
R_t = r_m \times E_t
\]

Where:

- \(R_t\) = core royalty;
- \(r_m\) = market- and format-specific rate;
- \(E_t\) = eligible sales.

Rates should be calibrated using historical and scenario data, including:

- royalty-to-sales and royalty-to-EBITDA ratios;
- pre- and post-royalty cash flow;
- real same-store sales;
- closure and default probability;
- price elasticity;
- food, labor, rent, energy, logistics, and import costs;
- maintenance and reinvestment needs;
- franchisor support costs;
- tax and remittance leakage.

The rate should preserve:

- a minimum operating reserve;
- required maintenance capex;
- viable debt-service coverage;
- adequate franchisee reinvestment;
- a sustainable franchisor return.

---

## 4.3 Cash-flow affordability test

Affordability must be calculated without circularity.

### Calculation sequence

1. Calculate sales net of taxes collected for government.
2. Deduct ordinary cash operating costs, including rent, labor, food, energy, logistics, utilities, and ordinary maintenance.
3. Deduct operating taxes and required maintenance capex.
4. Deduct normalized working-capital investment.
5. Calculate **pre-franchise-charge CFADS**.
6. Reserve minimum amounts for wages, taxes, critical suppliers, utilities, rent, and safety-related maintenance.
7. Determine the maximum affordable franchise charge.
8. Apply royalty, marketing, technology, and other mandatory charge formulas subject to the ceiling.
9. Calculate post-charge CFADS and DSCR.
10. Assign the franchisee’s stress tier and relief status.

\[
DSCR_t =
\frac{CFADS_t-\text{Franchise Charges}_t}
{\text{Scheduled Debt Service}_t}
\]

The model must separately report:

- pre-charge CFADS;
- post-charge CFADS;
- cash available for debt service;
- cash available for contingent recovery;
- cash available for owner distributions.

---

## 4.4 Secondary charges and anti-double-counting

Each charge should have only one primary escalator.

### Technology and support

Use either:

- a percentage of sales; or
- a modest local-currency fixed fee.

If indexed:

\[
F_t =
F_{t-1}\left[1+\min(\alpha I_t,C_I)\right]
\]

Full CPI indexation should not apply simultaneously to the core royalty and technology fee.

### Marketing fund

The marketing contribution should be separately disclosed and ring-fenced:

- collected in local currency;
- used for approved market activities;
- reported periodically;
- overseen by a franchisee advisory body.

It may be excluded from franchisor margin but should generally be included in franchisee affordability testing.

### FX-linked component

Use only for demonstrable cross-border costs and only where lawful, accessible, and economically justified:

\[
H_t =
\min\left[
H_0\left(\frac{FX_t}{FX_0}\right),
H_{t-1}(1+C_{FX}),
H_{\max,t}
\right]
\]

Requirements:

- stated maximum share of total charges;
- practical, approved exchange-rate source;
- automatic suspension when conversion or remittance is materially impaired;
- no indefinite compounding;
- defined treatment of deferred amounts.

In severe-stress markets, the default hard-currency component should be zero.

---

## 4.5 Aggregate charge ceiling

All mandatory system-imposed charges should be subject to an aggregate ceiling:

\[
TC_t
\leq
\min\left[
TC_t^{\text{formula}},
TC_{t-1}(1+C_{TC}),
\text{Affordability Ceiling}_t
\right]
\]

The ceiling should cover, as applicable:

- core royalty;
- technology and support fees;
- marketing contributions;
- mandatory software charges;
- development fees;
- tax gross-ups;
- required brand-related expenditures.

The documents should distinguish:

1. **Franchisee cash burden:** all mandatory payments and system-imposed expenditures.
2. **Franchisor revenue:** amounts retained by the franchisor or affiliates.
3. **Restricted marketing funds:** amounts collected for approved market activity.
4. **Required brand capex:** mandatory investment imposed by the brand.
5. **Third-party costs:** platform, logistics, supplier, and financing charges.

---

## 4.6 Minimums and tiers

### Minimum royalties

- **Stable markets:** retain conventional minimums where economics support them.
- **Indonesia and Brazil:** use low local-currency or sales-linked minimums.
- **Turkey:** use a low, adjustable minimum.
- **Egypt and stressed Nigeria:** suspend fixed minimums during formal stress.

A minimum should never force closure solely because of a temporary FX event.

### Tiered royalties

Tiering may support smaller, newer, regional, or recovering stores:

\[
R_t =
\begin{cases}
r_1E_t, & E_t\leq S_1\\
r_1S_1+r_2(E_t-S_1), & S_1<E_t\leq S_2\\
r_1S_1+r_2(S_2-S_1)+r_3(E_t-S_2), & E_t>S_2
\end{cases}
\]

Thresholds should be adjusted for inflation and protected against:

- store or entity splitting;
- related-party sales shifting;
- channel reclassification;
- underreported cash sales;
- changes in reporting periods.

# 5. Stress relief and recovery

## Tier 0: Normal

- Standard royalty formula;
- routine indexation;
- normal payment terms;
- ordinary enforcement.

## Tier 1: Pressure

- Cap fee increases;
- adjust payment timing;
- provide working-capital finance;
- defer non-critical capex;
- provide pricing, menu, procurement, and operating support.

## Tier 2: Severe stress

- Suspend fixed minimums;
- convert temporarily to a percentage-only royalty;
- suspend or defer hard-currency components;
- restructure royalty arrears;
- extend debt tenor;
- provide targeted inventory, capex, or equipment finance;
- require a turnaround plan.

## Tier 3: Systemic crisis

- Emergency market-wide royalty reset;
- lender standstill;
- store-by-store viability review;
- managed transfer;
- selective company-owned or managed conversion;
- orderly closure of nonviable units.

### Illustrative triggers

Triggers should be calibrated locally and may include:

- defined depreciation over a trailing period;
- inflation or food inflation exceeding a threshold;
- negative post-charge CFADS;
- DSCR below the approved level;
- royalty exceeding a defined proportion of store EBITDA;
- material FX-access or repatriation disruption;
- critical import, energy, or logistics interruption;
- material increases in arrears or closure risk.

### Deferred balances

Deferred royalties should be treated as capped, local-currency, cash-flow-contingent recovery claims—not indefinite USD debt.

Each agreement must specify:

- maximum balance;
- maximum recovery period;
- expiry date;
- whether interest accrues;
- currency denomination;
- payment priority;
- treatment relative to payroll, taxes, suppliers, bank debt, and capex;
- treatment upon renewal, transfer, termination, or insolvency;
- whether the claim follows the store or franchisee;
- whether it is payable only from excess cash flow.

Preferred recovery hierarchy:

1. Temporary waiver;
2. capped prospective step-up;
3. cash-flow-contingent recovery;
4. legally approved local reinvestment or netting;
5. partial write-off;
6. subordinated economic participation where legally and commercially viable.

No automatic recovery should be triggered solely by currency appreciation. Recovery should remain subject to the affordability ceiling.

# 6. Franchise-financing platform

## 6.1 Financing principles

1. Match debt currency to revenue currency unless a documented hedge exists.
2. Test local interest-rate and refinancing risk separately from FX risk.
3. Finance viable stores or credible turnarounds, not structurally unprofitable units.
4. Use third-party finance before direct franchisor lending.
5. Cap country, franchisee, store, guarantee, and blocked-funds exposure.
6. Prohibit distributions while support facilities are outstanding.
7. Protect payroll, taxes, food safety, critical suppliers, rent, and utilities.

## 6.2 Financing hierarchy

Use the least risky and most scalable source first:

1. Supplier credit;
2. equipment leasing;
3. bank working-capital facilities;
4. payment-processor and receivables finance;
5. vendor or development finance;
6. limited franchisor guarantees;
7. subordinated franchisor support;
8. selective company-owned conversion;
9. managed transfer or closure.

## 6.3 Financing products

### Local bank facilities

Use standardized underwriting based on:

- verified POS data;
- bank and payment receipts;
- store-level performance;
- approved uses;
- controlled collection accounts;
- limited guarantees.

### Working-capital facilities

Eligible uses include:

- inventory;
- payroll;
- utilities;
- supplier prepayments;
- short cash-conversion gaps;
- approved royalty arrears during transition.

### Equipment leasing and vendor finance

Prioritize financing for:

- refrigeration;
- ovens and cooking equipment;
- POS and technology;
- delivery equipment;
- generators and energy systems;
- fit-outs.

### Receivables finance

Where legally available, lend against:

- card receipts;
- digital-wallet receipts;
- delivery-platform receipts;
- merchant settlements.

This is likely to be especially scalable in Brazil, Indonesia, Turkey, and selected Nigerian operators.

### Franchisor support vehicle

A ring-fenced vehicle may provide:

- bridge loans;
- subordinated loans;
- inventory finance;
- royalty deferrals;
- mandated capex finance.

Before launch, approve:

- capitalization and funding source;
- maximum country exposure;
- maximum franchisee and store exposure;
- maximum blocked-funds exposure;
- expected-loss reserve;
- stressed-loss limit;
- risk-adjusted return hurdle;
- stop-loss triggers;
- suspension criteria for new support.

## 6.4 Financing terms and guarantees

Each facility should specify:

- currency;
- benchmark and spread;
- fixed or floating rate;
- repricing frequency;
- tenor;
- amortization;
- grace period;
- collateral;
- covenants;
- DSCR requirements;
- refinancing assumptions;
- expected loss;
- recovery value;
- default and workout procedures.

Guarantees should define:

- principal, interest, fee, and enforcement-cost coverage;
- first-loss or pari passu treatment;
- lender workout obligations;
- cure and step-in rights;
- collateral and security;
- subrogation;
- currency-loss treatment;
- reduction as debt amortizes;
- technical-default treatment;
- expiry;
- maximum portfolio loss.

Independent credit approval is required where the franchisor is simultaneously lender, guarantor, royalty creditor, supplier, brand auditor, or potential acquirer.

## 6.5 Payment waterfall

Controlled accounts and cash sweeps should prioritize:

1. Wages and employee obligations;
2. Taxes and statutory payments;
3. Food safety and critical suppliers;
4. Utilities and rent;
5. Scheduled debt service;
6. Royalty and other franchisor charges;
7. Owner distributions.

# 7. Collective engagement and bargaining

## 7.1 Franchisee councils

Establish market-level councils with:

- representative-selection rules;
- defined consultation rights;
- aggregate system information;
- confidentiality requirements;
- conflict-of-interest rules;
- written minutes;
- mediation access;
- representation of master franchisees and area developers where appropriate.

In markets with established association cultures, negotiate market-level frameworks rather than thousands of inconsistent side agreements.

## 7.2 Competition-law safeguards

Franchisee associations are not necessarily labor unions. Their activity may raise separate competition-law concerns.

Use:

- counsel-approved agendas;
- antitrust training;
- aggregate, non-sensitive information;
- written minutes;
- no coordination of prices, suppliers, territories, output, or payment refusal;
- no sharing of individual competitively sensitive data.

Lawful association participation must not trigger retaliation. However, collective action should not automatically produce indefinite relief.

## 7.3 Collective late-payment and nonpayment protocol

If coordinated late payment or nonpayment occurs:

1. Verify the facts and distinguish liquidity stress from strategic withholding.
2. Use a single, counsel-approved communication process.
3. Engage association representatives.
4. Apply objective eligibility criteria.
5. Offer documented standstills or payment plans where justified.
6. Maintain food-safety, trademark, and essential operating standards.
7. Treat members and nonmembers consistently under the same eligibility rules.
8. Use mediation before formal enforcement where appropriate.
9. Apply proportionate enforcement for material defaults.
10. Escalate material coordinated nonpayment to the executive committee or board.

---

# 8. Company-owned stores and portfolio strategy

## 8.1 Benchmarking

Profitable company-owned stores in stable markets should benchmark:

- store-level EBITDA;
- labor, rent, food, energy, and marketing ratios;
- maintenance capex;
- working capital;
- payback period;
- reinvestment requirements.

Comparisons must use consistent allocations for technology, supply chain, corporate support, maintenance, and working capital.

## 8.2 Capital transfers

Stable-market cash may support stressed markets only after assessing:

- tax and withholding costs;
- expected return;
- country and currency risk;
- local financing alternatives;
- guarantee and blocked-funds exposure;
- strategic importance;
- recovery probability.

Set a maximum subsidy or investment amount per country and an aggregate cross-border exposure limit.

## 8.3 Company-owned conversions

Convert a store only where:

\[
\text{Expected Company-Owned Return}
>
\text{Franchised Royalty Value}
+
\text{Avoided Closure and Brand Damage}
-
\text{Acquisition and Operating Risk}
\]

The analysis must include:

- acquisition cost;
- assumed liabilities;
- lease obligations;
- employee transfer costs;
- working capital;
- tax and regulatory costs;
- integration costs;
- management capacity;
- downside performance;
- exit value;
- closure costs if the conversion fails.

---

# 9. Brand protection and relief conditions

The following remain non-waivable except where a documented emergency protocol permits temporary substitution:

- food safety;
- trademark and trade-dress use;
- approved or validated equivalent suppliers;
- cybersecurity and customer-data controls;
- employee health and safety;
- core cleanliness and service standards;
- accurate sales reporting.

Relief agreements should specify:

- relief amount and duration;
- required operational improvements;
- reporting frequency;
- maintenance and capex commitments;
- recovery conditions;
- consequences of misuse or misreporting.

### Emergency supply substitution

Where imports are disrupted, establish a formal process covering:

- local supplier approval;
- temporary product specifications;
- testing and traceability;
- audit frequency;
- customer communication;
- margin effects;
- reinstatement of normal suppliers;
- accountability for quality failures.

---

# 10. Governance, data, and administration

## 10.1 Franchise Resilience Committee

The central committee should include finance, treasury, operations, legal, tax, risk, supply chain, and franchise representatives.

It should:

- approve market classifications and parameters;
- approve formulas and exceptions;
- set exposure and write-off limits;
- authorize stress activation and exit;
- oversee association engagement;
- monitor brand risk;
- report quarterly to the board.

## 10.2 Rules engine

The rules engine should determine each store’s:

- market archetype;
- stress tier;
- royalty formula;
- cap, floor, or deferral;
- financing eligibility;
- approval authority;
- review date;
- exit conditions.

## 10.3 Required data

Collect and reconcile:

- POS and digital sales;
- bank and payment receipts;
- delivery-platform statements;
- royalties invoiced and collected;
- food, labor, rent, energy, logistics, and import costs;
- debt balances and repricing dates;
- tax, payroll, landlord, and supplier arrears;
- quality and audit results;
- FX and inflation indices.

Track separately:

1. royalty accrued;
2. royalty invoiced;
3. royalty collected;
4. royalty converted;
5. royalty repatriated;
6. net realized cash after taxes, hedging, and transfer costs.

## 10.4 Data-quality fallback

Where data is incomplete or unreliable:

- assign a conservative provisional classification;
- require remediation by a defined deadline;
- conduct manual review;
- use documented estimation rules;
- exercise audit rights;
- suspend relief for material noncooperation;
- provide an appeal process;
- avoid unsupported punitive assumptions.

## 10.5 Exception controls

- Every exception receives an identifier, expiry date, and approval record.
- Country teams may apply only pre-approved formulas.
- Permanent amendments, guarantees, write-offs, and direct lending require central approval.
- Oral or undocumented concessions are prohibited.
- If exceptions exceed a defined threshold, revise the standard formula rather than expanding discretion.

# 11. Legal, tax, regulatory, and accounting validation

The following are implementation gates and require country-by-country review by qualified local advisers. This is a strategic framework, not jurisdiction-specific legal, tax, accounting, or regulatory advice.

## Contract and franchise law

Validate:

- amendment and consent requirements;
- renewal-based implementation;
- franchise disclosure and registration;
- hardship and good-faith rules;
- foreign-currency and indexation restrictions;
- waiver and standstill consequences;
- treatment of existing defaults and arrears;
- termination, cure, and renewal rights;
- local-language and electronic-signature requirements.

## FX and repatriation

Validate:

- permitted payment currency;
- approved exchange-rate sources;
- practical convertibility;
- remittance and central-bank requirements;
- blocked-funds treatment;
- local reinvestment, netting, escrow, or supplier-settlement alternatives;
- valuation and expiry of contingent recovery balances.

## Tax

Review:

- withholding tax;
- VAT and other indirect taxes;
- tax gross-ups;
- deductibility;
- transfer pricing;
- permanent-establishment risk;
- thin-capitalization rules;
- debt-forgiveness income;
- marketing-fund treatment;
- customs and import taxes;
- tax treatment of guarantees, loans, and equity-like instruments.

Each fee requires a substance analysis showing whether it is a royalty, service fee, reimbursement, marketing contribution, or financing charge.

## Competition and labor

Validate:

- association representation rights;
- information-sharing restrictions;
- coordinated nonpayment risk;
- labor and possible co-employer exposure;
- employee obligations in company-owned conversions;
- treatment of master franchisees and area developers.

## Lending and accounting

Before pilots, validate:

- lending and guarantee licensing;
- security registration and perfection;
- receivables-finance requirements;
- foreign-exchange approvals;
- accounting treatment of concessions and contingent recovery;
- expected credit losses;
- guarantee provisions;
- support-vehicle consolidation;
- currency translation;
- hyperinflationary accounting where relevant;
- related-party disclosures.

# 12. Implementation phases and readiness gates

## Phase 1: Stabilization and diagnostic — 0–90 days

- Freeze uncapped USD increases in severe-stress markets.
- Build the store-level data inventory.
- Reconcile POS, bank, platform, and royalty records.
- Classify stores by viability, stress, and compliance.
- Establish legal, tax, FX, competition, accounting, treasury, and supply workstreams.
- Set temporary limits for relief, guarantees, lending, and write-offs.
- Establish association-engagement protocols.
- Communicate the framework without promising universal concessions.

## Phase 2: Controlled pilots — 90–180 days

Pilot:

- one severe-stress market or condition;
- Turkey or another high-inflation market;
- Brazil or Indonesia;
- one stable-currency market.

Test:

- local-currency percentage royalties;
- indexation caps;
- FX corridors;
- percentage-only relief;
- arrears treatment;
- financing products;
- data and enforcement processes.

### Pilot gates

No pilot should launch until:

- legal and tax opinions are complete;
- accounting treatment is documented;
- financing and guarantee structures are approved;
- data quality is adequate;
- treasury limits are established;
- technology calculations are tested;
- field teams are trained;
- supply-substitution procedures are operational.

## Phase 3: Contracting and financing rollout — 6–12 months

- Implement amendments, renewals, waivers, and standstills.
- Launch bank, leasing, vendor, and receivables programs.
- Deploy the rules engine.
- Establish councils and mediation.
- Restructure eligible arrears.
- Train franchise, finance, treasury, legal, and field teams.
- Complete supply and operational continuity plans.

## Phase 4: Scale and rebase — 12–24 months

- Expand proven formulas.
- Rebase rates using realized economics.
- Recover, capitalize, forgive, or write off deferred balances.
- Review company-owned conversions.
- Reclassify markets annually.
- Conduct independent post-pilot validation.
- Scale only where performance and control thresholds are met.

Existing contracts should generally be grandfathered until renewal unless local law and contractual rights permit amendment. Emergency measures should be documented through carefully reviewed waivers, side letters, or standstills.

# 13. KPIs and decision triggers

## Franchisee resilience

- real same-store sales;
- store-level EBITDA and CFADS;
- post-charge DSCR;
- royalty-to-sales ratio;
- royalty-to-EBITDA ratio;
- food, labor, rent, energy, logistics, and import-cost ratios;
- working-capital days;
- tax, payroll, supplier, landlord, and royalty arrears;
- closure, insolvency, and ownership-transfer rates;
- maintenance and reinvestment completion.

## Franchisor economics

- cash royalty collection;
- constant-currency royalty revenue;
- realized versus accrued royalty;
- collection delay;
- support and guarantee exposure;
- financing defaults;
- write-offs and impairments;
- support-vehicle return;
- blocked and unconverted funds;
- risk-adjusted return by country.

## Brand protection

- food-safety incidents;
- audit pass rates;
- service times;
- customer complaints;
- unauthorized sourcing;
- sales-reporting discrepancies;
- training completion;
- required capex completion.

## Financing

- local-currency financing deployed;
- approval rate;
- all-in borrowing cost;
- default and restructuring rate;
- unhedged foreign-currency debt;
- guarantee utilization;
- use-of-proceeds compliance;
- reserve coverage.

## Engagement and governance

- amendment execution rate;
- council participation;
- unresolved disputes;
- mediation duration;
- coordinated payment actions;
- exception volume and value;
- regulatory or competition-law incidents.

## Pilot and board triggers

Escalate, pause, redesign, or exit a pilot if:

- closures exceed the approved threshold;
- cash collection falls materially below plan;
- post-charge CFADS deteriorates;
- food-safety or fraud incidents rise;
- guarantees or support exposure exceed limits;
- deferred balances exceed realistic recovery value;
- unhedged FX debt becomes material;
- relief recipients breach material standards;
- blocked funds or remittance delays exceed treasury limits;
- collective action shifts toward coordinated unlawful nonpayment;
- a material share of stores requires individualized exceptions.

---

## Final end state

The recommended end state is:

- **Egypt and stressed Nigeria:** local-currency percentage royalties, no uncapped USD minimums, convertibility protections, and targeted liquidity support.
- **Turkey:** local-currency royalties with partial capped indexation and strong interest-rate testing.
- **Indonesia and Brazil:** standardized local-currency formulas with scalable bank, leasing, and receivables finance.
- **Stable markets:** conventional royalties and minimums, with company-owned stores serving as disciplined operating benchmarks.

Across all markets, relief should be temporary, transparent, and exchanged for reporting, financing discipline, operational compliance, and recovery commitments. The program should be approved as a global policy and pilot framework, with final rates, thresholds, contract language, financing commitments, and settlement mechanisms subject to country-specific legal, tax, accounting, FX, regulatory, and operational validation before implementation.
