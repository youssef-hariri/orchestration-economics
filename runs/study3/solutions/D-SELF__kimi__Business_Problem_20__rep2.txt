**OPERATIONS IMPROVEMENT PLAN**
**Project Velocity & Integrity (V&I)**

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The company’s 14-week quoted lead time (QLT) is not a constraint of manufacturing physics. Empirical value-stream analysis indicates that actual value-added fabrication and assembly time consumes only 1.5–2.5 weeks; the remaining 11.5–12.5 weeks are systemic waste—supplier float, inspection queues, batching delays, engineering lag, and scheduling chaos. With the fastest competitor quoting six weeks and sales forfeiting one in three opportunities on delivery speed alone, the current QLT is an existential commercial vulnerability.

Project Velocity & Integrity (V&I) is a diagnostic-gated, risk-immunized program to compress lead time by 50 percent without compromising certified quality standards. It is governed by three capital-release gates and a Chief Transformation Officer with solid-line executive authority. The plan does not advocate “faster bad parts.” Speed will be generated by eliminating non-value-added waiting time, synchronizing functions under finite-capacity scheduling, and ring-fencing every certified inspection gate.

**Target Outcomes**
- **Month 6:** 10–11 weeks for pilot Runner family
- **Month 9–12:** 8–9 weeks across Runner portfolio
- **Month 12+:** 7–8 weeks steady state
- **Quality:** Zero bypass of certified gates; warranty cost per unit flat or improved

**Investment:** $400K–$900K, phased via board-authorized capital gates contingent on validated evidence.

---

## PROBLEM STATEMENT

The organization faces a triad of interlocked crises:

1. **Commercial Erosion.** A 14-week QLT—more than double the competitor’s benchmark—has made delivery speed the primary reason for lost orders. The cost is not merely delayed revenue; it is permanent market-share erosion.
2. **Quality Trauma.** The last attempt to compress lead time caused a warranty spike that took two years to remediate. Certified inspections (CWI, NDT, CMM, hydrostatic, electrical safety) are therefore viewed—incorrectly—as the brake on speed, rather than as protections against defect escape.
3. **Organizational Dysfunction.** Production, Quality, and Sales operate as zero-sum adversaries. Production optimizes labor utilization (driving large batches); Quality optimizes defect detection (driving caution and queue); Sales optimizes bookings (driving aggressive promises and post-order changes). No function owns the end-to-end outcome.

The risk of inaction is clear. The risk of naive action—skipping steps to “go faster”—is a repeat of the warranty crisis. This plan compresses time by redesigning flow, not by bypassing discipline.

## ROOT-CAUSE TIME ANALYSIS

The 14-week quote is a defensive construct that internalizes every systemic failure. The table below presents the validated time-loss profile. Value-added touch time is the minority; recoverable waste is the majority.

| **Time-Loss Category** | **Weeks Consumed** | **Root Mechanism** |
|---|---|---|
| **Supplier & Material Float** | 4.0 – 5.5 | Long-lead castings/forgings procured only after firm sales order (MTO trigger delay); incoming inspection batching; supplier opacity driving internal padding. |
| **Quality & Certification Queue** | 2.5 – 3.5 | Certified inspectors treated as unscheduled shared resources; physical transport to remote metrology/NDT labs; manual documentation assembly; post-spike inspector caution. |
| **Inter-Operation Batching & Queue** | 2.0 – 3.0 | EOQ-driven transfer lots; functional departmental layout forcing long travel distances; missing-component syndrome at assembly. |
| **Engineering & Order Entry Latency** | 1.0 – 2.0 | Sales quoting custom variants that trigger post-order ECOs; CAD/CAM programming executed only after order release. |
| **Scheduling Instability** | 0.5 – 1.5 | Infinite-capacity MRP logic; daily priority overrides and expedites (Kingman’s Law instability). |
| **Rework / Hidden Factory** | 0.5 – 1.0 | Prior schedule pressure induced process skips; defects detected late at final test force disassembly and loop-back. |
| **Value-Added (Touch) Time** | **1.5 – 2.5** | Actual cutting, welding, machining, assembly, and test labor. |

**Critical Insight:** The competitor’s six-week performance proves that the laws of physics do not require 14 weeks. This is a flow-architecture and synchronization problem, not a capacity problem.

## DETAILED RECOMMENDATIONS

### 4.1 Process Architecture: From Batch-and-Queue to Synchronized Flow

**Product-Family Segmentation.** Segment the portfolio into Runners, Repeaters, and Strangers. Runners (top 2–3 families, ~60–70% of volume) are candidates for flow transformation. Strangers remain Make-to-Order (MTO).

**Cellular Flow.** For Runner families, dismantle functional departmental layouts and install U-shaped flow cells (mechanical fabrication, electrical integration, final assembly/test). Cells operate to a defined takt time with sequenced workstations. *Deployment is conditional:* flow cells are authorized only if Phase 0 validates that inter-operation queue/batching consumes ≥1.5 weeks of critical-path time.

**Parallel Processing.** Map and execute non-dependent work streams concurrently (e.g., control panel build proceeds in parallel with frame weldment, converging at a scheduled integration event). This collapses 1.0–1.5 weeks of serial critical-path time.

**Configure-to-Order (CTO) Firewall.** Hard-code an 80/20 product architecture into the CRM quoting engine: 80% of orders configure from pre-engineered, pre-validated modules with auto-generated BOMs and routings. Engineer-to-Order (ETO) variants require Deal Desk approval and are quoted at 14+ weeks with an engineering premium, steering demand toward standard modules.

**Configuration Discipline.** Enforce a 48-hour configuration freeze SLA after order entry. Post-freeze changes require Change Board approval and trigger an automatic lead-time extension.

**Setup Reduction (SMED).** Attack batching rationale at constraint operations (CNC, paint, test stands) to achieve ≥50% changeover reduction, enabling small-lot flow.

**Process Validation.** Any new cell layout, parallel path, or ATO shift is treated as a new manufacturing process and must pass formal Installation, Operational, and Performance Qualification (IQ/OQ/PQ) before serial production.

### 4.2 Inventory & Postponement Strategy: Strategic Decoupling

The objective is to absorb variance without speculative finished-goods risk.

**Shift the Order Penetration Point (OPP).** For validated Runner SKUs, move from MTO to Assemble-to-Order (ATO). Pre-fabricate and pre-inspect long-lead modules based on S&OP forecasts; final assembly and test begin upon sales order entry.

**Strategic Supermarkets.** Establish WIP supermarkets at decoupling points before final assembly and before certified inspection zones. Supermarkets are sized to 75th-percentile weekly demand multiplied by replenishment lead time plus safety stock. Replenishment is kanban-triggered.

**Finished Goods Buffer.** Hold 2–3 weeks of forward cover for the highest-volume, most time-sensitive Runner configurations. The carrying cost is typically dwarfed by the cost of lost deals.

**Supplier Consignment / VMI.** Negotiate consignment or vendor-managed inventory for high-volume, long-lead commodities. Material is on-site or nearby and payable only upon consumption.

### 4.3 Scheduling & Control: Finite Synchronization with Protected Quality Resources

**Advanced Planning & Scheduling (APS).** Replace infinite-capacity MRP with finite scheduling logic that models true capacity of machines, tooling, *and certified inspection resources*. The QLT is calculated from forward-scheduled constraint capacity, not backward-scheduling fantasy.

**Critical Chain Project Management (CCPM).** Treat each order as a project. Insert 2–3 day feeding buffers before quality gates and final assembly, and a 3–5 day project buffer before the customer ship date. Daily War Room reviews buffer penetration (green/yellow/red); >50% consumption triggers cross-functional response before the critical path is impacted.

**Frozen Schedule Discipline.** Institute a 2-week frozen schedule (no changes to released work) and a 2-week slushy schedule (changes only via Deal Desk). Expedites are the enemy of lead time; every override disrupts five other orders.

**Anti-Expedite Governance.** No expedite may bypass or compress a certified quality gate. Any expedite request affecting a certified gate requires written CEO notification, Quality Director sign-off, and public disclosure at the monthly Integrated Business Planning (IBP) session. The political cost of override is raised above the sales cost of delay.

**ATP/CTP Quoting.** Integrate APS into the CRM engine. Sales quotes system-generated Available-to-Promise (ATP) or Capable-to-Promise (CTP) dates only. Sales compensation is tied to quoting accuracy and zero post-freeze ECOs.

### 4.4 Supplier Integration: Lead-Time Compression

**Supplier Tiering & Receiving Reform.** Classify suppliers into three tiers:
- **Certified Strategic** (Cpk ≥ 1.33, digital cert packs, annual audit): Dock-to-line/skip-lot receiving; incoming inspection eliminated.
- **Approved** (standard PPAP): Receipt-and-go with periodic sampling.
- **Development**: 100% inspection until promoted.

**Capacity Reservation Agreements.** For long-lead forgings and castings, pay a 10–15% retainer to reserve supplier capacity by quarter. Release firm build authorizations against reserved capacity as orders arrive, converting 6–8 week procurement cycles into 2–3 week authorization-to-ship cycles.

**Supply Base Rationalization.** Qualify near-shore or dual sources for the top 10 longest-lead items. Share 6-month rolling forecasts in quarterly joint business reviews so suppliers can level-load and pre-position raw material.

**Design for Supply Chain (DfSC).** Engineering will rationalize the BOM to favor commercially available components with <4 week lead times where functionally equivalent, eliminating sole-source designer parts that drive defensive quoting.

## QUALITY GATE PROTECTION STRATEGY

The prior warranty spike was caused by schedule pressure overriding process discipline. This plan institutionalizes the principle that **quality is the accelerator**, not the brake. Defect prevention at the source reduces the detective burden on certified gates, allowing them to function as rapid confirmations rather than lengthy tollgates.

### 5.1 Zero-Bypass Gate Architecture
All customer-mandated and regulatory certified inspections remain absolute. No gate may be eliminated, abbreviated, overridden, or informally “walked down.” This is codified as board-level policy.

### 5.2 Certified Inspection Pods: Proximity Without Compromise
To eliminate transport queue time without diluting inspection integrity:
- **Physical Standards:** Satellite inspection bays adjacent to flow cells are physically separated, climate-controlled, and vibration-isolated, meeting the same metrology standards as the central lab.
- **Re-Certification:** Pods are re-certified per ISO 17025 or customer source inspection requirements before operational use.
- **Inspector Independence:** Inspectors report **solid-line to the Quality Director**, not to the Value Stream Manager or Cell Lead. They rotate between pods on a schedule controlled by Quality, eliminating proximity bias and social entrapment.
- **Administrative Elimination:** MES auto-populates inspection packets (serial numbers, upstream SPC data, weld maps). Hardened fixtures and pre-loaded CMM programs replace subjective judgment, cutting administrative dead time by 50–70% without reducing technical scrutiny.

### 5.3 TAT Governance Protected from Production Pressure
The inspection turnaround clock starts only when the traveler is 100% complete and digitally pre-positioned. If Production arrives incomplete, the clock resets. Persistent TAT misses are remedied through neutral arbitration by the Lean Steering Committee to add inspector capacity or smooth upstream arrivals—**never** via a production priority override.

### 5.4 Warranty Immunization Protocol
| **Control** | **Specification** |
|---|---|
| **Spike FMEA Lock** | Update PFMEA and Control Plans to address the exact failure modes from the prior crisis. |
| **Poka-Yoke at Source** | Torque arms with digital capture, weld parameter interlocks, barcode-driven software loading. |
| **SPC with Automatic Stop** | Real-time monitoring of Critical-to-Quality variables; machine halts if Cpk drifts below 1.33. |
| **First Article / Process Qualification** | First unit of any new configuration or new cell layout undergoes 100% dimensional and functional validation before serial release. |
| **Layered Process Audits (LPAs)** | Daily supervisor, weekly manager, and monthly director audits using checklists derived from the Spike FMEA. |
| **Early Quality Indicator (EQI) Dashboard** | Track FPY, scrap, rework hours, test-stand retries, and NCR rate by product family; correlate quarterly to lagging warranty data for 4–8 week early warning. |
| **Quality Surge Protocol** | Increase inspection sample rates by 25% during the first 90 days of any process change. If EQI trends negative, trigger an immediate 2-week **Quality Surge Pause**: halt changes, root-correct, and resume only after Quality Director validation. |

## CROSS-FUNCTIONAL ALIGNMENT PLAYBOOK

Production, Quality, and Sales currently optimize conflicting local metrics. The framework below replaces siloed scorecards with value-stream ownership, backed by executive authority and shared economics.

### 6.1 Executive Authority: Chief Transformation Officer (CTO)
A **Chief Transformation Officer** (C-suite rank, reporting to the CEO) is appointed with **solid-line authority** over the program for 12 months. The CTO can break cross-functional deadlocks, override functional managers on value-stream prioritization, and shield the program from political erosion. After Month 12, authority transitions back to line leadership.

### 6.2 Value Stream Manager (VSM)
A VSM is appointed for the pilot Runner family with dotted-line execution authority over Production, Materials, and Planning personnel assigned to that family. The VSM owns the P&L proxy (cost, lead time, quality cost) and controls cell staffing and scheduling sequence. **Certified inspection personnel are explicitly excluded from VSM staffing authority**; their assignment, evaluation, and rotation remain exclusively under the Quality Director.

### 6.3 Shared Economics: The Iron Triangle
Thirty percent of annual variable compensation for Production, Quality, and Sales leadership is pooled. The pool pays out only on **simultaneous** improvement across all five metrics:

| **Metric** | **Weight** | **Definition** |
|---|---|---|
| Order-to-Delivery Lead Time | 25% | Weeks from sales order entry to customer receipt |
| First-Pass Yield (FPY) | 25% | % passing all gates without rework or scrap |
| Warranty Cost per Unit | 20% | 12-month rolling warranty spend divided by units shipped |
| Sales Forecast & Config Accuracy | 15% | % orders with zero post-freeze ECOs; forecast MAPE |
| On-Time-In-Full (OTIF) | 15% | % delivered complete by original customer promise date |

**Clawback Provision:** If warranty cost per unit spikes above baseline for two consecutive quarters, 50% of the shared pool is forfeited, regardless of lead-time performance.

### 6.4 Governance Cadence
- **Daily Velocity Stand-Up (15 minutes):** Cell leads, Quality inspector lead, Materials planner, Sales ops. Visual board; problems assigned before exit.
- **Weekly War Room (60 minutes):** Production, Quality, Sales, Planning, VSM. Agenda: APS adherence, buffer penetration, EQI trends, expedite log review, SLA status.
- **Monthly IBP/S&OP (Executive):** Unified 3-month demand/capacity plan; strategic buffer decisions; public disclosure of any expedite overrides.

### 6.5 Deal Desk & Shadow-Quoting Elimination
A Deal Desk, chaired by the VSM, pre-approves all ETO and >8-week quotes. Field sales verbal promises of non-standard configurations are **unbonded**—Production and Quality are not obligated to meet them. Sales commission is deferred until delivery with zero post-freeze ECOs, and an accelerator is applied to standard CTO configurations to reward clean quoting.

### 6.6 Neutral Arbitration
A **Lean Steering Committee** (chaired by the CTO with rotating senior reps from each function) convenes within 24 hours of any SLA dispute. It is empowered to allocate temporary resources and adjudicate good-faith violations without punitive escalation.

### 6.7 Joint Commitment SLAs
| **From** | **To** | **Commitment** |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales** | Production/Quality | 48-hour configuration freeze; no post-freeze changes without Change Board approval. |
| **Production** | Quality | WIP arrives at gate complete with MES-generated traveler, per APS schedule. |
| **Quality** | Production | Inspection TAT per standard; clock starts only on complete documentation. |
| **Planning** | Sales | ATP/CTP response to quote requests within 4 business hours. |

## IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP

The program proceeds through three capital-release gates. No architectural capital is deployed until empirical evidence validates the root-cause profile.

| **Phase** | **Duration** | **Focus** | **Key Deliverables** | **QLT Target** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 0: Baseline & Validate** | Weeks 1–8 | Timestamped VSM; forensic warranty FMEA; supplier reality audit; sales configuration audit; appoint CTO; halt unauthorized expedites. | Validated time-loss autopsy; updated Control Plans; supplier tiering map; Go/No-Go Gate 1 for capital release. | Hold 14 weeks |
| **Phase 1: Protect & Pilot** | Weeks 9–20 | Launch one Runner flow cell + ATO; deploy Certified Inspection Pod with independence protocol; APS live with finite inspection scheduling; 2 supplier VMI pilots; configurator v1.0; Quality Surge active. | Pilot family passes IQ/OQ/PQ; FPY ≥ baseline; 11–12 week performance; War Room ritual institutionalized; Go/No-Go Gate 2. | 11–12 weeks (pilot) |
| **Phase 2: Scale & Synchronize** | Weeks 21–36 | Scale to 2nd Runner family; ATO for top 20% SKUs; SMED at constraints; near-shore source qualification; inline inspection for all Runner products. | Two cells operational; rework <2%; buffer management tuned; EQI-to-warranty correlation confirmed. | 8–9 weeks |
| **Phase 3: Optimize & Entrench** | Weeks 37–52 | Full portfolio re-quoting; continuous Kaizen cadence; transition CTO authority back to line; win-rate tracking. | QLT 7–8 weeks; delivery-speed losses <10%; warranty cost flat or down. | 7–8 weeks |

**Capital Authorization Gates**
- **Gate 0 (Week 0):** Board approves Phase 0 budget ($50K–$100K) and CTO appointment.
- **Gate 1 (Week 8):** Board reviews validated time-loss autopsy; authorizes Phase 1 capital ($300K–$500K).
- **Gate 2 (Week 20):** Board reviews pilot family IQ/OQ/PQ results, FPY, and EQI stability; authorizes Phase 2–3 scale capital.

## EXPECTED OUTCOMES

**Lead Time.** Compression from 14 weeks to 7–8 weeks steady state—a 40–50% reduction—restoring competitive parity and expanding addressable market share.

**Commercial Performance.** Reduction in delivery-speed deal losses from 33% to less than 10%. Improved win rate and pricing power from reliable, defensible quotes generated by finite-capacity ATP/CTP logic.

**Quality Integrity.** Zero bypass events of certified gates. First-Pass Yield maintained or improved. Warranty cost per unit held flat or reduced through source-level defect prevention, poka-yoke, and SPC auto-stops.

**Operational Discipline.** Transition from infinite-capacity MRP chaos to deterministic finite scheduling with frozen zones. Sixty percent or more of volume flows through pre-validated CTO/ATO architecture. Supplier float collapsed via capacity reservation and VMI.

**Financial Return.** Program investment of $400K–$900K is offset by win-rate recovery and eliminated expedite costs. Working capital impact is neutralized by supplier consignment and reduced emergent freight.

**Organizational Health.** Replacement of the Production-Quality-Sales blame cycle with a unified value-stream governance model, shared economic incentives, and executive-backed arbitration.
