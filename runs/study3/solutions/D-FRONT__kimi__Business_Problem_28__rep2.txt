**TURNAROUND-OR-EXIT ASSESSMENT & BINDING PLAN**  
*Industrial Services Unit – Diversified Group*  
*Board Review Version | Final | 12-Month Horizon*

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

After three years of losses and two failed cost-led restructurings, the evidence is unambiguous: **the unit does not have a cost problem; it has a capital-and-revenue-architecture problem.** Prior restructurings addressed overhead while ignoring portfolio mix. The result was predictable—cuts slowed the cash bleed but could never restore profitability because **58% of the revenue base is structurally unprofitable** and consumes the surplus generated by the remaining 42%.

The unit is **insolvent in its current configuration**, not merely undermanaged.

**Board Resolution Required:** Approve a **Hard Portfolio Reset**—simultaneously executing a **contained turnaround of the Core** and an **active exit of the Non-Core**—with a binding decision gate at **Month 9**.

**The Month 9 Standard:** The Core must deliver:
1. **Two consecutive months of positive operating cash flow**;
2. **Trailing 90-day EBITDA margin ≥5%**; and
3. A **credible, funded path to 8% EBITDA by Month 18**.

If the Core fails any element of this standard, management will present a **binding exit recommendation** at the Month 10 Board meeting. No fourth restructuring will be entertained.

## 1. DIAGNOSIS: STRUCTURAL DEFECTS MASKED BY EXECUTION FAILURE

Prior restructurings failed because they treated symptoms (SG&A) while leaving the disease (portfolio mix and asset base). We diagnose **65% structural / 35% execution**.

### 1.1 Financial Baseline
| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Annual Revenue Run-Rate | $200 million |
| EBITDA Margin | −4% (−$8 million) |
| Monthly Cash Burn | ~$1.5 million |
| Cash on Hand | $8 million (~5.3 months runway) |
| Gross Margin | 18% (down from 28% three years ago) |
| Working Capital | $45 million (81 days) |
| Group G&A Allocation | $6 million/year |

### 1.2 Structural Screen: Five Tests (The 65%)
| Test | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross Margin Viability** | Gross margin at 18% sits below the 20–22% floor required to cover field-loaded SG&A and Group allocation. | **Structural.** Revenue quality has degraded; no amount of overhead reduction restores pricing power in commoditized lines. |
| **Fixed-Cost Rebasing Feasibility** | Revenue fell >30%, but fixed costs (depot network, fleet, salaried HSE/compliance staff) fell only ~12%. | **Structural.** The cost structure was built for a larger, diversified business model. Rebasing requires asset liquidation and lease exits that prior restructurings refused to touch. |
| **Demand Trajectory** | Open-market maintenance, staffing, and low-grade fabrication face secular share loss to non-union independents, in-sourcing, and digital labor platforms. Order books declining double-digits. | **Structural.** Execution cannot reverse a secular demand shift. |
| **Competitive Position** | The unit is **stuck in the middle**: lacking the scale/lean overhead of pure-play independents and the integrated pricing power of OEM-captive service arms. | **Structural.** Strategy, not management, created this positioning. |
| **Capital Intensity Mismatch** | Non-Core asset turns are <1.5x, generating negative economic profit after a 10% cost of capital charge. | **Structural.** These lines destroy Group value even at steady state. |

### 1.3 Execution Screen: Three Gaps (The 35%)
| Gap | Evidence | Fixability |
|---|---|---|
| **Cross-Sell Governance Failure** | Shared customers buy <15% of their services spend from this unit; Equipment/Chemicals divisions control relationships but face no penalty for outsourcing. | **Fixable in 90 days** via Group Account Protocol and cross-divisional compensation linkage. |
| **Talent Flight** | Best managers transferred out; remaining leadership is firefighting. Restructuring fatigue triggered a self-fulfilling decline. | **Fixable in 60 days** via targeted retention packages and Core-only leadership contracts. |
| **Bid Discipline Collapse** | Negative-margin project wins to fill capacity. No hurdle-rate governance or war-room reviews. | **Fixable in 30 days** via pricing authority centralization and bid kill-committees. |

### 1.4 Diagnostic Verdict
> **The prior restructurings addressed only the 35% execution layer.** Cutting SG&A in a business where 58% of revenue destroys capital is mathematically futile: the more Non-Core revenue retained, the more working capital and field overhead required to support it. A third round of across-the-board cuts would liquidate the Core’s human capital and strand the Group’s shared customers. The only viable path is to **shrink to grow**: reduce the revenue base to the segments that can carry the fixed cost, then rebase overhead to that smaller, higher-quality top line.

## 2. SEGMENT VIABILITY: PROFITABILITY RESTATEMENT

### 2.1 Segment Economic Map
Restated using **fully loaded** economics (cost-to-serve, working capital carrying cost, and a 10% capital charge):

| Segment | % of Revenue | Revenue ($M) | Gross Margin | Fully Loaded EBIT | ROCE | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. OEM-Captive Aftermarket** | 22% | $44 | 32–38% | +12–16% | >25% | **Core.** Warranty-mandated; infinite switching costs. Under-penetrated. |
| **B. High-Complexity Turnaround & Inspection** | 15% | $30 | 22–28% | +6–10% | 15–20% | **Core.** Certifications and safety IP create barriers. |
| **C. Digital Condition Monitoring** | 5% | $10 | 45–55% | +20–30% | >40% | **Core.** Recurring, asset-light, bundlable with Group equipment data. |
| **D. Reactive Break-Fix Maintenance** | 35% | $70 | 12–16% | −4 to −8% | Negative | **Non-Core.** Commoditized; local independents win on price. |
| **E. Technical Staffing / Augmentation** | 18% | $36 | 10–14% | −2 to −6% | Negative | **Non-Core.** Zero moat; staffing platforms outcompete. |
| **F. General Fabrication / Low-Grade Construction** | 5% | $10 | 8–12% | −6 to −10% | Negative | **Non-Core.** Competes with low-cost fabrication shops. |

**The Subsidization Math:** Segments A+B+C generate **~$11 million of fully loaded economic profit**. Segments D+E+F destroy **~$19 million**. The resulting −$8 million unit-wide EBITDA is not a cost problem; it is a **cross-subsidy problem**.

### 2.2 Core Operating Base Definition
- **Structural Core (A+B+C):** 42% of revenue (~$84M).
- **Convertible Non-Core:** An estimated 5–8% of total revenue (~$10–16M) within Segment D is tied to shared Group accounts that can be converted to bundled TCO contracts under the Group Account Protocol, lifting gross margin from 12–16% to 20–24%.
- **Total Core Operating Base:** **47–50% of current revenue** (~$94–100M).

### 2.3 Shared-Customer Value Chain
The Group’s profitable divisions represent a **captive demand pool** with quantifiable leakage:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total Group Accounts (>$50k product spend/year) | ~250 |
| Current Services Attach Rate | 15% of addressable services spend |
| Target Attach Rate | 40% within 24 months |
| Product Contract Renewal (with attached service) | 85% |
| Product Contract Renewal (without attached service) | 60% |

**Synergy Mechanism:** The Services unit must price at **arm’s-length gross margins of 20–24%**. The value capture occurs in the **product division P&L**: attached customers renew equipment and chemical contracts at materially higher rates. The Services unit’s role is to protect Group NPV, not to subsidize product sales.

### 2.4 Pro-Forma Breakeven Restatement
| Scenario | Revenue | Gross Profit | SG&A (Rebased) | EBITDA | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Current State** | $200M | $36M (18%) | $44M | **−$8M (−4%)** | Unsustainable. |
| **After Hard Reset (Core Only)** | $94–100M | $28–30M (30%) | $19–20M | **+$8–10M (+8–10%)** | Overhead rightsized; stranded costs eliminated or reabsorbed by Group. |
| **After Soft Reset (Cost Cut Only)** | $170M | $29M (17%) | $32M | **−$3M (−2%)** | Approximates prior failed restructurings. Fails. |

**Conclusion:** The unit is profitable at roughly **half its current scale** once Non-Core revenue and its attributable fixed cost are removed. The turnaround is a **reshape**, not a growth story.

## 4. EXIT ALTERNATIVES & TRANSACTION FRAMEWORK

If kill criteria are breached, the Board must select an exit path based on **Core separability** and **Group stranded-cost tolerance**.

### 4.1 Exit Path Decision Tree
| Core Condition | Recommended Path | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Core OCF positive; trailing EBITDA 3–5%; intact contracts | **Trade Sale** | Going-concern value exists; maximize recovery via competitive process. |
| Core OCF negative; no separable customer base; long-dated Non-Core contracts | **Managed Wind-Down** | Preserve cash; harvest working capital; avoid future liabilities. |
| Core OCF positive; strong mid-level managers; fast separation required | **MBO** | Speed and certainty trade off against valuation. |
| Cash runway <3 months; liabilities exceed going-concern value | **Immediate Liquidation** | Minimize further Group subsidy; protect shared customers via third-party pass-throughs. |

### 4.2 Exit Alternatives Ranked
| Rank | Alternative | Value Realization | Timeline | Stranded Cost Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Trade Sale of Core** (Strategic / PE) | **$28–42M** (4.0–6.0x normalized $7M Core EBITDA). | 6–9 months | Buyer assumes field ops; Group retains historical liability via 12–18 month TSA at arm’s-length fees (~$1.2M/year). |
| **2** | **Managed Wind-Down** | **$18–22M** (50–75% of recoverable net working capital). | 12–18 months | Group absorbs overhead progressively; target zero net cost by Month 18. |
| **3** | **Management Buyout (MBO)** | **$18–25M** (2.5–3.5x EBITDA). | 3–4 months | Seller financing or earnout to bridge value gap; Group retains environmental/warranty. |
| **4** | **Immediate Liquidation** | **$8–12M** (asset recovery minus exit costs). | 3–6 months | Group books one-time severance and lease termination; protects shared customers via third-party service pass-throughs. |

### 4.3 Separation Mechanics
- **Asset Partition:** Core fleet, customer contracts, and certifications transfer to sale vehicle. Legacy property and redundant plant remain with Group.
- **Liability Firewall:** Group retains asbestos, environmental, and pre-close warranty claims. Post-close operational liability follows the buyer/MBO team.
- **People:** TUPE/WARN obligations mapped by jurisdiction. Core technicians transfer with the business; Non-Core back-office absorbed or made redundant by Group.
- **Stranded Costs:** The Group must pre-allocate $4–5M of shared overhead (IT, finance, HSE) to the unit or negotiate a TSA. Without this, any sale valuation is discounted by 1.0–1.5x EBITDA.

## 5. DECISION CRITERIA: TURNAROUND VS. EXIT SCORECARD

At each monthly Board review, score the unit against five dimensions. Scoring is **pass/fail at the gate**, not an average.

| Dimension | **Green (Continue)** | **Red (Trigger Exit)** | **Weight** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Core Unit Economics** | Core generates ≥5% trailing EBITDA and positive OCF by Month 9. | No segment covers fully loaded cost of capital after 10% charge by Month 9. | **Critical** |
| **2. Shared-Customer Synergy** | >25% of Core revenue tied to Group divisions; product division churn risk is material. | <10% overlap; shared customers easily redirected to third parties without Group NPV loss. | High |
| **3. Management & Talent** | Core leadership intact; <5% voluntary attrition; credible Phase 2 team identified. | Irreparable talent flight; no internal candidate capable of running Core standalone. | High |
| **4. Cash Cost to Fix** | Cumulative cash required to reach breakeven ≤$20M (≤0.2x Core annual revenue of ~$95–100M). | Open-ended funding; cumulative burn projected >$25M without certainty. | High |
| **5. Market Trajectory** | Core order book expanding QoQ; end-markets stable or growing. | Secular decline; pricing pressure accelerating; commoditization inevitable. | Medium |

### 5.1 Decision Rules
- **Unanimous Green (all 5):** Approve Phase 2 turnaround investment ($8M Y2 capital ask).
- **Any Red on Dimension 1 (Core Economics):** **Automatic exit.** No exceptions.
- **≥2 Reds on Dimensions 2–5:** Automatic exit.
- **Amber / Mixed (1 Red on Dimensions 2–5):** Conditional 90-day extension with tightened milestones and reduced funding cap. Second Red during extension triggers immediate exit.

### 5.2 Month 10 Board Resolution Template
> *“RESOLVED: The Board approves [Turnaround / Exit] for the Industrial Services Unit based on Month 9 evidence. If Turnaround: the CEO is authorized to invest up to $8M in Phase 2 subject to quarterly review.
