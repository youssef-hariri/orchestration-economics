# REPOSITIONING STRATEGY FOR 70-YEAR HERITAGE BRAND
## Board-Ready Execution Document

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This 70-year heritage brand faces a structural revenue inflection: defend a shrinking 55+ base (65–75% of revenue, declining 2–3% annually) or build a genuine franchise with under-35 audiences (currently <10% of revenue). Previous modernization campaigns failed because they promised change in tone while leaving product, price, and distribution unchanged—a mismatch audiences instantly detected as inauthentic.

**This strategy resolves that failure through three integrated mechanisms:**

1. **Proof-Before-Promise:** All product, packaging, distribution, and pricing changes must exist and be verifiable before communication launches. No marketing claim without evidence.

2. **Asymmetric Messaging to Four Segments:** Deploy distinct messages rooted in the same durable truth through different channels and tonalities—not two brands, but one brand experienced differently by different audiences.

3. **Format Variety, Not Price Tiering:** Expand product availability through multiple formats (travel, bulk, refillable, occasion-specific) at core price points in all retail channels. Never introduce premium tiers that demote the original product.

**16-month execution roadmap** with clear advance criteria and rollback triggers. **Internal governance structure** with named decision authority.

**Outcome targets (Year 3):**
- Heritage Loyalist retention: ≥95% (flat revenue, <5% churn acceleration)
- Under-35 penetration: 12–15% of revenue (growth from current 5–10%)
- Lapsed Mid-Life User re-acquisition: 8–12% of new volume
- Blended NPS: +5–10 points driven by authenticity and transparency perception

## 1. THE REPOSITIONING

### Positioning Statement

**"Proven Through Time, Transparent in Reality: The Brand That Refused to Cheapen What Works"**

**Why This Works for Both Cohorts:**
- Heritage Loyalists hear: "You were right to choose us. We remain proven and consistent."
- Lapsed/Curious Under-35s hear: "This brand makes choices based on values, not margins. Here's the proof."

### Strategic Idea

**One Core Truth, Four Different Audiences**

The brand's heritage rests on a single uncompromising principle: in an era when competitors cut costs and cheat quality, we refused. This was true 70 years ago and remains true today. But it reads differently to different audiences:

- **Heritage Loyalists (55+):** "You proved this to us decades ago. We're grateful you haven't changed."
- **Lapsed Mid-Life Users (35–54):** "You remember using this. Here's why we still matter—and why you should return."
- **Curious Discoverers (under-35):** "You want proof, not theater. Here it is, unfiltered."
- **Values Skeptics (under-35):** "You're right to be skeptical. Here's what we've actually changed, what we haven't, and why."

### From → To: Meaning Shift Table

| What Changed | Old Meaning | New Meaning | How We Communicate It |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"Been around 70 years"** | "Reliable, I know it works" | "Refused to trend-chase; stands for something" | Lead with the *why* (values), not just the *that* (duration). Show specific choices the brand made |
| **"Available everywhere"** | "Convenient for my shopping habits" | "Accessible, anti-gatekeeping, egalitarian" | Emphasize choice and price consistency across channels—never apologize for mass distribution |
| **"Unchanged formula"** | "No surprises; I know what I'm getting" | "Refuses to cheapen for margins; that's integrity" | Explain the *choice* to not reformulate. Show competitor reformulations side-by-side |
| **"Good price"** | "Affordable, budget-friendly" | "Premium quality at accessible price; won't inflate" | Separate "price" from "cheap." Position as "price integrity" (holding the line against category inflation) |
| **"Used for decades"** | "My parents' brand; I inherited it" | "Cult following among pragmatists; proven in real life" | Shift from "inherited" to "discovered by people who care." Show testimonials from all ages |
| **"No marketing excess"** | "Simple, no-frills" | "Authentic, anti-corporate theater, substantive" | Lean into the *absence* of marketing as a feature. Explain why we spend on product, not ads |

## 2. PRESERVE / EVOLVE / RETIRE / INTRODUCE: COMPLETE ELEMENT MATRIX

### Decision Framework

**PRESERVE** (Sacred)
- Recognition equity built over 70 years
- Low dated-ness signal
- Anchors Heritage Loyalists OR bridges to new cohorts
- Example: logo mark, core product, retail presence in mass channels, core price

**EVOLVE** (Modernizable)
- Recognition equity exists
- High dated-ness signal but modernizable without losing code
- Example: logo rendering, color palette, packaging graphics, tone of voice

**RETIRE** (Expendable)
- Low recognition equity OR high dated-ness
- No bridge to new cohorts
- Graceful exit mechanism exists
- Example: generic tagline, dated mascot

**INTRODUCE** (Enable Discovery)
- No cannibalization of sacred elements
- Enables new cohorts to discover the brand
- Reinforces the new positioning
- Example: new product formats, DTC, specialty retail, owned content

## 3. PROOF BEFORE PROMISE: What Must Exist First

### Core Principle

**No marketing claim without evidence.** All product, packaging, distribution, pricing, and experience changes must exist and be verifiable before communication runs.

**Change Sequence:** Product proof → Packaging proof → Distribution proof → Pricing proof → Communication

---

### Phase 0: Proof Construction (Months 1–4)
*No external communication. No campaign. No announcement.*

#### Workstream 1: Product Proof (Weeks 1–12)

**Week 1–2:** Audit internal formulation records
- Confirm: Has formulation changed since [founding year]? If yes, document when and why
- Create internal "Formulation Commitment Statement": "Unchanged since [year]. Next review: [date]." Or if changed: "Changed in [year] for [reason]: [specifics]."

**Week 3–4:** Commission third-party lab testing
- Identify independent lab (not in-house, not competitor)
- Brief scope: Efficacy vs. 2–3 competitors, ingredient safety, durability, environmental impact
- Cost: $15–50K depending on scope
- Timeline: 8–12 weeks (continues into Month 3–4)

**Week 5–6:** Audit ingredients
- For each ingredient: sourcing, safety certifications, any controversies
- Document: Why we use this, where sourced, certifications, alternatives considered

**Week 9–10:** Loyalty perception test (50+ long-term customers)
- "If we publish third-party testing showing this product works, does that make you more or less confident?"
- Target: >80% "more confident" or "no change"; <5% "less confident"

**Advance Criterion:** >80% of loyalists perceive third-party testing as confidence-building
**Rollback Trigger:** If >15% worry testing will reveal problems, pause messaging and communicate: "We test because we're confident. Transparency is our commitment to you."

---

#### Workstream 2: Packaging Proof (Weeks 1–16)

**Week 1–2:** Design exploration
- Three directions: (A) Minimal evolution, (B) Moderate evolution, (C) Bold evolution

**Week 3–4:** Loyalist perception test (100 customers)
- Mock shelf with old and new side-by-side
- Measure: (1) Recognition >90%, (2) Quality perception >70%, (3) Authenticity >60%, (4) Trial intent improvement >20%

**Week 5–6:** Refine based on test; select single direction

**Week 7–8:** Finalize design and photography
- Integrate proof callouts ("Third-Party Tested," QR codes for ingredient transparency, core benefit clearly stated)

**Week 9–16:** Production setup and compliance review

**Advance Criterion:** Loyalist recognition >85%, under-35 "contemporary" perception >70%, retail shelf mock-up +15% differentiation
**Rollback Trigger:** If recognition <80% or under-35 "trustworthy" <55%, redesign before Phase 1 launch

---

#### Workstream 3: Distribution & Pricing Proof (Weeks 1–8)

**Week 1–2:** Audit current distribution
- What % of mass retail locations carry brand?
- Pricing consistency across channels (CVS, Walmart, Target, etc.)

**Week 3–4:** Specialty retail partnership identification
- Identify 3–5 potential partners aligned with positioning
- Approach and initial pitch

**Week 5–8:** Verify pricing stability
- Confirm core product price unchanged for past 12 months
- Baseline for "no increases for 18 months" commitment

**Advance Criterion:** Pricing consistency verified; <5% variance across channels. Distribution in >85% of baseline locations
**Rollback Trigger:** If inconsistent pricing found, resolve before messaging launch (retailers must agree to price parity)

---

#### Workstream 4: Efficacy Test Contingency Planning (Weeks 1–2)

**Critical Pre-Emptive Decision (Month 1, before lab testing):**

"If efficacy testing reveals product is below competitive standard on [metric], we will [A/B/C]:"
- **Option A:** Reformulate and delay campaign 6 months
- **Option B:** Reposition (retire "uncompromising standards" claim; shift to alternative truth)
- **Option C:** Kill campaign and return to status quo

**Decision must be made BEFORE testing, not after.**

Assign executive sponsor (CMO or CEO) to decide now, document decision, and hold firm.

---

### Content Pillars & Messaging Guardrails

#### **Pillar 1: "Why We're Uncompromising" (2–3 videos/month)**
**What it is:** Stories of specific choices the brand made to refuse cost-cutting
**NOT:** Vague brand heritage stories ("Our founder believed in quality")
**YES:** "In 1987, ingredient X cost rose 30%. Competitors reformulated. We didn't. Here's the data."

**Audience:** All cohorts (speaks to different reasons: Loyalists → proof of constancy; Under-35 → proof of anti-race-to-bottom)

#### **Pillar 2: "Proof" (1–2 videos/month)**
**What it is:** Third-party efficacy testing, sourcing transparency, ingredient deep-dives, environmental impact
**NOT:** Claimed benefits without evidence
**YES:** "Here's the third-party lab report. Here's what it shows. Here's what remains unclear."

**Audience:** Curious Discoverers, Values Skeptics (evidence-first)

#### **Pillar 3: "Ritual & Community" (1–2 videos/month)**
**What it is:** Customer stories, long-term users, testimonials showing real usage (not lifestyle imagery)
**NOT:** Polished testimonials or paid case studies
**YES:** "Here's Sarah. She's used this for 30 years. Here's why she stays."

**Audience:** Lapsed Mid-Life Users (permission to return), Heritage Loyalists (community validation)

#### **Pillar 4: "Category Reality" (1 video/month)**
**What it is:** Honest take on what's happening in category (competitor cost-cutting, marketing inflation, trend-chasing)
**NOT:** Positive-only brand content
**YES:** "Here's what the category did in 2023. Here's why we didn't follow. Here's the tradeoff."

**Audience:** All cohorts (transparency + category authority)

---

### Tone-of-Voice Specification

#### DO ✓

- "This costs more than Brand X because we use [specific ingredient]. Here's the cost difference. You might not care—buy Brand X. If you do care, here's why we chose this."
- "We tested this against [competitor]. Here's where we win, here's where they win. Depends what you need."
- "Our customer Sarah used this for 8 years. She switched to [competitor] last year. We asked her why. She said [honest reason]. We haven't solved that yet."
- "Here's what the research shows. Here's what remains unknown. Buy accordingly."
- "This doesn't cure [medical condition]. If you're looking for that, see a doctor. This does [specific benefit]."

#### DON'T ✗

- "Not your grandma's [brand]" (explicitly rejects grandmas as audience)
- "Let's unpack the science of [ingredient]" (corporate-pedagogical tone)
- "POV: You're tired of [category complaint]" (trend-speak, performative)
- "We're obsessed with [ingredient]" (brand enthusiasm, not peer perspective)
- "Here's the tea ☕" (slang appropriation)
- "Premium quality," "trusted by millions," superlatives without proof
- Any claim of "authenticity" or "honesty" (show it; never claim it)
- Influencer-style "unboxing" or product hero shots (too polished)
- Meme-jacking or trending sounds disconnected from brand truth
- Lifestyle imagery (family, romance, aspirational contexts)

#### Approval Gate for All Marketing Content
**Read-aloud test by outsider (not on creative team):**
- If it sounds like someone performing authenticity (pauses for effect, uses air quotes mentally), reject it
- If it contains language from DO NOT list, reject it
- If it makes a claim without proof link, reject it

---

### Creator Partnership Criteria (REJECTED for Year 1)

**Strategy eliminates paid creator partnerships entirely in Year 1 to maintain authenticity.**

**Instead:** Organic Sampling Program
- Identify creators who've *already mentioned brand* organically (Reddit, TrustPilot, Amazon, social listening)
- Reach out: "We saw you mention our product. We'd love to send you [new product/format]. No obligation to post. If you do, just keep it honest."
- Ship product with no brand brief, no message discipline
- If they post, it's organic mention (not paid), hence credible
- Track: Do organic mentions improve in volume and positive sentiment?

**Why this works:**
- Organic mention from existing user = credible (no money exchanged)
- Under-35s trained to detect paid partnerships; they'll trust organic mentions
- Lower cost than paying creators
- Authenticity is preserved (no performance of authenticity)

**Advance Criterion (Month 6):** 100–200 organic brand mentions/month
**If this doesn't materialize:** Option to reconsider micro-creator sampling ($1–3K per video, no creative direction, require honest reviews including caveats) in Phase 2
