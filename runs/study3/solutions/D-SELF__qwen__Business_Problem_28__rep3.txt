**CONFIDENTIAL BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Lead Corporate Restructuring Officer & Chief Strategy Officer  
**DATE:** October 24, 2023  
**SUBJECT:** Turnaround-or-Exit Assessment and 1-Year Strategic Plan for Industrial-Services Unit (ISU)  

---

### **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

The Industrial-Services Unit (ISU) has suffered three consecutive years of EBITDA and cash-flow losses despite two prior cost-reduction restructurings. The failure of these interventions to halt the financial bleeding confirms a critical thesis: **the ISU’s distress is not an operational efficiency problem; it is a structural portfolio and commercial alignment problem.** The Group cannot cut its way to profitability when the underlying revenue mix and market positioning are fundamentally broken.

This assessment diagnoses the root causes of value destruction, identifies actionable pockets of profitability using activity-based economics, and outlines an aggressive 1-year turnaround plan. Crucially, it establishes a hard, quantitative **Day-180 Go/No-Go Gate**. If the unit cannot prove its standalone viability or strategic synergy by Month 6, the Board will immediately pivot to a structured exit, ensuring capital is not deployed toward a structurally flawed asset.

---

### **PART 1: DIAGNOSTIC ASSESSMENT – STRUCTURAL VS. EXECUTION DRIVERS**

Prior restructurings failed because they treated symptoms (high SG&A) rather than the disease (misaligned portfolio and poor unit economics). We have bifurcated the root causes into Structural (requiring business model changes) and Execution (requiring operational discipline).

#### **1. Structural Drivers (Root Causes of Value Destruction)**
*   **The "Captive Customer" Pricing Trap:** The ISU serves customers who also buy from the Group’s profitable divisions. These overlapping customers are using the ISU as a pricing lever, extracting commoditized, low-margin pricing by leveraging the "threat" of taking their highly profitable division business elsewhere. The ISU is effectively subsidizing the Group’s broader market share at the expense of its own margins.
*   **Portfolio Misalignment (The Mix Problem):** The order book is shrinking because the ISU is over-indexed in low-margin, reactive "break-fix" labor, while under-indexed in high-margin, predictive/managed services. The market is shifting toward outcome-based contracts, rendering the ISU’s legacy service catalog obsolete.
*   **The Overhead Allocation Illusion:** The ISU’s P&L is burdened with arbitrary corporate overhead allocations. This masks the true cash-generating ability of the underlying field operations and creates a false narrative of insolvency.

#### **2. Execution Drivers (The Operational Drag)**
*   **Poor Field Utilization & Cost-to-Serve:** Analysis indicates sub-optimal technician routing, high idle time, and low "first-time fix" rates. This drives up the direct cost of sales, destroying gross margins even on well-priced contracts.
*   **Commercial Execution Failure:** The sales team lacks the mandate, training, or collateral to transition customers from time-and-materials billing to value-based or tiered SLA pricing. 
*   **Leadership Deficit (Lagging Indicator):** The brain drain of top managers is not the cause of the decline; it is the result of high-performing leaders recognizing the structural flaws and realizing they lack the mandate to fix a broken operating model.

---

### **PART 2: PORTFOLIO RATIONALIZATION & PROFITABLE SEGMENTS**

To identify realistic, data-driven profitable segments, we must abandon traditional absorption costing and utilize **Activity-Based Costing (ABC)** to determine the true contribution margin of every service line and customer. 

We will segment the portfolio using a **Contribution Margin vs. Strategic Synergy** matrix:

**1. "Core & Scale" (High Margin, High Strategic Synergy)**
*   **Profile:** Advanced/predictive maintenance services sold to overlapping customers who also buy from profitable divisions.
*   **Data Metric:** >20% Contribution Margin; Cross-sell index > 2.
*   **Action:** **Protect and Bundle.** Integrate ISU sales with divisional sales. Lock these customers into 3-to-5-year outcome-based contracts. This is the future core of the business.

**2. "Cash Cows" (High Margin, Low Strategic Synergy)**
*   **Profile:** Niche, highly specialized technical services where the ISU holds strong pricing power, but the customer does not buy from other Group divisions.
*   **Data Metric:** >25% Contribution Margin; Cross-sell index < 1.
*   **Action:** **Milk for Cash.** Minimize CapEx and overhead. Use the cash generated here to fund the turnaround of the "Core" segment. 

**3. "Strategic Loss-Makers" (Low Margin, High Strategic Synergy)**
*   **Profile:** Basic, commoditized services (e.g., standard inspections) bought by the Group's most important profitable-division clients.
*   **Data Metric:** <5% Contribution Margin; Cross-sell index > 3.
*   **Action:** **Reprice or Carve-In.** Immediately reprice these services to market rates. If the customer refuses, the Group must decide if the lost volume is worth the margin sacrifice. If kept, remove these services from the ISU P&L and absorb them into the profitable divisions as a strategic loss-leader.

**4. "Value Destroyers" (Low Margin, Low Strategic Synergy)**
*   **Profile:** Legacy reactive services, highly customized low-volume work, and non-overlapping customers who demand deep discounts and strain field resources.
*   **Data Metric:** <5% Contribution Margin; Cross-sell index < 1.
*   **Action:** **Immediate Exit.** Issue 60-day non-renewal notices. Drop these accounts to instantly free up field capacity and management bandwidth.

### **PART 3: THE 1-YEAR TURNAROUND PLAN**

This plan operates on a dual-track mandate: execute a severe operational pivot while simultaneously preparing the unit for a potential sale.

#### **Phase 1: Triage & Cash Preservation (Days 1–90)**
*   **Talent Ring-Fencing:** Deploy a targeted, milestone-based retention pool for the top 15% of critical technical and commercial leaders. Crucially, this will include a specific **"transaction success bonus"** to align management incentives and prevent brain drain during exit preparation.
*   **Financial Ring-Fencing:** In Week 1, the CFO will mandate and lock a **"standalone cost baseline"** for the ISU, preventing corporate shared-service centers from artificially shifting costs back to the unit.
*   **Portfolio Pruning:** Execute non-renewals for the bottom 20% of unprofitable customers (Value Destroyers). 
*   **Cash Conservation:** Institute a zero-based CapEx freeze. Renegotiate payment terms with the top 20 suppliers to extend Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) by 15 days.
*   **Milestone 1 (Day 90):** 
    *   *Financial:* Reduce weekly cash burn by 40%.
    *   *Operational:* Increase field technician utilization rate from 65% to 80%.
    *   *Commercial:* Halt the decline in the bid-hit ratio.

#### **Phase 2: Commercial Realignment (Days 91–180)**
*   **Unified Key Account Management:** Launch joint-sales pods (ISU + Profitable Divisions) for the top 50 overlapping clients. 
*   **Pricing Overhaul:** Implement a strict pricing floor. Transition 50% of the renewing book from fixed-fee to outcome-based or tiered SLA pricing.
*   **Milestone 2 (Day 180) - THE HARD GO/NO-GO GATE:** (See Part 5). The Board will evaluate quantitative thresholds to either fully fund the final 6 months or initiate exit protocols.

#### **Phase 3: Operational Optimization (Days 181–270)** *(Assuming GO decision)*
*   **Field Digitization:** Deploy dynamic routing and field-service management software to reduce windshield time and improve first-time fix rates, driving down the direct cost-to-serve.
*   **Supply Chain Rationalization:** Consolidate the vendor base for parts and materials to capture volume discounts, improving gross margins.
*   **Milestone 3 (Day 270):** 
    *   *Financial:* Achieve positive run-rate Operating Cash Flow.
    *   *Operational:* Reduce cost-to-serve per ticket by 15%.

#### **Phase 4: Value Realization (Days 271–360)**
*   **Cultural & Performance Reset:** Roll out a new compensation model heavily weighted toward contribution margin and cash collection, rather than top-line revenue.
*   **Transaction Execution:** Finalize the exit transaction (if divestment was chosen) or transition the unit to standard Group reporting (if turnaround succeeded).
*   **Milestone 4 (Day 360):** Deliver a sustained 8-12% EBITDA margin. Complete exit OR achieve standalone cash-flow breakeven.

### **PART 4: EXIT ALTERNATIVES**

If the Day-180 Gate dictates an exit, or if the turnaround ultimately fails, the Board will evaluate the following alternatives, ranked by optimal risk-adjusted value realization:

1. **Internal Absorption / "Carve-In" (Highest Strategic Value, Lowest Cash Value)** 
   *   *Mechanism:* Dismantle the ISU. Absorb the "Core & Scale" service lines and overlapping customers directly into the profitable divisions. Sell or liquidate the "Cash Cows" and "Value Destroyers."
   *   *Pros:* Retains strategic customer lock-in; eliminates unit-level overhead; protects Group brand.
   *   *Cons:* No cash inflow; high risk of cultural clash and integration disruption within the profitable divisions.
2. **Trade Sale / Full Carve-Out (Highest Cash Value)**
   *   *Mechanism:* Sell the entire ISU (post-portfolio pruning) to a larger regional or national industrial services competitor or a strategic Private Equity buyer.
   *   *Pros:* Maximizes financial return; buyer can realize synergies the Group cannot; clean break.
   *   *Cons:* Takes 6-9 months to execute; requires high management bandwidth; risk of losing key managers during the process.
3. **Strategic Joint Venture / Minority Carve-Out (Balanced Risk/Return)**
   *   *Mechanism:* Sell a 49% minority stake to a strategic industry partner or mid-market PE firm, retaining 51% to maintain strategic customer access.
   *   *Pros:* Brings in outside capital and operational expertise; retains Group synergies; shares the downside risk.
   *   *Cons:* Complex governance; requires finding a partner willing to invest in a distressed asset.
4. **Management Buyout (MBO)**
   *   *Mechanism:* Sell to the current management team backed by a PE firm.
   *   *Pros:* Ensures continuity of customer relationships.
   *   *Cons:* Highly dependent on the very managers who are currently fleeing; PE will demand a steep discount due to the unit's poor financial history.
5. **Orderly Run-Off / Liquidation (Last Resort)**
   *   *Mechanism:* Fulfill existing backlog, halt all new bidding, sell off hard assets (vehicles, equipment, IP), and terminate staff.
   *   *Pros:* Absolute certainty; stops cash bleed immediately.
   *   *Cons:* Destroys customer relationships; yields lowest financial return; incurs severe severance and contract-breakage costs.

---

### **PART 5: DECISION CRITERIA – THE DAY-180 GO/NO-GO GATE**

At **Day 180**, the Board will evaluate the ISU against the following strict, quantitative thresholds. The decision is binary. If the ISU fails to meet the "GO" thresholds in at least three of the four categories, the Board will immediately pivot to the "NO-GO" (Exit) path.

| Evaluation Category | Criteria for CONTINUING Turnaround (GO) | Criteria for PIVOTING to Exit (NO-GO) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Cash & Margin Trajectory** | Weekly operating cash flow is strictly positive. Run-rate EBITDA margin has improved by >300 bps from baseline. | Cash burn continues or is only temporarily masked by working capital manipulation. Margin improvement is flat. |
| **2. Commercial Execution** | Bid-hit ratio has stabilized or improved. >50% of the renewing book has successfully transitioned to new, higher-margin pricing models. | Bid-hit ratio continues to drop. Sales team is unable to enforce new pricing; customers are churning due to rate increases. |
| **3. Operational Efficiency** | Field technician utilization rate has increased by >10%. Cost-to-serve per ticket has decreased by >10%. | Utilization remains flat. Field costs remain bloated due to poor routing or low first-time fix rates. |
| **4. Strategic Synergy** | Joint-sales pods have generated at least **$5.0 million** in new bundled pipeline revenue from overlapping clients. | Profitable divisions view the ISU as a distraction. Cross-selling yields zero material results. |

*Note: The CFO will also provide a "Market Valuation Check" at Day 180. If preliminary M&A advisor indications suggest a Trade Sale valuation exceeds the Net Present Value (NPV) of the projected turnaround cash flows, the Board will default to a NO-GO decision, regardless of operational metrics.*

---

### **CONCLUSION & IMMEDIATE NEXT STEPS**

The Industrial-Services Unit cannot be saved by cutting costs; it must be saved by changing *what* it sells, *who* it sells to, and *how* it prices. The previous restructurings failed because they treated a revenue-mix and strategic alignment problem as an operational efficiency problem. 

**Immediate Next Steps for the Board (Next 7 Days):**

1. **Approve Phase 1 Mandates:** Authorize the targeted retention pool (including the transaction success bonus) and the immediate pruning of the bottom 20% of unprofitable customers.
2. **Establish the Joint Steering Committee:** Mandate the CEOs of the profitable divisions to co-chair a commercial integration committee with the ISU Head to drive cross-selling.
3. **Lock the Financial Baseline:** Direct the CFO to establish and freeze the ISU standalone cost baseline in Week 1 to prevent corporate overhead creep.
4. **Initiate Plan B Valuation:** Mandate the CFO to begin confidential, preliminary outreach to 2-3 M&A advisory firms to gauge trade-sale valuation. The Board must have a realistic "Plan B" number in hand before the Day-180 decision gate.

By enforcing a strict, quantitative Day-180 Go/No-Go gate, the Board ensures that capital is not thrown after a structurally flawed business, while simultaneously giving a highly focused, commercially realigned unit the best possible chance to survive and thrive.
