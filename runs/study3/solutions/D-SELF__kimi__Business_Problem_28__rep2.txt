## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Industrial-Services Unit (ISU) is not experiencing a temporary operational lapse. It is a **structurally impaired portfolio** that has destroyed value for three consecutive years because prior restructurings cut overhead while preserving toxic revenue. The evidence is unambiguous: **45% of the business operates at negative unit economics** that no volume or efficiency can cure; internal conflict with profitable group divisions suppresses pricing on the **30% that is genuinely defensible**; and the departure of top managers signals that the organization itself has lost faith.

**The only credible path is a shrink-to-grow reset:** exit the bottom 45% of revenue immediately, reprice a marginal 25%, and concentrate all resources on the core 30% where ISU possesses captive access to group equipment platforms. If this core can expand, the unit can generate run-rate breakeven on roughly 55% of its current top line. If it cannot, there is no alternative worth funding.

We ask the board to approve an **$8 million turnaround investment cap** and a **single, irreversible hard gate at Month 9 (September 30)**. A Month 6 checkpoint will serve only as an early-warning trigger to accelerate exit preparation; it is not a final verdict. June 30 is too early to judge core growth momentum, which requires 90–120 days post-restructuring to materialize. September 30 provides the final proof point while preserving Q4 for clean exit execution.

By December 31, the board will have one of two outcomes: **(1) a self-sustaining, profitable core** with a validated forward plan, or **(2) a signed binding exit transaction** for the core and a board-authorized wind-down of the remainder. There is no status quo.
