# Industrial-Services Unit  
## Board Turnaround-or-Exit Report

**Status:** For board approval of a capped diagnostic and stabilization phase  
**Decision horizon:** Final retain, reduced-core, or exit decision by day 365  
**Important:** Financial thresholds and funding amounts marked **[TBD]** require validation by day 30. No unvalidated assumption should be treated as a forecast or commitment.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The unit has lost money for three consecutive years despite two restructuring programs that reduced costs without restoring profitability. Its order book is shrinking, experienced managers are transferring out, and some customers also purchase from profitable group divisions.

The evidence supports a **mixed structural-and-execution diagnosis**, not a conclusion that the entire business is irrecoverable:

- **Structural issues** may include weak pricing power, unattractive service lines, excess capacity, insufficient scale, declining demand, and loss-making contracts.
- **Execution issues** may include poor estimating, margin leakage, weak project controls, under-recovered change orders, low utilization, rework, and management attrition.
- The aggregate result may conceal a smaller profitable core, but this has not yet been demonstrated.
- Cross-group customer relationships may have strategic value, but any benefit must be independently quantified and compared with transfer, outsourcing, or other alternatives.

### Recommended path

Approve a **capped 90-day stabilization and fact-validation program**, with parallel exit readiness. Do not approve an open-ended one-year turnaround budget at this stage.

Continue beyond day 90 only if management demonstrates that:

1. A defined viable core exists or has a quantified, credible route to viability.
2. The core can cover its retained fixed-cost, working-capital, maintenance-capital, and risk burden.
3. Positive cash break-even by month 9 is achievable without unsupported volume growth.
4. Risk-adjusted backlog and pipeline support the reduced cost base.
5. Critical management and technical capability is available or replaceable.
6. Group-customer value is evidenced and cannot be preserved more economically through transfer or outsourcing.
7. No material contract, liability, safety, environmental, or liquidity issue invalidates the case.
8. The risk-adjusted value of turnaround exceeds the best feasible exit alternative.
9. Maximum funding exposure and liquidity limits are approved.

If a mandatory condition fails, the board should initiate the best available transfer, sale, outsourcing, partnership, or wind-down route rather than authorize a third broad restructuring.

## 3. Preliminary diagnosis

### Structural indicators to test

The losses should be treated as structural where, after reasonable execution correction:

- Contribution remains negative by service line or customer segment.
- Demand is declining or the market is chronically oversupplied.
- Customers resist price increases and services are readily substitutable.
- The unit lacks scale or carries excess fixed capacity.
- Required assets, certifications, or specialist labor cannot be supported by demand.
- The backlog is predominantly low-margin or high-risk.
- Recovery depends on unsupported volume growth.

### Execution indicators to test

The losses should be treated as execution-driven where:

- Jobs are profitable at award but loss-making in delivery.
- Losses are concentrated in particular branches, contracts, managers, or project types.
- Actual labor, overtime, subcontractor, rework, or claims costs exceed estimates.
- Change orders are not captured, billed, or collected.
- Utilization is below credible break-even levels.
- Discounting or scope creep reduces realized margins.
- A meaningful subset of customers or service lines already generates acceptable contribution.

### Required loss-causation bridge

For each material loss-making contract:

\[
\text{Actual loss}
=
\text{pricing gap}
+\text{volume/mix effect}
+\text{productivity variance}
+\text{materials/subcontractor variance}
+\text{schedule variance}
+\text{change-order leakage}
+\text{claims/rework}
+\text{capacity or allocation burden}
\]

Each component must be classified as:

- Recoverable within 12 months.
- Recurring but not recoverable.
- Nonrecurring.
- Unvalidated.

The likely outcome is a **mixed portfolio**: a smaller viable core, activities requiring repricing or operational repair, and activities that should be harvested, transferred, outsourced, or exited.

## 5. Profitability and viable-core framework

The correct unit of analysis is:

> **Customer × service line × contract/job × geography × delivery model**

### Required profitability views

1. **Management contribution view** — used for job, customer, and service-line decisions.
2. **Standalone economic view** — includes resources required to operate the retained perimeter.
3. **Group cash view** — includes transferred costs, stranded costs, residual liabilities, and group-wide effects.

### Contribution waterfall

\[
\text{Revenue}
-\text{direct labor}
-\text{materials}
-\text{subcontractors}
-\text{equipment}
-\text{travel and mobilization}
-\text{rework, warranty, claims, and penalties}
-\text{unrecovered change orders}
=\text{Job contribution}
\]

Then deduct:

- Avoidable branch or service-line costs.
- Dedicated management and support.
- Required maintenance capital.
- Incremental working capital.
- Retained infrastructure and residual costs.

A contribution-positive activity is not necessarily standalone profitable or portfolio-profitable.

### Portfolio classification

| Classification | Definition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Invest/grow | Positive contribution, defensible demand, acceptable cash conversion | Prioritize capacity and sales |
| Fix/selectively retain | Viable after defined pricing or execution actions | Set owner, action, deadline, and cash target |
| Harvest/reprice | Existing relationship has value but new work is unattractive | Reprice, narrow scope, or require approval |
| Exit/transfer | Structurally negative, high-risk, or nonstrategic | Stop bidding, complete, sell, transfer, outsource, or wind down |

### Viable-core tests

A retained activity must demonstrate:

- Positive contribution after direct delivery, rework, claims, travel, and attributable support.
- Positive or credibly recoverable cash after working capital and maintenance capital.
- Defensible demand and pricing.
- Risk-adjusted backlog sufficient for the reduced cost base.
- Remediable execution leakage.
- Acceptable customer quality, renewal, payment, and concentration risk.
- Required skills, certifications, managers, and assets available or replaceable.
- If standalone economics are below target, protected group contribution greater than the incremental cost of retention.

Potential candidates, subject to evidence, include recurring maintenance, compliance, specialist, certified, framework, and managed-service activities.

## 6. Customer interdependencies with profitable divisions

Customer overlap must be tested through an independent counterfactual. Revenue at risk is not equivalent to contribution at risk, and contribution at risk is not necessarily protectable.

### Required calculations

\[
\text{Unit contribution}
=
\text{unit revenue}
-\text{direct and attributable cost to serve}
\]

\[
\text{Protectable other-division contribution}
=
\text{other-division contribution}
\times \text{independently supported probability of loss}
\]

\[
\text{Group value of retention}
=
\text{unit contribution}
+\text{protectable other-division contribution}
-\text{transfer and relationship costs}
-\text{incremental receiving-division cost}
\]

### Independent evidence must include

- Customer interviews independent of the unit’s sales team.
- Contractual bundling or change-of-control provisions.
- Procurement and tender rules.
- Historical customer behavior when the service was unavailable.
- Competitive alternatives.
- Whether another group division can retain account ownership.
- Customer willingness to use an external provider.
- Outcomes from prior transfers or lost accounts.

### Customer classification

| Category | Treatment |
|---|---|
| High-risk and protectable | May justify an explicit, time-limited, beneficiary-funded subsidy |
| High-risk but transferable | Transfer or outsource delivery |
| Low-risk | Exit without assuming material group-customer loss |
| Unverified | No retention subsidy approved |

Any profitable division requiring below-market support must provide an explicit transfer price or strategic-service charge.

## 7. Cost, stranded-cost, and liability treatment

For every turnaround, transfer, outsourcing, carve-out, and wind-down option, classify costs as:

- Direct variable.
- Avoidable fixed.
- Transferable.
- Unavoidable.
- Stranded.
- Allocated.
- One-time.
- Contingent liability.

### Required option waterfall

\[
\text{Gross cost removed}
-\text{costs transferred}
-\text{costs retained}
-\text{separation costs}
-\text{residual liabilities}
=\text{Net recurring group cash benefit}
\]

The board should not approve a partial exit without this analysis by branch, service line, and option.

The analysis must include:

- Leases and facilities.
- Systems and shared services.
- Employees, pensions, benefits, and redundancy costs.
- Guarantees, warranties, claims, and performance bonds.
- Environmental, tax, legal, and regulatory exposure.
- Customer and supplier consents.
- Residual corporate costs.
- Costs incurred by any receiving division.

## 11. Exit alternatives and decision criteria

### Exit options

| Alternative | Appropriate when | Principal issues |
|---|---|---|
| Whole-unit sale | Buyer can create scale, utilization, or procurement synergies | Buyer interest, diligence, management retention, consents, liabilities |
| Partial sale or carve-out | Selected service lines, branches, or customers are viable | Separation complexity, stranded overhead, contract novation |
| Transfer to profitable division | Capability or customer relationship is valuable but standalone overhead is not | Transfer pricing, receiving-division cost, liability allocation |
| Outsourcing or partnership | External provider has structurally better delivery economics | Quality, customer acceptance, dependency, liability |
| Management buyout | Local management has customer and technical value | Financing, liability allocation, vendor-loan risk |
| Asset sale | Assets or licenses have standalone value | Disposal proceeds, leases, environmental and residual costs |
| Orderly wind-down | No alternative creates superior value | Contract completion, claims, redundancies, customer continuity |

Exit preparation must begin by day 30 and include legal entities, contracts, consents, employees, pensions, benefits, licenses, systems, data, intellectual property, guarantees, claims, environmental obligations, tax, transitional services, stranded overhead, and customer handoff.

### Exit decision criteria

Choose turnaround only if:

\[
\text{Risk-adjusted turnaround value}
>
\text{Best feasible exit value}
\]

and all mandatory liquidity, liability, and execution conditions are satisfied.

Choose partial exit, transfer, or outsourcing if the full unit fails but a defined perimeter has superior risk-adjusted economics.

Choose full exit if:

- No viable core exists by day 90.
- The core cannot cover its retained cost and risk burden.
- The case depends on unsupported volume or pipeline assumptions.
- Cash remains negative by the validated month-nine or month-ten threshold.
- Critical capability cannot be retained or replaced.
- Group value can be preserved through another route.
- Exit value exceeds turnaround value.
- A material liability, safety, or liquidity issue makes continuation uneconomic.

## 12. Turnaround versus exit valuation

The board should require a five-year, probability-weighted group cash model.

### Turnaround value

\[
\text{Turnaround value}
=
PV(\text{future free cash flow})
+PV(\text{terminal value})
-\text{turnaround investment}
-\text{downside costs}
-\text{residual liabilities}
\]

### Exit value

\[
\text{Exit value}
=
\text{net proceeds or avoided losses}
+\text{protectable group contribution}
-\text{separation costs}
-\text{residual costs}
-\text{retained liabilities}
\]

The model must include:

- Downside, base, and upside cases.
- Probability of backlog conversion, repricing, execution improvement, customer retention, and talent retention.
- Probability and timing of each exit route.
- Working-capital funding.
- Tax, financing, guarantees, claims, warranties, and pension costs.
- Cost of management time.
- Cost of deterioration during delayed exit.
- Terminal value assumptions.
- Maximum cash exposure.
- Value of the capped 90-day diagnostic as an information option.

The weighted decision matrix may support judgment but cannot substitute for cash valuation.

## Final recommendation

Approve the **capped 90-day diagnostic and stabilization program with parallel exit readiness**.

Do not yet approve an open-ended one-year turnaround. The unit should be retained only if a smaller, economically disciplined core demonstrates sustainable positive cash generation, defensible demand, adequate management capability, and risk-adjusted value superior to exit.

The most credible outcomes are either:

1. A smaller specialist business built around recurring, defensible, cash-generative work; or
2. A structured transfer, partial divestment, outsourcing arrangement, sale, or wind-down that preserves profitable group relationships while stopping further cash destruction.

At day 365, lower headcount or narrower losses will not be sufficient. Retention requires validated, risk-adjusted positive operating profit and free cash flow after maintenance capital, working capital, residual costs, liabilities, and required investment.
