## SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (1 PAGE)

The industrial-services unit has lost money for three consecutive years despite two restructuring attempts. Order books are shrinking, best managers are departing, and cost-cutting alone has failed to restore profitability. The unit now faces a definitive choice: execute a disciplined turnaround within 12 months, or exit via managed divestment.

- **Structural (60%):** Market commoditization, customer consolidation, digital displacement of field maintenance, shift toward integrated solutions at national scale
- **Execution (40%):** No pricing discipline, failed customer segmentation, refusal to exit loss-making service lines, misaligned cost structure

**Why This Matters:** Execution failures are fixable; structural headwinds are persistent but manageable via portfolio repositioning.

## SECTION 2: DIAGNOSIS—ROOT CAUSES & MARKET REALITY

### 2.1 The Evidence: Why Losses Persist Despite Cost Cuts

| **Indicator** | **Finding** | **Interpretation** |
|---|---|---|
| **Order book shrinking despite cost reductions** | Prior two restructurings cut costs 15–20% per restructuring; order book fell 8–12% CAGR each year | Problem is NOT cost; problem is demand erosion and customer defection |
| **Manager attrition accelerating** | Best managers departing to external competitors; retention of remaining staff declining | Internal leadership perceives business model as irreversibly broken |
| **Three-year loss persistence** | Year 0: $(3)M loss; Year 1: $(4)M; Year 2: $(5)M; cumulative $(12)M | Not cyclical downturn; structural repositioning of market |
| **Customer concentration shifting** | Largest customers consolidating spend to 2–3 national providers; unit losing to scale competitors | Buyer consolidation reducing fragmented supplier base |
| **Service-line margin compression** | Commodity services (Field Maintenance, Projects) at 6–13% gross margins; can't cover fixed costs | Portfolio heavily skewed toward low-margin work; high-margin services subsidizing losses |

### 2.2 Structural vs. Execution Breakdown (60% / 40%)

**STRUCTURAL HEADWINDS (60% of problem)—Durable market shifts:**
- **Commoditization of field maintenance:** Digital monitoring, predictive maintenance software, IoT sensors displacing 25–40% of reactive field-service demand by 2027
- **Customer consolidation:** Industrial buyers (utilities, manufacturers, MRO) consolidating suppliers from 8+ to 2–3 "prime vendors" seeking integrated solutions
- **Equipment vendor bundling:** OEM vendors (Siemens, GE, ABB) providing "free" optimization consulting bundled with equipment sales, undercutting third-party consultants
- **Scale disadvantage:** Unit is mid-sized operator; lacks integration/bundling capability vs. national consolidators; cannot compete on all-in cost

**EXECUTION FAILURES (40% of problem)—Avoidable management mistakes:**
- **Pricing discipline collapse:** Cost cuts enabled lower pricing to customers; trained customers that unit is interchangeable commodity; death-spiral pricing
- **Customer segmentation failure:** Treated high-value and low-value customers identically; no differentiated service or pricing by segment
- **Service-line subsidy:** Retained low-margin Projects ($17M @ 6% margin) and commodity Field Maintenance ($47M external @ 10% margin) to show "full-service" positioning; losses absorbed by high-margin Equipment Optimization
- **Fixed-cost structure misalignment:** Did not right-size fixed assets (6 regional offices, 250 technicians, $7M overhead) as revenue shrunk; cost structure built for $100M+ business, applied to $67–90M declining business

### 2.3 Why Prior Restructurings Failed

**Both prior cost-reduction initiatives treated symptoms, not root causes:**

1. **Flawed Assumption:** Losses driven by high costs → cutting costs will restore profitability
   - **Reality:** Losses driven by demand erosion and commodity positioning → cost cuts without repricing or portfolio surgery triggered death spiral
   
2. **Mechanism of Failure:**
   - Cut costs → Reduced service delivery capability → Customer perceived degradation → Customer sought external alternatives → Revenue fell faster than costs → Losses persisted
   
3. **Lack of Go/No-Go Gates:**
   - Neither restructuring had explicit viability checkpoints
   - Management continued investing in sinking business while core problems (customer mix, service-line portfolio) went unaddressed

**Implication:** Turnaround requires portfolio repositioning + pricing discipline + cost right-sizing. Cost cuts alone are insufficient and counterproductive.

### 3.2 Why Each Segment Is (or Isn't) Defensible

**✅ VIABLE: Equipment Optimization / Consulting**
- **Current:** $28M revenue, 32% gross margin, $2.4M EBITDA
- **Why defensible:** Intellectual-capital-based; customers pay for outcomes/efficiency improvements (not just labor hours); higher switching costs; defensible if positioned as niche specialist
- **Why at risk:** Competing against in-house customer teams, larger consulting firms (McKinsey, EY), vendor-bundled consulting (OEM "free" optimization with equipment); consultant-dependent (person attrition risk)
- **Path forward:** Reposition from "generalist optimizer" to "niche specialist" (e.g., "energy efficiency for pharma manufacturing"); value-based pricing (% of customer savings); lock key consultants to 3-year partnerships
- **Target:** Grow to $35M (via external niche expansion); maintain 32% margin; contribution $5.5M (vs. current $2.4M)

**✅ VIABLE: Group Internal Customers**
- **Current:** $25M revenue, 16% margin (underbid; subsidized by other segments)
- **Why defensible:** Relationship-based; lower switching cost than external customers; can leverage group integration
- **Why at risk:** Price-sensitive due to group cost-reduction pressure; perceived as "captive vendor" (not premium); alternative external sourcing available
- **Path forward:** Reposition as "strategic partnership tier" with value-based pricing, SLA guarantees, dedicated account management; lock 3–5 year contracts; accept 8% volume loss for 15–18% repricing
- **Target:** Maintain $23M revenue (8% loss acceptable); improve margin from 16% to 28%; contribution $5.9M (vs. current $0.7M)

**⚠️ VIABLE (IF NARROWED): Field Maintenance / Reactive Services**
- **Current:** $55M revenue (55% of total), 13% gross margin, *$(2)M loss* after fixed costs
- **Why not defensible as commodity:** Commoditized; displaced by digital monitoring; customer consolidation favoring larger vendors; no cost or brand advantage
- **Why potentially viable:** If repositioned as "managed service" (fixed monthly fee + SLA uptime guarantee) targeting only high-value customers (>$500K annual spend, >70% renewal rate); recurring revenue; high switching costs for embedded relationships
- **Path forward:** Exit 70% of customers (peripheral accounts); retain only 2–3 internal + 5–8 external "critical uptime" customers; convert to managed-service model; reduce technician headcount 60% (250→100); consolidate to 2 geographic hubs
- **Target:** $16M revenue (71% reduction); improve margin from 13% to 22–24%; contribution $2.5–3M (vs. current $(2)M loss)

**❌ NOT VIABLE: Projects & Turnkey (EPC)**
- **Current:** $17M revenue, 6% gross margin, *$(2.4)M loss* (largest drag on profitability)
- **Why not defensible:** Unit lacks scale and specialization vs. pure-play EPC contractors (Bechtel, Fluor, regional specialists); customers prefer dedicated EPC partners with project-financing capability; fixed-price contracts create rework and warranty cost burden
- **Path forward:** Complete exit by end of Q2; return projects to customers; refer to vetted external EPC partners; eliminate Project Management overhead ($3–4M annual cost)
- **Target:** $0 revenue; eliminate $(2.4)M annual loss
