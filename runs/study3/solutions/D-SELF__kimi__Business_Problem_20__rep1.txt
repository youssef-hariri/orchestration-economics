## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The 14-week quoted lead time is not a capacity problem; it is a **flow, certification-handoff, and coordination problem**. Value-add manufacturing, code-mandated processing, and required inspection consume less than 25% of the current cycle. The remaining 75% is latent time—engineering release delays, procurement queues caused by late material certification, batch-and-queue WIP, inspection bathtubs, and cross-functional schedule instability.

Our fastest competitor does not win on delivery by bypassing quality. It wins by eliminating waiting time through synchronized planning, pre-positioned strategic inventory with valid traceability, and inspection architectures that parallelize non-sequential activities without violating code-required hold points. The previous warranty spike occurred because compression was attempted by forcing overtime and bypassing inspection depth—a failure of control, not of physics.

This plan defines a 12-month roadmap to compress quoted lead time to **8 weeks within 6 months** (covering 70% of standard volume) and **6 weeks within 12 months**, while holding first-pass yield and warranty cost-per-unit at or below current baselines. It is built on four synchronized strategic levers: **Flow Redesign, Strategic Inventory Positioning, Scheduling Discipline, and Supplier Integration.** Certified quality gates—including all third-party inspector (TPI) hold points, code-mandated sequences, and material traceability requirements—are preserved and hardened, not eroded.

Execution requires modest incremental working capital for strategic WIP and finished-goods buffers, a disciplined Management of Change (MOC) protocol for all process modifications, and a CEO-chartered governance model that replaces functional feuding with shared economic fate.

## 1. STRATEGIC DIAGNOSIS: WHERE THE 14 WEEKS ARE LOST

In heavy industrial equipment, the quoted cycle decomposes as follows. “Value-add” includes necessary code-mandated thermal processing and inspection.

| Stage | Time Allocated | Value-Add / Code-Required Time | Primary Time Loss Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Order Entry & Engineering Release** | 1.5–2.5 weeks | 2–3 days | Sales configuration errors; custom engineering “one-offs” for standardizable features; credit/engineering hold loops; missing material grade selection delaying certificate requests. |
| **Procurement & Supplier Ramp** | 4–6 weeks | 5–7 days | Long-lead castings, forgings, and electronics procured *after* drawing release; no strategic raw material/WIP positioning; certificate-validity buffers absorbed internally; TPI hold points at sub-suppliers not pre-scheduled. |
| **Production Manufacturing** | 3–4 weeks | 6–10 days | Large-lot batching to maximize local OEE; poor line balancing; crane contention between work centers; unplanned stress-relief cycles; excessive WIP between discontinuous operations. |
| **Quality Inspection & Test** | 2–3 weeks | 3–5 days | Serial inspection architecture—partly code-mandated (e.g., weld → PWHT → NDT → hydrotest), partly administrative queueing; centralized QC bottlenecks; rework loops re-entering general queues; TPI/witness-test scheduling conflicts. |
| **Final Release, Pack, Ship** | 3–7 days | 1 day | Final documentation reconciliation; customer hold for witness tests not pre-scheduled; logistics booking latency; export license delays. |
| **Management Buffer** | 1–2 weeks | 0 | Unstated padding inserted by Production/Scheduling to absorb internal variance and functional blame avoidance. |

**Root Cause:** The 14-week lead time is dominated by **five interdependent time sinks**:

1. **Procurement latency** triggered by late engineering release and material-certificate gaps.
2. **Batch-and-queue production** optimized for local machine utilization rather than flow.
3. **Inspection as a serial bottleneck**—partly physics-bound by metallurgical code sequence, partly due to administrative queueing and poor TPI coordination.
4. **Schedule instability** caused by unfiltered Sales expedites and the absence of frozen planning zones.
5. **Cross-functional gridlock**, where opaque priorities and misaligned metrics destroy end-to-end velocity.

**Critical Distinction:** Code-mandated sequences (e.g., post-weld heat treatment before hydrostatic test) are inviolate. Time will be recovered by eliminating transport, queue, and certification delays *between* stations—not by reversing physics.

## 2. THE IMPROVEMENT PORTFOLIO: FOUR SYNCHRONIZED LEVERS

### A. Flow & Process Redesign (Eliminate Waiting, Not Work)

- **Deploy product-family cells:** Replace process layouts with physical cells for high-volume sub-assemblies (<5 tons) and **fixed-position virtual cells** for large fabrications. In virtual cells, the crane, welding stations, tooling, and inspectors are reserved to a product family, eliminating inter-departmental material handling. *Target: 40% reduction in production WIP queue time.*
- **Implement a rules-based sales configurator:** Restrict true custom engineering to configurations >20% variance from base models. Standard modules trigger instant release via pre-approved BOMs and weld maps; only exceptions route to Engineering. The configurator must feed directly into the Inspection and Test Plan (ITP) generator to auto-populate hold points (H-points) and witness points (W-points). *Target: Cut order-entry-to-release time from 2+ weeks to 2–3 days.*
- **Execute advanced procurement with certificate reservation:** Release long-lead purchase orders (forgings, castings, electronics) against family-level forecasts *before* firm order entry. Institute a **certificate reservation protocol**: material is procured to forecast, but EN 10204 material certificates are held unassigned until order entry, preventing heat-number mismatches that stall release. *Target: Start procurement on day zero, not week three.*
- **Parallelize administrative inspection:** Co-locate dimensional, paint/coating inspection, and documentation review stations at the cell so they run parallel to NDT. Code-mandated sequences remain intact; time is saved by eliminating transport and queue delays between stations.
- **Launch a hybrid digital thread pilot:** Connect machine controllers and test equipment to a Manufacturing Execution System (MES) to auto-populate certified quality records. Begin with dimensional and functional test data; retain PDF-signed NDT and hydrotest reports until the system is validated per Notified Body requirements. *Do not attempt a big-bang paperless transition during compression.*

### B. Inventory & Supply Chain Positioning (Strategic Pre-Positioning)

- **Create post-long-lead decoupling points:** Pre-machine and pre-inspect common sub-assemblies (frames, manifolds, drive units) to a family-level forecast. Customer orders trigger final assembly and software configuration, converting 14-week make-to-order into 4–6-week configure-to-order. Preserve heat-number traceability via super-BOM or phantom BOM structures that marry existing certificates to new work orders upon order entry.
- **Establish VMI/consignment for C-items:** Shift fasteners, seals, fittings, and raw bar stock to supplier-managed consignment on the shop floor. Pressure-retaining or safety-critical fasteners require lot traceability and physical segregation; all other low-risk items move to dock-to-line receipt.
- **Hold a protective finished-goods buffer for top SKUs:** Size a 2–3 week FG buffer for the highest-volume, most delivery-sensitive products using demand-variability analysis (coefficient of variation), not blanket rules. Start with the top 5% of SKUs. Carrying cost (18–25% annualized) is offset by margin recovery from improved win rates.
- **Stand up rework containment cells:** Isolate minor non-conformance disposition in a fast-track station with pre-approved Repair Procedures and WPS/PQR requalification (per ASME IX / ISO 15614-1). Prevent rework from re-entering the standard production queue.

### C. Scheduling & Control Architecture (Stabilize to Flow)

- **Institute frozen and slushy schedule zones:** Establish a **4-week frozen zone** (no sequence changes without C-suite sign-off) and a **2-week slushy zone** (capacity-neutral swaps only). The frozen horizon must exceed the longest internal lead-time operation (excluding raw material).
- **Adopt Drum-Buffer-Rope (DBR) scheduling:** Identify the true system constraint—typically the heat-treat furnace, large CNC boring mill, or overhead crane serving the test bay—and schedule all upstream operations to its drumbeat. Measure **throughput dollar-days** and **protective buffer penetration**, not work-center utilization. Use dynamic buffers by product family.
- **Drive daily management by queue time:** Shift tiered daily huddles from “units completed” to “queue time reduction.” Track order-to-release lead time, quality-gate queue time (distinguishing code-mandated dwell from administrative delay), procurement certificate status, and crane hook-time utilization.
- **Formalize integrated S&OP:** Institute weekly Sales & Operations Planning with joint decision rights. Inputs are data only: forecast accuracy by family, actual vs. planned queue times, first-pass yield, and TPI schedule adherence. No function may veto a decision without offering a countermeasure.

### D. Supplier Integration (Compress the Supply Base Clock)

- **Dual-source long-lead critical items:** For items with >4 week lead times, qualify a second source to create competition and volume flexibility. Second-source qualification must include first-article inspection (FAI) and, for castings/forgings, source inspection by TPI or internal Quality auditors before shipment.
- **Move A-grade suppliers to ship-to-line:** Replace 100% incoming inspection with dock-to-line receipt for top-tier suppliers, requiring digital certificates (EN 10204, NDT reports) with every Advance Shipping Notice (ASN). Redeploy incoming inspection resources to in-process audit and supplier surveillance, not elimination.
- **Regionalize fragile supply lanes:** Conduct total-cost-of-ownership exercises for suppliers with chronic 6+ week lead times. Regional premiums are frequently offset by inventory carrying-cost reduction, lower freight damage, and recovered deal margin.
- **Provide forecast visibility and capacity reservations:** Issue critical suppliers a **6-month rolling forecast, 3-month firm projection, and 1-month material-capacity reservation**, formalized under blanket purchase orders (BPOs) with release call-offs.

## 3. QUALITY GATE PROTECTION & WARRANTY PREVENTION

Time will be removed by eliminating waiting, not by compressing value-add execution or bypassing certified gates.

### Non-Negotiable Gate Architecture
- **Preserve all ITP hold points:** H-points and W-points are repositioned to eliminate transport and queue delays, but never eliminated. If hydrotest is an H-point, the test bay is reserved the moment the weldment enters the cell—not when it reaches the end of a queue.
- **Harden digital record integrity:** Auto-capture all certified test data from calibrated equipment (ISO/IEC 17025 traceable) with timestamps and operator IDs. Enforce role-based access and audit trails to prevent manual “pencil-whipping” under schedule pressure.
- **Enforce independent Quality stop authority:** Quality maintains line-stop authority at every cell. Confirm the Quality Manager reports to the highest level of management (or holds a dotted line to the CEO) per organizational freedom requirements. If first-pass yield drops below the trailing 12-week baseline by more than two sigma, the cell stops. Production cannot override without a documented concession approved by the Plant Manager and VP of Quality.

### Shift from Detection to Prevention
- **Deploy in-process SPC and Weld Data Monitoring (WDM):** Install statistical process control at machining stations and real-time welding parameter monitoring (amperage, voltage, wire feed speed). Deviations outside the qualified WPS range trigger automatic hold, stopping defects before they reach inspection.
- **Install poka-yoke at assembly:** Replace manual torque and alignment checks with error-proofing fixtures and sequential digital torque tools where feasible.
- **Execute supplier source inspection:** Move critical inspections (casting soundness, forging grain structure) to the supplier floor with certified auditor sign-off. Prevents defective material from entering the factory and consuming six weeks before discovery.

### Management of Change (MOC) & Early Warning
- **MOC protocol for all changes:** Every process change follows a formal protocol: Change Request → Risk Assessment (FMEA) → Approval by Quality & Engineering → Validation/Verification → Training → Document Update (ITP, WPS, BOM) → Pilot → Release. Skipping MOC invalidates certifications and exposes the firm to liability.
- **Pilot-before-scale mandate:** All lead-time compression changes pilot on one product family for **90 days or 50 units**, whichever yields statistical significance. Warranty and yield data must validate the pilot before enterprise rollout.
- **Post-change verification audits:** For 90 days after any change, Quality conducts 100% audit on the affected parameter, ramping to statistical sampling only after Cpk ≥ 1.33 is proven.
- **Warranty Early Warning System (WEWS):** Monitor in-process defect PPM and field failures weekly by product family. If defect rates exceed baseline by >25%, trigger an automatic 48-hour cross-functional review using 8D methodology.

### Rework Discipline
- All rework requires an approved Repair Procedure, not ad hoc shop-floor instruction. Rework on welded pressure parts requires re-qualification (re-PWHT, re-NDT, re-hydrotest). If it cannot be repaired under an approved procedure, it is scrapped or returned to the supplier.

## 4. ALIGNMENT & GOVERNANCE: ENDING THE FUNCTIONAL DEADLOCK

The feud is structural. Each function optimizes for a local metric that conflicts with system throughput.

| Function | Current Metric | Resulting Behavior | Conflict Created |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Production** | Cost-per-unit, machine OEE, labor efficiency | Large batches, long runs, resistance to changeovers | Extends lead time; blames Sales for chaos |
| **Quality** | Defect rate, audit compliance, zero-escape | Conservative release, queued inspection | Blamed for bottleneck; viewed as obstructionist |
| **Sales** | Revenue, bookings, market share | Promises custom specs, expedites, ignores capacity | Blames factory for instability; forces shortcuts |

### Unified Metrics & Shared Fate
Replace siloed KPIs with **three shared leading indicators** displayed on a plant-floor control tower:
1. **Customer Request-to-Ship Cycle Time** (Planning & Sales).
2. **First-Pass Yield at Final Test** (Quality & Production).
3. **Quote-to-Order Win Rate on Delivery** (Sales & Operations).

Tie **25–30% of variable compensation** for Production, Quality, and Sales directors to these shared metrics. Use **Hoshin Kanri** to cascade the 8-week target into function-specific tactics reviewed monthly in a **Nemawashi** pre-alignment process.

### The Order Fulfillment War Room
Establish a permanent, co-located **Customer Delivery Room** staffed by rotating representatives from Production, Quality, Planning, and Sales. Leadership rotates quarterly among functions to prevent dominance and build empathy. The room tracks protective buffer penetration hourly and enforces a **trade-off analysis** before any order enters expedite status (identifying which order will be delayed to make room).

### Service Level Agreements with Teeth
- **Sales → Operations:** Sales commits to 95% forecast accuracy at the family level and respects the frozen zone. In exchange, Operations commits to 95% on-time delivery (OTD). If Sales violates the frozen zone, it funds overtime; if Operations misses OTD, it funds expedited freight.
- **Operations → Quality:** Operations commits to never bypassing a certified gate. Quality commits to 24-hour turnaround on inspection disposition. If Quality cannot meet 24 hours, it issues a provisional “inspection in-process” tag allowing downstream non-test activities (e.g., painting prep) to continue without compromising the H-point.
- **Quality → Sales:** Quality provides a “Product Capability Document” aligned to the configurator. Sales compensation includes a **clawback provision** if a deal is booked as standard but triggers custom engineering due to configuration errors.

### Behavioral Alignment
- **VOC Immersion:** Require Production supervisors and Quality engineers to attend quarterly reviews where deals were lost to delivery speed. Require Sales to observe a failed hydrotest or NDT reject that triggers teardown.
- **Monthly Cross-Functional Gemba Walks:** Each function identifies one time-waste in another function’s area and proposes a countermeasure.
- **Executive Escalation Protocol:** Disputes stalling >48 hours escalate to a **tri-functional steering committee** (VP Ops, VP Quality, VP Sales). The default decision rule: *“What maximizes throughput dollar-days without compromising certified conformity?”* The CEO or COO serves as the binding tie-breaker to prevent executive-level regression to siloed behavior.

**The Strategic Imperative**

Time is not saved by working faster on the shop floor. It is saved by **removing the reasons work sits idle**—in procurement certificate queues, in WIP bathtubs, in inspection queues caused by poor TPI scheduling, and in cross-functional gridlock. Quality is not the enemy of speed; it is the prerequisite for sustainable speed. In heavy industrial equipment, **certified quality is the license to operate; lead-time compression must be built around the code, not through shortcuts.**
