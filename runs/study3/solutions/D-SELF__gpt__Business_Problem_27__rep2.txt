# Executive Recommendation

Pursue **controlled acceleration through staged, blended financing**—not maximum leverage, a large equity round, or a purely organic plan.

Recommended approach:

1. **Equipment finance and a committed, selectively drawn working-capital facility** for capacity assets, receivables, inventory, and other uses with identifiable repayment support.
2. **Limited minority equity or patient preferred equity** for critical hiring, market expansion, product development, implementation capacity, and liquidity—uses with uncertain timing of returns.
3. **Retained cash flow** for implementation and the highest-return bottleneck removal.
4. **Optional follow-on capital**, released only after operating and commercial milestones validate the first stage.

Initially fund only **9–12 months of the highest-confidence expansion plan**, not the entire theoretical opportunity.

The founders should target approximately **10–12.5% fully diluted post-money dilution**, with **15% as an exceptional reference ceiling requiring explicit approval**. These are negotiation parameters, not universal rules. Founder control must be protected through board rights and financing terms, not ownership percentage alone.

If acceptable financing cannot fund the minimum viable plan while preserving liquidity and downside resilience, do not close merely because capital is available.

# 1. Option Comparison

| Criterion | Debt | Minority equity | Hybrid financing | Disciplined organic growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership dilution | None, except warrants or convertibles | Direct dilution | Potential dilution plus preferences | None |
| Founder control | Can be constrained by covenants and remedies | Can be preserved with disciplined governance | Exposed to both debt and equity controls | Highest |
| Company financial risk | Fixed repayment and default risk | Lowest insolvency risk | Can combine repayment and dilution risk | Lowest financing risk |
| Founder personal risk | Possible guarantees and collateral exposure | Usually lower | Structure-dependent | Lowest |
| Speed | Generally fastest after underwriting | Moderate | Moderate and often complex | Immediate funding, slower execution |
| Cash cost | Usually lower nominally, but fees and restrictions matter | No scheduled repayment, but permanent dilution | Often highest all-in cost | No financing cost, but opportunity cost |
| Flexibility | Moderate to low | High if terms are clean | Moderate | Highest |
| Principal strategic risk | Forced de-risking if growth slows | Investor influence or exit pressure | Refinancing and hidden dilution | Losing customers, talent, or market position |

These are conditional comparisons. Debt is preferred for assets and working capital if downside cash flow supports it. Equity is preferred for uncertain-return growth if governance terms are acceptable. Hybrid capital is a fallback only. Organic growth is appropriate only if delay is unlikely to cause permanent strategic loss.

## Debt

Use debt for:

- Equipment and capacity assets;
- Facility improvements;
- Receivables and inventory;
- Purchase orders or contracted demand;
- Investments with predictable payback.

Do not use debt primarily for speculative market entry, unproven products, recurring losses, or hiring and marketing whose economics depend on revenue doubling on schedule.

Debt should be sized to the lesser of:

1. Financeable assets and eligible receivables or inventory;
2. Downside cash-flow repayment capacity; and
3. The amount that preserves liquidity and covenant compliance.

**Illustrative starting parameters—not universal standards:**

- Initial net leverage of approximately **1.0–1.5x EBITDA**;
- Base-case fixed-charge coverage above approximately **2.0x**;
- Downside coverage above approximately **1.3–1.5x**, with separate liquidity tests;
- At least **25–30% undrawn revolver availability** after initial expansion.

All calculations must define gross versus net debt, funded revolver usage, leases, existing debt, adjusted EBITDA, taxes, maintenance capex, working capital, and mandatory amortization.

## Minority equity

Use equity for:

- Critical leadership and specialist hires;
- Sales and marketing;
- New markets and channels;
- Product development;
- Customer implementation capacity;
- Liquidity protection during the ramp.

Equity is justified at a lower valuation if it materially reduces the risk of permanently losing customers, talent, channels, pricing power, or market share.

Illustratively, at a $30 million pre-money valuation:

| Equity raised | Approximate post-money dilution |
|---:|---:|
| $3.33 million | 10% |
| $4.29 million | 12.5% |
| $5.29 million | 15% |

These figures are illustrative only. Dilution must be calculated on a fully diluted basis, including option-pool changes, warrants, convertibles, and accrued preferred economics.

## Hybrid financing

Use a hybrid only if it bridges a temporary valuation issue and is demonstrably better than a reduced-scope equity, debt, or organic plan.

The instrument must clearly model:

- Maximum cash repayment;
- Accrued PIK or dividends;
- Maximum dilution;
- Conversion mechanics;
- Change-of-control proceeds;
- Ranking relative to senior debt;
- Default consequences;
- Founder ownership and governance after conversion.

Long-dated, patient capital is preferable to short-dated convertible debt with an aggressive valuation cap.

## Organic growth

Retain organic growth as the fallback plan and operating discipline:

- Remove the highest-value bottleneck first;
- Prioritize existing customers and high-margin segments;
- Fund only essential hires;
- Defer speculative markets and products;
- Use limited equipment finance where payback is clear.

Abandon the organic plan if capacity-related customer losses, service failures, competitor hiring, or channel commitments indicate that delay will cause permanent strategic damage.

# 2. Proposed Financing Structure and Sequencing

## Tranche 1: Asset-matched debt

Secure:

- Equipment finance for identified assets;
- A receivables- or inventory-backed revolver;
- A modest term facility only for capacity with visible utilization and repayment support.

The revolver should be committed but largely undrawn at closing. It should fund short-term working-capital timing, not permanent capacity or recurring operating losses.

Seek:

- Amortization matched to asset life;
- Interest-only or limited amortization during the ramp;
- No material maturity cliff during the first 24 months;
- Realistic covenants reflecting seasonality;
- Cure periods for covenant breaches;
- Permitted hiring, capex, leases, and ordinary-course debt;
- No cash dominion before a defined trigger;
- No uncapped founder guarantees;
- Flexibility for later equity and refinancing.

## Tranche 2: Minority equity or patient preferred equity

Raise only the minimum equity needed for uncertain-return growth and liquidity.

Preferred terms should include:

- One-times, non-participating liquidation preference;
- No mandatory redemption before the company is comfortably cash-generative;
- Broad-based weighted-average anti-dilution only;
- No full-ratchet protection;
- No forced sale right without founder approval;
- Narrow reserved matters;
- No veto over ordinary hiring, pricing, products, customers, or approved-budget spending;
- Pro rata rights where practicable.

## Tranche 3: Follow-on capital

Negotiate access to additional debt or equity, but do not commit to drawing it. Follow-on capital should require fresh approval after performance validation.

Distinguish clearly between:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cash funded at closing | Immediately available balance-sheet capital |
| Committed availability | Legally committed but undrawn financing |
| Contingency reserve | Capital reserved for defined downside or timing needs |
| Optional follow-on | Capital requiring later approval or milestone validation |

The minimum liquidity reserve must be funded by cash or highly reliable committed sources; it should not depend solely on undrawn borrowing-base capacity.

# 3. Funding Waterfall and Use of Proceeds

Management should prepare three executable plans before signing:

| Plan | Funding purpose | Priority uses | Deferred uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan A: Controlled acceleration | Capture the market window | Priority capacity, critical hires, high-confidence commercial expansion, liquidity reserve | Speculative products and markets |
| Plan B: Constrained acceleration | Execute with less capital | Contracted-demand capacity, essential hires, existing customers, highest-margin segments | Broad marketing, new geographies, nonessential hiring |
| Plan C: Organic bottleneck removal | Proceed without acceptable external capital | Highest-return bottleneck, essential process improvements, limited equipment finance | Most new-market investment |

### Funding priority

1. Fund the minimum liquidity reserve.
2. Use equipment finance for specific assets with clear payback.
3. Use internal cash for implementation only while the reserve remains intact.
4. Use the revolver for temporary receivables and inventory timing.
5. Use equity or patient preferred capital for uncertain-return hiring and expansion.
6. Use hybrid capital only after full scenario analysis.
7. Use additional debt or equity only after milestone validation.

If equity is below target, reduce speculative expansion before increasing leverage. Debt may close without equity only if it is asset-matched, downside cash flow supports service, and the company can execute a narrower plan without debt-funded uncertain-return growth.

---

# 4. Illustrative Use of Proceeds

Actual percentages should follow the company’s model. A reasonable planning framework is:

- **30–40%:** Capacity, equipment, and facility expansion;
- **15–20%:** Working capital and inventory;
- **15–20%:** Critical hires and leadership capacity;
- **15–20%:** Sales, marketing, and market expansion;
- **5–10%:** Product, technology, and process improvements;
- **10–15%:** Liquidity reserve and contingency.

The company should separate maintenance capex from expansion capex and maintain sufficient unrestricted cash to cover fixed operating costs, scheduled debt service, and the documented working-capital ramp.

# 5. Milestone-Based Staging

## Before closing

Complete:

- A 24-month monthly integrated model;
- Base, downside, severe-downside, and reverse-stress cases;
- Capacity-to-revenue and capacity-to-cash analysis;
- Hiring and productivity plans;
- Working-capital and borrowing-base analysis;
- Fully diluted capitalization table;
- Sources-and-uses schedule;
- Competitive-window scorecard;
- List of discretionary costs removable within 60 days.

At least **60–70% of the first capacity tranche** should be supported by signed orders, recurring demand, or risk-adjusted qualified pipeline. Speculative pipeline should not count as committed demand.

## Initial deployment

Release the first tranche only when:

- The liquidity reserve is funded;
- The downside case does not require emergency capital;
- Critical execution roles are identified;
- Reporting systems are operational;
- The investment has credible utilization or bottleneck support;
- Optional commitments can be deferred or cancelled without material penalties.

## Six-month release test

Measure monthly and on a trailing-three-month basis. Release further capital only if:

- The selected bottleneck metric reaches approximately **60% of target**;
- Revenue is at least **85–90% of plan**;
- Gross margin is within approximately three percentage points of plan;
- The selected retention metric remains within approximately 5% of historical performance;
- Critical hiring is within 10% of budget;
- Cash conversion is at least 50–60% of EBITDA, unless a documented working-capital build explains the shortfall;
- Customer concentration remains within board-approved limits;
- Unrestricted cash and borrowing-base availability remain above minimums;
- No material covenant breach or repeated unplanned borrowing has occurred.

The bottleneck metric—physical utilization, billable utilization, onboarding throughput, implementation capacity, or another business-specific measure—must be selected and defined before closing.

## Twelve-month acceleration test

Commit further capital only if:

- Utilization reaches approximately **70–80%**, or risk-adjusted backlog supports at least six months of planned capacity;
- Revenue is at least 90% of plan for two consecutive months;
- Customer acquisition payback is within approximately 20% of the approved threshold;
- Net leverage remains within the approved ceiling;
- Fixed-charge coverage is approximately 2.0x in the base case and 1.5x in the downside case;
- Margin, retention, service quality, and cash conversion remain acceptable;
- Major investments demonstrate expected contribution margin and payback;
- The company can fund at least 12 months of debt service without assuming another equity raise.

A single temporary miss should trigger remediation. Two consecutive misses or any liquidity red trigger should suspend discretionary expansion pending board review.

# 6. Governance and Control Implications

While the founders collectively retain at least a negotiated voting threshold, they should have the contractual right to appoint a **majority of voting directors**.

If an investor receives a board seat, the board must still contain enough founder-appointed directors to preserve that majority. A mutually agreed independent director should not eliminate founder board control unless the founders expressly approve that outcome.

Investor protections should be limited to fundamental matters:

- Issuing securities senior to or materially adverse to the investor;
- Materially adverse constitutional amendments;
- Sale of substantially all assets;
- Merger or change of control;
- Winding up;
- Related-party transactions outside the ordinary course;
- Debt materially above agreed limits.

The investor should not control ordinary hiring, compensation, pricing, products, customers, marketing, approved budgets, or routine capex.

The documents should specify:

- Board appointment and removal;
- Chair appointment and casting votes;
- Quorum and voting thresholds;
- Investor-rights sunset thresholds;
- Founder rights after future financings;
- Option-pool and future-round treatment.

Model founder ownership, voting power, and board control after at least two potential future financings.

# 7. Walk-Away Conditions

## Debt

Walk away if:

- The base case does not comply with covenants;
- A reasonable revenue shortfall or six-month ramp delay causes immediate default;
- Debt service depends on another financing;
- A near-term bullet maturity lacks committed refinancing;
- Cash sweeps impair operations;
- Uncapped founder guarantees or personal-asset security are required;
- The lender can block ordinary operations;
- Future equity or senior working-capital financing is restricted;
- Fees, warrants, guarantees, and covenants make debt’s effective cost comparable to or higher than reasonable equity.

## Minority equity

Walk away if:

- The required financing exceeds the approved dilution range without quantified strategic value;
- The investor demands control-like board or operating rights;
- Terms include participating preference, full-ratchet protection, mandatory redemption, or forced sale;
- The investor can control ordinary hiring, budgets, pricing, products, or customers;
- Strategic exclusivity restricts future growth;
- Maximum dilution cannot be calculated on a fully diluted basis;
- The financing funds an unvalidated plan rather than high-confidence demand.

## Hybrid financing

Reject if:

- Maximum cash repayment or dilution cannot be calculated;
- More than half of principal becomes due in any 12-month period;
- Redemption requires another financing;
- PIK, dividends, warrants, fees, and repayment cannot be supported in the downside case;
- The instrument ranks ahead of senior debt or impairs refinancing;
- Its base, downside, conversion, redemption, or sale economics are inferior to available alternatives;
- The investor can unilaterally accelerate, convert, or force a sale.

## Organic growth

Abandon the purely organic plan if:

- Capacity-related lost or delayed qualified demand exceeds an agreed threshold;
- Delivery or service performance falls below agreed levels;
- Critical talent or distribution is being secured by the rival;
- Required hires cannot be funded without weakening liquidity;
- The rival captures a defined share of priority accounts, channels, or critical talent;
- Delay is likely to permanently reduce pricing power or market share.

# 8. Key Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Slower capacity ramp | Stage draws, match debt to assets, defer optional commitments |
| Working capital consumes cash | Use a committed revolver; monitor receivables, inventory, and borrowing base monthly |
| Debt restricts strategy | Negotiate operating baskets, cure rights, and no ordinary-course vetoes |
| Investor gains effective control | Secure founder board-majority rights and narrow reserved matters |
| Low valuation causes excessive dilution | Raise less equity, use patient capital, or defer follow-on funding |
| Hybrid has hidden costs | Model cash repayment, dilution, ranking, and governance in all scenarios |
| Competitor accelerates | Track lost opportunities, rival hires, pricing, channels, backlog, and service levels quarterly |
| Growth damages quality | Tie releases to retention, margin, delivery, and customer-service metrics |
| Future rounds dilute founders | Negotiate pro rata rights and model option-pool and follow-on effects |
| Refinancing becomes difficult | Match maturities, avoid redemption cliffs, and establish intercreditor terms |
| Rates or currencies move adversely | Evaluate fixed, capped, or hedged debt and match debt currency to revenues |
| Contracts restrict financing | Review assignment, change-of-control, exclusivity, and consent provisions |
| Equipment is delayed | Review deposits, cancellation rights, delivery conditions, and financing availability |
| Reporting is inadequate | Assign a finance owner and implement monthly reporting before the first draw |
| Legal, tax, or insurance issues arise | Obtain specialist review before execution |

# 9. Immediate Next Steps

1. **Build the decision model:** Prepare Plan A, Plan B, and Plan C with company-specific capital requirements, uses, liquidity, leverage, milestones, and expected outcomes.
2. **Define founder risk appetite:** Approve maximum funded leverage, target and maximum dilution, minimum liquidity, personal-guarantee policy, and board-control requirements.
3. **Validate the competitive window:** Establish baselines and thresholds for capacity-related lost demand, delivery times, rival customer wins, hiring, pricing, and channel activity.
4. **Run parallel financing processes:** Obtain proposals for equipment finance, a revolver, minority equity, and a hybrid only if necessary.
5. **Compare terms on an all-in basis:** Include fees, interest, warrants, preferences, dilution, security, covenants, governance, maturity, taxes, and refinancing constraints.
6. **Complete diligence:** Review customer contracts, equipment commitments, insurance, tax treatment, interest-rate and currency exposure, option-pool needs, and reporting capability.
7. **Close only the first stage:** Fund the liquidity reserve and the highest-confidence 9–12-month plan; leave follow-on facilities undrawn.
8. **Establish monthly governance:** The CFO should report milestones monthly. Management may pause discretionary spending after a red trigger; additional draws, expansion capex, or hiring above plan require board approval.
9. **Reassess at six and twelve months:** Release further capital only if the agreed tests are met. Otherwise, shift to the constrained or organic plan.

## Bottom Line

Use the **minimum debt that downside cash flow can safely service**, the **minimum equity necessary to fund uncertain-return growth**, and preserve follow-on optionality. Accept limited dilution where it materially protects a time-sensitive market opportunity, but walk away from financing that threatens solvency, founder control, refinancing flexibility, or the ability to operate if growth is slower than planned.
