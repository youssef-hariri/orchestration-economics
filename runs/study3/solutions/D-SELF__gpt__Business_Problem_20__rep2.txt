# Operations Improvement Plan  
## Reduce Quoted Lead Time Without Compromising Certified Quality

## 1. Executive outcome

The company should launch a controlled reduction program, beginning with one stable, high-volume standard product family. It should **not replace the 14-week company-wide promise with an unsupported six-week commitment**.

The lead-time gap should be closed by removing:

- Queueing, batching, and uncontrolled work-in-process
- Supplier and material variability
- Incomplete order information
- Late engineering changes
- Rework and repeat testing
- Inspection and test scheduling delays
- Serial approvals and documentation delays
- Undifferentiated quotation buffers

**Certified inspections, required testing, traceability, calibration, independent release authority, and complete quality records remain mandatory.** No schedule, sales, financial, or executive objective authorizes bypassing a quality, regulatory, customer, safety, or certification requirement.

### Target state

| Time horizon | Customer promise objective | Decision rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Today | 14 weeks | Retain until the baseline is complete |
| First 30–60 days | No broad reduction | Diagnose, stabilize, and complete pilot controls |
| Pilot product family | 10–12 weeks | Use only after defined service-level and quality criteria are met |
| Qualified standard families | 8–10 weeks | Scale only after proven capacity, supply, quality, and reliability performance |
| Longer term | 6–8 weeks selectively | Product-specific objective, not an enterprise-wide promise |
| Custom/high-risk products | Differentiated lead times | Based on engineering, sourcing, certification, and risk content |

---

# 2. What is known, assumed, and still requires validation

## Confirmed business facts

- Current quoted lead time: **14 weeks**
- Fastest competitor quote: **6 weeks**
- Sales reports losing approximately **one in three deals** on delivery speed
- The product requires **certified inspections at multiple stages**
- A prior compression effort caused a significant increase in warranty claims
- Production, quality, and sales disagree about the primary causes of delay

## Planning assumptions

The following are hypotheses, not established facts:

- A meaningful share of the 14 weeks is waiting, queue, rework, or quotation buffer.
- Supplier variability and incomplete kits delay production.
- Orders enter planning before technical and commercial readiness is complete.
- Engineering changes occur after release.
- Production batching, WIP, and priority changes extend flow time.
- Inspection and test resources are scheduled reactively.
- Documentation and approvals are performed too late and serially.
- Standard, configurable, custom, and high-risk products are being managed under one lead-time policy.

Each assumption will have a named owner, validation method, due date, and consequence if disproven. No assumption becomes a customer target until validated.

# 3. Establish the fact base before changing the promise

The Planning and Industrial Engineering lead will reconstruct at least six months of shipped-order history, preferably twelve months. The dataset must include standard, configurable, custom, late, reworked, and warranty-associated orders.

## Required order timestamps

- Order receipt
- Customer information complete
- Technical review start and completion
- Configuration freeze
- Drawing, BOM, and routing release
- Purchase-order release
- Supplier promise, shipment, and receipt
- Incoming inspection completion
- Kit-complete date
- Production start and completion by operation
- Inspection request, start, and completion
- Test request, start, and completion
- NCR/rework start and completion
- Final documentation completion
- Quality release
- Shipment booking, release, and actual shipment

## Required baseline measures

| Area | Measures |
|---|---|
| Flow | Median, P75, and P90 order-to-ship; touch time; queue time; WIP; backlog |
| Production | Cycle and setup time; schedule adherence; priority changes |
| Materials | Shortage hours; kit completeness; stockouts; incoming-release delay |
| Suppliers | OTIF; lead-time variability; defects; partial shipments; certificate errors |
| Engineering | Review and clarification time; post-release changes; change-related rework |
| Quality | First-pass yield; final-test pass rate; NCRs; repeat inspection; escapes |
| Inspection/test | Request-to-start queue; capacity; failures; deferred work |
| Commercial | Quoted versus actual lead time; promise changes; lost-order reasons |
| Reliability | Warranty claims; early-life failures; returns; service calls by cohort |
| Financial | Inventory, expedite, poor-quality, capacity, and lost-opportunity cost |

## Lead-time loss tree

Each elapsed interval receives one primary reason code:

1. Customer or order readiness
2. Engineering
3. Supplier delay
4. Material shortage or incoming release
5. Production queue or batching
6. Inspection or test queue
7. Rework or retest
8. Approval
9. Documentation or shipment release
10. Quotation buffer

### Baseline completion condition

No material quote reduction is implemented until:

- At least 90% of pilot-family orders have complete timestamp coverage.
- At least 90% of delay intervals have reason codes.
- The top three delay causes are quantified.
- The quote clock and service-level definition are documented.
- Quality and warranty baselines are established.

# 4. Product-family operating model

Products will be managed as four distinct categories:

| Category | Characteristics | Initial treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Approved design, stable routing, qualified suppliers | First pilot candidate |
| Configurable | Standard platform with controlled options | Shorter promise after standard-family proof |
| Engineered-to-order | Significant design or customer approval | Separate lead-time model |
| High-risk/regulated | Additional certification, testing, or documentation | Separate controlled route and promise |

Each category will have its own:

- BOM and routing
- Inspection and test plan
- Supplier requirements
- Capacity model
- Quote percentile
- Configuration-freeze rule
- Change-order policy
- Reliability-monitoring plan

Sales may not use the standard-family promise for custom or high-risk work.

# 5. Future-state order and engineering process

## 5.1 Controlled order statuses

The ERP or planning system will use defined statuses:

1. Commercial review
2. Technical clarification
3. Pre-release procurement permitted
4. Production-ready
5. Configuration frozen
6. In production
7. Inspection hold
8. Test complete
9. Quality release
10. Shipped

An order enters the finite-capacity production schedule only when:

- Configuration is approved.
- Drawings, BOM, and routing are released.
- Customer information is complete.
- Certification, inspection, and test requirements are known.
- Material and supplier plans are confirmed.
- Labor, tooling, inspection, and test capacity are available.
- The promise date is generated under the approved quotation rules.

## 5.2 Controlled pre-release work

Pre-release work is permitted only within the following limits:

| Activity | Permitted before final production release? | Required controls |
|---|---|---|
| Generic raw-material procurement | Yes, where specified | Frozen specification, traceability, obsolescence owner |
| Common-part procurement | Yes | Approved product family and commercial commitment |
| Customer-specific components | Normally no | Technical and commercial commitment |
| Common subassembly | Yes, if approved | Frozen interfaces and complete traceability |
| Safety-critical or configuration-dependent work | Normally no | Released design and formal change control |
| Final configuration/software | No | Configuration freeze required |
| Certified product assembly | No | Full release and approved route required |

Pre-release material and work must be clearly identified in the ERP system. A pre-release status may not be treated as full production release.

## 5.3 Configuration freeze and change control

After the defined freeze date:

- Customer changes require formal impact assessment.
- Date, cost, material, certification, and rework effects are recorded.
- The promised date may move.
- No informal deviation or “temporary” workaround is permitted.

Changes are classified as administrative, controlled process, or significant product/process changes. Significant changes require applicable engineering, quality, validation, training, document revision, traceability, and customer/regulatory notification.

# 6. Production flow, scheduling, and capacity

## 6.1 Identify the constraint

The constraint may be:

- Specialized machining or fabrication
- Skilled assembly
- Engineering release
- Certified inspection
- Final testing
- A long-lead component
- Documentation release

The constraint is determined from queue time and shipment impact, not utilization alone.

For the active constraint:

1. Prevent starvation from missing material, information, tooling, or inspection preparation.
2. Prioritize work that supports customer shipment.
3. Limit upstream release to available constraint capacity.
4. Add labor, shifts, subcontracting, equipment, or inspection capacity only when supported by data.
5. Reassess after each improvement because the constraint may move.

## 6.2 Finite-capacity scheduling

The schedule must include:

- Equipment and labor capacity
- Qualified inspectors
- Test-stand capacity
- Tooling and fixture availability
- Supplier commitments
- Planned maintenance
- Shipping windows

| Planning horizon | Operating rule |
|---|---|
| Frozen horizon | Near-term changes are restricted and recorded |
| Planning horizon | Weekly capacity, material, engineering, inspection, and test review |
| Forecast horizon | Supplier, staffing, tooling, and inventory planning |

## 6.3 WIP and batch control

For the pilot family, implement:

- Smaller controlled batches
- WIP limits by work center
- Fixed handoff windows
- Pull signals for common parts
- Visible shortage and NCR boards
- Fewer discretionary priority changes
- Parallel work only where interfaces are defined and inspection sequencing is preserved

Batch-size reductions require a setup-time and downstream-capacity review. Success is measured by total order lead time, not local utilization.

## 6.4 Overtime and expedites

Overtime or additional shifts require qualified labor, supervisor and quality coverage, calibrated equipment, fatigue controls, and shift-level quality monitoring.

Expedites require:

- Eligible product family
- Confirmed material
- Confirmed finite capacity
- Inspection and test availability
- Designated approval
- Visibility of impact on other orders
- No quality-gate bypass

Escalation deadlines require acknowledgement, containment, ownership, and communication; they do not require premature technical disposition.

# 7. Quality, certification, and measurement protections

## 7.1 Mandatory quality route

The controlled route will normally include:

1. Design/configuration release
2. Incoming material certification and inspection
3. Critical fabrication or machining inspection
4. In-process inspection
5. Assembly verification
6. Functional and final testing
7. Certified final inspection
8. Complete documentation and shipment release

Each gate defines:

- Requirement and acceptance criteria
- Required evidence
- Responsible performer
- Independent or third-party witness requirements
- Traceability
- Rework and retest requirements
- Release authority

Quality retains independent authority to stop release. No other function may override a mandatory quality or regulatory hold.

## 7.2 Reduce waiting around quality gates

Quality and Operations will:

- Schedule inspectors and test equipment against the forward production plan.
- Use daily inspection windows for standard work.
- Complete readiness checks before requesting inspection.
- Pre-stage specifications, drawings, gauges, calibration records, and forms.
- Prepare records concurrently while controlling their status.
- Add qualified inspection or test capacity only where data proves it is the constraint.
- Permit production checks only where the approved quality system allows them and required independence is preserved.

Any change to inspection location, sequence, sampling, acceptance criteria, or witness requirements requires documented quality and regulatory review.

## 7.3 Measurement-system controls

Before the pilot:

- Verify calibration of gauges, fixtures, and test equipment.
- Complete measurement-system analysis for critical characteristics.
- Confirm resolution, repeatability, reproducibility, bias, and stability as applicable.
- Define action for out-of-calibration equipment.
- Correlate alternate test stations with the approved reference.
- Record equipment identification with inspection and test results.

Electronic quality records require validated access control, revision control, audit trails, electronic-signature controls, retention, backup, and recovery. Uncontrolled spreadsheets or shared folders may not replace required certified records.

## 7.4 SPC and critical characteristics

Engineering and Quality will identify safety-, regulatory-, performance-, and warranty-critical characteristics.

The pilot control plan will define:

- Measurement method and MSA status
- Sampling frequency
- Control-chart type
- Capability expectation where applicable
- Reaction plan
- Containment boundary
- Data owner and record location

An out-of-control signal requires containment, identification of the last known acceptable unit, correction of the assignable cause, and verification before restart. Inspection frequency may not be reduced solely because average yield is high.

# 8. Supplier and inventory changes

## 8.1 Supplier actions by timeframe

### Immediate: 0–90 days

- Share rolling forecasts.
- Confirm delivery dates.
- Use blanket orders or framework agreements.
- Reserve capacity for critical standard parts.
- Improve certificate and packaging completeness.
- Establish shortage escalation.
- Hold controlled safety stock where justified.

### Medium term: 3–9 months

- Correct recurring supplier defects.
- Obtain process-capability evidence for critical characteristics.
- Improve supplier packaging, traceability, and documentation.
- Develop suppliers with repeated delivery or quality problems.

### Long term: 6–18 months

- Qualify alternate sources.
- Complete first article or validation.
- Obtain required engineering, quality, customer, and regulatory approvals.
- Monitor ramp-up by supplier lot and production cohort.

An alternate supplier may not be used for a critical component until qualification and approval are complete.

## 8.2 Supplier action register

For each critical supplier and part, record:

- Criticality
- Current median and P90 lead time
- OTIF
- Defect and certificate-error rates
- Single-source status
- Immediate action
- Owner and due date
- Expected lead-time effect
- Qualification requirement
- Contingency if the supplier fails

## 8.3 Inventory policy

Stock only items with a quantified service and financial case, such as:

- Common raw materials
- Standard fasteners and consumables
- High-runner components
- Long-lead, low-obsolescence items
- Standard certified subassemblies

For every stocked item, document:

- Demand and variability
- Supplier lead-time distribution
- Target service level
- Safety-stock and reorder-point calculation
- Min/max limits
- Working-capital requirement
- Shelf-life and obsolescence risk
- Lot traceability and inspection method
- Inventory owner and monthly review

Generic inventory and customer-specific inventory must be separately controlled.

# 9. Warranty and reliability protection

The prior warranty event is a primary design constraint for this program.

## Required controls

For each significant historical failure mode, document:

- Failure mechanism
- Manufacturing or supplier cause
- Contribution of schedule pressure
- Prevention control
- Detection control
- Required inspection/test evidence
- Pilot metric
- Stop threshold
- Required observation period

Update the PFMEA, control plan, inspection plan, and final-test coverage accordingly.

Pilot units must be tracked by:

- Serial number
- Supplier lot
- Process revision
- Shift
- Test station
- Configuration

## Leading indicators

Review weekly:

- First-pass yield
- Final-test failures by mode
- Rework hours
- Repeat inspection
- NCR recurrence and aging
- SPC signals
- Measurement-equipment exceptions
- Missing or corrected records
- Work-instruction deviations
- Supplier certificate errors
- Overtime and fatigue exposure
- Customer changes after release
- Priority changes
- Early-life failures and service calls

## Immediate stop or containment triggers

- Missed mandatory inspection or test
- Lost serial, lot, or material traceability
- Unapproved deviation, substitution, or use-as-is decision
- Uncalibrated or unsuitable measurement equipment
- Incomplete or suspect inspection evidence
- Out-of-control signal on a critical characteristic
- Invalid test result
- Safety-related nonconformance
- Incomplete shipment-release records
- Recurrence of a designated historical failure mode

# 10. Pilot design and decision gates

## Pilot scope

The pilot will cover one stable, high-volume standard product family with:

- Approved design and routing
- Established test methods
- Qualified suppliers
- Moderate certification complexity
- No unresolved major warranty trend

New designs, unusual options, unqualified suppliers, and major engineering changes are excluded unless specifically included in the pilot objective.

## Pilot entry criteria

Before production begins:

- Product-family compliance matrix is complete.
- Quality gates and release authorities are documented.
- Work instructions and control plans are released.
- Operators, inspectors, and engineers are trained.
- Gauges and test equipment are calibrated and suitable.
- ERP statuses and timestamps function correctly.
- Supplier and material controls are active.
- Baseline quality and reliability data exist.
- Rollback routing is available.

## Pilot timing

| Period | Activity |
|---|---|
| Days 0–30 | Baseline, governance, compliance, warranty analysis |
| Days 31–60 | Pilot preparation, training, controls, readiness improvements |
| Days 61–120 | Pilot execution and data collection |
| Day 90 | Interim continue/modify/stop review only |
| Day 120 or later | Operational scale decision after sample and duration criteria are met |
| Day 180 or later | Commercial expansion review with early-life reliability evidence |

## Sample and duration

The Quality/Statistics lead will set sample size using:

- Baseline variation
- Minimum improvement worth scaling
- Acceptable decision risk
- Failure-mode severity
- Required configuration, shift, supplier-lot, and test-station coverage

Twenty to thirty orders and 8–12 weeks are planning estimates, not automatic approval thresholds. The pilot must cover normal configuration variation, relevant shifts, at least one critical supplier replenishment cycle, normal inspection/test demand, and realistic operating variation.

## Scale-up gates

### Gate A: Operational scale-up

Permitted only when:

- The approved P75 or P90 lead-time target is achieved.
- On-time performance meets the defined service level.
- No mandatory quality gate is missed or bypassed.
- Required records are complete.
- First-pass yield and final-test results meet guardrails.
- SPC and measurement systems are stable.
- Supplier and constraint capacity support the intended volume.
- No critical historical failure mode recurs.

### Gate B: Full commercial expansion

Requires:

- Defined 30-, 60-, 90-, and 180-day cohort reviews where applicable
- Risk-specific reliability observation periods
- Stable early-life failure and service-call rates
- Warranty-reserve review
- Confirmation that process, supplier, and configuration changes have not increased risk

Operational scale-up does not automatically authorize the shortest customer promise for all configurations or volumes.

# 11. Quotation policy

## 11.1 Promise matrix

The quote is generated from an operations-controlled matrix using:

- Product family and configuration
- Material availability
- Supplier risk
- Engineering status
- Finite capacity
- Inspection and test loading
- Certification burden
- Customer information requirements
- Historical lead-time distribution
- Required confidence level

The matrix defines:

- Normal promise
- Eligible expedited promise
- Custom/high-risk promise
- Customer information deadline
- Configuration-freeze date
- Change-order impact
- Exception rules

Sales may communicate and commit within the matrix but may not manually select an optimistic date or override a required quality gate.

## 11.2 Quote to a defined service level

The company will explicitly define:

- Quote start point
- Calendar versus working weeks
- Treatment of customer-caused waiting
- Treatment of payment, approvals, transit, export clearance, and holidays
- Required on-time service level
- Product-family percentile used for the promise

Median lead time is an internal flow measure. A customer promise must be based on the approved P75 or P90 performance level.

A six-week promise is permitted only when the relevant family repeatedly achieves six weeks at the required service level with stable quality, supplier, capacity, inspection, test, and reliability performance.

## 11.3 Competitor comparability

Before using the competitor’s six-week figure as a benchmark, document:

- Equivalent product scope and configuration
- Certification and inspection inclusions
- Test and documentation requirements
- Customer approval assumptions
- Shipping and transit terms
- Inventory assumptions
- Actual delivery performance where available
- Warranty and service differences
- Price or expedite conditions

# 12. Governance and accountability

Members: Sales, Operations, Quality, Engineering, Supply Chain, Planning, Finance, and Service/Reliability.

The council maintains:

- Product-family lead-time standards
- Pilot decisions
- Promise matrix
- Inventory and supplier tradeoffs
- Capacity recommendations
- Quality guardrails
- Cross-functional escalations

The executive sponsor controls investment and commercial policy but cannot override mandatory quality, regulatory, safety, or certification requirements.

## Functional decision rights

| Area | Accountable function |
|---|---|
| Customer requirements and quote | Sales within the promise matrix |
| Technical configuration and design release | Engineering |
| Production sequence, WIP, and labor planning | Operations and Planning |
| Supplier commitments and inventory | Supply Chain |
| Inspection, certification, NCR disposition, and product release | Quality |
| Warranty and reliability monitoring | Service/Reliability with Quality |

| Investment and cross-functional tradeoffs | Executive Sponsor |

Each accountable role must designate a named owner, backup, due date, and data source for its deliverables.

- **Quarterly:** product-family promises, audits, warranty, and competitor benchmark

### Mandatory guardrails

A speed initiative is stopped or contained if it causes:

- Uncontrolled SPC behavior on a critical characteristic
- Increased early-life failures or service calls beyond the defined threshold
- Material deterioration in promise accuracy
- Financial exposure beyond the approved case

# 14. Rollback and containment

When a stop condition occurs:

1. Stop affected work and contain product.
2. Define affected serial, lot, supplier, shift, configuration, and process boundaries.
3. Quarantine WIP and inventory as required.
4. Notify Quality, Engineering, Operations, Supply Chain, Service, and the executive sponsor.
5. Revert to the prior approved routing, work instruction, inspection plan, and ERP status.
6. Preserve all records and identify affected shipments.
7. Notify customers, regulators, or certification bodies where required.
8. Complete root-cause and impact analysis.
9. Define corrective action and revalidation.
10. Restart only after Quality and Engineering confirm that release criteria are met.

Rollback is a controlled process, not a discretionary response to schedule pressure.

# 15. Immediate actions versus longer-term changes

## Immediate: next 30 days

- Retain the 14-week broad promise.
- Appoint council members, owners, backups, and escalation contacts.
- Select the pilot product family.
- Build the order-level baseline and loss tree.
- Analyze the prior warranty failure modes.
- Complete the product-family compliance and quality-gate matrix.
- Establish the order dashboard and common KPI definitions.
- Freeze unsafe compression practices.
- Identify initial material, inspection, test, engineering, and production constraints.

## Near term: days 31–120

- Implement order-readiness and configuration-freeze rules.
- Establish controlled pre-release statuses.
- Schedule inspectors and test equipment forward.
- Improve inspection-package readiness.
- Introduce WIP limits and smaller controlled batches.
- Correct kitting and shortage causes.
- Establish supplier delivery and documentation escalation.
- Apply measurement-system, SPC, and reaction-plan controls.
- Run the controlled pilot.

## Longer term: months 4–18

- Scale proven standard work.
- Qualify alternate suppliers.
- Add targeted inspection, test, tooling, labor, or equipment capacity.
- Expand modular design and postponement.
- Implement validated electronic quality records.
- Replace blanket buffers with product-family percentile rules.
- Rebalance inventory using actual supplier variability.
- Evaluate 6–8-week promises selectively after reliability evidence is available.

---

## Final recommendation

Approve the diagnostic program, governance structure, quality-preserving process changes, and controlled standard-family pilot.

Do **not** approve an immediate general promise of 10, 8, or 6 weeks. Customer-facing reductions become effective only when the applicable product family meets its defined percentile, on-time, quality, supplier, capacity, financial, and reliability criteria.

The company’s fastest path is not to weaken certified work. It is to ensure that work starts with complete information, materials are ready, constraints are scheduled realistically, inspections are planned rather than awaited, defects are prevented rather than reworked, and only demonstrated performance is converted into a customer promise.
