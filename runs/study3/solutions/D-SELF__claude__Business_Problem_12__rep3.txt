## PART 1: GOVERNANCE FOUNDATION

### PRINCIPLE 1: COMMUNITY SOVEREIGNTY WITH BINDING AUTHORITY

**What This Means:** Indigenous and local communities retain binding decision-making power over all tourism development affecting their lands, heritage, and livelihoods.

#### Mechanism 1A: Free Prior Informed Consent (FPIC) Protocol

**How It Works:**

```
6-MONTH PROCESS FOR EVERY TOURISM DEVELOPMENT

MONTH 1-2: Community Identification & Notification
├── Identify all affected communities (5km radius + cultural claims)
├── Deliver written notice in local language
├── Arrange accessible community meeting (transportation, meals, childcare provided)
└── Budget: $170,000/year (FPIC Coordinator + facilitators)

MONTH 2-4: Information Sharing & Consultation
├── Provide comprehensive information package:
│   ├── Project description (what, where, how many visitors)
│   ├── Environmental impact assessment
│   ├── Cultural impact assessment
│   ├── Economic analysis (revenue projections, job estimates)
│   ├── Risk analysis (climate, market, social risks)
│   └── Operator background (track record, financials)
├── Hold 3 separate community meetings (not single event)
├── Conduct separate vulnerable group consultations (elders, women, youth, minorities)
└── Fund comparative site visits (delegations visit other tourism projects)

MONTH 4-6: Community Deliberation & Formal Consent
├── Communities given 6-8 weeks to decide internally (following their traditions)
├── Document all viewpoints (support, concerns, conditions)
├── Issue formal written consent resolution (legally binding contract)
│   └── Specifies: revenue-sharing %, employment commitments, cultural protocols, enforcement
└── **Community can say "NO"** (project doesn't proceed if voted down)

ONGOING: Monitoring & Enforcement
├── Quarterly compliance checks (verify promised conditions met)
├── Annual community-operator review
└── Violation penalties: $5K fine (Level 1) → $50K fine (Level 3) → License revocation (Level 4)
```

**Enforcement Authority:** Community Heritage Council (Section 1B)
**Budget:** $170,000/year (0.3% of tourism revenue)

#### Mechanism 1B: Community Heritage Council

**Governance Structure:**

```
15-MEMBER COUNCIL WITH BINDING AUTHORITY

Composition:
├── 5 Cultural Knowledge Holders (master practitioners, selected by community)
├── 5 Community Representatives (elected by community assembly)
├── 3 Indigenous/Minority Representatives (appointed by indigenous councils)
└── 1 Independent Expert (heritage professional, appointed by Council)

DECISION-MAKING AUTHORITY (Binding - Not Advisory):
├── Approve/reject all cultural tourism activities
├── Establish & enforce cultural protocols (sacred site access, photography rules, etc.)
├── Control heritage interpretation (communities tell their own stories)
├── Approve traditional knowledge licensing (retain ownership, ensure fair compensation)
├── Set cultural practitioner minimum rates
└── Revoke licenses for protocol violations

ACCOUNTABILITY:
├── Community recall: 20% petition triggers member replacement
├── Annual audit: External assessment of Council performance
├── Monthly public meetings: Decisions documented, voting records published
└── Quarterly community assemblies: Council reports to communities

BUDGET & STAFFING:
├── Executive Director: $40,000/year
├── Support staff (4 positions): $80,000/year
├── Operations (meetings, monitoring, documentation): $145,000/year
└── **TOTAL: $265,000/year** (3% of tourism revenue - grows proportionally)
```

**Key Authority: Indigenous Veto Protection**
- Indigenous representatives cannot be outvoted on decisions affecting "their" heritage
- Prevents cultural majority from dominating cultural minority
- Unique protection at international standards level

### PRINCIPLE 2: ENVIRONMENTAL INTEGRITY WITH ENFORCED CARRYING CAPACITY

**What This Means:** Ecosystem health is not negotiable. Strict visitor capacity limits are enforced with consequences for violations.

#### Carrying Capacity Limits (Legally Enforced)

**Science-Based Visitor Limits by Ecosystem:**

```
ALPINE TREKKING AREAS
├── Current: 15,000 visitors/year
├── Sustainable Limit: 12,000 visitors/year (soil erosion threshold)
├── Distribution: 60/day peak (May-Sept) → 35/day shoulder → 6/day off-season
├── Enforcement: Electronic counters, advance permits ($25 peak, $10 off-season)
├── Auto-closures: If daily capacity reached, site closed (no entry)
└── Penalty for exceeding: $500 fine (1st) → $2,000 (2nd) → License revoked (3rd)

MARINE & COASTAL SITES
├── Current: 25,000 visitors/year
├── Sustainable Limit: 42,000 visitors/year (distributed across sites & seasons)
│   ├── Diving: 1,000/year per reef site (max 3,000 total)
│   ├── Snorkeling: 3,000/year per beach (max 24,000 total)
│   └── Casual beach: 15,000/year (distributed)
├── Enforcement: Mooring buoys (eliminate anchor damage), water quality monitoring
├── Auto-closures: If coral cover <25% or fish biomass <80 kg/hectare, site closes
└── Same penalty structure as alpine zones

ARCHAEOLOGICAL SITES
├── Current: 18,000 visitors/year
├── Sustainable Limit: 19,900 visitors/year
├── Enforcement: Timed entry permits, structural integrity monitoring, heritage wardens
└── Auto-closures: If stone erosion >0.3mm/year, site closes for restoration

TOTAL REGIONAL CAPACITY: 96,900 visitors/year
Plan growth target: 88,200 visitors/year (ACHIEVABLE within capacity)
```

**Enforcement Structure:**

```
NAMED OFFICERS WITH AUTHORITY & ACCOUNTABILITY

3 Zone Wardens (Alpine, Marine, Archaeological):
├── Responsibility: Daily capacity monitoring, environmental sampling, violation citations
├── Authority: Can turn away visitors if daily limit reached
├── Salary: $35,000/year each + equipment
├── Accountability: Environmental Council + Community Committees (monthly review)
└── Dismissal authority: Communities can request removal if unresponsive

Environmental Council (Regional Oversight):
├── Composition: 7-9 members (scientists, environmental NGOs, communities, government)
├── Authority: Approve capacity limits, adjust if monitoring shows problems
├── Power: Can recommend site closure for ecological protection
├── Meetings: Quarterly (public, open observation)
└── Budget: $50,000/year

Community Monitoring Committees (1 per tourism zone):
├── Authority: Community oversight of wardens & enforcement
├── Power: Can report violations, recommend warden removal
├── Function: Monthly environmental reviews, quarterly community updates
└── Escalation: Can report to Environmental Council
```

**AUTOMATIC ADJUSTMENT TRIGGERS:**

If environmental thresholds breached, capacity automatically REDUCED (not negotiated):
- Soil erosion >1.2 tons/hectare/year → Reduce Alpine capacity 1,000 visitors
- Coral cover <25% → Marine site closure for season
- Fish biomass <80 kg/hectare → Fishing-free zone + tourist access restricted
- Stone erosion >0.3mm/year → Archaeological site closure

### PRINCIPLE 4: CULTURAL PRESERVATION WITH COMMUNITY CONTROL

**What This Means:** Tourism involving cultural heritage is designed, managed, and benefited from by cultural knowledge holders—not external operators.

#### Sacred & Sensitive Site Classification

**Protection Framework:**

```
SITE CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM (Community Determines)

SACRED SITES (No Tourism Allowed - Period)
├── Definition: Spiritually/ceremonially important to communities
├── Access: Community ceremonial use ONLY
├── Tourism: PROHIBITED (no revenue-sharing justifies commodification)
├── Enforcement: Trespassing = criminal penalty
├── Examples: Burial grounds, initiation sites, spiritual sanctuary
└── Community authority: Absolute (can never be opened to tourism)

LIMITED-ACCESS SITES (Tourism Allowed with Community Conditions)
├── Access: Conditional (requires community approval)
├── Tourism requirement: 80%+ community support + Heritage Council approval
├── Community controls: Visitor limits, seasonal access, protocols
├── Revenue-sharing: 50%+ of revenue to community
├── Requirements:
│   ├── Visitor code of conduct (mandatory briefing)
│   ├── Photography/video restrictions
│   ├── Dress code expectations
│   ├── Behavioral standards (quiet, respect)
│   ├── Tour guide credentials (heritage training certification)
│   ├── Maximum visitor numbers (community-set)
│   └── Seasonal restrictions (if appropriate)
├── Enforcement: Heritage Wardens monitor compliance
└── Penalties: Visitor removal, operator fines, license suspension

OPEN SITES (Tourism Permitted, Community Guidelines)
├── Access: Public, tourism permitted
├── Community involvement: Still required
├── Benefits: Still shared with communities
├── Standards: Still enforced (not uncontrolled tourism)
└── Examples: Public beaches, day-trip archaeological sites, viewpoints
```
