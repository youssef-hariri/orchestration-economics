# Board Decision Document: Transition to Subscription Revenue

## Executive recommendation

Approve a **phased, renewal-led transition** from perpetual licenses to subscriptions. Do not terminate or impair existing contractual rights, and do not make an abrupt portfolio-wide switch.

The company should:

1. Preserve each customer’s contractual perpetual-license, support, maintenance, security, and upgrade rights.
2. Maintain a credible path for new perpetual sales for **24–36 months**, while distinguishing that commercial window from existing contractual rights.
3. Make subscription materially better through cloud operations, security, faster innovation, flexibility, analytics, and customer success—not merely through payment timing.
4. Offer standard, cloud, hybrid, bridge, private-cloud, and customer-hosted options.
5. Prioritize technically ready, renewal-ready, high-value cohorts.
6. Use capped, time-limited migration credits, price protection, dual-use periods, and implementation support.
7. Redesign sales compensation before external launch around net, activated, retained, profitable ARR.
8. Fund the modeled revenue, gross-profit, and cash trough under the downside case.
9. Report accounting revenue, ARR, bookings, billings, cash flow, and remaining performance obligations separately where relevant.
10. Use explicit customer, financial, operational, and liquidity triggers before accelerating or restricting perpetual expansion.

The objective is to maximize **retained lifetime gross profit and installed-base value**, not short-term subscription bookings.

---

# 1. Board decisions requested

The board is asked to approve:

- The phased, renewal-led transition strategy.
- Phase 0 readiness work and controlled customer pilots.
- The four-offer architecture described below.
- A 24–36-month commercial continuity period for new perpetual offers, subject to product and segment review.
- A capped migration-credit envelope, with final amounts subject to approved unit-economic thresholds.
- Redesign of sales compensation before external rollout.
- A downside-funded liquidity plan.
- Quarterly board reporting against the KPI and trigger framework.
- Conditional authority to scale the program only after pilot, retention, activation, margin, and liquidity gates are met.

Broad restrictions on new perpetual sales, external numerical guidance, and large fixed cloud investments should remain conditional on completion of the readiness requirements.

# 2. Strategic objectives and guardrails

## Objectives

Over a five-year transition, the company intends to:

- Increase recurring revenue and reduce revenue volatility.
- Preserve the installed base and avoid a competitor-style loss of approximately 20%.
- Establish scalable subscription, cloud, implementation, renewal, and customer-success capabilities.
- Improve forecastability while maintaining acceptable gross and contribution margins.
- Create repeatable migration motions by customer segment.
- Finance the transition without impairing core operations.
- Give the board and investors objective evidence for continuing, slowing, or redesigning the program.

## Non-negotiable guardrails

1. **No retroactive loss of rights.** Existing contractual rights remain intact.
2. **No coercive migration.** Supported perpetual products must not be deliberately degraded solely to force conversion.
3. **No relabeling.** Maintenance, subscription, ARR, bookings, billings, recognized revenue, cash flow, and remaining performance obligations remain distinct measures.
4. **No unprofitable ARR.** Deals must meet approved margin, implementation, collection, and payback thresholds.
5. **No broad rollout before readiness.** Product, security, migration, support, accounting, billing, and liquidity requirements must be met.
6. **No universal deadline before evidence.** Any future restriction on new perpetual sales must be product-specific, published, contract-compliant, and board-approved.
7. **No reliance on universal annual prepayment.** The base case must include quarterly and monthly billing.
8. **No full quota credit for gross multi-year TCV.**
9. **No use of unactivated or highly cancellable ARR as equivalent to healthy ARR.**
10. **No legal or accounting assurances in investor messaging** until reviewed under the applicable contracts and reporting framework.

# 4. Offer architecture

A product-level entitlement matrix must be approved before launch. The term “conversion” must not be used generically; each contract must state whether perpetual rights are retained, suspended, or voluntarily surrendered.

## 4.1 Perpetual Continuity

Available as a new commercial path for at least **24–36 months**, subject to product and segment review.

May include, subject to the applicable contract:

- Continued use of purchased perpetual licenses.
- Annual support and maintenance.
- Contractual bug fixes and security updates.
- Contractual upgrade rights.
- Additional perpetual licenses where approved.
- Published product lifecycle dates.

It does not automatically include:

- Subscription-only modules.
- Cloud hosting.
- New services.
- Unlimited users, sites, or capacity.
- Functionality never covered by contract.

The 24–36-month period governs availability of new commercial perpetual offers; it does not shorten existing contractual rights.

## 4.2 Support and maintenance

A separate annual service that may include:

- Technical support.
- Bug fixes and security patches.
- Maintenance releases.
- Documentation.
- Contracted service levels.
- Upgrade access where applicable.

Support must not be represented as a subscription or as surrender of perpetual ownership.

## 4.3 Subscription Standard

A time-limited entitlement during the paid term to:

- Use the current software.
- Receive releases during the term.
- Obtain support and maintenance.
- Access included integrations and reporting.
- Receive defined customer-success services.
- Add hosting if separately selected.

Offer one-year terms for flexibility and three-year terms with annual billing for most migration customers.

## 4.4 Cloud/Premium Subscription

Includes:

- Hosted operations.
- Monitoring, backup, and recovery.
- Security controls.
- Availability commitments.
- Premium support.
- Advanced analytics, automation, or AI capabilities.
- Migration and onboarding assistance.
- Data export and termination assistance.

Contracts must specify service levels, data ownership, export format and timing, security responsibilities, subprocessors, service credits, price changes, and termination assistance.

## 4.5 Hybrid and bridge offers

Standard options include:

- Converting selected sites, workloads, users, or modules.
- Retaining perpetual deployments for other workloads.
- Managed hosting of a perpetual deployment.
- A six- to twelve-month dual-use period.
- A temporary bridge subscription during migration.
- Private-cloud or customer-hosted subscription.
- A defined conversion bank for future capacity.

These options are particularly important for strategic, regulated, customized, and technically complex customers.

# 5. Customer segmentation and migration sequence

## Priority segments

### Strategic enterprise accounts

Use executive sponsors, named migration teams, individual two- to three-year roadmaps, hybrid or private-cloud options, and negotiated implementation support. Avoid artificial deadlines.

### High-support-cost or technically aged customers

Prioritize standardized cloud or managed subscription, remediation, and data migration. Require technical-readiness approval and link incentives to activation.

### Mid-market and growth customers

Use standard packages, simple contracts, fixed-scope implementation, guided pilots, and annual or three-year terms. This should be the first broad rollout cohort.

### Stable, low-touch perpetual customers

Maintain a transparent perpetual-plus-maintenance path and present subscription at renewal. Do not force migration where customer value is weak.

### Regulated, government, and security-sensitive customers

Offer private cloud, customer-hosted subscription, data-residency options, and longer transition windows where procurement requirements dictate.

### Channel-led customers

Provide partners with recurring renewal economics, migration and implementation opportunities, account protection, and clear direct-versus-channel ownership rules.

### Do-not-migrate-yet accounts

Exclude accounts with unresolved rights, poor customer health, severe customization, product or security gaps, data-migration risk, imminent competitive risk, or insufficient procurement readiness. These accounts require a retention or remediation plan.

## Account prioritization

Use an initial readiness score, subject to pilot validation:

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Revenue and gross profit | 20 |
| Renewal or upgrade timing | 15 |
| Technical readiness | 15 |
| Customer willingness and value case | 15 |
| Competitive displacement risk | 10 |
| Expansion potential | 10 |
| Support-cost opportunity | 5 |
| Contract simplicity | 5 |
| Reference value | 5 |

Legal exposure, security readiness, customer health, and procurement constraints override the score.

# 7. Migration incentives and pricing

## Recommended migration package

For eligible customers:

- Support or upgrade credit.
- Price protection during the initial term.
- Migration assessment.
- Data conversion and integration assistance.
- Six- to twelve-month dual use.
- Co-termed renewal dates.
- Flexible capacity ramp.
- Selected subscription-only features with genuine incremental value.
- Named customer-success ownership.
- Defined downgrade, rollback, and transition assistance.

## Credit waterfall

1. Determine gross subscription price.
2. Apply standard discount.
3. Apply support or upgrade credit.
4. Apply approved license-conversion credit.
5. Calculate net ACV.
6. Test contribution margin, payback, and cash impact.
7. Reject or redesign any deal that fails approved economic floors.

Initial test bands, not final policy:

- Credit **50%–100% of the most recent annual support payment** against year-one subscription fees.
- Credit **10%–30% of eligible recent perpetual purchases** against years one and two.
- Cap total credits at the lower of an approved percentage of first-year fees or the amount consistent with contribution-margin and payback floors.

Credits should be time-limited, noncash, and generally nontransferable. Eligibility must be based on an identifiable economic rationale, such as unused capacity, a replaced planned upgrade, or a voluntary rights surrender—not all historical license spend.

## Pricing

Start with segment-specific testing around:

- Software-only subscription: **20%–22% of comparable perpetual economic value per year**.
- Cloud/Premium: **15%–30% above software-only subscription**, depending on infrastructure and service levels.
- Perpetual maintenance: Continue under applicable contracts and approved normal pricing policy.

| Pricing band | Likely effect | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 22%–25% | Slower adoption; stronger realization and margin | Proven value or lower switching cost |
| 20%–22% | Balanced adoption, margin, and trough | **Recommended starting band** |
| 17%–20% | Faster initial adoption; greater discount and renewal risk | Controlled pilots or strategic cases only |

Use:

\[
Expected\ Lifetime\ Gross\ Profit =
ARR \times Gross\ Margin \times Expected\ Retention\ Life
- Credits - Implementation\ Cost - Incremental\ Support\ Cost
\]

Price protection should apply through the initial two- or three-year term, with a defined renewal-increase cap for committed scope. At signing, disclose promotional pricing, expected renewal pricing, credit expiry, minimum commitment, capacity assumptions, and downgrade or termination rights.

# 8. Financial model and scenario framework

Finance must produce:

- A quarterly five-year cohort model.
- A monthly 36-month cash forecast.
- Separate revenue, gross-profit, contribution-profit, and cash troughs.
- A reconciliation to historical financial statements.

Model by customer cohort, segment, product, and offer:

- Perpetual licenses.
- Support and maintenance.
- Upgrade revenue.
- Subscription conversions.
- New-logo subscription.
- Cloud revenue.
- Professional services.
- Churn, contraction, and expansion.
- Credits and discounts.
- Implementation and cloud costs.
- Sales commissions.
- Billings, collections, deferred revenue, and remaining performance obligations where relevant.

## Required distinctions

\[
ARR \neq Bookings \neq Billings \neq Cash \neq Recognized\ Revenue
\]

- **Bookings:** Contracted commercial commitments.
- **ARR:** Annualized recurring value, not total contract value.
- **Billings:** Amount invoiced under contract terms.
- **Cash flow:** Collections less cash operating, implementation, commission, and other cash costs.
- **Recognized revenue:** Revenue recognized under the applicable accounting framework.
- **Remaining performance obligations:** Contracted obligations for goods or services not yet recognized as revenue, where applicable.

## Core formulas

\[
Ending\ ARR_t =
Beginning\ ARR_t
- Churn_t
- Contraction_t
+ Expansion_t
+ Converted\ ARR_t
+ New\ Logo\ ARR_t
\]

\[
Converted\ ARR_t =
Eligible\ Base_t
\times Conversion\ Rate_t
\times Scope\ Retained_t
\times Price\ Realization_t
\times (1-Discount_t)
\]

\[
GRR_t = 1 - Logo\ Churn_t - Contraction_t
\]

\[
NRR_t =
\frac{Beginning\ ARR - Churn - Contraction + Expansion}
{Beginning\ ARR}
\]

## Illustrative scenarios

These are internal planning cases, not forecasts.

### Base case: Balanced, renewal-led transition

- Perpetual expansion remains available for 24–36 months.
- Conversion occurs mainly at renewal or upgrade.
- 25%–40% of eligible installed-base ARR converts by year three.
- Migration churn is 1–3 percentage points above normal initially.
- GRR improves from the high-80s to the low-90s.
- NRR reaches 100%–105% after initial renewal cohorts.
- Net credits are 5%–15% of first-year ACV.
- Annual upfront billing is 50%–70%.
- Cloud gross margin reaches the low-to-mid 60% range after ramp.

| Year | Revenue | Recurring mix |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | $132M–$138M | 48%–53% |
| 2 | $125M–$135M | 56%–63% |
| 3 | $132M–$145M | 64%–70% |
| 4 | $145M–$165M | 72%–78% |
| 5 | $165M–$190M | 78%–84% |

Illustrative revenue trough: **5%–15% below baseline**, lasting **6–10 quarters**.

### Upside case: Value-led early adoption

- 40%–55% conversion by year three.
- Migration churn no higher than normal.
- NRR of 105%–115%.
- New-logo subscription bookings growth of 25%–40% annually.
- Lower credits and stronger annual prepayment.
- Cloud gross margin in the high-60s to low-70s.

Illustrative revenue trough: **0%–8% below baseline**, lasting **3–6 quarters**.

### Downside case: High cannibalization and weak renewal

- 15%–30% conversion by year three.
- Migration churn 5–10 points above normal.
- GRR in the low-to-mid 80s and NRR below 100%.
- Credits and discounts of 15%–30% of first-year ACV.
- Scope retention of 60%–80%.
- Annual upfront billing of 30%–50%.
- Cloud gross margin below 55%.
- Implementation and sales capacity overloaded.

Illustrative revenue trough: **15%–30% below baseline**, lasting **8–14 quarters**. Cash may trough more deeply than revenue.

The liquidity reserve must be based on the downside case, not the base case.

# 9. Sales compensation redesign

Compensation must change before customer launch.

## Scorecard

| Component | Transition year | Steady state |
|---|---:|---:|
| New subscription ARR/ACV | 50% | 55% |
| Renewal and expansion | 15% | 20% |
| Perpetual and maintenance revenue | 20% | 10% |
| Margin and deal quality | 10% | 10% |
| Strategic objectives | 5% | 5% |

## Compensation rules

- First-year **net** subscription ARR receives full quota credit.
- Future-year value receives a lower weighted credit.
- Gross TCV does not receive full quota credit.
- Services revenue does not inflate subscription ARR.
- Credits and discounts reduce commissionable value.
- Cloud commissions may be based on margin-adjusted ARR.
- Ten to twenty percent of commission is held back until defined activation, collection, and quality milestones.
- Renewal and expansion credit is allocated explicitly among sales, customer success, renewals, and partners.
- Provide 12–18 months of transition protection, including approved migration quotas and partial income guarantees.

## Illustrative example

For a three-year contract with:

- Gross annual recurring value: $100,000.
- Gross TCV: $300,000.
- Approved discount and credit: $20,000.
- Net first-year ACV: $80,000.
- Future-year quota factor: 40%.

Quota credit is:

- Year one: $80,000.
- Years two and three: $80,000 × 40% × 2 = $64,000.
- Total: **$144,000**, not $300,000.

The compensation plan must define:

- Gross ACV.
- Net ACV.
- Qualified ARR.
- Activated ARR.
- Minimum committed ARR.
- At-risk ARR.
- Quota-credit ARR.

Clawbacks should distinguish ordinary contraction, nonpayment, misconduct, unauthorized discounting, and company-caused product or implementation failure.

Communicate consistently that:

- Existing perpetual rights are protected.
- Subscription adds product, operating, security, and flexibility benefits.
- The transition may temporarily reduce recognized revenue and margins.
- ARR may improve while revenue remains below baseline.
- Cash may improve before revenue through billing timing, or deteriorate despite ARR growth through implementation costs, commissions, weak collections, or refunds.
- The downside case is funded.
- Success is measured by retained, activated, profitable ARR and customer outcomes.
- Numerical scenarios are internal planning cases unless formally disclosed as guidance.
- Management will slow or redesign the program if retention, activation, margin, product readiness, or liquidity deteriorate.

- Security and privacy incidents.

## Initial governance bands

These are proposed starting thresholds and should be recalibrated after pilots.

| Metric | Green | Amber | Red/action |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Subscription GRR | ≥90% | 85%–<90% | <85% for two quarters: pause affected segment |
| First-renewal GRR | ≥90% | 85%–<90% | <85%: redesign pricing, onboarding, or scope |
| Migration churn uplift versus matched control | ≤3 pts | >3–5 pts | >5 pts: pause segment and restore continuity |
| Activated sold capacity | ≥90% | 80%–<90% | <80%: restrict new sales until delivery improves |
| Cloud gross margin after ramp | ≥60% | 50%–<60% | <50%: reprice, redesign, or limit offer |
| Migration payback | ≤24 months | >24–30 months | >30 months: tighten credits and compensation |
| Nonstandard deals | ≤15% | >15%–25% | >25%: simplify packages and approvals |
| Liquidity reserve | Above approved reserve | Within 10% | Below reserve: freeze expansion and activate cash plan |

Use immediate escalation for security or privacy incidents, data loss, material rights disputes, covenant risk, liquidity below minimum reserve, and material service-level failures.

If perpetual revenue or bookings decline materially faster than **activated** subscription replacement, restore or extend the perpetual offer. Subscription bookings alone do not justify restricting perpetual expansion.

# 12. Roadmap and exit criteria

## Phase 0: Design and readiness — months 0–3

Complete:

- Historical baseline reconciliation.
- Contract-rights inventory.
- Product entitlement matrix.
- Customer segmentation and scoring.
- Customer TCO and competitive analysis.
- Quarterly cohort model.
- Monthly liquidity model.
- Pricing and credit test bands.
- Accounting position paper.
- Offer and contract templates.
- Compensation plan.
- Partner plan.
- Billing and reporting changes.
- Customer, investor, employee, lender, and regulator communications.
- Rollback procedures.

**Exit criteria:** Downside case is financeable; rights exposure is resolved; compensation is approved; product, cloud, billing, reporting, and customer-remedy plans are ready.

## Phase 1: Design-partner pilots — months 3–9

Use 20–50 customers across favorable, skeptical, complex, regulated, and channel-led cohorts.

Test:

- Price and credit elasticity.
- Customer TCO.
- Migration duration.
- Activation.
- Support burden.
- Cloud and implementation margin.
- Renewal intent.
- Discounting.
- Customer outcomes.
- Sales productivity.

Use matched non-migrated customers where practical.

**Exit criteria:** Activation at least 80%; migration failure within tolerance; customer outcomes demonstrated; unit economics meet payback floors; no material rights disputes; implementation capacity sufficient.

## Phase 2: Controlled installed-base rollout — months 9–18

Prioritize renewal-ready customers, cloud-requesting customers, aged or high-support-cost accounts, standard mid-market accounts, and customers with clear operational, security, or innovation needs.

## Phase 3: Scale and rationalize — months 18–36

- Expand to additional segments.
- Reduce credits gradually.
- Standardize implementation.
- Shift sales capacity toward renewals and expansion.
- Concentrate differentiated new modules in subscription where appropriate.
- Stage infrastructure against activated demand.
- Reassess perpetual expansion by product and segment.
- Validate first-renewal economics before reducing price protection.

## Phase 4: Steady state — after month 36

Subject to board approval and cohort evidence:

- Retain continuity for strategic, regulated, or contractually constrained customers.
- Restrict new perpetual sales to defined exceptions.
- Publish any future end-of-sale policy with adequate notice.
- Maintain all existing contractual rights.
- Operate primarily through subscriptions, renewals, expansion, cloud services, and recurring support.

---

# Final recommendation

Approve the **balanced, renewal-led transition** and Phase 0 readiness program.

The company should preserve perpetual rights, maintain 24–36 months of commercial continuity, offer multiple migration paths, use capped incentives, test pricing near 20%–22% of comparable perpetual economic value, compensate sellers on net activated and retained ARR, and fund the downside revenue, gross-profit, and cash trough.

This approach accepts temporary financial pressure in exchange for protecting customer trust, limiting installed-base loss, validating economics by cohort, and building a durable subscription business rather than merely relabeling perpetual revenue.
