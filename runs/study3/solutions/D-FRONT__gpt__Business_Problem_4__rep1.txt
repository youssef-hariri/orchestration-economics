# KOMOREBI SNACKS  
## Everyday Japanese flavor, three distinctive ways to crunch

### Target-market insight

**Evidence-based observation:** Japan has an established packaged-snack culture, frequent flavor innovation, strong convenience-store distribution, and broad familiarity with rice, miso, nori, edamame, kombu, ume, yuzu, and sesame.

**Market opportunity hypothesis:** Urban working adults may respond to portable, accessible-premium snacks that combine recognizable Japanese flavor logic with clearly differentiated textures—without feeling like souvenir food, health food, or complicated fusion.

**Initial target:** Urban working adults aged 25–39 in Tokyo, with Osaka and Nagoya as follow-on test markets. Primary occasions are weekday commuting, desk breaks, between-meal hunger, and convenience-store impulse purchases.

---

## Product-line platform

### Brand promise  
**Familiar Japanese flavors. Distinctive everyday textures.**

The line is unified by:

- Selective, accurate use of Japanese flavor references
- Clear separation between products through texture and eating experience
- Single-serve packs for immediate consumption
- Resealable packs for home and office use
- Contemporary packaging without tourist imagery
- Everyday positioning rather than wellness or luxury claims

The platform does not require every product to be rice-based. Its coherence comes from **Japanese flavor logic plus texture contrast**.

## Launch concepts

| Concept | Format and sensory experience | Flavor direction | Primary consumer and occasion | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nori & White Miso Thin Rice Crisps** | Thin, lightly ridged toasted rice crisps with a clean snap | Toasted rice → rounded white miso → toasted nori | Working adults; desk breaks, commuting, convenience-store impulse | Familiar ingredients create a low trial barrier; the thin structure and flavor progression provide the innovation |
| **Roasted Edamame with Kombu Seaweed** | Whole roasted edamame with a firm, satisfying bite | Edamame, kombu umami, subtle savory-sweet seasoning | Adults seeking a more substantial snack; between-meal hunger, desk use, travel | Adds a legume-led format and stronger satiety cue without relying on unverified protein claims |
| **Tangy Ume Salt Corn Puffs** | Air-puffed corn pillows with a fast-melting crunch | Ume, salt, balanced acidity, light sweetness | Younger urban impulse shoppers; convenience stores, gaming, movies, gatherings | Provides the clearest contrast in flavor and texture through a bright sour-salty profile |
| **Yuzu Black Sesame Soft Rice Bites** *(follow-on)* | Individually separated soft-chewy rice bites | Bright yuzu and roasted black sesame | Premium confectionery shoppers; tea breaks and small indulgences | High novelty and premium potential, but texture, shelf life, and safety require further validation |
| **Roasted Sweet Potato & Black Sesame Clusters** *(deferred)* | Crisp clusters with tender sweet-potato pieces | Roasted sweet potato, black sesame, light sweetness | Premium sweet-snack shoppers; afternoon and tea occasions | More focused than the original matcha version, but competes with granola and snack bars and may require a separate manufacturing route |

### Product-language principles

- Use plain-language descriptors alongside terms such as **ume** and **kombu** where needed.
- Do not use “healthy,” “portion-controlled,” “high-protein,” “plant-based,” or “gluten-free” unless the final formulation and Japanese requirements support the claim.
- Confirm all soy, sesame, wheat, nut, dairy, and seafood-derived ingredients before packaging.
- Treat **KOMOREBI SNACKS** as a working name pending Japanese-language name testing and trademark screening.

## Recommended launch architecture

### Core launch: three SKUs

1. **Nori & White Miso Thin Rice Crisps**  
   The accessible flagship and strongest everyday product.

2. **Roasted Edamame with Kombu Seaweed**  
   The substantial savory option, differentiated by its legume base and roasted texture.

3. **Tangy Ume Salt Corn Puffs**  
   The bold impulse product, adding sourness and an airy format to the range.

Together, the products create a clear texture ladder:

> **Clean snap → firm roasted bite → airy melt**

They also cover three purchase motivations:

- **Familiarity:** rice, white miso, and nori  
- **Substance:** roasted edamame and kombu  
- **Adventure:** tangy ume and corn-puff texture  

### Follow-on strategy

- Develop **Yuzu Black Sesame Soft Rice Bites** as a premium or limited-edition product after safety, texture, shelf-life, and repeat-purchase validation.
- Defer **Sweet Potato & Black Sesame Clusters** until its occasion, economics, and competitive role are clearer.
- Decide whether Ume Puffs are permanent or seasonal based on repeat purchase and retail velocity—not trial alone.

## Initial pack and channel direction

| Product | Initial pack | Priority channels |
|---|---|---|
| Rice Crisps | 28–32 g single serve; five-pack or 100–120 g resealable pouch | Convenience stores, supermarkets, drugstores, online |
| Roasted Edamame | 30–35 g single serve; 90–120 g resealable pouch | Convenience stores, supermarkets, drugstores, online |
| Ume Corn Puffs | 25–30 g single serve; optional multipack | Convenience stores, supermarkets, drugstores, kiosks |
| Soft Rice Bites | Six to eight separated or wrapped pieces | Premium convenience, supermarkets, specialty confectionery |
| Sweet-Potato Clusters | 30–35 g pocket pack; larger pouch if validated | Supermarkets, premium grocery, seasonal channels |

Packaging should use strong product color-coding, appetizing ingredient imagery, clear texture cues, and Japanese-first or bilingual naming. Avoid generic cultural symbols such as Mount Fuji, torii gates, fans, or excessive cherry blossoms.

## Ranked launch recommendation

| Rank | Concept | Recommendation | Rationale |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | Nori & White Miso Thin Rice Crisps | **Launch first** | Broadest familiarity, strongest everyday occasion, and comparatively straightforward manufacturing |
| **2** | Tangy Ume Salt Corn Puffs | **Include in core launch** | Distinctive flavor and texture with manageable technical complexity; useful for trial and younger shoppers |
| **3** | Roasted Edamame with Kombu Seaweed | **Include in core launch** | Gives the range a substantial, legume-led product; hardness, value, and oxidation require testing |
| **4** | Yuzu Black Sesame Soft Rice Bites | **Controlled follow-on** | Strong premium differentiation, but chewy texture and safety requirements create greater risk |
| **5** | Sweet Potato & Black Sesame Clusters | **Defer or reformulate** | Clearer than the original concept but less central, more competitive, and potentially less efficient to manufacture |

The ranking is a development hypothesis, not established market evidence. No product should proceed if it fails safety, regulatory, quality, cost, shelf-life, or repeat-purchase requirements.

## Concise validation plan

### 1. Competitive and retail assessment
Audit Japanese convenience stores, supermarkets, drugstores, and online retailers in Tokyo, Osaka, and Nagoya. Benchmark:

- Senbei, rice crackers, and arare
- Potato and corn snacks
- Roasted edamame, beans, peas, soy, and nuts
- Ume- and kombu-flavored products
- Packaged mochi and soft confectionery
- Granola clusters and snack bars

Capture pack size, price, price per 10 g, category terminology, texture, flavor intensity, claims, and shelf visibility.

### 2. Consumer and naming research
Conduct Japanese-language qualitative research with target consumers to test:

- Flavor intensity and comprehension
- Meaning of “light,” “substantial,” and “premium”
- Desk and commuting occasions
- Pack-size adequacy and value
- “KOMOREBI” versus alternative brand names
- Japanese, English, and bilingual product names
- Domestic, imported, premium, childish, or souvenir perceptions

### 3. Quantitative concept and price testing
Use a Japan-based sample aligned to the primary and secondary target segments. Test:

- Concept appeal
- Purchase and repeat-purchase intent
- Format and flavor trade-offs
- Pack size and price
- Competitive alternatives
- Dietary and product-claim interpretation

A realistic choice exercise should be used alongside stated willingness to pay.

### 4. Sensory and in-home testing
Test each launch SKU blind and branded against relevant Japanese competitors. Measure:

- Overall liking
- Flavor clarity and intensity
- Texture liking
- Satisfaction and portion adequacy
- Purchase intent and repeat purchase
- Desk-use factors such as noise, aroma, powder, and mess

Place the three products in homes for one to two weeks to observe actual occasions, freshness, pack convenience, substitution, and repeat behavior.

### 5. Technical, regulatory, and commercial gates
Before package lock or commercial claims, confirm:

- Japanese allergen and nutrition labeling
- Animal-derived ingredient status
- Cross-contact controls
- Shelf life and package integrity
- Moisture, breakage, seasoning, oxidation, and hardness performance
- Supplier capacity, MOQ, lead time, and cost
- Retail price, margin, promotion, and break-even economics

### 6. Staged retail pilot
Pilot in Tokyo first, then expand to Osaka and Nagoya if agreed thresholds are met. Track:

- Units per store per week
- Trial-to-repeat conversion
- Replenishment
- Price and promotional response
- Waste and returns
- Quality complaints
- Retailer acceptance

---

## Recommendation

Proceed with a staged Japan launch led by:

1. **Nori & White Miso Thin Rice Crisps**  
2. **Roasted Edamame with Kombu Seaweed**  
3. **Tangy Ume Salt Corn Puffs**

The strongest platform is not simply “Japanese ingredients in modern formats.” It is:

> **Everyday Japanese flavor logic delivered through three clearly different textures: clean snap, roasted bite, and airy melt.**
