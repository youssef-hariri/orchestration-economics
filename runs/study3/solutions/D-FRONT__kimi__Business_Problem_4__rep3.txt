**JAPAN SNACK PORTFOLIO PROPOSAL**
*Occasion-Driven Artisan Snacking for the Japanese Market*

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Japan’s snack market rewards products that honor *shun* (seasonality), textural precision, and ritualized eating occasions. This proposal introduces a five-SKU ambient snack line engineered specifically for Japanese convenience retail, café culture, and gifting channels. Each product maps to a distinct moment in the consumer’s day—from post-work highballs to spring picnics, afternoon desk breaks, Shinkansen travel, and nighttime home leisure.

Rather than importing Western snack architectures, the portfolio builds on native formats (*okaki*, *arare*-style clusters, glazed puffs, and *wagashi*-inspired gummies) while leveraging defensible regional provenance. Every SKU is designed for ambient shelf stability, portion restraint (35–45 g), and clean-finger eating etiquette. The line balances an accessible everyday price band with premium ingredient storytelling, creating both repeat impulse purchase and seasonal giftability.

## PRODUCT LINE BREAKDOWN

### 1. Yuzu-Shio & Ao-Togarashi Okaki
**Flavor Profile**  
Bright, aromatic citrus from Kochi yuzu peel and juice, grounded by clean Okinawa sea salt and a brief, sharp flicker of Kyushu green chili. Umami is delivered through Rishiri *konbu-cha* (kelp tea) powder—clear and oceanic, never competing with the yuzu top note.

**Key Local Ingredients**  
- Niigata Koshihikari rice flour  
- Kochi yuzu zest and juice  
- Oita-style yuzu kosho (trace, for aromatic depth)  
- Kyushu *ao-togarashi*  
- Rishiri *konbu-cha* powder  
- Okinawa *nuchi-masu* sea salt  

**Format & Packaging**  
Dense, barrel-shaped baked *okaki* with a hard, resonant crunch. Bite-sized for neat, one-at-a-time eating. Packaged in a 35 g matte silver resealable pouch with a laser-cut rising-sun window.

**Consumption Occasions**  
- *Otsumami* alongside highballs, *chuhai*, or craft beer  
- Konbini grab-and-go for commuters seeking a lighter, salt-forward alternative to fried chips  

**Rationale**  
Japan’s beer-snack aisle prizes clean refreshment. This SKU delivers salty-citrus clarity without the grease of traditional potato chips, while the durable *okaki* format survives the logistics of convenience distribution.

### 2. Sakura Kinako Hana Crunch *(Spring Limited Edition)*
**Flavor Profile**  
Warm, toasted Kyoto Tanba *kinako* (roasted soybean flour) forms the dominant base, lifted by Yoshino sakura petals cured in umezu and mellow Tokushima Wasanbon sugar. Bound in creamy Hokkaido milk white chocolate. Deliberately simplified—no competing matcha or medicinal grains—allowing the toasty, floral spring comfort to shine.

**Key Local Ingredients**  
- Yoshino sakura petals cured in plum vinegar  
- Kyoto Tanba *kinako*  
- Tokushima Wasanbon sugar  
- Hokkaido milk white chocolate  
- Niigata glutinous rice puffs  

**Format & Packaging**  
Uniform bite-sized “hana” (flower) clusters—puffed genmai and delicate crisps bound with kinako-white chocolate, broken into consistent 2 cm snap-able pieces. Flat paperboard tray wrapped in a seasonal washi-paper sleeve stamped **春限定** (Spring Limited), with a transparent petal-shaped window.

**Consumption Occasions**  
- Hanami (cherry-blossom viewing) picnics under *yozakura*  
- Afternoon tea in *kissaten* alongside matcha or hojicha  
- Seasonal *omiyage* (souvenir gifting)  

**Rationale**  
Limited spring editions command premium velocity in Japan. By simplifying the flavor architecture and adopting a native cluster format, this SKU captures both social picnic sharing and the high-margin gift market.

### 3. Showa-Retro Saikyo Miso Caramel Glazed Okaki
**Flavor Profile**  
The beloved *amajoppai* (sweet-salty) duality, executed with Kyoto Saikyo white miso—mellower and more caramel-friendly than dark *aka* miso. A thin, brittle toffee glaze crackles over nutty Kagoshima black sesame and puffed brown rice, finishing with a hint of buttered starch syrup.

**Key Local Ingredients**  
- Kyoto Saikyo white miso  
- Akita *mizuame* (starch syrup)  
- Kagoshima black sesame  
- Puffed Niigata genmai cores  
- Hokkaido cultured butter (trace)  

**Format & Packaging**  
Individual puffed brown-rice pillows carrying a thin, glassy miso-caramel glaze. The moisture-barrier toffee shell ensures shelf stability without sogginess. 40 g standing resealable pouch with Showa-Retro (昭和レトロ) aesthetic: faded cadmium-red typography, 1950s–70s *dagashiya* shop motifs, and cream backgrounds.

**Consumption Occasions**  
- 3 p.m. office desk snack  
- Café pairing with iced coffee or hojicha latte  
- Nostalgic “adult” solo indulgence (*hitori-gurashi*)  

**Rationale**  
*Amajoppai* is a proven tension in Japanese confectionery. The Showa-Retro design taps a current cultural revival trend, while the glazed *okaki* format solves the shelf-stability challenges of dipped or coated crackers.

---

### 4. Ume-Shiso Katakuchi Gummy
**Flavor Profile**  
An aggressively sour-salty opening of Wakayama Nanko-ume puree, immediately cooled by red *shiso* (perilla) extract, finished with Okinawa sea salt. Designed as a palate-awakening “refresh” jolt—sharp, clean, and long-lasting.

**Key Local Ingredients**  
- Wakayama Nanko-ume paste  
- Red *shiso* leaf extract and powder  
- Konnyaku and pectin base  
- Okinawa sea salt  

**Format & Packaging**  
Chewy, translucent 2 cm diamond-shaped gummies dusted with sour ume-shiso powder. Individually wrapped in wax paper within a minimalist 45 g envelope-style pack. Ambient shelf-stable.

**Consumption Occasions**  
- Shinkansen bullet-train travel  
- Exam-season study breaks  
- Konbini checkout “wake-up” treat  

**Rationale**  
The travel and study occasions demand a durable, non-messy format that delivers an intense sensory jolt. The pectin-konnyaku matrix provides the chewy resistance consumers expect while eliminating the moisture and pH conflicts of starch-based formats.

---

### 5. Hokkaido Tokibi Dashi-Butter Pops
**Flavor Profile**  
Sweet Hokkaido Tokibi corn natural sweetness enveloped in rich butter, layered with smoky-savory Rausu kombu and Makurazaki *katsuobushi* umami. A touch of mirin provides caramelized depth. Comforting, *natsukashii* (nostalgic), and deeply savory.

**Key Local Ingredients**  
- Hokkaido Tokibi corn powder and grits  
- Hokkaido cultured butter powder  
- Rausu kombu extract  
- Makurazaki *katsuobushi* powder  
- Mirin granules  

**Format & Packaging**  
Dense, bite-sized 1.5 cm solid spheres for maximum crunch, powder retention, and neat one-at-a-time consumption. 35 g slim resealable standing pouch decorated with retro Showa-era Hokkaido farming illustrations in muted cream and barn-red.

**Consumption Occasions**  
- *Yoru no home-snacking* while watching TV or gaming  
- Outdoor summer festivals (*matsuri*) and fireworks viewing  
- Movie nights as a local alternative to Western popcorn  

**Rationale**  
Corn, butter, and dashi are a commercially proven flavor cluster in Japan. By specifying Tokibi cultivar and named kombu/*katsuobushi* sources, this SKU commands premium provenance while remaining the most accessible entry point for mass-market trial.

## GO-TO-MARKET NOTE

**Launch Sequencing**  
Lead with **Hokkaido Tokibi Dashi-Butter Pops** as the year-round “entry hero” to build brand recognition through broad appeal. Follow with **Showa-Retro Saikyo Miso Caramel Glazed Okaki** and **Yuzu-Shio & Ao-Togarashi Okaki** to establish everyday premium placement. Introduce **Sakura Kinako Hana Crunch** as a March–May seasonal limited drop to drive omiyage velocity and PR. Position **Ume-Shiso Katakuchi Gummy** in travel corridors (train stations, airports) and checkout lanes for impulse refreshment.

**Channel Strategy**  
- **Convenience (Konbini):** Primary channel for ambient singles; 35–40 g packs priced within the 200–250 yen impulse band.  
- **Specialty/Depachika:** Spring and gift assortments for premium *omiyage* presentation.  
- **Travel Retail:** Ume-Shiso Gummy and Hokkaido Corn Pops for Shinkansen kiosks and regional airport vending.

**Regulatory & Supply Chain**  
All regional designations are backed by contracted supply chains or qualified on-pack as “*-fu* / *-style*” per Japan’s Act against Unjustifiable Premiums. Mandatory allergens (dairy, wheat/soybean, sesame) and voluntary seafood disclosures (Rausu kombu, Makurazaki *katsuobushi*) are declared explicitly by name on every unit.

**Portfolio Positioning**  
The line occupies the space between everyday *dagashi* and ultra-premium *meibutsu*: authentic enough for regional credibility, polished enough for Tokyo and Osaka convenience retail. By rotating seasonal SKUs and anchoring the year with the Hokkaido corn concept, the brand can secure repeat shelf resets and sustained consumer curiosity.
