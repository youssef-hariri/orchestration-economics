**CONFIDENTIAL BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Corporate Strategy Office  
**DATE:** October 24, 2023  
**SUBJECT:** Industrial-Services Unit (ISU): Turnaround-or-Exit Assessment and 12-Month Plan  

---

### **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**
The Industrial-Services Unit (ISU) has recorded three consecutive years of losses despite two aggressive cost-cutting restructurings. The failure of these prior initiatives confirms a fundamental thesis: **the ISU cannot be "cost-cut" into profitability; its underlying business model is structurally broken.** Compounding this, the exodus of top technical talent and a shrinking order book signal a critical loss of market confidence.

This memorandum provides a definitive diagnostic of the ISU’s value destruction, a unit-economic portfolio rationalization strategy, and a realistic 12-month turnaround plan. Crucially, this plan mitigates the risk of dropping critical "wedge" products via Day-1 operational ring-fencing, secures Group-level governance for internal transfer pricing, bridges the inevitable cash-burn "J-curve" with guaranteed intercompany liquidity, and establishes strictly quantitative "kill criteria" to determine whether to execute a turnaround or initiate an immediate exit.

---

### **1. DIAGNOSTIC ASSESSMENT: ROOT CAUSES OF VALUE DESTRUCTION**

To design a viable path forward, we must separate external market shifts from internal execution failures.

**A. Structural Market Shifts (External Reality)**
*   **CapEx to OpEx Transition:** Industrial customers are shifting from traditional "break-fix" models to outcome-based, predictive, and "as-a-service" models. The ISU’s legacy services are trapped in the depreciating "break-fix" segment.
*   **Technological Disruption:** IoT sensors and digital twins are reducing the need for physical, routine on-site inspections, structurally shrinking the market for manual maintenance.
*   **Supply Chain Localization:** Demands for faster, localized response times have structurally increased logistics costs, compressing margins on geographically dispersed contracts.

**B. Structural Business Model Flaws (Internal Reality)**
*   **The "Shared Customer" Subsidy Trap:** The ISU shares customers with the Group’s profitable equipment divisions and has been implicitly used as a loss-leader. The Group has tolerated ISU underpricing to secure broader enterprise contracts, masking the unit's true standalone unviability.
*   **Misaligned Cost-to-Serve:** Fixed overhead and field-deployment costs are structurally misaligned with pricing power in commoditized service lines.

**C. Execution Failures (Accelerants)**
*   **Talent Hemorrhage:** The loss of Tier-1 technical experts is driven by poor change management, lack of autonomy, and a toxic "decline" culture.
*   **Flawed Prior Restructurings:** Previous turnarounds focused on blunt headcount reduction rather than process optimization, leaving a demoralized workforce with broken workflows and degraded service quality.

**Verdict:** The ISU requires a fundamental business model reset focused on revenue quality, pricing discipline, and synergy monetization—not a third round of blunt cost-cutting.

---

### **2. PORTFOLIO RATIONALIZATION: UNIT ECONOMICS & STRATEGIC VALUE**

To achieve profitability, we must ruthlessly prune the portfolio based on **Unit Economics (Contribution Margin vs. Strategic Value)**.

*   **Segment A: Outcome-Based & Predictive Services (The "Crown Jewels")**
    *   *Unit Economics:* High gross margins (35%+), high recurring revenue, low marginal cost-to-serve.
    *   *Action:* **Protect and Scale.** Reallocate freed capital and top talent here. Bundle with profitable divisions' offerings to create "stickiness" and justify premium pricing.
*   **Segment B: Specialized Engineering & Commissioning (The "Cash Cows")**
    *   *Unit Economics:* High margins (25-30%), project-based, highly specialized technical labor.
    *   *Action:* **Harvest and Optimize.** Run as disciplined, lean profit centers with strict project management protocols to prevent scope creep.
*   **Segment C: Routine Maintenance & Break-Fix (The "Value Destroyers" & "Strategic Wedge")**
    *   *Unit Economics:* Low to negative contribution margins (5-10%), highly labor-intensive.
    *   *Action:* **Strategic Run-Off with a "Wedge" Carve-Out.** Exit the bottom 80% of unprofitable contracts. Retain a curated top 20% at key shared-customer sites, strictly priced at break-even, to maintain physical vendor access for cross-selling Segment A. **This retained 20% will be serviced by a dedicated, operationally isolated "Wedge Team" from Day 1** to prevent legacy operational drag from infecting the Segment A turnaround.
*   **Segment D: Subsidized Entry Services (The "Hidden Bleed")**
    *   *Unit Economics:* Negative standalone margins, historically provided to secure Group-level equipment sales.
    *   *Action:* **Monetize via Governed Transfer Pricing.** Implement a strict "Shadow P&L." Profitable divisions must now pay the ISU market rates for these services.

### **3. 12-MONTH TURNAROUND PLAN & MILESTONES**

*Strategic Note: Dropping unprofitable revenue will cause a top-line and EBITDA "J-curve" dip. The plan is sequenced to manage this cash burn via working capital optimization while aligning fixed costs to the new revenue base.*

#### **Phase 1: Triage, Pricing, and Talent Stabilization (Months 1–3)**
*Objective: Stop the cash bleed, fix pricing architecture, secure Group alignment, and halt the brain drain.*
*   **Month 1:** 
    *   Implement targeted "Stay Packages" (cash + accelerated vesting) for the top 20 critical technical experts.
    *   **Operational Ring-Fencing:** Formally isolate the Segment C "Wedge Team" into a distinct, lean cost center with dedicated technicians and dispatchers.
    *   **Group Governance:** Secure Board approval for the Transfer Pricing model. Delegate dispute resolution to the Group COO to prevent local divisional P&L managers from unilaterally dropping ISU services.
    *   **Cash-Bridge Mechanics:** Establish a $10M working capital facility, structured as a **Group Treasury intercompany loan**, to bridge the cash burn spike expected in Months 3-5.
    *   **Change Management:** Execute a targeted communication plan to mitigate change fatigue among the broader workforce.
*   **Month 2:** 
    *   Implement the governed Transfer Pricing model for Segment D. 
    *   Conduct a zero-based pricing review of the top 20 customer contracts; terminate those below the cost of capital.
*   **Month 3 (Milestone 1):** 
    *   *Financial:* Reduce monthly net cash burn by 40% vs. baseline.
    *   *Talent:* Retain 100% of the identified top 20 critical technical staff.
    *   *Operational:* Complete the exit of the bottom 80% of Segment C revenue; transition the 20% "wedge" contracts to break-even pricing.

#### **Phase 2: Cost Right-Sizing and Restructuring (Months 4–6)**
*Objective: Aggressively align the fixed cost base with the new revenue reality to achieve break-even.*
*   **Month 4:** 
    *   Restructure the sales force commission model from "volume-based" to "margin-and-recurring-revenue-based."
    *   Initiate aggressive fixed-cost right-sizing: consolidate field offices, reduce middle-management layers, and optimize the supply chain.
*   **Month 5:** 
    *   Launch a joint-task force with profitable divisions to design bundled service offerings for shared customers.
*   **Month 6 (Milestone 2 - Mid-Term Review):** 
    *   *Financial:* Achieve **0% Adjusted EBITDA margin** (Break-even on a monthly run-rate basis). 
    *   *Operational:* Fixed cost base reduced by 20%. Gross margin improved by 500 bps.

#### **Phase 3: Synergy Activation and Growth (Months 7–9)**
*Objective: Scale profitable segments, cross-sell, and prove the new model.*
*   **Month 7:** 
    *   Roll out bundled service offerings. Target a 15% increase in wallet share within the existing shared-customer base.
    *   Reprice Segment A to reflect current market inflation and specialized value.
*   **Month 8-9:** 
    *   Initiate digitization of service delivery for Segment A (e.g., remote monitoring). *Note: Digitization CapEx will depress short-term margins. The Month 9 EBITDA target is driven entirely by pricing power and portfolio mix-shift; digitization savings will realize post-turnaround.*
*   **Month 9 (Milestone 3):** 
    *   *Financial:* Achieve **8% Adjusted EBITDA margin**. Generate positive Free Cash Flow for two consecutive months.
    *   *Pipeline:* >70% of the order book backlog is now high-margin Segment A & B work.

#### **Phase 4: Validation and Exit Prep (Months 10–12)**
*Objective: Prove sustainable profitability and prepare for the Board's final decision.*
*   **Month 10-11:** 
    *   Stress-test the new operating model. Ensure >80% of the order book is high-margin work.
    *   If exiting, engage investment banks to prepare the data room and Confidential Information Memorandum (CIM).
*   **Month 12 (Final Milestone):** 
    *   *Financial:* Deliver a full-year audited P&L showing **10% Adjusted EBITDA margin** (bridged from Month 9 via transfer pricing markups, exit of remaining low-margin tail work, and fixed-cost absorption) and positive annual Free Cash Flow. 
    *   *Strategic:* Present the final "Turnaround vs. Exit" recommendation to the Board.

### **4. EXIT ALTERNATIVES & QUANTITATIVE "KILL CRITERIA"**

If the turnaround fails, we must execute an exit. Because the ISU shares customers with profitable divisions, **any exit must be structured to protect the Group’s core business relationships.**

#### **Exit Alternatives**
1.  **Strategic Sale to an Adjacent Player:** Sell to a firm in a related, non-overlapping industrial sector. *Condition:* Strict non-solicitation and customer ring-fencing clauses must be legally enforced.
2.  **Management Buyout (MBO) backed by Private Equity:** Take the unit private. *Condition:* Strict non-solicitation and customer ring-fencing clauses must be legally enforced.
3.  **Internal Absorption (Carve-in):** Dissolve the ISU as a standalone P&L and absorb Segments A & B into profitable divisions. *Disposition of the Wedge:* The retained 20% Segment C "wedge" will either be transitioned to the absorbing divisions under a strict, market-rate internal SLA, or liquidated if deemed unnecessary for divisional customer retention.
4.  **Orderly Liquidation:** Fulfill existing contracts, halt all new bidding, and close the unit.

#### **The "Kill Criteria": Strictly Quantitative Triggers**
The Board will not wait until Month 12 to decide. Hard, objective "Kill Criteria" will be evaluated at the **Month 6 Mid-Term Review**. If *any* of the following triggers are hit, the turnaround is aborted, and the exit process begins immediately.

**Financial & Operational Triggers:**
*   **Trigger 1 (Profitability):** Month 6 Adjusted EBITDA Margin is **< 0%** (Failure to reach break-even after fixed-cost right-sizing).
*   **Trigger 2 (Cash):** Cumulative Free Cash Flow from Month 1 to 6 **exceeds -$12 Million**, or ISU cash reserves **fall below $4 Million** (breaching authorized liquidity limits).
*   **Trigger 3 (Margin Mix):** Gross Margin improvement from baseline is **< 300 bps** by Month 6.

**Talent, Market & Synergy Triggers:**
*   **Trigger 4 (Talent):** Retention of the top 20 critical technical staff falls **below 80%** by Month 4.
*   **Trigger 5 (Customer):** Loss of **> 2** of our top 10 "shared customers" *for high-margin Segment A or B services* to competitors by Month 6. *(Note: Intentional run-offs of the bottom 80% of Segment C contracts are explicitly excluded from this metric).*
*   **Trigger 6 (Leading Synergy Indicators):** Qualified pipeline for bundled offerings is **< $10 Million**, or LOI conversion rate from shared customers is **< 10%** by Month 6.

We must accept a short-term revenue contraction to achieve long-term margin expansion, carefully managing cash and customer-relationship risks through Day-1 operational ring-fencing and guaranteed liquidity.
