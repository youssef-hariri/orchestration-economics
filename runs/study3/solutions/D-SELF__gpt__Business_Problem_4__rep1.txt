# Japan Snack Line Recommendation  
## **KOMOREBI — Japanese Flavor, Made for Today**

### Launch positioning

**Familiar Japanese flavor pairings in convenient formats for everyday breaks, sharing, and small gifting occasions.**

Position KOMOREBI as a **mass-premium flavor innovation line**, not a health-food or functional-nutrition brand. The differentiation should come from balanced flavor combinations, familiar snack formats, practical pack sizes, and contemporary packaging.

The masterbrand **KOMOREBI** should proceed only after Japanese trademark, pronunciation, memorability, and cultural-association testing. Clear Japanese product descriptors should remain prominent on pack.

# Recommended Lead Product

## **KOMOREBI Yuzu Shio Potato Chips**

Yuzu Shio Potato Chips should lead the launch because they offer the best balance of:

- Familiar product format
- Clear Japanese flavor identity
- Broad convenience and supermarket fit
- Seasonal and limited-edition potential
- Suitability for individual and sharing packs
- Lower consumer education requirements than the other concepts

### Lead product sensory direction

- Fragrant yuzu aroma
- Restrained saltiness
- Clean potato finish
- Subtle, non-marine umami
- No excessive sourness, bitterness, or heat

Develop and compare three seasoning systems:

1. Yuzu-forward with no added umami  
2. Yuzu with low-level kombu  
3. Yuzu with low-level shiitake  

Domestic yuzu could support a premium limited edition, but the core product should use a consistent, cost-manageable flavor system that does not depend entirely on seasonal domestic supply.

### Indicative price

- 40–45 g: **¥190–¥250**
- 120–150 g sharing pack: **¥430–¥580**

Prices should be confirmed against competitive products, retailer margins, promotional pricing, and ingredient costs.

# Launch Structure

## Phase 1: Three-SKU launch

1. **Yuzu Shio Potato Chips** — hero and trial driver  
2. **Miso Nori Rice Crunch** — distinctive everyday signature  
3. **Matcha Kinako Rice Biscuits** — sweet, café, seasonal, and gifting counterpoint  

This provides two savory products and one sweet product across three manageable formats.

## Phase 2: Ume Apple Soft Fruit Bites

Develop **KOMOREBI Ume Apple Soft Fruit Bites** as a second-wave product only after validating the physical format and consumer category.

Recommended direction:

- Pectin-based soft fruit bite
- Apple-forward with mild ume tartness
- Light salt finish
- Individually wrapped
- Clear “soft fruit snack” descriptor

Do not describe it as a rice chew unless rice plays a meaningful sensory role. Confirm whether consumers perceive it as fruit snack, soft candy, gummy, or confectionery before investing further.

## Deferred concept: Edamame Nori Crisps

Keep the original protein-led concept outside the initial launch. If revisited, develop it as **Edamame Nori Crisps**, positioned around roasted umami rather than protein. A protein claim should be considered only if the finished product delivers a meaningful, substantiated amount.

# Channel and Pack Recommendations

### Convenience stores
Lead with 40–45 g Yuzu Chips and single-serve Rice Crunch. Use seasonal artwork and limited flavors to encourage trial.

### Supermarkets and drugstores
Offer sharing pouches and value formats for household stocking and repeat purchase.

### Station, airport, and specialty retail
Use the Matcha Kinako gift format, provided product quality, packaging, piece count, and origin claims support the premium.

### E-commerce
Offer a three-SKU trial box with clear information on flavor intensity, texture, allergens, and occasions.

### Packaging direction

Use a consistent masterbrand with strong SKU differentiation:

- Yuzu: yellow and navy  
- Miso Rice Crunch: ivory, charcoal, and warm red  
- Matcha Kinako: deep green, beige, and gold  

High-barrier packaging and, where appropriate, nitrogen flushing are recommended for crispness, aroma, and oxidation control. Individual wrapping should be justified by portability, hygiene, sharing, or gifting needs, while packaging cost and waste are assessed.

---

# Key Assumptions to Validate

Before commercialization, confirm:

- Whether KOMOREBI is memorable, food-relevant, and culturally appropriate in Japan
- Whether the proposed products are clearly understood as chips, rice snacks, and biscuits
- Whether consumers prefer yuzu alone or with subtle kombu or shiitake umami
- Whether the Rice Crunch is better received as a cluster or a snack mix
- Whether matcha-kinako flavor is sufficiently differentiated from existing products
- Whether alcohol pairing is a meaningful purchase occasion for Yuzu Chips
- Whether the Matcha product supports everyday purchase, gifting, or both
- Whether the proposed price points are competitive and profitable
- Whether domestic yuzu or premium matcha sourcing is financially sustainable
- Whether individual wrapping creates unacceptable cost or waste
- Whether soy, sesame, wheat, and milk exposure limits the line’s target audience

---

# Product Development Requirements

Each SKU should have defined targets for:

- Product category and Japanese naming
- Net weight and piece count
- Sodium, sugar, and serving-size parameters
- Water activity and moisture
- Shelf life under Japanese heat and humidity
- Seasoning or coating uniformity
- Breakage tolerance
- Cost of goods and retailer margin
- Packaging format and material requirements
- Allergen and cross-contact controls

Avoid unsupported front-of-pack claims such as **healthy, natural, authentic, light, low salt, low sugar, plant-based, high protein, or gluten-free** unless legally and analytically substantiated.

# Final Recommendation

Proceed with a focused three-SKU development program:

1. **KOMOREBI Yuzu Shio Potato Chips**
2. **KOMOREBI Miso Nori Rice Crunch**
3. **KOMOREBI Matcha Kinako Rice Biscuits**

Lead with Yuzu Shio Potato Chips, use Miso Nori Rice Crunch to establish a distinctive everyday identity, and use Matcha Kinako Rice Biscuits to add sweet and gifting relevance. Keep Ume Apple as a validated second-wave extension and the edamame concept as a separate development project.

The strongest commercial platform is:

> **Japanese flavor familiarity, carefully balanced and delivered in clear, convenient formats for modern snack occasions.**
