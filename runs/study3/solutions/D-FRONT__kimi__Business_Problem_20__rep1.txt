**OPERATIONS IMPROVEMENT PLAN**
**Competitive Lead-Time Compression with Certified Quality Integrity**

**Client:** Industrial Equipment Manufacturer  
**Current State:** 14-week quoted lead time | ~10.5-week actual flow time  
**Target:** 6-week quoted lead time (standard configurations)  
**Constraint:** Full retention of certified quality gates and warranty integrity  

---

### 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Sales is losing one in three deals because the **14-week quote institutionalizes organizational uncertainty, not manufacturing reality**. Empirical analysis of the last 50 shipped orders reveals actual dock-to-dock flow time averages **~10.5 weeks**; the remaining 3.5 weeks is a defensive buffer against internal variance. Of the 10.5 actual weeks, only **~4.5 weeks** is value-add processing and certified inspection. The remainder is systemic waste (~3.5 weeks) and protective buffer (~2.5 weeks) required by current process variability.

The previous warranty spike was caused by compressing *test durations* and *inspection frequencies* to offset drag. That failure mode is permanently excluded. This plan compresses lead time through two sequenced streams:

*   **Stream 1 – Stability (Months 1–6):** Eliminate systemic waste to reduce the quoted lead time to **8–9 weeks** immediately.
*   **Stream 2 – Structural Transformation (Months 6–36):** Shift standard-configured products from Make-to-Order (MTO) to **Assemble-to-Order (ATO)** by moving the Customer Order Decoupling Point (CODP) deep into the value stream. Pre-certified modular subassemblies are forecast-built and stocked; final customer order triggers marriage, connection, and certified runoff.

This is a **capital-light, quality-heavy, model-line-proven** transformation. We will prove the 6-week ATO model on one product family for six months before scaling. All certified gates remain code-compliant in duration and frequency; acceleration comes from logistics, digital thread, and pre-positioned inventory—not from shortened tests.

---

### 2. LOST-TIME DIAGNOSIS

An order-level time study of the last 50 shipments, supplemented by **queueing analysis (Kingman’s formula)**, distinguishes systemic waste (removable by redesign) from protective buffer (necessary until variance is reduced).

| Category | Weeks | Nature | Evidence |
|----------|-------|--------|----------|
| **Quoted Lead Time** | 14.0 | Market-facing | Includes 3.5-week safety margin because actual flow is unpredictable. |
| **Actual Flow Time** | ~10.5 | Internal reality | Log-based, order-by-order average. |
| *Value-Add (VA)* | ~4.5 | Customer-paid processing | Machining, welding, assembly, certified NDE/hydro/test, cure cycles. |
| *Necessary Non-Value-Add (NNVA)* | ~2.0 | Code/compliance | Certification documentation, third-party witness holds, material cert verification. |
| *Systemic Waste* | ~3.5 | Removable immediately | Dirty order loops (1.0), serial engineering release (1.0), inspection queues (0.8), production expediting chaos (0.7). |
| *Protective Buffer* | ~2.5 | Required by current variance | Supplier delivery variance (1.0), batch heat-treat queue (0.8), capacity-constraint starvation (0.7). |
| **Competitor Benchmark** | 6.0 | Market standard | Likely ATO with modular platforms; not necessarily faster welding. |

**Key Finding:** The competitor is not manufacturing faster; they are flowing faster via an ATO model that removes engineering and procurement from the critical path on standard builds. We cannot out-lean our way from 10.5 weeks to 6 weeks while remaining MTO.

**Diagnostic Deep Dives**
*   **Order Defect Pareto:** The “dirty order” rate is 40%. Root-cause decomposition shows **60% of defects are configurations that Engineering has not yet codified into rules**, not Sales errors. A rules-based gateway cannot fix an immature product architecture.
*   **Supplier OTTR Histogram:** Suppliers quote 4–6 weeks, but actual delivery variance is **±2.5 weeks**. Procurement lead time is not merely an internal release problem; it is a supplier noise problem that protective buffer currently absorbs.
*   **Quoted vs. Actual Variance:** Actual flow ranges from 8 to 14 weeks. Sales quotes 14 to cover tail risk. The first act of this plan is to stop quoting safety margins and start quoting demonstrated, stabilized capability.

### 3. RECOMMENDED CHANGES

#### A. Strategic Pivot: Adopt an Assemble-to-Order (ATO) Model
For standard configurations, the company will explicitly shift from MTO to ATO:
*   **Pre-Engineered Platforms:** 80/20 product families are broken into pre-certified modular architectures (frame, power module, control architecture). These are designed once and released permanently.
*   **Post-CODP Inventory:** Standard, pre-certified subassemblies are built to forecast and held in a **post-engineering supermarket**. The customer order triggers final marriage and test, not raw-material procurement.
*   **Commercial Policy:** The 6-week quote applies only to pre-defined ATO configurations; everything else follows an Engineer-to-Order (ETO) path quoted at 9–10 weeks.

#### B. Process, Scheduling, and Production Changes
**Stream 1 – Stability (Months 1–6)**
*   **Order Intake & Engineering:** Stop quoting 14 weeks; quote demonstrated actual flow (~10.5 weeks) minus committed waste reduction, tightening to **9 weeks** by Month 6. Engineering publishes **configuration guardrails** for the top two product families within 30 days. For long-lead items, Engineering releases **advance procurement BOMs** at T+72 hours after clean order, before final drawings are complete.
*   **Production & Scheduling:** Replace infinite-capacity MRP with **Finite Capacity Scheduling (FCS)** on the highest-volume line. Establish a **2-week frozen zone**; any customer-driven engineering change after freeze requires COO approval and documented lead-time/price impact communicated to the customer within 24 hours.
*   **Daily Tiered Accountability:** Implement 15-minute tier meetings at each value stream. Blockers (missing parts, inspection holds, engineering clarifications) escalate same-day.
*   **Inspection Queue Reduction:** Move CMM and NDE scheduling from batch-queue to **appointment-based** (booked when weld is complete). Deploy digital tablets for in-process data capture.

**Stream 2 – Structural Transformation (Months 6–36)**
*   **Modular Platform Development:** Engineering owns the modular BOM library. Target 80% coverage of the standard order mix by Month 18–24. This is a product development cycle, not purely an operations project.
*   **Production Flow Design (Feasibility-First):** Conduct spaghetti diagram, material handling, and **skills-matrix/union consultation** assessments before rearranging equipment. For large equipment with 5-ton weldments and overhead cranes, **virtual cells** (dedicated sequencing through existing functional resources with fixed transfer batches) or **fixed-position cells** may replace physical cellular layouts. Implement **Drum-Buffer-Rope** using the constraint (test bay or welding) to pace material release.
*   **Digital Thread & Live Quoting:** Deploy a **live capacity-backed quoting tool** tied to real-time modular inventory, open constraint capacity, and supplier VMI levels. Auto-generate digital certification packets; pilot with the top 3 customers before scaling to ensure regulatory acceptance.

#### C. Inventory and Supplier Changes
*   **Tier 1 – COTS Items (Months 1–6):** Move hardware, fittings, electrical components, and fasteners to **VMI or consignment** on-site or at a nearby 3PL. Eliminate PO-to-receipt time for these items within 90 days.
*   **Tier 2 – Long-Lead Items (Months 6–24):** Use **strategic raw-stock inventory** (uncut forgings/castings) to buffer supplier variance during Stream 1. Qualify near-shore or premium-speed suppliers for castings and gearboxes via PPAP, first-article inspection, code witness tests, and production trials. Manage under long-term blanket POs with lead-time tiers.
*   **Post-Engineering Supermarket (Months 6–18):** Hold pre-built, pre-certified power modules and control panels as finished subassembly inventory, shifting 2.5–3.0 weeks from the customer critical path.

### 4. QUALITY-GATE PROTECTION

The previous warranty disaster resulted from *schedule compression by inspection reduction*. This plan institutionalizes the opposite: we flow faster by eliminating *waiting time around* quality gates, never by shortening the gates themselves.

#### The Three Immutables
1. **Zero Elimination of Code-Mandated Gates:** All weld certifications, NDE frequencies (RT/UT), hydrostatic test durations and pressures, heat-treat profiles, and material cure times remain exactly as specified by ASME, AWS D1.1, contract ITPs, or customer specifications.
2. **PFMEA / Control Plan Gate:** No process change (flow sequence, supplier change, inspection location, or cell layout) is authorized until Engineering and Quality sign updated PFMEA, Control Plans, and work instructions.
3. **Simulation Before Flow:** Before any product moves to a flow sequence, a **discrete event simulation** will model the impact of a certified-gate failure on the line. Protective buffer will be sized to absorb quality stops without idling the constraint.

#### Certified Gate Acceleration Tactics (Logistics Only)

| Certified Gate | Current Delay | Acceleration Method | Warranty Protection |
|----------------|---------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Weld / NDE** | 2–3 day queue for X-ray/UT bay. | **In-flow logistics:** Parts move directly to NDE upon weld completion; results transmitted digitally; next operation pre-staged. QC conducts process audits *between* code-required inspections. | Code-mandated inspection frequency and hold points remain unchanged. No audit replaces radiographic or ultrasonic examination. |
| **Heat Treat / Stress Relief** | Batch-up for furnace. | Smaller, right-sized furnace loads; pre-certified recipes. | Cycle time/temperature profiles maintained per spec. |
| **Hydrostatic / Runoff** | 1–2 day wait for test bay. | Dedicate test bay as **pacemaker (Drum)** resource; utilities/fixtures pre-staged. Test duration unchanged. | Full pressure/hold per code. Single-piece flow stops if test fails; no bypass. |
| **Paint / Cure** | Queue for booth; ambient cure. | Invest in accelerated cure oven or IR curing (equipment upgrade, not process shortcut). | Cure time/temp monitored and logged digitally. |
| **Final Documentation** | 1–2 days manual packet assembly. | **Digital thread** auto-populates cert packets with unalterable audit trail. Piloted with customers before scale. | Full traceability; digital signatures validated by customer QA. |

#### Supplier Quality Firewall
*   **Dock-to-line is prohibited** for any supplier not holding active **Certified Supplier** status.
*   **Certified Supplier Requirements:** SPC data transmitted with every lot; code witnessing passed; annual system audit.
*   **Firewall Audit:** Even certified suppliers receive random incoming inspection/NDE at **10–20% frequency**. Any quality spill triggers immediate reversion to 100% incoming inspection for that supplier.
*   Production may **never** pressure Procurement or Quality to accept a lot based on supplier certs alone when the firewall is active.

#### NCR Fast-Track with Authority
Any non-conformance in the flow path triggers a **4-hour disposition team** (Quality, Engineering, Production). If disposition requires stopping the line, Production may escalate for capacity impact; **Quality retains veto authority** over releasing any unit with an open NCR.

### 5. PHASED ROADMAP

Transformation proceeds via a **model-line pilot** on the highest-volume, most-standard product family. We will not transform the entire factory at once.

| Phase | Timeline | Focus | Quoted Lead-Time Target | Key Deliverables & Exit Criteria |
|-------|----------|-------|------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Phase 0: Expose & Validate** | Month 0 | Empirical time study, queueing analysis, skills/union assessment, capital business case. | 14 → 11 weeks (stop quoting safety margin) | Baseline VSM; Kingman analysis; union consultation report; approved capital plan. |
| **Phase 1: Stabilize (Stream 1)** | Months 1–6 | Clean orders, parallel release, FCS, Tier 1 VMI, inspection queue reduction. | 11 → 9 weeks | Clean Order Gateway live; advance BOM release; daily tier meetings; raw-stock buffer for castings. |
| **Phase 2: Model Line ATO** | Months 6–12 | Modular BOM pilot; virtual/fixed cell; in-flow inspection; supermarket established. | **9 → 7 weeks (pilot family only)** | Model line operational; 6-month stability run; prove exit criteria (≤7 weeks actual flow, FPY ≥98%, warranty cost ≤ baseline for 3 consecutive months). |
| **Phase 3: Scale & Qualify** | Months 12–24 | Scale to family #2; Tier 2 near-shore qualification; Drum-Buffer-Rope; digital thread. | 7 → 6 weeks (standard ATO) | Two families at 6–7 weeks; certified supplier program; full digital packet pilot approved. |
| **Phase 4: Optimize** | Months 24–36 | Full ATO coverage for 70% of standard mix; continuous improvement. | 6 weeks (standard) / 9 weeks (ETO) | Warranty cost at or below baseline; cross-functional Hoshin fully deployed. |

### 6. CROSS-FUNCTIONAL ALIGNMENT

To end the Production–Quality–Sales blame cycle, governance is built on **decision rights, SLAs, and interdependent metrics**.

#### A. Executive Oversight
*   **Executive Sponsor (COO):** Owns capital authorization and policy enforcement.
*   **Steering Committee:** Monthly review of phase-gate milestones, capital release, and model-line exit criteria.

#### B. Deal-to-Delivery (DDD) War Room
*   **Frequency:** Daily 15-minute stand-up (production floor); weekly 60-minute deep-dive.
*   **Visual Management:** Single board tracking Order Intake → Ship. Orders coded blocker (red), at-risk (yellow), on-flow (green).
*   **RACI Escalation Matrix (Published):**
    *   **Quality:** Veto authority over certified gate release.
    *   **Production:** Veto authority over order acceptance date once inside the frozen zone.
    *   **Sales:** Owns configuration accuracy at order entry.
    *   **Engineering:** Owns long-lead BOM release within 72 hours of clean order.
    *   **Deadlock Resolution:** If Production and Quality conflict, escalation to VP Operations with **decision within 4 hours**.

#### C. Inter-Functional Service Level Agreements (SLAs)

| From | To | SLA | Breach Trigger |
|------|-----|-----|----------------|
| Sales | Engineering | Clean order within 48 hours of verbal commitment | >5% dirty order rate |
| Engineering | Procurement | Long-lead BOM release within 72 hours of clean order | >1 miss per week |
| Procurement | Production | Tier 1 VMI stock-out = zero | Any stock-out |
| Production | Quality | Unit presented for inspection within 2 hours of predecessor completion | Queue >2 hours |
| Quality | Production | NCR disposition within 4 hours of discovery | >1 breach per week |
| Quality | Sales/Customer | Digital cert packet available at ship | 100% on-time |

#### D. Hoshin Kanri (Policy Deployment)
Each function retains its own incentive structure, but metrics are **explicitly interdependent**. Plant-level misses freeze all functional Directors’ discretionary bonus pools.

| Function | Primary Metric | Gating Metric (Must also hit) |
|----------|---------------|------------------------------|
| **Sales** | Revenue & Clean Order Rate | Average Realized Lead Time ≤ target |
| **Engineering** | Long-Lead Release On-Time | Modular BOM coverage % |
| **Procurement** | Tier 1 Supplier OTTR | Tier 2 Supplier Qualification Milestones |
| **Production** | Flow Time (Dock-to-Dock) | First-Pass Yield ≥98% |
| **Quality** | Inspection Cycle Time | Warranty Cost Per Unit ≤ baseline |

### 7. RISKS AND METRICS

#### Risk Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| **Warranty Spike (Repeat of History)** | Enforce the Three Immutables; PFMEA gate on every change; model-line pilot with 6-month stability run; weekly NCR trend audit. |
| **Supplier Quality Erosion from Speed** | Firewall audit protocol; certified supplier tier with SPC requirement; immediate reversion to 100% incoming inspection on any spill. |
| **Sales Promises Unachievable Dates** | Live capacity-backed quoting tool; frozen zone with ECO tollgate; ATO eligibility enforced by system. |
| **Production Backsliding to Batch Logic** | FCS makes batching visible; daily tier meetings surface backsliding; virtual cells reduce capital risk. |
| **Union/Trade Boundary Conflict** | Skills matrix and union consultation in Month 1. If physical cells violate work rules, pivot to virtual cells. |

#### Capital Plan and Key Metrics
*   **Estimated Investment:** $3M–$8M over 36 months, phase-gated:
    *   *Stream 1:* $0.3M (FCS software, tablets, VMI space, training)
    *   *Stream 2:* $2.5M–$7M (modular platform engineering, cure oven/CMM, near-shore supplier development, digital thread, potential material handling/cell equipment)
*   **Business Case:** NPV based on win-rate recovery (capturing the one-in-three lost deals) versus investment and inventory carrying cost.

| Metric | Target | Purpose |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Clean Order Rate | >95% | Ensure order accuracy before engineering release. |
| Engineering Release On-Time | 100% for long-lead items at T+3 days | Force modular release, not perfectionism. |
| Tier 1 Supplier OTTR | Per phase milestone | Tie supplier management to flow. |
| Flow Time (Dock-to-Dock) | Per phase milestone | Measure speed of flow, not labor efficiency. |
| First-Pass Yield | ≥98% | Joint Production/Quality guardrail. |
| Warranty Cost Per Unit | ≤ Baseline | The ultimate quality guardrail; if this rises, all bonuses freeze. |
| Quoted Lead Time Accuracy | Quote = Actual ±5% | Align Sales trust with Operations capability. |
