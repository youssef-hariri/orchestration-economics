**REPOSITIONING STRATEGY**
# PROJECT ORIGINAL STANDARD

**Fortify the Core. Evolve the Expression. Weaponize the Proof.**

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The previous modernization failed because the brand performed youthfulness instead of demonstrating standards. The path forward is not to chase relevance, but to enforce a simple operating principle: **If it erodes trust with people who’ve bought us for 20+ years, we don’t do it. Modern relevance must pass through that filter first.**

This strategy removes the logo and the core product from debate permanently, introduces low-risk trial mechanics to unlock under-35 experimentation, and treats the brand’s ubiquity in 40,000+ retail doors as its most powerful media channel. We will not apologize for being 70 years old. We will not split the product into generational tiers. And we will not ask loyalists to validate us to their grandchildren.

We speak to standards, not generations. The same proof point is heard by the loyalist as *“still made right”* and by the 25-year-old as *“made before planned obsolescence existed.”*

---

## 1. NEW POSITIONING STATEMENT & CORE BRAND IDEA

### Core Brand Idea
**“The Original Standard.”**  
The brand is not a nostalgia object. It is the benchmark that every trend-driven competitor is still trying to beat. Our 70-year history is not a story about the past; it is continuous evidence that the standard we set still holds.

### Operating Rubric
The following replaces the traditional paragraph-length positioning statement. It is the decision tool for every internal team, agency, and retailer:

> **“If it erodes trust with people who’ve bought us for 20+ years, we don’t do it. Modern relevance must pass through that filter first.”**

### Product Truth (Non-Negotiable)
The core SKU formulation is **locked**. There is no reformulation debate. The product loyalists trust is identical to the one new users will try. All innovation occurs in format, packaging ergonomics, or adjacent line extensions—never in the core recipe.

### Bridging Narrative
- **To loyalists (65+):** *“You built this reputation. We’re defending it from people who think ‘new’ always means ‘better.’”*
- **To under-35s:** *“Before ‘authentic’ was a marketing word, it was just how things were made. This is what that looks like now.”*

## 2. BRAND ELEMENTS: PRESERVE, RETIRE & INTRODUCE

### Preserve (Sacred Equity)

| Element | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Masterbrand Wordmark / Core Logo** | 70 years of unaided awareness reside here. **Hands off for 18 months.** If digital legibility is a genuine issue (e.g., illegible in a mobile avatar), a **screen-only digital lockup** may be created. The primary on-pack wordmark is not redrawn, re-kerninged, or modernized. |
| **Signature Color** | The ownable shelf/blocking color is the strongest memory structure. The hero color is locked. The surrounding palette may evolve. |
| **Origin / Founder Record** | Replace lore with proof. Use archival documents, dated photographs, original ledgers, and production records. The narrative is a **record**, not a mythology. |
| **Iconic Silhouette / Pack Architecture** | The physical form—bottle shape, box proportion, cap design—is tactile proof of legitimacy. Modernize materials and ergonomics; maintain the silhouette. |
| **Core SKU Formulation** | No reformulation. This removes the most explosive variable from internal debate and signals to loyalists that the product they trust is untouched. |
| **Quality / Process Cues** | “Made the slow way,” specific ingredient sourcing, and original production methods. These are premium features for younger consumers and validation for loyalists. |

### Retire (Dated or Damaged Equity)

| Element | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Dated Secondary Typography (e.g., 1990s corporate serif)** | Replace with a contemporary type system rooted in craft (e.g., a modern grotesque or revived industrial face). Signals present-day competence without sacrificing authority. |
| **Previous “Modernization” Campaign Assets** | The mocked slang, forced memes, neon gradients, and pandering taglines from the failed campaign. Complete purge. They carry reputational debt with both audiences. |
| **Generic “Happy Family” Stock Aesthetic** | Replace with documentary-style, real-user photography. The old visual language feels staged to younger viewers and unnecessary to loyalists. |
| **Outdated Mascot / Spokesperson** | If a secondary character never achieved deep equity, retire via **absolute internal directive** with a hard SKU-by-SKU cutoff date. No quiet fade. Inconsistency reads as dysfunction. |
| **The Old Tagline** | If it sounds like a mission statement written by a committee, kill it. **Do not go tagline-free.** Replace immediately with a transitional proof-point line: **“Made to outlast trends.”** |

### Introduce (New Equity to Unlock Trial)

| Element | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Entry-Tier / Trial-Size SKU** | A low-risk format (e.g., smaller size, sample pod, travel format) placed at checkout or front-of-store. Removes economic friction for under-35s who will not spend $18 to experiment against a $9 DTC alternative. |
| **Functional Sub-Brand Architecture** | If product iteration is required, label by **use case, not user age**: “Concentrated,” “Refill Pouch,” “Travel Format.” Never “Heritage” vs. “Modern.” Never generational labels. |
| **Digital Lockup** | A screen-optimized version of the logo for social avatars and mobile web only. Does not replace the primary on-pack mark. |

## 3. CHANNEL & CONTENT APPROACH: SHARED PROOF, DIFFERENT RESONANCE

Do not ghettoize audiences into separate channel universes. A 65-year-old on TikTok will feel excluded by “youth-only” channel segregation; a 28-year-old who learns their parents received a “gratitude” mailer they didn’t will perceive a brand at war with itself. Instead, operate a **“Shared Channels, Tailored Cuts”** model. The confidence to be seen by all ages is itself the proof of relevance.

### Shared Channel Strategy

| Channel | Tactic |
|---|---|
| **Retail (Primary Medium)** | The brand is infrastructure in 40,000+ doors. Use shelf blades, checkout sampling, and entry-tier SKU placement. Signage contrasts 70 years of proof against the novelty brand that bought its way into the same aisle via Instagram ads. **Messaging: “The original is already in your store.”** |
| **YouTube** | Long-form proof. Parallel Monologues (see below). Archival documentaries. |
| **Podcasts** | Host-read ads on culture, history, craft, and entrepreneurship shows. Loyalists skew history/business pods; under-35s skew culture/entrepreneurship. Same brand voice, different contexts. |
| **Owned Social (Instagram / TikTok)** | Strict craft/process brief. Deconstruction, ASMR production, archival footage. No dancing. No slang. |

### Content Pillars by Audience

**For Loyalist Resonance (Retention)**
- **Continuity:** “Same formula. Same standard.”
- **Sovereignty:** “You built this; we defend it.”
- **Gratitude:** Story-collection campaigns that ask for their history with the brand, not their money.

**For Under-35 Trialists (Acquisition)**
- **Deconstruction:** Show the product being made. Transparency is the antidote to “corporate” suspicion.
- **Utility:** Cost-per-use, anti-obsolescence, sustainability credentials.
- **Standard:** Why longevity is a flex in a market built on disposable novelty.

**Shared Proof (No Forced Handshakes)**
- **Parallel Monologues:** Let a 70-year-old master craftsperson talk to camera about standards. Let a 28-year-old talk to camera about why they switched from a DTC competitor. **Same visual grammar. Same proof points. No intergenerational dialogue, no forced handshake.** This avoids the well-documented cringe genre of corporate “two generations talk” theater.

### Retail as Workshop (Not Museum)
The brand is alive in 40,000 doors. It is infrastructure, not a museum piece. Use **Workshop/Foundry/Studio** language—spaces where craft is practiced, not preserved.

### Weaponize Ubiquity: The Retail-Native Trial Strategy
- Place entry-tier SKUs and samples at checkout or high-traffic endcaps where younger consumers already shop.
- Shelf signage: Side-by-side contrast of the brand’s 70-year production record versus the DTC competitor’s 18-month existence.
- QR codes on-pack linking to the archival record (founder documents, ledgers, process film).

### The Household Network
Loyalists are often the literal parents of under-35 targets. If the household buyer feels alienated, the brand disappears from the bathroom cabinet or pantry before the younger consumer ever decides. Never create “parent” vs. “child” product tiers. The goal is to keep the brand in the household ecosystem.

### The Retailer as Stakeholder
Mass retail buyers hate stealth changes and SKU complexity. All packaging and signage refreshes must be announced in trade press **90 days ahead** and pass the buyer’s sniff test. Mass brands do not get to be shy; confidence, not camouflage, wins shelf support.

### Content Rules
- **No dancing. No slang. No memes. No generational labels on product.**
- If it feels like a brand trying to be invited to the party, kill it.
- If it feels like a master craftsman showing their workshop, greenlight it.

## 4. 18-24 MONTH PHASED TRANSITION ROADMAP

**Guiding Principle:** Sacred cows (logo, core SKU) are removed from debate immediately. Internal consensus is built on what the brand **refuses** to do, not on what it approves.

The phases below are sequenced by objective. Monthly markers indicate intent, but progression is gated by readiness, not by arbitrary calendar deadlines.

### Phase 1: Lock the Core (Months 1–6)
*Goal: Stabilize trust. Say nothing publicly about “repositioning.” Demonstrate correction through excellence, not apology.*

- **Heritage Council:** Convene **3 trusted loyalists + 1 archivist.** No youth representatives. Youth insight comes from ethnography (shop-alongs, closet audits), not council seats. The Council aligns on non-negotiables; they do not art-direct.
- **Publish the Internal “No List”:** Codify what the brand refuses to do for the next 18 months: no logo redraw on-pack, no core SKU reformulation, no generational product splits, no dancing, no apologies for our age.
- **Heritage-Forward Proof Launch:** Release a limited batch using original methods, or publish a deep-dive archival story. **Do not reference the previous failed modernization.** Never apologize for the past; simply overwhelm it with proof.
- **Trial-Size Architecture:** Secure supply chain and retail buyer approval for entry-tier SKUs.
- **Inner Circle Preview:** Notify the top 1,000 longest-tenured customers of upcoming packaging ergonomics (e.g., easier grip, recycled cap, clearer ingredient label) **before** they hit shelves. *“The formula you trust is identical. You’ll see these changes on March 1.”* Control the narrative; do not let the shelf control it.

### Phase 2: Confident Expression (Months 7–12)
*Goal: Update visual and verbal expression publicly. If you are ubiquitous, you cannot soft-launch.*

- **Public Visual Refresh:** Roll out new typography and photography guidelines. Accompany with a small **“Est. [Year]”** lockup and the line **“New look. Same standard.”** Announce in trade press. Mass brands own their evolution; they do not hide it.
- **Launch Parallel Monologues:** Release the content series.
