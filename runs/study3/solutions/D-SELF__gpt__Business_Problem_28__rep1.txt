# Industrial-Services Unit  
## Final Turnaround-or-Exit Assessment and Plan

## 1. Executive recommendation

The unit’s losses are most likely **a combination of structural weakness and execution failure**. Three years of losses, two unsuccessful cost restructurings, a shrinking order book, and continuing management attrition demonstrate that overhead reduction alone has not addressed the problem. They do not yet prove that the entire business is structurally unviable.

The board should authorize **one final, cash-capped, 12-month mandate** with two tracks operating from day one:

1. **Selective turnaround:** retain only profitable or demonstrably repairable customers, contracts, service lines, and locations.
2. **Exit preparation:** simultaneously prepare a strategic sale, financial sale, partnership, integration, service-line sale, and orderly wind-down.

The unit should be retained only if it proves that a smaller core can:

- generate positive normalized controllable EBIT;
- generate positive operating cash flow after maintenance capex and working-capital movements;
- cover avoidable fixed costs at realistic utilization;
- maintain sufficient quality-adjusted backlog;
- earn acceptable returns on incremental capital;
- retain or replace critical management capability; and
- operate without recurring, undocumented group subsidies.

### Board approvals requested

| Decision | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Mandate | Approve a 12-month dual-track program |
| Leadership | Appoint one accountable turnaround leader with authority over operating priorities and portfolio choices |
| Funding | Approve an interim day-one spending limit; set the full cash envelope at day 30 |
| Controls | Freeze discretionary growth capex, unapproved bids, and nonessential hiring |
| Gates | Require formal decisions at days 30, 90, 180, 270, and 365 |
| Exit preparation | Begin buyer mapping, carve-out preparation, and wind-down analysis immediately |
| Failure response | Convert to exit-led execution if the month-six gate is missed |

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a turnaround-led program through the month-six gate, but remain exit-ready from the outset. If the retained core has not demonstrated repeatable contribution, improving cash, customer acceptance of necessary pricing, and a credible path to normalized controllable profitability by month six, the group should stop funding a broad turnaround.

## 2. Structural versus execution diagnosis

### Current conclusion

The available facts establish persistent economic underperformance and a high risk of mixed structural and execution problems. The relative contribution of market economics, pricing, scale, contract leakage, and management execution remains to be proven through the first 30–45 days.

### Required profit framework

| Level | Definition | Board use |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution margin | Revenue less costs that vary over the relevant period | Tests whether the work itself is economically viable |
| Controllable EBIT | Contribution less avoidable branch, service-line, and operating costs, after relevant depreciation | Tests whether the retained core can cover its operating structure |
| Reported EBIT | Controllable EBIT less unavoidable shared and group costs | Tests the true group economics after integration, sale, or closure |

Costs must be classified as:

- immediately variable;
- avoidable within 90 days;
- avoidable within 6–12 months;
- unavoidable; or
- stranded after exit.

### Contract bid-to-cash bridge

Each material contract must be reconciled through:

\[
\text{Quoted margin}
\rightarrow
\text{Contracted margin}
\rightarrow
\text{Current forecast}
\rightarrow
\text{Realized margin}
\rightarrow
\text{Cash collected}
\]

The analysis must quantify:

- estimating errors;
- unauthorized discounts;
- overtime and idle time;
- travel and mobilization;
- unbilled changes;
- rework and warranty;
- customer-caused delays;
- equipment downtime;
- claims not recovered;
- disputes and retentions; and
- aged receivables or work in progress.

### Diagnostic interpretation

| Evidence | Diagnosis | Likely action |
|---|---|---|
| Negative contribution across most work | Structural pricing, market, or capability problem | Reposition, sell, or exit |
| Positive quoted margin but negative realized margin | Execution and commercial-control problem | Repair estimating, delivery, scheduling, and claims |
| Positive contribution but negative controllable EBIT | Scale or footprint problem | Consolidate, resize, or integrate |
| Positive controllable EBIT but negative reported EBIT | Allocation or stranded-cost problem | Assess carve-out, shared services, or integration |
| Losses concentrated in a few contracts | Contract governance or selection problem | Renegotiate, complete, or exit |
| Accounting profit but weak cash conversion | Working-capital or contract-term problem | Improve billing, collections, terms, and cash controls |
| Falling renewals and price acceptance despite sound delivery | Potential market or customer-power problem | Test competitive position and willingness to pay |

## 3. Target customers and service lines

The following are **hypotheses to test**, not assumptions to build into the business case.

### Potentially attractive service lines

- recurring maintenance, inspection, and compliance work;
- specialist or regulated services requiring certifications or scarce expertise;
- reliability, retrofit, and mandatory compliance work;
- emergency or high-consequence work where premium pricing is enforceable;
- asset-light, high-utilization services; and
- targeted cross-sell services for existing group accounts.

### Potentially unattractive service lines

- standardized commodity work won mainly on price;
- fixed-price work with volatile labor or material exposure;
- geographically dispersed work with high travel and supervision costs;
- services requiring dedicated assets without adequate utilization;
- contracts with recurring disputes, claims, safety exposure, or poor payment.

### Service-line decisions

| Category | Test | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Invest | Positive realized economics, defensible capability, demand, cash generation, and return above hurdle | Add only selective capacity or capability |
| Fix | Attractive market or capability, but correctable pricing or execution leakage | Execute a time-bound repair plan |
| Harvest | Existing work can be completed, but future economics are weak | Complete without new investment |
| Exit | Negative contribution, no pricing power, or no defensible right to win | Stop bidding, sell, transfer, or close |

A service line may move from **fix** to **invest** only after demonstrating:

- positive realized contribution;
- price above the approved floor;
- acceptable cash conversion;
- realistic break-even utilization;
- sufficient backlog or repeat demand;
- manageable risk and claims;
- capable leadership; and
- return on incremental invested capital above the group hurdle.

### Customer segments

| Segment | Characteristics | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Core value accounts | Positive contribution, recurring demand, acceptable payment and complexity | Protect and selectively expand |
| Strategic repair accounts | Negative today but credible group value or repricing potential | Reprice, redesign, or formally subsidize |
| High-value growth accounts | Positive economics and credible adjacent demand | Targeted cross-sell |
| Harvest accounts | Existing work can be completed but future economics are weak | Complete without new investment |
| Value-destructive accounts | Persistently negative, risky, complex, and nonstrategic | Exit or refuse renewal |
| Concentration-risk accounts | Material dependence with weak pricing power | Reprice and reduce exposure |

### Strategic-account rules

For overlapping customers, calculate:

\[
\text{Total group value}
=
\text{Unit customer value}
+
\text{Incremental value to other divisions}
-
\text{Unit losses}
-
\text{Incremental group support}
\]

Strategic value must be supported by evidence such as:

- historical broader-account retention;
- customer confirmation;
- documented cross-sell opportunity and timing;
- competitor replacement difficulty; or
- observed customer behavior following prior service changes.

Any subsidy must specify:

- direct unit loss;
- incremental group benefit;
- amount and funding source;
- accountable sponsor;
- duration;
- success measure;
- expiry or review trigger; and
- accounting, tax, and transfer-pricing treatment.

Group revenue alone is not strategic value.

## 4. Backlog, market, and competitive viability

### Quality-adjusted backlog

\[
\text{Expected backlog contribution}
=
\text{Remaining revenue}
\times
\text{Execution probability}
\times
\text{Expected realized contribution margin}
-
\text{Risk and working-capital cost}
\]

Report monthly:

- backlog by service line, customer, location, and contract;
- margin at award versus current forecast;
- cancellation and deferral risk;
- three-, six-, and twelve-month coverage;
- book-to-bill;
- renewal backlog;
- labor and equipment requirements;
- customer concentration;
- backlog below pricing floor;
- claims and change-order exposure; and
- expected cash receipts and funding requirements.

A shrinking backlog is not automatically negative if low-margin work is being rejected and weighted contribution is improving. The required test is whether profitable demand can replace rejected work.

### Definition of high-confidence demand

Demand qualifies as high-confidence only if supported by one or more of:

- signed contract or purchase order;
- documented renewal commitment;
- approved framework with reliable historic call-off;
- customer-confirmed award with a defined probability;
- margin above the approved pricing floor; and
- acceptable cash, capacity, and risk terms.

Uncontracted management pipeline is not sufficient to justify growth investment.

## 5. Utilization, fixed costs, and cash

### Break-even utilization

\[
\text{Break-even utilization}
=
\frac{\text{Avoidable fixed operating cost}}
{\text{Available billable hours}\times\text{Contribution per billable hour}}
\]

Calculate by service line, branch, crew, and asset.

Required utilization close to theoretical maximum indicates likely structural weakness. Utilization below break-even with available demand indicates a potential scheduling, sales, footprint, or management problem.

### Cash definitions

- **Operating cash flow:** cash generated after working-capital movements, restructuring cash costs, taxes, and maintenance capex, but before discretionary growth capex and financing flows.
- **Normalized controllable EBIT:** controllable EBIT excluding one-off claims, restructuring credits, asset gains, temporary subsidies, and other nonrecurring items.
- **Sustainable run rate:** three consecutive months of normalized positive controllable EBIT and positive operating cash flow, supported by new or renewed work and a downside-tested forward plan.

### Funding limits

The board should approve separately:

1. **Maximum incremental group funding.**
2. **Maximum peak liquidity requirement.**
3. **Maximum contingent exposure** from guarantees, claims, warranties, closure obligations, and similar liabilities.

The full 12-month cash envelope must include:

- operating losses;
- working-capital peak;
- maintenance capex;
- severance;
- lease exits;
- claims and warranties;
- pension and tax obligations;
- retention payments;
- advisers;
- separation or wind-down costs; and
- a minimum liquidity buffer.

Until day 30, an interim limit should cover only:

- payroll and essential labor;
- safety and regulatory obligations;
- essential maintenance;
- contractual delivery;
- critical customer continuity; and
- approved diagnostic and exit-preparation costs.

### Automatic cash escalation

Immediately freeze discretionary investment and notify the board if:

- forecast cumulative cash use exceeds the approved envelope by the agreed tolerance;
- cash burn exceeds plan for two consecutive months;
- liquidity falls below the approved minimum; or
- new funding, guarantees, or contingent exposure are required.

## 6. One-year turnaround roadmap

| Timing | Required work and evidence | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Days 0–30 | Profit bridge, cash forecast, backlog quality, contract triage, critical-role plan, buyer universe | Set cash envelope and approve diagnostic conclusions |
| Days 31–60 | Service-line and customer segmentation, pricing floors, utilization, exitability, footprint options | Approve retained-core and resizing actions |
| Day 90 | Reduced negative contribution, repricing evidence, stable weighted backlog, management coverage, controlled cash | Continue turnaround-led or switch to exit-led |
| Month 6 | Positive retained-core contribution, improving cash, quantified EBIT bridge, stable backlog | Continue only if final-year test is credible |
| Month 9 | Positive controllable-EBIT trend or contracted path, buyer indications, separation readiness | Select turnaround or exit route |
| Month 12 | Sustainable run rate, positive cash, backlog support, management depth, risk-adjusted value | Retain and invest or execute selected route |

### Days 0–30: Stabilize and establish facts

- Appoint the turnaround leader.
- Establish weekly cash, backlog, and contract reviews.
- Freeze discretionary growth capex, nonessential hiring, and unapproved bids.
- Build the three-year monthly financial bridge.
- Complete customer, contract, service-line, branch, and asset economics.
- Identify the largest loss-making contracts and highest-value accounts.
- Classify backlog as protect, fix, harvest, or exit.
- Launch collections, claims, and change-order recovery.
- Identify critical managers and certification holders.
- Begin buyer mapping, carve-out analysis, and wind-down planning.

Minimum controls:

- 100% of new bids reviewed under the new process;
- 100% of material contracts assigned an owner;
- all major overdue receivables and unbilled work identified; and
- no discretionary growth investment.

### Days 31–90: Select and execute the viable core

- Establish pricing floors.
- Reprice, redesign, complete, or exit loss-making contracts.
- Stop bidding in structurally unattractive segments.
- Calculate break-even utilization and cash break-even.
- Consolidate clearly subscale sites and support functions.
- Redeploy or sell surplus equipment.
- Launch strategic-account plans.
- Establish service-line P&Ls.
- Pilot scheduling, utilization, quality, and billing improvements.
- Test customer willingness to pay.
- Prepare confidential exit materials.

Each material loss-making contract must be classified as:

- repricable immediately;
- repricable at renewal;
- terminable for convenience;
- terminable for cause;
- assignable with consent; or
- non-exitable without material liability.

### Day-90 gate

Continue turnaround-led execution only if:

- negative contribution has materially reduced from the day-30 baseline;
- the retained core is defined;
- the board-approved proportion of required repricing value has been accepted, replaced, or covered by approved subsidy;
- quality-adjusted contribution backlog is stable or improving for two reporting periods;
- critical managers are retained or replaced;
- cash remains within the approved envelope; and
- no material service line depends primarily on speculative growth.

If the gate fails:

- discretionary growth investment stops;
- uncommitted bids are reviewed;
- the board is notified;
- an exit route is selected within 30–60 days; and
- operations continue only to preserve value and meet contractual obligations.

### Months 4–6: Prove repeatable economics

- Complete branch, facility, workforce, and fleet resizing.
- Embed service-line P&Ls.
- Standardize contract forecasting and margin reviews.
- Improve utilization, route density, first-time-right performance, and invoice velocity.
- Negotiate escalation and risk-sharing provisions.
- Transfer or sell noncore services.
- Prepare audited carve-out financials.
- Conduct formal buyer and partner outreach.
- Implement critical-role retention and succession measures.

#### Month-six gate

The retained core must demonstrate:

- positive contribution for at least two consecutive months;
- a quantified bridge to positive controllable EBIT;
- positive or clearly improving operating cash flow before discretionary growth capex;
- stable or rising quality-adjusted backlog;
- improving quoted-to-realized margin variance;
- coverage for all critical roles by an incumbent, successor, or interim leader;
- no material deterioration in safety, quality, or customer retention; and
- a credible path to the month-twelve test without reliance mainly on uncontracted pipeline.

Failure automatically triggers the operating restrictions above and requires the board to select a preferred exit route within 30–60 days.

### Months 7–9: Scale the core or prepare separation

**Turnaround track**

- Scale only services meeting margin, cash, utilization, and risk thresholds.
- Add capacity only against contracted or high-confidence demand.
- Expand proven profitable cross-selling.
- Secure escalation clauses and risk-sharing.
- Continue monthly customer and contract reviews.

**Exit track**

- Obtain qualified indications of interest.
- Resolve assignment and customer-consent issues.
- Prepare transitional service agreements.
- Separate systems, leases, people, IP, certifications, and guarantees.
- Quantify stranded costs and closure liabilities.
- Prepare customer, employee, regulator, lender, and supplier communications.

### Months 10–12: Final decision

The final board paper must show:

- actual versus plan performance;
- normalized controllable EBIT and reported result;
- operating cash generation after maintenance capex;
- retained service-line and customer economics;
- quality-adjusted backlog and forward pipeline;
- management stability and succession;
- strategic-account value and subsidy exposure;
- future capital requirements;
- risk-adjusted values for all alternatives;
- stranded costs and contingent liabilities; and
- residual execution, legal, market, and stakeholder risks.

The one-year requirement is an executable board decision and route. A transaction need not close by month twelve if funding, controls, separation planning, and a credible long-stop date are approved.

## 7. KPIs and trigger thresholds

Exact financial amounts should be set from the day-30 baseline. The definitions and direction of travel should be fixed at launch.

| KPI | Day 90 | Month 6 | Month 12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Negative-contribution work | Material reduction from baseline | Isolated to approved harvest/exit contracts | No material retained exposure |
| Repricing value | Board-approved proportion accepted, replaced, or subsidized | Majority implemented in retained core | New and renewed work above pricing floor |
| Quality-adjusted backlog | Stable or improving for two periods | Supports resized cost base | Supports next 12 months |
| Retained-core contribution | Improving | Positive for at least two consecutive months | Positive and repeatable |
| Controllable EBIT | Quantified bridge established | Clear path to positive | Positive normalized run rate for three months |
| Operating cash flow | No uncontrolled deterioration | Positive or clearly improving | Positive after maintenance capex |
| Quoted-to-realized margin variance | Improving | Within approved tolerance | Within approved tolerance consistently |
| Utilization | Measured against break-even | At or near break-even | Sustainable at target level |
| Critical roles | All identified and actioned | Incumbent, successor, or interim cover for each | Stable management depth |
| Customer retention | At-risk accounts identified | No material deterioration | Within approved target |
| Safety and quality | No uncontrolled deterioration | Within approved limits | Within approved limits |
| Subsidies | All documented and approved | Within cap and expiry terms | No recurring undocumented support |

### Automatic escalation triggers

Escalate to the board and freeze discretionary investment if:

- the month-six gate is missed;
- cash-envelope or liquidity limits are breached;
- quality-adjusted backlog declines for two consecutive periods;
- repricing acceptance is materially below plan;
- a major customer or contract is lost;
- a critical certification or manager cannot be replaced within the approved period;
- a serious safety, quality, environmental, or regulatory event occurs; or
- a qualified buyer offer exceeds the risk-adjusted turnaround value.

## 8. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Customers reject repricing | Redesign scope, alter service levels, add escalation, or exit at the earliest permitted date |
| Backlog falls faster than costs | Accelerate resizing and exit; do not chase unprofitable volume |
| Cash exceeds limit | Freeze discretionary spend, revise forecast, and seek board approval before further funding |
| Margin improves but cash does not | Intensify billing, collections, deposits, claims recovery, and working-capital controls |
| Loss of largest customer | Activate downside plan, resize capacity, and assess sale or integration of affected operations |
| Critical manager or certification holder leaves | Deploy interim leadership, protect customer continuity, reduce intake, and reassess service-line viability |
| Major safety, quality, license, or regulatory event | Stop-work authority, independent review, remediation, and board escalation |
| Customer consent unavailable | Complete, reprice, transfer where permitted, or quantify closure liability |
| Labor shortage or union resistance | Use interim capacity, renegotiate timing, reduce intake, and revise closure assumptions |
| Buyer withdraws | Move to qualified alternative buyer, service-line sale, integration, or wind-down |
| Strategic subsidies expand | Require CFO approval, a fixed cap, expiry date, and measurable group benefit |
| Integration hides losses | Require receiving-division standalone economics and a signed transfer plan |
| Litigation, environmental, pension, or tax exposure emerges | Obtain specialist advice, reserve cash, and revalue each alternative |
| Group funding becomes constrained | Prioritize contractual and safety obligations and accelerate exit-led actions |

## 9. Exit alternatives

### Strategic sale

Preferred where the unit has differentiated capabilities, certifications, customer access, geographic density, or assets that create buyer synergies.

Value should reflect:

- normalized earnings;
- recurring revenue;
- quality-adjusted backlog;
- customer retention;
- required investment;
- liabilities and warranties;
- separation costs; and
- probability of completion.

### Financial buyer or private equity

Suitable only if the business is separable and has credible operational upside. Likely constraints include lower valuation, seller financing, extensive warranties, and buyer concern over cash requirements and management stability.

### Management buyout

Consider only if:

- a committed management team remains;
- the post-separation core is viable;
- financing is credible;
- customer retention is demonstrable;
- seller support is limited and defined; and
- conflicts of interest are governed.

### Joint venture or strategic partnership

Appropriate where the unit needs scale, capital, capability, or geographic density. It is not a solution to negative underlying contribution and may leave the group with governance and contingent liabilities.

### Integration into profitable divisions

Transfer only profitable, strategically valuable activities. The receiving division must sign off on:

- contracts;
- employees;
- assets;
- liabilities;
- customer obligations;
- capacity;
- management bandwidth;
- post-transfer margin and cash targets; and
- stranded-cost funding.

### Asset or service-line sale

Appropriate where selected capabilities, contracts, equipment, licenses, or customer relationships are valuable but the whole unit is not. Residual liabilities and stranded costs must be modeled separately.

### Orderly wind-down or selective closure

Complete value-preserving work, stop new loss-making work, sell assets, close sites, settle claims, and manage employee and customer obligations.

The wind-down case must include a cash and liability waterfall covering:

- severance;
- leases;
- contract penalties;
- warranties;
- environmental matters;
- pensions and benefits;
- equipment realization;
- customer remediation;
- tax and regulatory costs; and
- timing of cash outflows.

### Exit timetable

| Period | Exit action |
|---|---|
| Days 0–30 | Define perimeter, buyer universe, separation assumptions, and legal constraints |
| Days 31–60 | Appoint advisers, begin carve-out accounts, legal review, and data-room preparation |
| Days 61–90 | Prepare confidential information materials and initial buyer approach |
| Months 4–6 | Conduct confidential market test |
| Months 7–9 | Obtain qualified indications of interest and identify preferred route |
| Months 9–12 | Negotiate, obtain approvals, and prepare execution |
| Post-month 12 | Close transaction or implement approved fallback under interim funding and operating controls |

### Exit fallback sequence

Unless value evidence indicates otherwise, the fallback sequence should be:

1. strategic sale;
2. financial buyer or service-line sale;
3. integration of profitable activities;
4. controlled wind-down.

Continued marketing after the agreed process deadline requires board approval and an updated value comparison.

## 10. Turnaround-versus-divestment decision criteria

### Choose turnaround if

By month six, and finally by month twelve:

- at least one service line has demonstrated attractive realized contribution;
- losses are concentrated in identifiable and addressable areas;
- customers accept required repricing or scope redesign;
- the retained core covers avoidable fixed costs at realistic utilization;
- quality-adjusted backlog supports the resized business;
- cash requirements remain within the approved limits;
- critical management capability is retained or replaced;
- profitability does not depend primarily on speculative growth;
- strategic benefits are evidenced and quantified;
- downside performance remains acceptable; and
- risk-adjusted turnaround value exceeds the best exit alternative.

### Choose divestment, integration, partnership, or wind-down if

- contribution remains negative after realistic corrective action;
- no defensible service line exists;
- repricing is rejected;
- break-even utilization is unrealistic;
- backlog quality continues to deteriorate;
- cash burn remains material;
- critical management capability cannot be restored;
- strategic benefits can be preserved through another structure; or
- risk-adjusted exit value exceeds the turnaround case.

### Common valuation framework

\[
\text{Risk-adjusted turnaround value}
=
\text{Probability-weighted future cash value}
-
\text{Turnaround funding}
-
\text{Execution risk}
\]

\[
\text{Risk-adjusted exit value}
=
\text{Sale or recovery proceeds}
-
\text{Separation, closure, tax, claims, and stranded costs}
\]

All alternatives must be compared using:

- controlled continuation;
- turnaround success, partial success, and failure;
- strategic sale;
- financial sale;
- MBO or partnership;
- integration;
- service-line sale; and
- wind-down.

Each case should include after-tax cash flows, timing, transaction costs, debt and guarantees, liabilities, working capital, stranded costs, completion probability, and sensitivity to price, volume, utilization, customer loss, collections, and timing.

## 11. Assumptions and data required

### Assumptions

- The unit’s financial, contract, customer, and operational data can be reconciled within 30–45 days.
- The group can impose temporary bid, capex, and funding controls.
- Key employees and customers can be retained long enough to preserve value.
- Contract rights and customer-consent requirements can be established promptly.
- At least some exit alternatives remain available before further deterioration.
- The group can fund the approved interim limit and, subject to board approval, the full cash envelope.

These assumptions must be tested and reported with confidence ratings.

### Data required

**Financial**

- Three-year monthly P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow.
- Contract, customer, service-line, branch, and asset profit bridges.
- Recurring versus one-time restructuring benefits.
- Cost avoidability by time horizon.
- Working capital, guarantees, debt, and contingent liabilities.
- Maintenance and growth capex.
- Tax consequences of each alternative.

**Commercial**

- Backlog by customer, service line, margin, timing, and risk.
- Pipeline conversion, win/loss, pricing, and competitor data.
- Renewal, churn, satisfaction, and share of wallet.
- Customer willingness to accept repricing.
- Contract escalation, termination, assignment, claims, and change-order rights.
- Strategic value to other group divisions.

**Operational**

- Utilization, productive hours, overtime, subcontracting, travel, and rework.
- Equipment and fleet utilization.
- Branch and facility break-even.
- Safety, quality, warranty, environmental, and regulatory exposure.
- Labor availability and certification requirements.
- Capacity required for retained backlog.
- Shared-resource constraints and opportunity costs.

**Organization and legal**

- Attrition by role and destination.
- Critical managers, specialists, and certifications.
- Succession coverage and vacancy risk.
- Incentive plans and decision rights.
- Employee-transfer, redundancy, pension, and collective-agreement obligations.
- Lease, litigation, environmental, insurance, IP, license, and permit exposure.
- Transitional service requirements.
- Buyer universe and preliminary indications of interest.
- Asset liquidation values and wind-down cash requirements.

## Final board decision agenda

At the next board meeting, management should present and seek decisions on:

1. **Mandate:** approve the 12-month dual-track program.
2. **Leadership:** appoint the accountable turnaround leader and confirm authority boundaries.
3. **Funding:** approve the interim day-one spending limit and timetable for setting the full cash envelope.
4. **Controls:** approve bid, capex, hiring, subsidy, guarantee, and cash-approval rules.
5. **Diagnostic:** require the day-30 profit, cash, backlog, contract, customer, and management fact base.
6. **Portfolio:** approve the methodology for classifying customers and service lines as invest, fix, harvest, or exit.
7. **Strategic accounts:** require documented group value and capped, time-limited subsidies.
8. **Gates:** confirm the day-90 and month-six thresholds and automatic consequences of failure.
9. **Exit readiness:** authorize buyer mapping, carve-out preparation, and wind-down analysis from day one.
10. **Final decision standard:** confirm that month-twelve retention requires normalized controllable profitability, positive operating cash after maintenance capex, sufficient quality-adjusted backlog, management depth, and risk-adjusted value superior to exit.
11. **Cost of delay:** require the board to review the cash and value impact of continuing beyond each failed gate.
12. **Decision at month twelve:** retain and invest, sell, integrate, partner, divest selected service lines, or wind down based on risk-adjusted net value.
