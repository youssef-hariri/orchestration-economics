**CONFIDENTIAL BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Corporate Strategy & Turnaround Office  
**DATE:** October 24, 2023  
**SUBJECT:** Industrial-Services (IndServ) Unit: Final Turnaround-or-Exit Assessment and 1-Year Execution Plan  

---

### **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**
The Industrial-Services (IndServ) unit has incurred consecutive losses for three years. Two prior restructuring cycles focused exclusively on cost reduction, which failed to stem the bleeding. This confirms that the unit’s challenges are not merely operational, but fundamentally structural. Compounding this, the attrition of top management and a shrinking order book signal a critical loss of market and internal confidence.

This document provides a definitive diagnostic of the unit’s failures, isolates the profitable core of the business, outlines a rigorous 1-year turnaround plan, and establishes clear criteria and alternatives for divestment. To preserve corporate capital and protect our profitable divisions, leadership has been mandated to produce a credible path to profitability within 12 months. 

Crucially, the Board will not wait until Month 12 to decide the unit's fate. A strict, quantifiable **Go/No-Go Gate** will be executed at the end of Month 6 (End of Q2) to determine whether to continue the turnaround or initiate immediate divestment.

---

### **1. STRATEGIC DIAGNOSTIC: STRUCTURAL VS. EXECUTION DRIVERS**
Prior cost-cutting attempts treated execution as the root cause. The persistence of losses proves the failure is primarily **structural**, compounded by poor execution. We cannot cost-cut our way out of a flawed revenue model.

**Structural Drivers (The Root Causes):**
*   **The "False Synergy" Trap:** IndServ is structurally mispriced. It is utilized as a loss-leader to secure equipment sales for the group's profitable divisions, but lacks a formal transfer-pricing mechanism to compensate for the margin sacrifice. The group is structurally subsidizing IndServ’s losses.
*   **Portfolio Misalignment:** The unit is over-indexed in commoditized, low-barrier routine maintenance. The market is structurally shifting toward OEM-direct and predictive maintenance, rendering IndServ’s legacy service mix obsolete.
*   **Working Capital Trap:** Current contract structures structurally trap cash in Operating Working Capital (OWC), funding the losses through cash bleed rather than just P&L erosion.

**Execution Drivers (The Compounding Factors):**
*   **Misaligned Commercial Incentives:** Sales teams discount IndServ services heavily to protect shared customer relationships because their compensation is tied to total account revenue, not IndServ’s gross margin.
*   **Poor Project Scope Management:** Execution on fixed-price legacy contracts lacks rigorous scope control, leading to margin erosion when unforeseen technical issues arise.
*   **Reactive Talent Management:** The failure to retain top managers is an execution failure in leadership communication, resulting in a loss of institutional knowledge and client trust.

---

### **2. PORTFOLIO RATIONALIZATION & SHARED-CUSTOMER PROTECTION**
To find a path to profitability, we must ruthlessly segment the business and prune value-destroying elements while strictly protecting the group's core profitable divisions.

**Customer & Service Line Segmentation:**
*   **Tier 1: Strategic Shared Customers (The Core).** Customers who buy from both IndServ and profitable divisions. *Action:* Retain and grow via integrated "Total Uptime" solutions. High switching costs justify premium pricing.
*   **Tier 2: Independent Niche Customers.** Customers exclusive to IndServ requiring specialized, high-margin services. *Action:* Selectively retain.
*   **Tier 3: Transactional/Price-Sensitive Customers.** Customers buying commoditized services purely on price. *Action:* **"Price-to-Shed" run-off.** (See mitigation strategy below).
*   **Service Lines:** Exit commoditized routine maintenance; scale specialized technical services; invest in integrated digital/predictive solutions.

**Shared-Customer Risk Mitigation Strategy:**
Aggressive cost-cutting in IndServ threatens the revenue of the group’s profitable divisions. We will mitigate this collateral damage through the following protocols:
*   **Ring-Fenced Account Management:** IndServ and profitable divisions will operate under Joint Account Plans for Tier 1 customers. No unilateral price hikes or service degradations will occur without joint approval.
*   **Value-Engineered Transitions:** For shared customers facing price increases, we will bundle low-cost/high-perceived-value digital tools (e.g., basic predictive monitoring) to justify the increase and maintain the total value proposition.
*   **Decoupled Sales Incentives:** Equipment division reps will no longer be penalized if IndServ loses a shared customer due to price increases. IndServ reps will be bonused strictly on *margin*.
*   **"Price-to-Shed" for Tier 3:** For non-shared, unprofitable customers, we will not breach contracts. We will let them run off to completion. Upon renewal, we will reprice contracts 15-20% above market rates, allowing the customer to naturally churn without legal or reputational risk.

### **3. THE 1-YEAR TURNAROUND EXECUTION PLAN**
This plan shifts the unit from a "cost-center" mindset to a "value-creation" mindset, executed with strict commercial discipline.

#### **Quarter 1 (Days 1-90): Triage, Cash Control, and Incentive Realignment**
*   **Objective:** Stop the cash bleed, optimize working capital, and fix commercial incentives.
*   **Key Actions:** Implement a hard "Price-to-Win" gate (no new bids <22% GM). Optimize OWC by extending Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) by 15 days and enforcing 30-day AR collections. Roll out new margin-based sales compensation and targeted retention packages for top technical talent.
*   **Milestones:**
    *   Monthly cash burn reduced by 50% from baseline.
    *   Gross margin on all *new* bids strictly >22%.
    *   Top 15 critical talent retention at 100%.

#### **Quarter 2 (Days 91-180): Portfolio Pruning & Go-to-Market Pivot**
*   **Objective:** Shed commoditized work and launch the integrated "Total Uptime" offering.
*   **Key Actions:** Execute "Price-to-Shed" renewals for all Tier 3 contracts expiring in Q2/Q3. Launch joint "Total Uptime" sales campaign targeting the Top 20 Tier 1 shared customers. Renegotiate the bottom 20% of legacy fixed-price contracts with Tier 1 customers, introducing inflation-linked escalators.
*   **Milestones:**
    *   Blended gross margin expands by 300 bps vs. baseline.
    *   Tier 1 shared customer retention remains at 100% (zero churn due to IndServ friction).
    *   Pipeline for high-margin specialized/predictive services grows by 25%.

#### **Quarter 3 (Days 181-270): Operational Restructuring & Variable Cost Shift**
*   **Objective:** Rightsize the cost base to match the new, higher-margin revenue footprint.
*   **Key Actions:** Shift 30% of routine field maintenance to a vetted, variable-cost subcontractor network. Consolidate IndServ back-office functions (HR, IT, Finance) into the parent group’s shared services center. Deploy predictive maintenance software pilot on Top 10 Tier 1 accounts.
*   **Milestones:**
    *   SG&A overhead reduced by 15% via shared-services migration.
    *   Field labor productivity (Revenue per Technician) increases by 20%.
    *   Unit achieves monthly EBITDA breakeven.

#### **Quarter 4 (Days 271-365): Validation, Scale, and Strategic Review**
*   **Objective:** Prove the new operating model generates cash and prepare for the Month 12 Board decision.
*   **Key Actions:** Scale the predictive maintenance pilot to all Tier 1 accounts. Sustain the new operating model to generate positive Free Cash Flow (FCF). Prepare comprehensive data room and management presentations for the final strategic review.
*   **Milestones:**
    *   Achieve target EBITDA margin of 10-12%.
    *   Generate positive FCF for three consecutive months.

### **4. EXIT ALTERNATIVES & VALUE MAXIMIZATION**
If the Month 6 gate triggers a "No-Go," or if the Month 12 review dictates an exit, we will pursue the following alternatives, ranked by value preservation:

1.  **Contract Carve-Out & Customer Migration (Primary Strategy):** 
    *   *Mechanism:* Transfer the service contracts of the Tier 1 shared customers directly into the profitable divisions (capturing the synergy). Sell the remaining standalone IndServ assets, Tier 2 customers, and field workforce to a Trade Buyer or PE firm.
    *   *Rationale:* Maximizes group value by retaining the strategic shared customers while shedding the unprofitable standalone operational burden.
2.  **Trade Sale to a Strategic Competitor:** 
    *   *Mechanism:* Full sale of the standalone IndServ unit. 
    *   *Rationale:* Highest standalone valuation, but requires strict clean-room protocols to prevent the buyer from poaching Tier 1 shared customers during diligence.
3.  **Private Equity Carve-Out:** 
    *   *Mechanism:* Sale to a PE turnaround firm, potentially with the group retaining a 10-20% minority stake and providing vendor financing.
    *   *Rationale:* Faster execution than a trade sale, but yields a lower multiple.

### **5. MONTH 6 GO/NO-GO DECISION MATRIX**
The Board will evaluate the following strict, quantifiable criteria at the end of Month 6 (End of Q2). There is no subjective judgment; the data will dictate the path.

**Criteria to CONTINUE the Turnaround (The "Go"):**
*   **Financial:** Blended gross margin has expanded by **≥ 300 bps** from the baseline, and monthly cash burn has reached **$0**.
*   **Commercial:** The order book has stabilized, and the pipeline for high-margin specialized services has grown by **≥ 25%**.
*   **Shared-Customer Risk:** **100%** of Tier 1 shared customers have been retained; **≥ 3** new integrated, multi-year "Total Uptime" contracts have been signed.
*   **Talent:** Top 15 critical managers have signed 3-year retention agreements; attrition among this group is **0%**.

**Criteria to INITIATE DIVESTMENT (The "No-Go"):**
*   **Financial:** Blended gross margin expansion is **< 150 bps**, or monthly cash burn remains **> 20%** of the baseline.
*   **Commercial:** Order book volume continues to shrink by **> 10%** despite the pivot, or "Price-to-Win" on new bids remains structurally below the cost of delivery.
*   **Shared-Customer Risk:** Loss of **any** Tier 1 shared customer due to IndServ pricing/service friction, OR retaliation from a shared customer against the profitable divisions.
*   **Talent:** Attrition of the top 15 critical technical staff exceeds **15%**.
