# **COMPREHENSIVE GLOBAL SUPPLY CHAIN RESILIENCE & ETHICAL SOURCING STRATEGY**

## **Executive Summary**

This document presents a **decision-ready, integrated strategy** to simultaneously enhance supply chain resilience, ensure ethical and legal compliance, and maintain competitive cost positioning across all tiers of a global manufacturing supply chain.

**Strategic Objective:** Build a supply chain that delivers:
- **Resilience:** Survive and rapidly recover from geopolitical, environmental, pandemic, and logistics disruptions
- **Ethical Compliance:** Meet binding regulatory obligations (EU CSDDD, US UFLPA, German LkSG, UK Modern Slavery Act) and align with stakeholder expectations
- **Economic Competitiveness:** Without unsustainable cost inflation or degradation of supplier relationships

**Scope:** All tiers (raw material origin through Tier 1 component suppliers); all material categories (direct materials, production supplies, capital equipment).

**Implementation Timeline:** 24 months (Phases 0–3); ongoing governance thereafter.

**Key Findings:**
- Single-source and geographic concentration expose the typical manufacturer to 10–15% annual disruption probability, with business impacts of $10–50M per major incident
- Multi-layered resilience investments (dual-sourcing, friend-shoring, inventory buffering, multi-tier visibility) cost approximately 0.3–0.5% of COGS annually, delivering 2–3x ROI through disruption avoidance and inventory optimization
- Ethical compliance is no longer optional—regulatory exposure (CSDDD fines €5M or 5–10% revenue; UFLPA goods seizures; Modern Slavery Act prosecution) exceeds resilience investment costs
- Audit-based compliance alone is insufficient; effective due diligence requires multi-layered approach: risk identification, transparency, announced/unannounced audits, worker voice mechanisms, forensic verification, continuous monitoring, and documented remediation

**Top 5 Priority Actions (Next 90 Days):**
1. Establish Steering Committee and issue Tier 1 Supplier Disclosure Questionnaire (Month 1)
2. Complete single-source criticality mapping and TTR/TTS analysis for all materials (Months 1–3)
3. Begin TCO modeling and friend-shore supplier identification (Months 2–4)
4. Draft and board-approve Human Rights Due Diligence Policy aligned with UNGP/OECD/CSDDD (Month 2)
5. Establish third-party grievance mechanism and initiate unannounced audit program (Months 2–3)

## **SECTION 1: STRATEGIC CONTEXT & RISK LANDSCAPE**

### **1.1 Current-State Vulnerabilities**

Manufacturing companies in multi-country supply chains face compounding risks across eight integrated dimensions:

| **Risk Dimension** | **Key Exposure** | **Business Impact** | **Base-Rate Probability** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Geopolitical & Trade** | Tariffs, sanctions, export controls, regionalization | China tariffs 25%; semiconductor supply disruption; UFLPA enforcement | 10–20% annually (by region) |
| **Natural & Climate** | Earthquakes, typhoons, floods, water stress, seasonal disruptions | Taiwan typhoons (4–5/year); Xinjiang water stress; monsoon disruptions SE Asia | 5–15% annually (by geography) |
| **Logistics & Infrastructure** | Port congestion, carrier bankruptcy, choke points | LA port congestion (2021–2023); Suez closure (2021); Panama Canal drought (2023) | 8–12% annually (affected routes) |
| **Supplier-Specific** | Financial distress, single-source dependency, capacity constraints, quality issues | Supplier bankruptcy 2–5%/year; ransomware 10–15%/year; capacity shortages endemic in semiconductors | 2–5% per supplier annually |
| **Cybersecurity & Digital** | Ransomware, supply chain software compromise, data breaches, system availability | SolarWinds (2020), 3CX (2023), Evonik (2020); 3–6 week production shutdowns | 5–10% per supplier annually |
| **Pandemic & Labour** | Infectious disease outbreaks, labour unrest, skill shortages, migration restrictions | COVID-19 precedent: 6–12 week production losses; avian flu emerging risk | 3–8% annually (by region/sector) |
| **Regulatory & Compliance** | Environmental, labour, trade, product safety regulations | CSDDD fines €5M or 5–10% revenue; UFLPA seizures; Modern Slavery Act prosecution | 5–20% annually (by geography/material) |
| **ESG & Reputational** | Forced labour, child labour, wage theft, environmental externalities, conflict minerals | Uyghur Forced Labour Prevention Act enforcement; NGO campaigns; investor divestment | 10–30% annually (high-risk geographies) |

**Quantified Vulnerability Summary:**
- 88% of procurement leaders report inadequate supplier financial monitoring
- Only 42% of companies have visibility beyond Tier 1 suppliers
- Single-source dependency exists for 30–50% of critical components in most industries
- Typical disruption cost: 10–50x monthly component cost (example: $1M component annual spend → $10–50M business impact if supply lost)
- Expected annual supply chain disruption cost (baseline): 8–15% × 5–10% revenue impact = 0.4–1.5% of annual revenue

### **1.2 Regulatory Framework & Compliance Obligations**

| **Regulation** | **Applicability** | **Key Requirement** | **Penalty** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EU CSDDD** | EU companies (>€500M turnover) + non-EU companies selling into EU | Documented due diligence across all tiers; continuous monitoring; remediation; public reporting | €5M or 5–10% global turnover |

**Problem:** Existing supply chains are simultaneously fragile (vulnerable to disruption) and non-compliant (exposed to regulatory and reputational risk).

**Solution:** Integrated resilience + ethics strategy that addresses both through coordinated sourcing, visibility, verification, and governance actions.

**Value Proposition:**
- **Risk reduction:** Shift from 10–15% annual disruption probability to 5–8%; reduce expected disruption cost by $3–5M annually
- **Compliance:** Eliminate regulatory exposure (CSDDD, UFLPA, Modern Slavery Act); reduce legal/reputational liability by $10–50M+
- **Cost:** Investment of 0.3–0.5% COGS ($600K–$1M annually for a $500M revenue manufacturer) for resilience + compliance program

### **2.1 Supplier Diversification & Multi-Sourcing (Resilience Pillar 1)**

#### **2.1.1 Strategic Framework**

**Target Sourcing Matrix (by Material Criticality):**

| **Material Category** | **Primary Supplier** | **Secondary Supplier** | **Strategic Buffer** | **Geographic Spread** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Critical components** (semiconductors, specialized fasteners, ECUs) | 60% volume | 40% volume; qualified, interchangeable specs | 1–2 units/year inventory | Americas + Asia-Pacific OR Europe + Asia |
| **Standard components** (fasteners, connectors, housings) | 50% volume | 50% volume; cross-qualified ISO/DIN | Readily switch per signal | 3+ geographic regions |
| **Raw materials** (metals, resins, chemicals) | 60% supplier + diversified origin | 40% supplier + alternate origin | 4–8 weeks strategic inventory | Separate continents where feasible |

#### **2.1.2 Phase 1a: Current-State Audit (Months 1–3)**

**Activity 1: Single-Source Risk Mapping**
- **Scope:** Audit 100% of SKUs; identify materials with ≤2 qualified suppliers
- **Classification:** Lead-time (weeks), annual volume ($), switching cost ($), revenue impact if unavailable (%)
- **Heat Map:** High-Risk (single-source, >8 weeks, >5% revenue impact); Medium (single-source, 4–8 weeks, 2–5%); Low (multi-source, <4 weeks)
- **Target:** ≥95% SKU coverage
- **Owner:** Procurement + Engineering
- **Measurement:** Number of high/medium/low-risk SKUs; % revenue in each category

**Activity 2: TTR/TTS Analysis**

For each high-risk material, calculate:
```
Time-to-Recover (TTR) = Recognition delay (2 days) 
                      + Secondary activation (3 days) 
                      + Order-to-delivery (lead-time) 
                      + Validation (2–3 weeks)

Time-to-Survive (TTS) = (Safety stock + Strategic buffer + In-transit inventory) 
                       / Daily consumption

Decision Rule: If TTS < TTR + 2 weeks → UNACCEPTABLE RISK
```

**Example:** Microcontroller (12-week lead-time, no secondary, 4-week safety stock):
- TTR = 2 + 3 + 84 + 14 = 10 weeks
- TTS = 4 weeks
- **Gap: 6 weeks unmet demand** → High-risk; immediate mitigation required

- **Owner:** Procurement + Operations
- **Measurement:** % of critical materials with TTS ≥ TTR + 2 weeks (target: 100%)

**Activity 3: Value-at-Risk (VaR) Quantification**

For each high-risk material:
```
VaR = Disruption Probability × Revenue Impact (%)

Example: Semiconductor supplier
  Disruption probability:   15%/year
  Revenue at risk if supply lost:  6% of $500M = $30M
  VaR = 15% × $30M = $4.5M/year expected disruption cost
```

- **Owner:** Procurement + Finance
- **Measurement:** Total supply chain VaR; top 20 materials by VaR; aggregate risk trend

#### **2.1.3 Phase 1b: Secondary Supplier Qualification (Months 4–12)**

**Activity 1: Tender & Supplier Selection (Months 4–6)**

For top 20–30 high-risk SKUs:
- Issue RFQ to 3–5 alternative suppliers
- **Selection Criteria:**
  - Cost premium: Accept up to 10% if lead-time <10% longer
  - Certifications: ISO 9001, IATF 16949 (automotive), IPC (electronics)
  - Financial health: D&B score >70; no payment defaults
  - Capacity: Commit to 30–40% volume within 6 months
- **Capacity Reservation Contract:**
  - Secondary maintains 20–30% idle capacity for your material
  - Standby fee: 2–5% of annual contract value
  - Surge activation: You commit to 30-day activation window
  - Term: 3–5 years

- **Owner:** Procurement
- **Measurement:** Secondary suppliers selected for 20+ SKUs; contracts signed

**Activity 2: Design Flexibility & Compatibility (Months 4–8, parallel)**

- Engineering work: Create designs accepting 2–3 supplier architectures (same pinout, firmware-agnostic)
- **Example:** Microcontroller PCB accepts STM32, NXP, TI variants
- **Cost:** $50K–$200K NRE per component; payback ~12 months via disruption risk reduction
- **Owner:** Engineering + Procurement
- **Measurement:** % of critical components with dual-compatible design

**Activity 3: Qualification (Months 7–12)**

- Process: First-article inspection (FAI), process FMEA, qualification runs, PPAP level 3
- Timeline: 8–12 weeks per supplier
- Quality agreement: Defect rates, on-time delivery, communication SLAs
- **Owner:** Quality + Engineering + Procurement
- **Measurement:** Secondary suppliers PPAP level 3 qualified; dual-sourcing contracts signed; first orders executed

**Financial Impact (Power Semiconductor Example):**

| **Metric** | **Single-Source** | **Dual-Source** | **Benefit** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit cost | $1.00 | $1.06 (+6%) | Resilience gain |
| Annual disruption cost (expected) | $4.5M (15% × 6% impact) | $1.5M (5% × 6% impact) | **$3M savings** |
| Lead-time | 12 weeks | 8 weeks avg | Inventory ↓ $2–3M |
| Annual NRE | $0 | $150K | One-time investment |
| Year 1 net benefit | — | -$150K NRE + $3M disruption reduction + $2.5M inventory = **$5.35M** |
| Year 2+ annual benefit | — | -$80K secondary cost + $3M disruption reduction = **$2.92M/year** |

### **2.2 Geographic Diversification & Friend-Shoring (Resilience Pillar 2)**

#### **2.2.1 Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Model**

**Formula:**
```
TCO = Unit cost + Freight cost + Tariffs + Inventory holding cost 
      + Quality/rework cost + Supply disruption risk cost

Disruption Risk Cost = Disruption Probability × Business Impact (%)
```

**Example—Power Semiconductor (200 units/week; $500M company):**

**OPTION A – Offshore (China):**
```
Unit cost:                         $1.00
Freight (sea, 8 weeks):            +$0.15
Tariffs (25% China):               +$0.25
Holding cost (8 weeks):            +$0.12
Quality/rework (3% defect):        +$0.05
Supply disruption risk:            +$0.20 (15% × 6% impact × $500M)
─────────────────────────────────────────
TCO TOTAL:                         $1.77/unit
```

**OPTION B – Friend-Shore (EU/Mexico):**
```
Unit cost:                         $1.30
Freight (truck, 2 weeks):          +$0.06
Tariffs:                           $0
Holding cost (2 weeks):            +$0.03
Quality/rework (0.5% defect):      +$0.01
Supply disruption risk:            +$0.04 (5% × 2% impact)
─────────────────────────────────────────
TCO TOTAL:                         $1.44/unit

RESULT: Friend-shore TCO 19% lower despite 30% higher unit cost
```

#### **2.2.2 Sourcing Segmentation Strategy**

| **Category** | **Sourcing Strategy** | **Geographic Split** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Critical, high-mix components** (semiconductors, ECUs) | Near-shore or Friend-shore primary | 70–100% EU/US/Mexico/Canada | Margins absorb premium; rapid iteration; geopolitical insulation |
| **Standard volume components** (fasteners, connectors, housings) | Hybrid: Offshore primary + Friend-shore secondary | 70% offshore (Vietnam, India) + 30% friend-shore (Poland, Mexico, Portugal) | Dual-source resilience; leverage offshore cost; maintain backup |
| **Raw materials** (metals, resins, chemicals) | Diversified by origin country | 60% cost-optimized + 40% alternate developed-economy supplier | Spread geopolitical risk; ensure continuity if primary disrupted |
| **High-risk materials** (rare earths, conflict minerals, polysilicon, cotton) | Certified, traceable suppliers only | 100% from non-conflict, ethically verified sources | Regulatory compliance (CSDDD, UFLPA); no cost exemption |

#### **2.2.3 Implementation Plan**

**Phase 2a: TCO Modeling & Segmentation (Months 1–3)**

**Activities:**
1. Build TCO model (spreadsheet or tool) for all material categories
2. Identify categories where friend-shore wins on total cost
3. For categories where offshore still cheaper: Plan dual-sourcing (70/30 for resilience)
4. Create business case: Total COGS impact if shift to 30% friend-shore; expected disruption risk reduction; inventory optimization benefit
5. Obtain board approval for program costs

**Phase 2b: Friend-Shore Supplier Selection (Months 4–10)**

**Activities:**
1. Identify criteria: Price (within 10–20% of offshore), lead-time (<4 weeks), certifications (ISO 9001, IATF 16949, IPC, SA8000), regulatory compliance
2. Issue RFQ to 5–10 candidates per material category
3. Select 2–3 final suppliers per category
4. Negotiate long-term agreements (3–5 year terms):
   - Pricing: Lock in for 3 years; annual escalation max +2–3%
   - Capacity: Supplier maintains 50% idle capacity for surge orders
   - Forecast sharing: You provide rolling 18-month demand forecast
   - Co-funding: You pay 50–70% of NRE for new product setup

**Phase 2c: Phased Volume Transfer (Months 10–36)**

**Year 1 (Months 12–24): Transfer 20% of volume to friend-shore**
- Execute first orders; monitor on-time delivery %, defect rate, cost adherence
- Support: Quality audits, process capability studies, monthly business reviews
- Adjust: If underperformance, reduce allocation; replace with alternative

**Year 2 (Months 24–36): Transfer 30% of volume to friend-shore**
- Increase if Year 1 performance meets targets
- Add additional suppliers if needed
- Negotiate volume discounts

**Year 3+: Stabilize at target (30–40% friend-shore)**
- Continuous improvement: Work on cost reduction (annual productivity targets: -2–3%)
- Quarterly performance reviews

### **2.3 Multi-Tier Visibility & Supply Chain Transparency (Resilience Pillar 3)**

#### **2.3.1 The Challenge**

Only 42% of companies have visibility beyond Tier 1. Compliance violations (forced labour, child labour, environmental externalities, conflict minerals) frequently embed 3+ levels deep. Example: Tier 1 assembler (Vietnam) → Tier 2 capacitor supplier (Taiwan) → Tier 3 rare-earth processor (China, potentially Xinjiang).

#### **2.3.2 Visibility Strategy**

**Goal:** 80%+ transparency to Tier 3 within 24 months; 100% for high-risk materials.

**Phase 3a: Tier 1 Disclosure (Months 1–3)**

**Activity: Mandatory Questionnaire**
- Sent to ALL Tier 1 suppliers; contract requirement
- **Questions:**
  - List all Tier 2 suppliers providing >5% BOM value for our materials
  - For each Tier 2: Location, products, certifications (ISO 9001, SA8000, RBA, Sedex, RMI-CMRT, FSC)
  - Single-source Tier 2 dependencies?
  - Tier 2 supplier changes in past 12 months?
  - Chain-of-custody documentation (invoices, bills of lading, mill certificates)
- **Enforcement:** 30-day response deadline; non-response triggers escalation (contract review, payment hold)
- **Owner:** Procurement + ESG/Compliance
- **Measurement:** Response rate ≥95%; data completeness ≥80%

**Phase 3b: Tier 2 Risk Segmentation (Months 3–6)**

**Activities:**
1. Classify all Tier 2 suppliers by risk:
   - **High-Risk Geography:** DRC, Myanmar, Pakistan, Bangladesh, India, Mexico, Guatemala
   - **Medium-Risk Geography:** Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia, Brazil, Turkey
   - **Low-Risk Geography:** Germany, US, Canada, EU, Japan, South Korea

2. Create Strategic Watch List:
   - Top 50–100 Tier 2 suppliers (70–80% of material volume)
   - For each: Gather certifications, prior audit findings, financial health, regulatory compliance
   - Cross-check against: OFAC/EU/UN sanctions, NGO reports, news alerts

3. **Owner:** Procurement + ESG/Compliance
4. **Measurement:** 100% of Tier 2 risk-rated; watch list complete; red-flag count documented

**Phase 3c: Tier 2/3 Mapping & Audits (Months 6–18)**

**For High-Risk Tier 2 Suppliers (Top 20–30):**

**Activity 1: In-Person Audits**
- 3–5 day on-site audit (labour, environment, health & safety, capacity, Tier 3 sourcing)
- Audit protocol: SA8000 or RBA-aligned (minimum)
- Interview procurement manager on Tier 3 supplier contracts, payment terms, capacity planning
- Review documentation: Tier 3 contracts, purchase orders, invoices, material certifications
- **Owner:** Third-party auditor + Internal oversight
- **Measurement:** High-risk Tier 2 suppliers audited (100% of top 20–30)

**Activity 2: Tier 3 Traceability by Material**

**Electronics (Semiconductors, Capacitors):**
- Trace ceramic/electrolytic material source
- Collect: CMRT responses (Conflict Minerals Reporting Template)
- Verify: No Xinjiang exposure (UFLPA compliance)
- Forensic testing: Laboratory analysis for origin markers (carbon isotope for polysilicon, DNA for cotton)

**Metals (Steel, Aluminium, Copper):**
- Trace: Mill location → Primary ore/scrap source → Country of origin
- Collect: Mill certificates (third-party assay), country of origin documentation
- Verify: No conflict minerals (ICMM certifications for artisanal mining regions)
- Assay testing: Random samples per supplier annually ($500–$1,500/sample)

**Chemicals (Solvents, Resins, Surfactants):**
- Trace: Feedstock source (oil refinery, processor) → Country of origin
- Collect: Safety Data Sheets (SDS), technical data sheets, origin attestation
- Verify: No banned substances (REACH compliance, banned phthalates, PCBs)
- Lab analysis: Third-party testing for purity, banned substances ($1–3K per batch)

**Textiles & Cotton:**
- Trace: Cotton gin → Farm → Region of origin
- Collect: Farm certifications (organic, fair trade, Rainforest Alliance)
- Verify: No Xinjiang exposure; pesticide compliance
- DNA testing: Confirm origin (non-Xinjiang); cost $500–$1K per batch

**Owner:** Procurement + Quality + ESG
**Measurement:** % of materials with mapped Tier 3 sourcing; % with origin certification; % with forensic verification

### **2.4 Inventory & Buffer Strategy (Resilience Pillar 4)**

#### **2.4.1 Three-Tier Inventory Architecture**

| **Inventory Type** | **Purpose** | **Duration** | **Decision Criteria** | **Target Level** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Safety Stock** | Buffer against normal demand variation | Weekly/monthly | Demand variability; lead-time | 2–4 weeks standard; 4–8 weeks high-variability |
| **Strategic Reserve** | Insurance for critical/single-source materials if supplier fails | Quarterly/annually | Lead-time, supplier risk, business impact | 4–12 weeks critical materials; 1–3 units for long-lead components |
| **Cycle Stock** | Normal operational inventory in supply chain | Varies | Min order quantity, batch size, transport | 1–2 weeks typical; adjust per lead-time |

#### **2.4.2 Implementation by Material Category**

**Category A (Critical, Long Lead-Time, Single/Dual Source):**
- **Examples:** Semiconductors (16+ weeks), specialized castings, rare materials
- **Safety stock:** 8–12 weeks
- **Strategic reserve:** 4–8 weeks additional
- **Reorder trigger:** When inventory reaches 12 weeks demand
- **Storage:** Supplier-managed inventory (consignment) to reduce carrying cost
- **Cost:** 2–5% supplier cost premium for consignment; justified by disruption avoidance

**Category B (Important, Medium Lead-Time, Dual Source):**
- **Examples:** Standard fasteners, connectors, semiconductors (4–8 weeks)
- **Safety stock:** 4–6 weeks
- **Strategic reserve:** None (dual source sufficient)
- **Reorder trigger:** 6-week lead-time signal
- **Storage:** Distributed between warehouse + suppliers

**Category C (Commodity, Short Lead-Time, Multi-Source):**
- **Examples:** Plastic pellets, sheet metal, commodity fasteners (<4 weeks)
- **Safety stock:** 2–3 weeks
- **Strategic reserve:** Minimal
- **Reorder trigger:** 3-week signal

#### **2.4.3 Strategic Reserve Funding & Storage Options**

**Option 1: Supplier-Managed Inventory (SMI) / Consignment**
- Supplier holds 4–8 weeks safety stock at their facility, on your account
- You pay only for consumed inventory; supplier bears holding cost
- Negotiate 2–5% cost premium to compensate supplier
- **Benefit:** Resilience without balance-sheet inventory; reduced your carrying cost
- **Risk:** If supplier fails, you lose the inventory
- **Owner:** Procurement
- **Cost:** $200K–$500K annually in supplier premium; $0 balance-sheet impact

**Option 2: Captive Buffer Warehouse (JIT Buffer Hub)**
- Rent small, shared warehouse near manufacturing plants (100–500 pallet positions)
- Store critical materials + safety stock for Category A/B items
- **Cost:** $3–8/pallet/month (~$500–$2,000/month for 500 pallets)
- **Benefit:** Short line-of-sight (days vs. weeks); can repack/stage quickly
- **Shared cost:** Partner with 3–4 nearby suppliers/customers to reduce your share
- **Owner:** Supply Chain + Logistics
- **Cost:** $6K–$24K annually

**Option 3: Supply Chain Resilience Reserve Fund**
- Establish annual budget (0.5–1% of COGS) for resilience investments
- Use reserve to: Pay SMI premiums, fund buffer warehouse, finance dual-sourcing NRE, support supplier capacity development
- **Example:** $500M COGS → $2.5–5M annual reserve
- **Owner:** Finance + Procurement
- **Cost:** 0.5–1% COGS; treats resilience as operational insurance

### **2.5 Demand-Supply Sensing & Early-Warning Systems (Resilience Pillar 5)**

#### **2.5.1 Real-Time Visibility Architecture**

**Integrated Data Flows:**

```
DATA INPUTS:
  ├─ Demand: POS data, customer orders, forecast updates, market intelligence
  ├─ Supply: Supplier delivery performance, quality metrics, financial health, logistics status
  ├─ Risk: Geopolitical news, sanctions, currency/commodity prices, climate/weather alerts
  └─ Operations: Production schedules, inventory levels, quality issues, safety incidents

↓

ANALYTICS ENGINE:
  ├─ Demand forecasting: AI-driven; incorporate POS variability, seasonality, promotional events
  ├─ Supplier KPI dashboards: Real-time on-time %, quality %, cost variance, payment health
  ├─ Supply chain simulation: "What if" scenarios (supplier disruption, port closure, demand spike)
  └─ Risk scoring: Automated alerts if supplier risk score drops >20 points, demand spikes >25%, logistics delays >5 days

↓

AUTOMATED ESCALATION:
  ├─ Critical alerts: Notify procurement manager; create exception task
  ├─ Decision support: Auto-suggest actions (increase safety stock, activate secondary, expedite freight)
  ├─ Stakeholder notifications: Update operations, finance with supply/demand status
  └─ Governance: Escalate material issues to Steering Committee for decision
```

#### **2.5.2 Key Metrics & Alert Rules**

**Demand-Side Early Warnings:**

| **Signal** | **Threshold** | **Action** |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast demand increase vs. prior month | >25% increase | Notify suppliers; request capacity confirmation; increase safety stock for critical materials |
| Demand volatility (coefficient of variation) | >30% | Increase safety stock to 8 weeks; negotiate flexible agreements with
