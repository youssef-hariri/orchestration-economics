# Subscription Transition Plan — Board-Ready Decision

## 1. Decision requested

Approve a **36-month, voluntary transition** from perpetual licenses to subscriptions, subject to these gates:

- Subscription becomes the default for all new customers immediately.
- Existing perpetual rights are honored and clearly documented.
- A controlled six-month pilot precedes broad installed-base migration.
- Migration incentives are time-limited, separately reported, and approved against customer-level economics.
- Sales compensation shifts toward collectible ARR, retention, expansion, collections, and implementation quality.
- Broad rollout occurs only after the pilot meets approved customer, financial, operational, and margin thresholds.
- Liquidity is secured against the downside case, including installed-base attrition approaching 20%.

The objective is **durable, collectible, profitable recurring revenue**, not contract volume.

---

## 2. Offer structure

### New customers

Subscription is the default offer, with three tiers:

**Core**

- Current functionality
- Support and maintenance
- Updates and upgrades
- Standard security and compliance
- Defined seat or usage allowance
- Standard onboarding

**Professional**

Adds advanced analytics, administration, integrations, support, and usage allowances.

**Enterprise**

Adds enterprise-wide or business-unit rights, premium service levels, advanced security, dedicated customer success, governance, and multi-product or volume commitments.

### Deployment

Offer vendor-hosted/SaaS and customer-hosted subscription options where commercially necessary. Hosted prices must recover infrastructure, security, support, and operating costs. Customer-hosted contracts must define entitlement, upgrade, support, and security responsibilities.

### Standard terms

- Annual commitment and prepayment: default.
- Quarterly payment: 3–5% premium.
- Monthly payment: higher effective price and payment protection.
- Three-year term: 5–10% effective term benefit.
- Renewal increases: generally capped at 3–5%, with explicit treatment of added scope, overages, deployment changes, inflation, and foreign exchange.
- Usage pricing: only with measurable usage and a committed minimum.

## 3. Existing-customer migration paths

| Path | Customer need | Structure | Intended outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perpetual continuation | Customer is not ready | Retain purchased licenses; defined support and upgrade policy | Preserve relationship |
| Full conversion | Customer values continuous innovation | Convert eligible estate with credit and price protection | Standard subscription |
| Hybrid conversion | Procurement or sunk-investment concern | Existing core remains perpetual; new scope becomes subscription | Managed transition |
| Ownership-credit conversion | Customer fears paying twice | Subscription plus expiring credit for eligible prior investment | Accelerate conversion |

### Customer rights charter

Before migration begins, publish a plain-language charter confirming:

- Continued use of purchased perpetual licenses and modules.
- Existing user, site, deployment, and usage entitlements.
- Contractual support and maintenance rights.
- Data ownership and reasonable export rights.
- Published security, compatibility, and lifecycle commitments.
- Supported versions and end-of-support dates.
- Treatment of recently purchased upgrades.

The company will not remove purchased functionality, deliberately break interoperability, withhold committed security updates, or use punitive support changes to force migration.

Customers with major upgrades purchased within 24 months receive graduated treatment:

- Highest credit within 12 months.
- Lower credit during months 13–24.
- Pro-rated unused-maintenance credit.
- Executive review for unusually large recent purchases.

Subscription contracts must define renewal notices, cancellation windows, data export, transition assistance, fees, read-only access where applicable, and data-deletion timing.

### Hybrid policy

Hybrid arrangements are a bridge, normally lasting **24–36 months**. Every agreement must specify:

- Perpetual and subscription entitlements.
- Support and upgrade obligations.
- Renewal mechanics.
- Migration milestones.
- Maximum duration.
- Target end state.
- Account profitability.

Extensions require executive approval.

## 4. Pricing and migration incentives

### Pricing method

Each account must pass three tests:

1. **Customer value:** productivity, security, compliance, innovation, or operating benefit.
2. **Historical economics:** normalized license, upgrade, maintenance, support, and hosting spend.
3. **Vendor economics:** contribution margin, implementation cost, support burden, retention probability, expansion potential, and commission.

Historical benchmarks of 70–90% of normalized annual spend or approximately 20–30% of relevant perpetual license value may inform pricing but do not determine it. Account-level contribution margin is the governing standard.

### Migration incentives

| Commitment | Standard benefit | Conditions |
|---|---:|---|
| One year | 5–10% migration credit | Annual prepayment preferred |
| Three years | 10–20% migration credit | Minimum scope and payment requirements |
| Strategic enterprise | Individually approved | Must satisfy margin, cash, and retention tests |

Additional benefits may include:

- Unused maintenance credit.
- Recent-upgrade credit.
- Standard migration services.
- First-year premium features.
- Expansion credit.
- Two-year price protection.
- Training and onboarding.

Credits must be:

- Time-limited.
- Shown separately from list price.
- Nonrecurring at renewal.
- Linked to minimum term, scope, payment, and implementation milestones.
- Reported by segment, product, seller, and partner.

### Economic approval rule

Finance must compare:

1. Continued perpetual relationship.
2. Subscription conversion without incentive.
3. Subscription conversion with incentive.

Approve an incentive only when:

\[
E[NPV_{\text{conversion with incentive}}]
>
E[NPV_{\text{perpetual continuation}}]
\]

and:

\[
E[NPV_{\text{conversion with incentive}}]
>
E[NPV_{\text{conversion without incentive}}]
\]

The model must include probability-weighted conversion, retention, expansion, contraction, collections, hosting, support, customer success, migration labor, commissions, working capital, financing cost, taxes where material, refunds, service credits, termination exposure, and terminal value. Churn must not be deducted twice.

Every exception requires CFO approval, positive customer contribution margin, an approved cash-payback period, and no material unpriced service obligation.

## 5. Revenue trough, scenarios, and liquidity

Finance will maintain a 36-month customer-level model reconciling:

- Counterfactual perpetual revenue.
- Subscription revenue recognition.
- ARR.
- Billings and collections.
- Deferred revenue.
- Gross margin.
- EBITDA.
- Free cash flow.
- Cash balance.
- Financing requirements.

The model must reflect actual upgrade cycles, maintenance renewals, conversion timing, churn, contraction, expansion, implementation costs, credits, and payment terms.

### Planning scenarios

These are internal planning cases, not public guidance until validated.

| Assumption | Base | Downside | Severe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 1 eligible-account conversion | 15% | 8% | 5% |
| Year 2 cumulative conversion | 35% | 20% | 12% |
| Year 3 cumulative conversion | 55% | 35% | 25% |
| Migrated gross retention | 93% | 82% | 75–80% |
| Net retention | 105% | 95% | 85–90% |
| Average migration credit | 12% | 18% | 22% |
| Year 1 revenue decline | 10–12% | 18–22% | 25%+ |
| Annual prepayment mix | 70%+ | 55% | 40% |

The severe case must include attrition approaching 20% of the installed base, correlated loss among major customers, lower prepayment, higher implementation costs, and weak expansion.

### Board thresholds

Before pilot launch, the board must approve company-specific dollar and percentage thresholds for:

- Maximum revenue decline.
- Maximum quarterly negative free cash flow.
- Minimum cash balance and runway.
- Maximum migration credit.
- Minimum fully loaded subscription margin.
- Minimum migrated-customer retention.
- Maximum booked-but-not-live ARR.
- Minimum annual-prepayment mix.
- Maximum implementation-cost variance.
- Maximum time to value.
- Financing trigger.

Initial management limits for calibration are:

- Base-case Year 1 revenue decline: no more than 12%.
- Downside decline: no more than 22% without cost or financing action.
- Cash: minimum 12 months of operating expense plus transition contingency.
- Average credit: no more than 15% without CFO approval; 20% only with executive approval.
- Booked-but-not-live ARR: no more than 10% of reported subscription ARR.
- Annual prepayment: at least 65% in the base case.
- Implementation cost: no more than 110% of approved budget.
- At least 90% of pilot customers live within contracted target time.

Margin floors must be defined consistently, including hosting, support, customer success, implementation, migration, security, and partner costs.

If the severe case cannot be funded without unacceptable dilution, debt, or solvency risk, migration must slow or additional capital must be secured before broad rollout.

## 6. Sales compensation

Compensation changes only after product and quote-to-cash readiness is demonstrated.

### New-logo sales

- Primary measure: first-year collectible ARR.
- Future-year accelerator: 10–25% only for economically committed, collectible years.
- Quality modifiers: collection and implementation.
- Price-floor penalty.
- Staged payout at execution, collection, and implementation.
- Clawback for early cancellation or material contraction.
- Reduced but nonzero commission on perpetual deals during transition.
- Higher credit for subscription conversion and qualifying multiyear commitments.

No full credit for unpaid, non-live, below-floor, broadly cancellable, uncommitted-usage, or heavily credit-funded contracts.

### Account management

Measure:

- Gross retention.
- Renewal price realization.
- Expansion ARR.

### Customer success

Measure:

- Adoption.
- Time to value.
- Renewal-risk coverage.
- Gross retention for managed accounts.

Each role should have no more than two or three material measures. Modifiers such as collection, implementation, and clawbacks should be formulaic, not separate discretionary objectives. Define exclusions and appeals for bankruptcy, M&A, regulatory prohibition, management-directed product discontinuation, reassignment, and other uncontrollable events.

### Transition protection

For 12–18 months:

- Quota relief for migration-heavy territories.
- Reasonable earnings protection where necessary.
- Shadow calculations for one quarter.
- Clear crediting for hybrid deals, credits, amendments, renewals, and expansions.
- Published sunset date for full perpetual commissions.

### Partners

Partners receive defined conversion, renewal, expansion, and migration-services economics, with account ownership, deal registration, credit splits, and dispute resolution documented before the pilot.

## 7. Implementation roadmap

### Phase 0 — Readiness, Months 0–3

Complete and approve:

- Customer-rights charter.
- Offer and pricing architecture.
- Counterfactual perpetual and subscription financial model.
- Transition budget and liquidity plan.
- Product and security readiness.
- Billing, entitlement, provisioning, and contract-management systems.
- Revenue-recognition, tax, regulatory, and legal review.
- Compensation and channel-crediting rules.
- Migration-capacity model.
- Pilot methodology and control cohorts.
- KPI definitions and disclosure controls.
- Rollback and containment procedures.
- Accountable executive RACI.

Mandatory operational capabilities include billing accuracy, amendments, upgrades, downgrades, cancellations, renewal notices, usage reporting, data export, security controls, customer administration, and finance-system feeds.

### Phase 1 — Controlled pilot, Months 4–9

Pilot:

- New customers.
- 5–10% of eligible existing accounts.
- Enterprise, mid-market, and channel segments.
- Full and hybrid conversion.
- One-year and three-year terms.
- Multiple credit levels and payment options.
- Hosted and customer-hosted deployment.
- Different onboarding packages.

Set minimum sample size, segment representation, observation period, confidence standard, outlier treatment, and defect thresholds before launch.

Measure conversion, retention, adoption, implementation cost, time to value, support burden, cash collection, gross margin, customer satisfaction, and ARR quality.

### Pilot gate for broad rollout

Require:

- Gross retention of at least 90%.
- Conversion within approved range.
- Average credit no more than 15%.
- Fully loaded margin above floor.
- At least 90% live within target.
- Booked-but-not-live ARR below 10%.
- Annual prepayment of at least 65%.
- Customer satisfaction not materially below the perpetual baseline.
- No material billing, entitlement, accounting, or security defects.
- First-year cash contribution within plan.
- Delivery demand within capacity.

If conversion is strong but retention, adoption, margin, cash, or customer satisfaction fails, do not scale.

### Phase 2 — Broad launch, Months 10–18

- Subscription default for new customers.
- Segment-based migration.
- Material shift in compensation toward ARR.
- Standard migration packages.
- Quarterly external reporting.
- Hybrid options remain available.

### Phase 3 — Scale and rationalize, Months 19–36

- Subscription becomes the primary model.
- Perpetual sales require approval.
- Credits decline and expire.
- Hybrid customers move toward documented end states.
- Customer-success coverage follows economics and risk.
- Cohort-based forecasting becomes standard.

### Phase 4 — Steady state, Month 36 onward

- Majority of new bookings are recurring.
- Strategic customers are migrated or on approved hybrid plans.
- Perpetual revenue is managed as a residual stream.
- ARR, retention, margin, and cash are integrated operating measures.

## 8. KPIs and governance

### Financial

- Revenue by perpetual, subscription, services, and other.
- ARR by new logo, conversion, expansion, and renewal.
- Billings, collections, and deferred revenue.
- Gross margin by deployment and cost definition.
- EBITDA and free cash flow.
- Cash balance and downside-case runway.
- Commission expense as a percentage of ARR.
- Financing requirement.

### Customer

- Logo retention.
- Gross revenue retention.
- Net revenue retention.
- Migrated-customer retention.
- Churn, downgrade, and contraction.
- Conversion by segment.
- Adoption and time to value.
- Support burden.
- Customer satisfaction.
- Renewal-risk coverage.

### Operations and sales

- First-year collectible ARR per seller.
- Quota attainment.
- Average price and migration credit.
- Early cancellation.
- Collection and implementation completion.
- Quote-to-cash cycle.
- Billing-error rate.
- Provisioning time.
- Implementation duration.
- Services hours per migration.
- Contract exceptions.
- Revenue-recognition exceptions.
- Capacity utilization.

### ARR governance

Report separately:

- Contracted ARR.
- Provisioned ARR.
- Live ARR.
- Collectible ARR.
- Booked-but-not-live ARR.
- Renewal-eligible ARR.
- Management-forecast renewal ARR.

ARR excludes one-time services, credits, taxes, pass-through costs, and uncommitted usage. Finance owns definitions, reconciles contract systems to billing and the general ledger, certifies quarterly results, and obtains Legal and Audit Committee review for material changes.

Quarterly bridges must show:

\[
\text{Opening ARR}
+ \text{New-logo ARR}
+ \text{Conversion ARR}
+ \text{Expansion ARR}
- \text{Contraction ARR}
- \text{Churned ARR}
=
\text{Ending ARR}
\]

Also provide revenue, billings, cash, customer-count, and gross-margin bridges.

## 9. Principal risks and responses

| Risk | Trigger | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Installed-base churn | Migrated retention below threshold for two quarters | Pause price increases; expand hybrid options; increase executive coverage |
| Severe attrition | Loss approaches 20% of installed base | Slow migration; preserve legacy options; launch recovery program |
| Revenue trough | Results worse than downside case for two quarters | Reduce discretionary spend; slow migration; increase collections; activate financing plan |
| Liquidity shortfall | Cash below approved minimum or runway | Tighten payment terms; reduce costs; secure financing; slow migration |
| Discount escalation | Average credit exceeds plan by five points | Enforce price floors; shorten window; require approval |
| Margin compression | Fully loaded margin below floor | Reprice; alter deployment economics; reduce service scope |
| Product or billing failure | Operational defect thresholds exceeded | Pause affected segment; remediate; execute containment plan |
| Capacity failure | Demand exceeds implementation or support capacity | Cap migrations; add resources; prioritize high-probability accounts |
| ARR deterioration | Booked-but-not-live ARR exceeds threshold | Tighten ARR and commission eligibility |
| Hybrid complexity | Support cost or renewal confusion rises | Standardize terms; enforce end dates |
| Investor concern | Questions about metric credibility or liquidity increase | Expand reconciliations and scenario disclosure; preserve definitions |

## 10. Investor and board communications

### Initial communication

Explain:

- Rationale for the transition.
- Customer rights and migration choices.
- 36-month timetable.
- Revenue, margin, cash, and financing implications.
- Base, downside, and severe cases.
- Migration-credit policy.
- Product and adoption investments.
- Board gates and corrective actions.
- Definitions of ARR, retention, billings, and credits.

Clearly distinguish historical results, internal planning cases, public guidance, and long-term objectives.

### Quarterly board reporting

Include:

- Actual versus base, downside, and severe cases.
- Revenue, ARR, billings, cash, margin, and customer bridges.
- Cohort retention and adoption.
- Credit and price realization.
- Implementation performance.
- Capacity utilization.
- Compensation effects.
- Liquidity and financing status.
- Trigger status and decisions required.

### Quarterly investor reporting

Report subscription, perpetual, services, revenue, ARR, billings, cash, retention, gross margin, credits, and cohort performance together. ARR must never substitute for revenue, earnings, or cash flow.

Finance certifies recurring metrics; Legal reviews disclosures; the Audit Committee oversees material changes. Scenario revisions must identify the changed assumptions and their financial effect.

### Stakeholder messages

**Customers:**  
“You retain the perpetual rights you purchased. Subscription is an option providing continuous innovation, security, support, and predictable access. We will provide a structured migration path and will not require you to pay twice for existing investments.”

**Employees:**  
“Compensation will reward collectible ARR, retention, expansion, collections, implementation, and customer outcomes, with clear crediting rules and transition protection.”

**Partners:**  
“Partners will be compensated for conversion, renewals, expansion, and migration services, with defined account protection and crediting.”

**Investors:**  
“The transition includes a planned and quantified trough. Management is prioritizing retention, ARR quality, margin, and cash—not uneconomic bookings. The board has approved explicit gates and downside actions.”

## 11. Required approvals before pilot launch

Approve:

1. Customer-rights charter and lifecycle matrix.
2. Four migration paths and hybrid end-state policy.
3. Standard pricing, credit, and renewal rules.
4. Probability-weighted NPV and contribution-margin framework.
5. Counterfactual perpetual and 36-month subscription model.
6. Quantified transition budget, capacity plan, and liquidity reserve.
7. Numeric rollout, pause, and financing thresholds.
8. Product, billing, entitlement, accounting, tax, and reporting readiness.
9. Role-based sales and partner compensation rules.
10. Pilot sample, test cells, controls, observation period, and pass/fail criteria.
11. ARR definitions, data lineage, certification, and disclosure controls.
12. Rollback and containment procedures.
13. Named executive accountability for each workstream.

## Final recommendation

Approve readiness work and the controlled pilot. Do not authorize broad migration until actual evidence demonstrates acceptable retention, adoption, implementation quality, fully loaded margin, cash collection, discount discipline, ARR quality, and delivery capacity.

Proceed with a transition that is **voluntary, phased, customer-fair, economically tested, liquidity-funded, and governed by hard gates**.
