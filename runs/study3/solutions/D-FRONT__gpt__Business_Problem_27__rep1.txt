# Board Memo: Staged Growth-Financing Strategy

**To:** Board of Directors  
**Subject:** Funding capacity expansion while preserving control and strategic flexibility  
**Recommendation:** Approve the financing framework and negotiation mandate below. Do not approve a specific facility, valuation, draw, or hybrid instrument until financial, legal, tax, accounting, and commercial diligence is complete.

## 1. Recommendation

The company should **move now, but stage risk**:

1. Use operating cash for rapid-payback capacity bottlenecks, validated working capital, and hiring tied to visible demand.
2. Seek a **committed senior facility** comprising:
   - a working-capital revolving line; and
   - equipment or term financing for identifiable capacity investments.
3. Seek a second tranche concurrently, but distinguish between:
   - a legally committed delayed-draw facility available on objective conditions; and
   - a non-binding financing option, which must be excluded from the base-case liquidity plan.
4. Prefer subordinated or growth debt for the second tranche only if repayment is supportable in the downside case and senior-lender terms are acceptable.
5. Use preferred equity where expansion is long-payback or cash conversion is too uncertain for additional debt.
6. Reserve a capped convertible for a small, time-sensitive bridge where valuation remains disputed.
7. Defer a large priced minority-equity round unless debt capacity is insufficient, competitive urgency requires risk-bearing capital, or the investor provides measurable strategic value.
8. Retain disciplined organic growth as the fallback if acceptable financing is unavailable or the investment case deteriorates.

This approach is faster than relying solely on retained earnings, preserves substantially more founder ownership than an immediate equity raise, and limits fixed repayment obligations until the expansion is proven.

---

## 2. Strategic comparison

| Path | Benefits | Principal risks | Best use | Board view |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Organic growth** | No dilution, debt, or lender restrictions | Slower scale; risk of losing customers, talent, channels, or suppliers | Rapid-payback and reversible investments | Use for first dollars and fallback, but not as the sole plan if delay is strategically costly |
| **Senior debt** | No ownership dilution; generally lowest external cash cost | Fixed payments, covenants, security, and refinancing risk | Equipment, capacity assets, and predictable working capital | Preferred first external source, sized to downside cash capacity |
| **Minority common equity** | No mandatory repayment; strong downside resilience | Permanent dilution, governance rights, and low-valuation risk | Long-payback or uncertain expansion; strategic acceleration | Contingency or strategic source, not the base case |
| **Hybrid capital** | Can bridge debt capacity and equity dilution | Complexity, maturity cliffs, preferences, warrants, and intercreditor risk | Second-stage or urgent risk-bearing investment | Use selectively and only after full economic modeling |

### Hybrid hierarchy

1. **Subordinated/growth debt** if post-expansion cash flow is predictable and total leverage remains prudent.
2. **Preferred equity** if cash conversion is uncertain or the investment is long-payback.
3. **Venture debt** only if provider eligibility, revenue quality, and repayment visibility are confirmed.
4. **Capped convertible** only as a tightly limited bridge.
5. **Revenue-based financing** only for a separately modeled, short-payback sales program—not for broad capacity investment.

## 3. Funding structure and use of proceeds

### Stage 0: Operating cash

Fund:

- rapid-payback capacity debottlenecks;
- working capital for contracted or highly reliable demand;
- hires tied to signed demand or measurable utilization needs;
- sales and channel programs with demonstrated contribution economics.

Defer or gate:

- speculative geographic expansion;
- fixed overhead not tied to capacity or revenue conversion;
- capacity committed solely against unconverted pipeline;
- marketing without measurable payback.

The board should approve a ranked plan separating essential capacity, revenue-conversion investment, and discretionary expansion.

### Stage 1: Senior facility

Seek:

- a revolving facility for receivables and working capital;
- equipment finance or term debt for identifiable capacity assets; and
- a legally committed delayed-draw tranche, if available on objective conditions.

Equipment and working-capital facilities should be separated where practical because their collateral and repayment mechanics differ.

Senior debt should be sized to the lower of:

- sustainable cash available for debt service;
- downside-case debt-service capacity;
- collateral or borrowing-base availability;
- the amount preserving the approved liquidity reserve;
- the amount consistent with covenant headroom; and
- the amount repayable or refinancable without a distressed transaction.

Debt capacity must be assessed after taxes, maintenance capital expenditure, working-capital investment, and the approved growth program—not on operating profit alone.

### Stage 2: Second tranche

The company should seek a second tranche alongside the senior facility, but treat it as available only if it is legally committed and subject to objective draw conditions.

If it is merely an option or subject to broad lender discretion, it must not be included in the base-case funding or liquidity plan.

### Stage 3: Minority equity, if required

Use priced minority equity only where:

- additional debt would create covenant or refinancing fragility;
- spending precedes revenue and cash generation by a material period;
- the competitive opportunity justifies dilution; or
- the investor provides measurable distribution, customer, recruiting, regulatory, partnership, or other strategic value.

The round should be no larger than required to reach the next value-creating milestone.

## 4. Milestone schedule and draw conditions

Each gate should specify the forecast baseline, measurement period, tolerance, data source, responsible owner, approval authority, and consequence of failure. Thresholds must be based on company data and documented before signing.

### Gate 1: Initial senior draw

Release the first tranche only after:

- demand is supported by signed contracts, backlog, purchase orders, or reliable repeat demand;
- demand analysis distinguishes contractual demand, repeat demand, qualified pipeline, and speculative pipeline;
- lost-demand analysis confirms that capacity—not weak demand or pricing—is the binding constraint;
- pricing and incremental contribution margins are validated;
- equipment, suppliers, facilities, permits, and critical hires are sufficiently secured;
- the commissioning budget and schedule are approved; and
- the downside case preserves the approved liquidity reserve.

Qualified pipeline should receive limited credit unless historical conversion rates support it.

### Gate 2: Additional capacity and hiring

Release further senior debt when:

- commissioning remains within approved cost and schedule tolerances;
- utilization reaches the threshold specified in the investment case, measured against practical capacity;
- actual deliveries demonstrate expected contribution margins;
- critical hiring is substantially complete;
- receivables, inventory, and cash conversion remain within approved tolerances; and
- no covenant breach is probable over the next testing periods.

### Gate 3: Hybrid or junior-capital draw

Draw the second tranche only when:

- utilization is tracking toward the approved target;
- retention, pricing, and customer-acquisition payback remain acceptable;
- cash conversion is within the approved forecast tolerance;
- the downside case supports repayment, refinancing, or conversion;
- at least two specific repayment or refinancing paths have been identified; and
- management has sufficient capacity to execute the next phase.

### Competitive acceleration

A rival’s fundraising alone is not a draw trigger. Acceleration requires evidence of deployment, such as targeted customer wins, channel commitments, talent hiring, supplier reservations, or pricing action.

Management should classify the threat as:

- **Watch:** funding is an information signal only;
- **Prepare:** targeting evidence exists; financing should be ready;
- **Accelerate:** permanent loss is reasonably likely without prompt action.

For an acceleration recommendation, management must document:

1. the opportunity at risk;
2. evidence of rival deployment;
3. probability and timing of permanent loss;
4. incremental value from acting;
5. financing cost, dilution, and control impact; and
6. downside survivability if the opportunity does not convert.

Any acceleration beyond approved leverage, dilution, security, maturity, or liquidity limits requires board approval.

### Stop conditions

Suspend further draws if:

- liquidity falls below the approved reserve;
- a covenant breach is probable without an optimistic recovery;
- utilization, contribution margin, or cash conversion materially misses plan;
- demand is principally uncommitted pipeline;
- critical execution or supply constraints remain unresolved; or
- repayment would depend on a distressed equity raise or refinancing.

## 5. Founder-control and dilution mandate

Before soliciting final equity or hybrid proposals, the founders and board should approve:

- minimum founder voting ownership;
- maximum fully diluted dilution, including warrants and conversion;
- acceptable board composition;
- prohibited operational vetoes;
- acceptable reserved matters;
- maximum preference burden;
- acceptable collateral;
- maximum maturity and refinancing exposure;
- minimum liquidity and covenant-headroom requirements.

Every proposal must include:

- a fully diluted capitalization table;
- ownership after conversion of all notes, warrants, and options;
- future option-pool effects;
- board and veto rights;
- founder proceeds under low, base, and high exit values;
- liquidation preferences and accrued returns; and
- down-round and anti-dilution outcomes.

Equipment-specific security may be acceptable. A blanket lien over core operating assets requires separate board approval. Security over founder assets is prohibited under the ordinary financing mandate and would require separate founder and board approval after independent legal advice.

## 6. Negotiation red lines

### Senior bank debt

Walk away if:

- downside-case serviceability is not demonstrated;
- amortization begins before the ramp can reasonably generate cash;
- founder assets or essential operating assets require unacceptable security;
- the lender can block ordinary hiring, pricing, capex, or approved fundraising;
- the facility prevents the planned second tranche;
- broad subjective-default or material-adverse-change provisions create acceleration risk;
- cross-defaults capture immaterial obligations;
- borrowing-base reductions or draw-stop rights can withdraw essential liquidity without objective triggers; or
- the facility creates a refinancing cliff.

Customary restrictions on additional senior debt, liens, distributions, and materially adverse transactions may be acceptable. Lenders should not control ordinary operations or block a solvency-preserving equity raise.

### Venture debt

Walk away if:

- repayment depends realistically on a future equity raise;
- maturity precedes expected cash generation;
- warrants, fees, and interest create excessive effective dilution;
- missed operating milestones can trigger acceleration despite payment compliance;
- minimum-cash requirements threaten operations; or
- the company does not satisfy the provider’s underwriting requirements.

### Subordinated or growth debt

Walk away if:

- total leverage is unsafe in the downside case;
- payment-in-kind interest creates an unmanageable maturity balance;
- intercreditor terms are operationally restrictive;
- junior enforcement could disrupt a solvent recovery;
- rescue financing or equity issuance is restricted; or
- no specific repayment, refinancing, or conversion path exists.

### Minority equity

Walk away if:

- valuation materially undervalues the company without strategic compensation;
- the investor seeks control-like rights;
- preferences are participating, uncapped, or multiple;
- full-ratchet anti-dilution is required;
- redemption or forced-sale rights create control pressure;
- the round is larger than required; or
- the investor provides no meaningful value beyond capital.

### Preferred equity

Walk away if:

- the exit waterfall is unattractive in plausible scenarios;
- redemption could force a sale or refinancing;
- dividends make the instrument debt-like;
- investor protections control ordinary operations;
- preferences stack excessively; or
- conversion and anti-dilution mechanics cannot be modeled clearly.

### Capped convertible

Walk away if:

- the cap and discount imply unacceptable dilution;
- maturity creates a repayment crisis;
- multiple instruments make the cap table unpredictable;
- investors receive broad pre-conversion consent rights; or
- the instrument merely postpones a structural funding gap.

### Revenue-based financing

Use only for a separately modeled, short-payback sales or customer-acquisition program.

Walk away if:

- payments begin before the funded program generates revenue;
- the revenue share or minimum payment reduces liquidity below the approved reserve;
- the effective repayment multiple is excessive;
- revenue definitions are ambiguous; or
- the instrument is being used for long-payback capacity investment.

## 7. Fallback strategy

If acceptable senior or hybrid terms cannot be obtained:

1. fund only the highest-return capacity debottlenecks from operating cash;
2. hire against signed demand or measurable utilization needs;
3. finance working capital only for contracted or highly reliable orders;
4. defer speculative geography, overhead, and capacity commitments;
5. seek strategic minority or preferred equity, raising only enough to reach the next value-creating milestone;
6. consider revenue-based financing only for a narrow, short-payback sales program; and
7. reassess every 60 days against lost sales, customer migration, talent availability, supplier capacity, and rival deployment.

The company should not accept financing that creates a probable covenant breach, forced sale, distressed refinancing, excessive preference burden, or material loss of practical independence.

If operating performance deteriorates, the action ladder is:

1. stop discretionary draws;
2. freeze speculative hiring, capex, and expansion;
3. preserve critical payroll and customer delivery;
4. identify cancellable, deferrable, and non-cancellable commitments;
5. increase reporting from monthly to weekly if liquidity enters the warning zone;
6. engage lenders before a covenant breach;
7. raise equity only if the revised business case remains attractive; and
8. consider strategic alternatives if solvency is threatened.

The CFO should have authority to suspend discretionary draws immediately, with prompt notice to the CEO and board chair.

---

## 8. Immediate next actions

### Within 30 days

Management should deliver:

- a 24–36-month monthly integrated forecast;
- base, downside, and severe-downside cases;
- a ranked investment and capacity plan;
- quantified lost-demand analysis;
- cash-available-for-debt-service analysis;
- a competitive-window assessment;
- a fully diluted capitalization table; and
- proposed liquidity, leverage, covenant, dilution, security, and maturity limits.

### Within 60 days

Management should obtain market terms for:

- senior debt;
- subordinated or growth debt;
- venture debt, subject to eligibility;
- preferred equity; and
- a capped convertible or strategic minority equity as contingency.

### Within 90 days

The board should decide whether the proposed senior facility satisfies the approved limits. If not, management should activate the organic-growth fallback and pursue targeted strategic or preferred equity rather than accept poor debt terms under pressure.

### Ongoing

During deployment, report monthly on:

- bookings, backlog, and pipeline conversion;
- capacity utilization;
- margins;
- hiring and commissioning;
- receivables, inventory, and cash conversion;
- liquidity and covenant headroom; and
- rival deployment and market-share indicators.

The board may pre-authorize draws that satisfy documented objective gates and remain within approved limits. Draws outside those gates require specific board approval.

## Conclusion

Approve the framework and negotiation mandate—not a specific financing transaction.

The company should use operating cash and moderate senior debt for predictable, asset-linked growth; retain additional risk capital as a carefully defined option; use subordinated debt only where repayment is credible; use preferred equity where genuine risk-bearing capital is required; and reserve a capped convertible for a limited bridge.

Final terms require **financial, commercial, legal, tax, accounting, regulatory, and enforceability diligence**. No financing should be executed or treated as available until the board has reviewed the integrated model, definitive terms, control and dilution analysis, security package, covenant schedule, and downside plan.
