# Subscription Transition Plan

## 1. Strategic objectives and decision

Adopt a **three-year managed transition** from perpetual licenses and paid upgrades to subscription, while preserving contractual customer rights and funding the temporary revenue trough.

The recommended strategy is:

1. **Honor existing perpetual-license rights** under each customer’s contract.
2. Make subscription the default for new customers and new capabilities.
3. Give existing customers three controlled choices: perpetual plus maintenance, hybrid bridge, or full subscription.
4. Use a single, declining migration-credit formula.
5. Redesign sales compensation around qualified recurring value, collections, activation, retention, and expansion.
6. Scale migration only when retention, implementation quality, margin, and liquidity meet board-approved thresholds.
7. Report recognized revenue, recurring revenue, bookings, billings, cash flow, and contract obligations separately.

An abrupt transition should be rejected. It could repeat the competitor’s approximately 20% installed-base loss and create a deeper commercial, financial, and reputational shock.

### Illustrative planning assumptions

These are planning ranges, not forecasts.

| Item | Illustrative assumption |
|---|---:|
| Current annual revenue | $100 million |
| Perpetual licenses and upgrades | $60 million |
| Maintenance/support | $30 million |
| Services and other | $10 million |
| Comparable perpetual license | $500,000 |
| Comparable annual maintenance | $100,000 |
| Comparable subscription | $175,000–$210,000 ARR |
| Installed-base conversion by Year 3 | 35%–45% |
| Base-case revenue trough | 8%–12% below no-transition plan |
| Combined downside trough | 15%–20% below no-transition plan |
| Mature subscription gross margin | 78%–85% |

These assumptions must be replaced with customer-level data before formal guidance or full-scale rollout.

## 2. Define the transition endpoints

“Three-year transition” should refer to commercial migration, not expiry of customer ownership rights.

| Dimension | Default policy | Exception or qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Perpetual ownership | Continues under the original contract | Existing termination provisions apply |
| New business | Subscription-first immediately | Perpetual exceptions for regulatory, procurement, deployment, or strategic reasons |
| Perpetual upgrades | Available through a published date or product lifecycle | Existing contractual rights remain enforceable |
| New modules | Subscription-first or subscription-only by product matrix | Must be genuinely new and legally reviewed |
| Hybrid contracts | Available during a defined transition period | Strategic exceptions require approval |
| Legacy support | Release-specific lifecycle | Paid extended support may be offered |
| Revenue objective | Majority recurring economics over time | Measured separately from ownership and support |

The board should approve specific dates for:

- Ending new perpetual sales by product or segment.
- Ending selected perpetual upgrades where contracts permit.
- Ending standard hybrid offers.
- Reviewing whether legacy support remains economically justified.

# 3. Customer offer and migration paths

## Path A: Perpetual license plus maintenance

Existing customers retain the rights granted under their applicable agreements, including, where applicable:

- Use of purchased perpetual software.
- Contracted license quantities and environments.
- Maintenance and support while eligible.
- Contractual upgrade rights.
- Continued use of the perpetual version after support ends, subject to technical and contractual limitations.

The company should not promise indefinite support for every version. It should promise continued ownership of the perpetual license and a clear support lifecycle.

### Required customer-rights package

For material customers, provide:

1. A general rights statement.
2. An account-specific entitlement schedule.
3. Product/version support dates.
4. Security and critical-defect policy.
5. Treatment of lapsed maintenance.
6. Definition of subscription-only functionality.
7. Data-export and interoperability terms.
8. License-server and activation continuity policy.

Illustrative commitments, subject to cost and legal validation:

- At least five years of support for currently supported releases.
- At least 24 months’ notice before ending support for a release.
- Maintenance increases capped or pre-announced at approximately CPI plus 2%–3%, where contractually permissible.

Legal, product, engineering, and finance must confirm that these commitments are affordable and enforceable.

## Path B: Standard subscription

The subscription should include:

- Current software and standard upgrades.
- Support and security fixes.
- Hosting or approved deployment rights.
- Standard integrations.
- Defined service levels.
- Onboarding and customer-success services.

### Illustrative packages

| Package | Indicative ARR | Target customer |
|---|---:|---|
| Standard | $50,000–$100,000 | Small or departmental deployment |
| Professional | $125,000–$250,000 | Multi-team or integrated deployment |
| Enterprise | $300,000+ | Mission-critical, global, or regulated deployment |

Use a platform fee plus one clearly defined usage or capacity metric where appropriate:

\[
ARR = \text{platform fee} + \text{usage/capacity} + \text{modules} + \text{premium support}
\]

Avoid excessive combinations of seats, usage, modules, and service tiers. Launch with two or three packages and a limited number of approved variations.

### Standard terms

- Three-year commitment for enterprise and migration customers.
- Annual billing as the default.
- Optional annual prepayment discount of approximately 5%–8%.
- Annual escalation of approximately 3%–5% after the initial term.
- One-year contracts only for small or low-complexity customers, generally at a premium.
- Base-price locks apply only to the agreed scope; usage, capacity, and material scope expansion can be repriced.

## Path C: Hybrid bridge

For customers not ready for full conversion:

- The perpetual license remains in place.
- Subscription access is added for specified cloud services, modules, or continuous innovation.
- A defined credit is applied.
- The bridge lasts two or three years.
- A conversion or renewal decision is set at contract inception.

Hybrid contracts must include:

- Standard scope.
- A fixed end date.
- No repeated migration credits.
- A predetermined price step-up.
- A full-conversion price.
- A contribution-margin floor.
- Separate reporting of hybrid ARR, margin, churn, and conversion.

Set a maximum hybrid share of the installed base or ARR. Otherwise, hybrid may become a permanent, uneconomic refuge.

# 4. Migration incentives and pricing

## Eligibility

Standard migration economics should apply when the customer:

- Holds a valid perpetual license.
- Is current on maintenance or has paid eligible maintenance within the prior 12 months.
- Is not in material payment default.
- Uses a supported version or accepts an approved migration plan.
- Falls within implementation capacity.
- Signs a minimum three-year subscription.
- Converts substantially comparable product scope.

Strategic, regulated, lapsed-maintenance, or complex accounts may receive exceptions through a central deal desk.

## Single migration-credit formula

Use one formula to avoid conflicting promises:

\[
\text{Migration credit}
=
\min
\left(
50\% \times \text{eligible historical maintenance},
20\% \times \text{first-year ARR},
\text{approved dollar cap}
\right)
\]

This is an illustrative starting point requiring validation.

Additional rules:

- Calculate historical maintenance using a fixed 12–24-month reference period.
- Freeze the reference amount when the migration opportunity is registered.
- Exclude unusual discounts, refunded payments, unrelated products, and post-registration transactions.
- Permit an upgrade credit of up to 25% of eligible recent upgrade spend, subject to a separate cap.
- Require the credited upgrade and maintenance scope to correspond to the subscription scope.

### Total-concession ceiling

\[
\text{Total concession}
=
\text{cash credits}
+\text{upgrade credits}
+\text{waived fees}
+\text{fair value of free modules}
+\text{recurring discount}
+\text{expected service credits}
\]

Illustrative policy:

- Standard total first-year concession: no more than 25% of first-year ARR.
- Recurring-price discount: normally no more than 5%–15%.
- Exceptions require CFO and CRO approval; material exceptions require CEO visibility.
- Every deal must pass both the concession ceiling and a contribution-margin floor.

Every proposal should show:

1. List price.
2. Contracted recurring price.
3. Effective first-year price after temporary credits.
4. Renewal price and escalation mechanics.

## Incentive schedule

| Period | Offer |
|---|---|
| Months 0–18 | Standard credit formula, migration-fee relief within cap, three-year base-price protection, one first-year premium benefit |
| Months 19–36 | Lower maintenance-credit multiplier, standard implementation support, fewer free benefits, standard pricing |
| After Month 36 | Standard subscription pricing; exceptions only for approved strategic accounts |

The incentive should reward early migration without establishing a permanent lower price anchor.

## Price validation

Test at least three customer cases:

1. Customers that buy every upgrade.
2. Customers that buy upgrades occasionally.
3. Customers that remain mainly on maintenance.

For each, calculate:

- Five-year customer billings and present value.
- Customer implementation and internal costs.
- Avoided infrastructure and upgrade costs.
- Customer ROI and payback.
- Vendor recognized revenue.
- Vendor contribution margin.
- Retention and expansion assumptions.
- Break-even year.

A $190,000 subscription with 95% retention may be more valuable than a $220,000 subscription with 80% retention.

# 5. Revenue trough and adoption scenarios

The transition should be modeled monthly or quarterly by customer cohort, not through a single average conversion rate.

The model must include:

- Historical license and upgrade timing.
- Maintenance renewals.
- Conversion dates.
- Subscription start dates.
- Annual versus prepaid billing.
- Revenue-recognition patterns.
- Credits and discounts.
- Churn and contraction.
- Price realization.
- Sales productivity.
- Implementation capacity.
- Hosting, support, and customer-success costs.
- Commission expense.
- Collections and deferred revenue.
- The defined no-transition baseline.

### Illustrative scenarios

| Scenario | Year 1 revenue versus no-transition plan | Year 3 conversion | Management posture |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Abrupt | -20% to -30% | 40%–50% | Reject |
| Conservative | -5% to -8% | 20%–30% | Lower risk, slower improvement |
| Recommended base | -8% to -12% | 35%–45% | Approve |
| Accelerated managed | -12% to -18% | 50%–60% | Only after gates |
| Combined downside | -15% to -20% | Delayed 12 months | Funding case |

### Trough-mitigation levers

Use several measures together:

1. Three-year commitments with annual billing.
2. Selective prepayment discounts.
3. Hybrid bridges.
4. Selected perpetual upgrades during the transition.
5. Migration, implementation, training, and premium-service revenue.
6. Staged infrastructure and customer-success investment.
7. Hiring and discretionary-spend controls.
8. Committed financing or liquidity reserves.

Services revenue should be separately reported and subject to delivery capacity and margin requirements. It should not be used to obscure weak recurring economics.

---

# 6. Distinguish the financial metrics

The company must not treat these measures as interchangeable.

| Metric | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bookings | Contracted sales signed during the period |
| Billings | Amount invoiced under contracts |
| Cash collections | Cash actually received |
| ARR | Annualized recurring contractual value |
| Recognized revenue | Revenue recorded under applicable accounting rules |
| Deferred revenue | Billings collected or invoiced before revenue recognition |
| Remaining performance obligations | Contracted obligations not yet recognized |
| Cash flow | Cash generated or consumed after operating and investment activity |

A three-year, $570,000 subscription contract may produce approximately $190,000 of annual recurring revenue and ratable recognized revenue, while billings and cash depend on payment terms.

The board should receive separate bridges for:

\[
\text{Opening ARR}+\text{new ARR}+\text{expansion}-\text{contraction}-\text{churn}=\text{ending ARR}
\]

and:

\[
\text{Opening revenue}+\text{new business}+\text{renewals}+\text{expansion}-\text{churn}-\text{legacy decline}=\text{recognized revenue}
\]

The revenue bridge should separately identify pure recognition timing, lost upgrades, credits, churn, sales productivity, and cost effects.

---

# 7. Sales compensation redesign

Compensation must change before Phase 1 launch.

## Primary measure

Use **qualified new ARR** as the primary quota measure. Define it precisely, including:

- Minimum committed value.
- Treatment of variable usage.
- Discounts and credits.
- Implementation fees.
- Hybrid ARR.
- Prepayments.
- Cancellation rights.
- Activation requirements.
- Nonstandard service levels.

Do not use ARR and ACV interchangeably or grant full credit for total three-year contract value.

## Illustrative compensation weighting

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Qualified new ARR | 60%–70% |
| Contract term and payment quality | 10%–15% |
| Collection and activation | 10%–15% |
| Price realization, margin, and approved strategic products | 10% |

### Commission timing

- 60%–70% at signed contract and validated booking.
- 30%–40% after collection, activation, or implementation milestones.
- Lower immediate payout for highly customized, low-margin, or high-risk deals.
- Clawbacks only for clearly defined nonpayment, cancellation, misrepresentation, or seller-caused failure.

## Transition protection

For the first 12–18 months:

- Guarantee 80%–100% of prior target incentive opportunity only for sellers meeting training, pipeline, activity, and migration objectives.
- Credit incremental migration ARR, not existing maintenance.
- Adjust quotas for longer sales cycles and lower first-year revenue recognition.
- Sunset the guarantee automatically.

## Account management and customer success

Compensation should include:

- Gross retention.
- Net revenue retention.
- Expansion ARR.
- On-time implementation.
- Product adoption and time to value.
- Renewal forecast accuracy.

Publish rules for new-logo ownership, migration ownership, renewal credit, expansion credit, partner credit, and customer-success influence.

# 8. Customer adoption and retention

Prioritize cohorts in this order:

1. Healthy mid-market customers with clear subscription value.
2. Customers approaching a major upgrade.
3. Strategic accounts with executive sponsorship and manageable migration complexity.
4. Complex or regulated customers through hybrid bridges.
5. Low-engagement or at-risk customers only after remediation.

For each account, track:

- License entitlement.
- Maintenance status.
- Upgrade history.
- Usage and adoption.
- Renewal date.
- Support issues.
- Migration complexity.
- Competitive risk.
- Expected subscription economics.
- Dollar exposure.

### Retention safeguards

- No forced migration during the first 24 months.
- Customer-specific value case before conversion.
- Executive sponsor for strategic accounts.
- Renewal-risk review at least 180 days before renewal.
- Defined onboarding and adoption plan.
- Data portability commitment.
- Customer advisory council.
- Escalation process for entitlement disputes.

### Retention metrics

Define and report separately:

- Logo retention.
- Revenue gross retention.
- Net revenue retention.
- Converted enterprise retention.
- Converted mid-market retention.
- New-logo subscription retention.
- Legacy maintenance retention.
- Hybrid retention.
- Top-account dollar loss.

Illustrative targets:

- GRR: at least 92% during transition and 95% at maturity.
- NRR: above 105% by Year 3.
- No more than an internally approved incremental churn budget above the historical baseline.

The competitor’s 20% loss should be treated as a risk signal, not as a forecast. Set the company’s own transition-churn budget based on its customer concentration, contract rights, product readiness, and historical retention.

---

# 9. Implementation assurance and operating readiness

Every migration contract should specify:

- Vendor and customer responsibilities.
- Milestones and acceptance criteria.
- Remediation period.
- Service-credit or term-extension remedy.
- Data-export format.
- Rollback procedure and timing.
- Treatment of prepaid amounts.
- Termination or reversion conditions.
- Refund mechanics.
- Liability limits.

Test data export and rollback before broad rollout.

Capacity planning must cover:

- Implementation managers per cohort.
- Customer-success coverage ratios.
- Support staffing.
- Partner capacity.
- Maximum concurrent migrations.
- Cost per converted account.
- Maximum implementation backlog.
- Time-to-value targets.

Subscription launch gates should include legal, accounting, tax, privacy, security, product reliability, billing, and internal-control sign-off.

---

# 10. Liquidity and board governance

Before full-scale migration, the CFO should present and secure:

- Monthly cash curves for all scenarios.
- Minimum unrestricted cash floor.
- Peak cumulative cash shortfall.
- Required revolver or term-financing capacity.
- Liquidity buffer.
- Covenant headroom.
- Financing lead time.
- Draw authority.
- Cost-containment triggers.
- Restrictions on buybacks, acquisitions, dividends, and discretionary expansion.

The funding case must include migration credits, commissions, implementation investment, hosting buildout, customer-success costs, and working-capital effects. Prepayments should not be treated as unrestricted funding because they create future obligations.

## Transition Office

Establish a cross-functional office reporting to the CEO or COO, including finance, sales, product, engineering, customer success, legal, tax, security, hosting, revenue operations, internal controls, and investor relations.

The charter should name:

- Executive accountable owner.
- Budget owner.
- Pricing and deal-desk authority.
- Customer-remediation owner.
- Accounting-policy owner.
- Disclosure owner.
- Financing-draw authority.
- Acceleration and slowdown authority.

# 11. Roadmap and decision gates

| Timing | Key actions |
|---|---|
| First 30 days | Approve principles; establish Transition Office; begin contract audit; define scenarios and risk limits |
| By day 60 | Finalize offer hypotheses, credit formula, eligibility, pilot cohort, compensation design, and rights policy |
| By day 90 | Complete legal/accounting/tax/security review; implement billing and metric controls; quantify financing |
| Months 4–6 | Run 20–30-customer pilot; test pricing, implementation, portability, rollback, margin, and satisfaction |
| Months 7–18 | Subscription-first new business; migrate healthy cohorts; preserve selected upgrades; report quarterly |
| Months 19–36 | Expand to strategic accounts; reduce credits; increase subscription-only innovation where gates are met |
| Months 37–60 | Decide on ending new perpetual sales by product or segment; rationalize hybrid and legacy economics |

### Slowdown triggers

Slow or pause migration for two consecutive quarters if:

- Converted-cohort GRR falls below the approved segment threshold.
- Churn exceeds the legacy baseline by more than five percentage points.
- Win rate falls below 80% of the comparable perpetual offer.
- Sales productivity falls below 70% of legacy productivity.
- Implementation backlog exceeds 90 days.
- Contribution margin is more than 500 basis points below plan.
- Concessions exceed approved limits.
- Liquidity falls below the downside threshold.
- A material strategic account is lost or placed at imminent risk.

### Acceleration gates

Accelerate only if:

- Converted-cohort GRR is at least 95%.
- NRR exceeds 105%.
- Win rate reaches at least 90% of the perpetual equivalent.
- Sales productivity reaches at least 85% of legacy productivity.
- Implementation success exceeds 90%.
- Subscription gross margin reaches at least 78%.
- Customer satisfaction is stable or improving.
- Downside liquidity remains fully funded.

# 12. Investor and board communication plan

## Core message

> “We are moving from episodic license revenue to more durable recurring revenue through a controlled, customer-choice transition. Customers retain the perpetual rights granted under their agreements. Subscription provides continuous innovation, security, support, and operating services. We expect a temporary trough as the commercial and accounting model changes, but we are managing it within defined retention, margin, liquidity, and customer-trust limits.”

Management should acknowledge that the trough reflects both:

- Recognition timing; and
- Real economics, including credits, delayed upgrades, churn, implementation expense, and sales-productivity effects.

## Quarterly board and investor dashboard

Show actual, plan, prior period, trend, threshold, dollar exposure, management action, and owner for:

- Subscription new ARR.
- Maintenance ARR.
- Total recurring ARR.
- Bookings.
- Billings.
- Cash collections.
- Recognized revenue.
- Deferred revenue and remaining performance obligations.
- GRR and NRR by cohort.
- Churn reasons.
- Win rate.
- Net price realization.
- Migration-credit usage.
- Contribution margin.
- Implementation backlog and time to value.
- Sales productivity.
- Liquidity headroom.
- Hybrid ARR and margin.
- Top-account exposure.
- Exception volume.

## Communication calendar

| Event | Timing | Owner | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategy approval | Month 0 | CEO/CFO/IR | Rationale, rights protection, phases, illustrative trough range |
| Pilot readout | Month 6 | COO/CRO | Win rate, implementation, satisfaction, price, margin, retention |
| Phase 1 launch | Months 7–9 | CFO/CRO | ARR, bookings, billings, cash, customer adoption |
| First annual review | Months 12–15 | CFO/IR | Actual versus base and downside cases |
| Year 2 gate | Months 18–24 | CEO/Board | Accelerate, continue, or slow |
| Year 3 review | Months 30–36 | CEO/CFO | Conversion, retention, margin, new-perpetual policy |
| Ongoing | Quarterly | CFO/IR | Consistent metric definitions and bridges |

Before external communication, approve a metric dictionary and disclosure protocol covering:

- Material revenue or ARR deviations.
- Major customer losses.
- Financing needs.
- Security or service incidents.
- Changes in metric definitions.
- Guidance revisions.
- Historical recasts.

Do not describe the trough as merely an accounting artifact, and do not use ARR to obscure weak retention, margin, billings, or cash flow.

# 13. Principal risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Customers feel ownership is being confiscated | Contract audit, entitlement schedule, rights statement, grandfathering |
| Installed-base churn repeats competitor’s loss | No forced migration, cohort sequencing, executive sponsorship |
| Hybrid becomes permanent and uneconomic | Fixed term, standard scope, price step-up, margin floor |
| Credits erode price integrity | Single formula, fixed historical snapshot, caps, deal desk |
| Customers game eligibility | Exclude post-registration transactions and unusual discounts |
| Salespeople book low-quality ARR | Collection, activation, margin, and implementation gates |
| Revenue trough exceeds plan | Selected upgrades, services, staged costs, committed liquidity |
| Legacy support becomes too expensive | Release lifecycle, support-cost model, portfolio rationalization |
| Product is not subscription-ready | Pilot, reliability, security, portability, and rollback gates |
| Customers fail to adopt | Onboarding, usage telemetry, health scoring, executive reviews |
| Hosting and support dilute margins | Cohort contribution-margin reporting and cost controls |
| Investors misread the trough | Separate revenue, ARR, billings, cash, and economic bridges |
| Legal, accounting, tax, privacy, or security exposure | Hard launch gates and accountable workstreams |
| Customer concentration magnifies losses | Top-account downside modeling and executive retention plans |
| Sales compensation creates internal conflict | Formal ARR-crediting and account-ownership rules |
| Employee attrition during the transition | Time-limited guarantees, transparent quotas, retention planning |

## Final recommendation

Proceed with readiness work and a controlled pilot immediately. Full-scale migration should be conditional on:

1. A contract-specific entitlement and support matrix.
2. A customer-level revenue, ARR, billings, cash, and margin model.
3. A quantified and committed downside liquidity plan.
4. A coherent pricing, credit, contribution-margin, and compensation policy.
5. Pilot evidence that retention, implementation quality, price realization, sales productivity, and customer value are acceptable.

The company should move decisively but not abruptly: protect what customers purchased, make subscription the best commercial choice, compensate the sales force for durable recurring value, and slow the transition whenever retention, margin, implementation quality, price integrity, or liquidity falls outside approved limits.
