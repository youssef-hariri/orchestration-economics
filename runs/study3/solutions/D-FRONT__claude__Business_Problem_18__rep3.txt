# LEGACY BRAND REPOSITIONING STRATEGY
## Executive Summary & Phased Roadmap

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**POSITIONING STATEMENT**

*"[BRAND] is the reliable foundation for [category benefit]—chosen by millions because it delivers, respected across generations because it never pretended to be anything else."*

**CRITICAL PREREQUISITE:** This strategy is contingent on validated product truth (functional superiority, defensible innovation, or authentic values). Product audit is a non-negotiable gate before design/messaging work proceeds (Month 1-2). If product is category-equivalent with no defensible attribute, repositioning will fail. Recommend pivoting to affordability/values-based positioning instead, or investing in product innovation first.

---

## THE THREE BIGGEST DECISIONS UP FRONT

| Decision | What It Means | Why It Matters | Non-Negotiables |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Preserve >90% of 55+ revenue base while targeting under-35 growth** | Modernize design, tone, and distribution—but do NOT change core product formula, price core SKU above inflation, or abandon traditional retail. New channels are *additive*, not *replacement*. | Without 55+ revenue protection, brand cannot fund any repositioning and loses profit base. Repositioning is only viable if it protects existing cash while building new revenue. | Core SKU price increase capped at inflation rate + 2%. Core product formula unchanged. Traditional retail shelf presence maintained (not de-invested). Packaging/tone must score >85% recognition/trustworthiness with 55+ cohort. |
| **2. Validate product truth before scaling younger-cohort spend** | Test under-35 trial and repeat-purchase rates (target repeat >30%) in Phase 1 (online-only, small SKU variants) before Phase 2 (select retail rollout). Kill spend if repeat purchase is <20%; pivot strategy. | Previous campaigns failed because they were marketing-only, not business-real. CAC today is 3-5x lifetime value (uneconomical). Money spent on awareness without conversion proof is wasted. Small-scale testing isolates conversion barriers (product vs. availability vs. perception) before scaling. | Phase 1 budget: $5-10M (testing + small-scale production + organic/earned media). Phase 2 proceeding only if: Under-35 repeat purchase >20%, loyalist attrition <5%, trial conversion >3%. No exceptions. |
| **3. Sequence repositioning to de-risk authenticity backlash** | Roll out in phases: Phase 1 (Months 1-6) online-only with new SKUs and parallel loyalist monitoring; Phase 2 (Months 6-12) select retail with kill-switch gates; Phase 3 (Months 12+) gradual traditional retail expansion only if Phase 2 succeeds. Do not do "big bang" rebranding. | Previous campaigns were perceived as inauthentic because they were sudden, campaign-heavy, and didn't change reality. Slow rollout signals "honest evolution," not "chasing trends." Loyalists see consistency; younger cohort sees modernization. Parallel monitoring catches alienation before it scales. | If Phase 1 repeat purchase <20% OR loyalist attrition >5%, do not proceed to Phase 2. If Phase 2 trial <3% OR attrition >8%, do not proceed to Phase 3. Decision gates are non-political; metrics are clear. |

## 1. BRAND ASSET DECISIONS: PRESERVE / EVOLVE / RETIRE

| Asset Category | Decision | Key Actions | Testing Gates |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Logo / Mark** | EVOLVE (Targeted Refinement) | Flatten gradients; remove 1990s visual noise; simplify line weight; create digital-optimized secondary mark. Core form preserved (figurative symbols remain); execution modernized only. | >85% recognition in 55+ cohort; >50% "modern" perception in under-35. If recognition drops >15%, iterate before production. |
| **Wordmark / Typography** | EVOLVE | Modernize typeface (contemporary sans-serif or refined serif, not trendy); optimize spacing for digital legibility; build 2-font system (primary + body + optional accent). | >85% recognition across ages; <5% decline in "trustworthy" perception with 55+. |
| **Primary Color** | PRESERVE | Do not change. Primary color is highest-equity asset with loyalists and is recognizable across all touchpoints (packaging, retail, direct mail, digital). | N/A (non-negotiable). |
| **Secondary Colors** | EVOLVE | Introduce 1-2 complementary accent colors for design flexibility on digital/limited editions. Use conservatively; primary color remains dominant. | Ensure secondary palette harmonizes with primary and feels intentional, not random. |
| **Packaging Structure (Shape/Form)** | PRESERVE | Keep physical shape of primary SKU (box, bottle, tube, jar). Form is lockable in consumer memory and functional. | >90% recognition with 55+ cohort when shown new design. |
| **Packaging Material** | EVOLVE | Upgrade perceived quality if feasible (heavier cardstock, recyclable plastic alternatives, glass for premium). Material upgrade signals "brand invests in quality" to under-35 AND justifies margin or enables modest price increase. | Test consumer perception: "feels quality" >60% uplift with under-35. Phase-in gradually (certain SKUs first) to manage supply-chain cost. |
| **Packaging Surface Design** | EVOLVE (Substantially) | Replace dated photography with contemporary/naturalistic imagery or minimalist design. Simplify visual hierarchy; declutter. Modernize typography across all copy. Make ingredient list/sourcing/benefits clearly legible (younger consumers read labels). Use white space confidently. | >80% recognition in 55+; >50% perception uplift on "modern/quality/appealing" in under-35. Ingredient list legible at arm's length (8pt+, high contrast). |
| **Core Product Formula** | PRESERVE (Absolutely) | Do not reformulate to chase trends. Functional benefit claim unchanged. If product is unchanged since [year], claim "proven formula," not "innovation." If genuine improvements exist, document and communicate specifically. | Audit: Competitive testing, third-party validation (if applicable), patent/certification landscape. |
| **Product Portfolio & SKU Variants** | EXPAND (Carefully) | Keep core SKU unchanged. Add smaller form factors (travel packs, single-serve, trial sizes) priced at premium. Test subscription options and limited-edition bundles online-first in Phase 1. | Phase 1 gate: Variant repeat purchase >20% before Phase 2 rollout. Measure: Is variant additive to core or cannibilizing? Only roll winners forward. |
| **Pricing (Core SKU)** | PRESERVE Value; EVOLVE Tier Structure | Core SKU price increase capped at inflation rate + 2% (signals "no exploitation"). Introduce premium tiers (smaller sizes, limited editions, subscriptions) at higher price points for margin recovery. Do not use repositioning as justification for core SKU price hikes. | Price elasticity study with both audiences. Do not exceed pricing cap on core SKU without documented business justification and executive approval. |
| **Tone of Voice** | PRESERVE Trustworthiness; EVOLVE Directness | Move from formal/feature-heavy to direct benefit statements ("removes tough stains" not "formulated with advanced technology"). Remain conversational but not chummy. Add category-level self-awareness ("nobody is excited to do this; we make it suck less") but not brand-level jokes ("we're sooo Gen Z"). | Tone audit with both audiences: >80% of 55+ say "still trustworthy/professional" after shift; >60% of under-35 say "more relatable/honest." Provide 5-10 before/after messaging examples across touchpoints. |
| **Heritage Narrative** | PRESERVE Authenticity; EVOLVE Relevance | Keep genuine origin story only (no invention). Reframe from "longevity" to "obsession" ("founded to solve X; still solving it 70 years later"). Lead with product benefit; heritage as proof, not headline. Communicate authentic values only (sustainability, affordability, craft) backed by auditable business practices. | Heritage narrative testing with 10-15 people per audience: Does story feel authentic? Does it explain why product is good (not just that product is old)? Does it inspire trial (under-35) or reinforce loyalty (55+)? |
| **Product Claims & Benefit Statements** | PRESERVE Truth; EVOLVE Specificity | Move from vague ("powerful") to specific ("removes 95% of stains"). Shift from claiming to demonstrating (before-and-after photos, reviews, testing data). Add competitive transparency if differentiated ("lasts 50% longer than [competitor]" if provable). Never claim "natural/sustainable/eco-friendly" unless true and central to product. | Audit all claims against competitive testing data. Only specific, provable claims proceed to marketing. Red-team: Can critics find holes? If yes, iterate before launching. |
| **Digital Presence (E-Commerce, DTC, Social)** | BUILD (New Core Channel) | Product currently weak/absent in e-commerce, DTC, social commerce. This is blocking issue for under-35 trial. Build out: Amazon presence (full product assortment, high-quality pages with reviews, optimized A+); brand DTC site (mobile-first, fast checkout, subscription option); social commerce (TikTok Shop, Instagram Shop if applicable). | Phase 1: Soft launch on 1-2 channels (e.g., Amazon + DTC). Measure: Can customers easily find, understand, and buy product? Conversion rate >3% from product page to checkout? Repeat purchase rate >25% for online purchasers? |
| **Retail Presence (Traditional)** | MAINTAIN (Non-Negotiable) | Do not de-invest from traditional retail (pharmacies, supermarkets, discount retailers). Loyalists shop there exclusively; visibility is mandatory. Maintain or expand shelf space where possible. Coordinate packaging rollout with retail partners 90 days in advance. | Retail partner feedback during Phase 1: Shelf space maintained? Customer questions about new design escalated and addressed? In-store staff trained on redesign story? |

Options: pivot to different customer segment, values-based positioning, product innovation investment, or accept decline and optimize for profitability. |

## 3. CRITICAL OPERATIONAL GAPS: MUST-DO BEFORE PROCEEDING

| Gap | Why It Matters | What to Do Now | Owner | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Product Audit Process Undefined** | Entire strategy hinges on product truth validation. Without clear process/ownership/timeline, audit will be delayed or skipped, and strategy will be built on assumption. | (1) Assign external category expert + internal R&D lead to conduct audit. (2) Document: formulation comparison vs. competitors, third-party testing, patent analysis, values/practices audit. (3) Define decision thresholds: What counts as "functionally superior" (e.g., +20% efficacy)? What counts as "defensible innovation"? What counts as "authentic values"? Lab tests can pass but real-world attrition can spike. Need expanded protocol. | Expand testing to 3 phases: (1) Lab (recognition >85%, perception uplift). (5) Monitor & respond (track themes in customer service calls; if >5% are about quality concerns, escalate). (3) Social listening (automated, Reddit/Facebook): Flag negative sentiment spikes. Escalation: If any metric exceeds threshold, pause Phase 1 expansion; root-cause research; adjust before Phase 2. | Insights Lead + Loyalty Team | Month 1 (Phase 1 planning) |
| **Pricing Protection Governance Missing** | "Modest price increase" is vague. | **Phase 2 Failure Scenarios / Reversal Playbook Absent** | Strategy says there are "kill switches" but doesn't specify: What if Phase 2 trial <3%? What if loyalist attrition 8%? (3) Repeat purchase <20% (product/pricing issue; do not expand further). (4) Multiple misses (pause expansion; conduct strategy reset; consider repositioning pivot). (2) Social media (Instagram). (3) Email newsletter. (4) Website homepage. (5) Customer service response. (6) Product page. (7) Self-aware humor example. (8-10) Additional examples for ad copy, unboxing content, educational content. Provide counter-example of "too try-hard" for each to show guardrails. Share with creative team as north-star brief. | Creative Director | Month 1 (Phase 1 planning) |

Values-driven consumers? Without segmentation, media spend is unfocused. | Conduct segmentation study (or use existing audience data) to identify: (1) Demographic sweet spot (age range, likely Millennials 25-35 vs. Gen Z 18-24?). Expand to broader under-35 only if Phase 1 conversion validates. | Insights Lead + Performance Marketing Lead | Month 0-1 (Phase 1 planning) |

## 4. RISK REGISTER: WHAT CAN STILL GO WRONG

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation | Escalation Criteria |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Product Truth Audit Delayed/Skipped** | Medium | Critical (entire strategy built on unproven assumption; if product is commodity, repositioning fails and wastes investment) | Assign ownership now. Set hard Month-2 deadline. Require audit output for Phase 1 green-light. | If audit is not complete by end of Month 2, escalate to CEO; delay Phase 1 production until audit is done. |
| **Influencer Partnerships Perceived as Inauthentic/Paid-Shill** | Medium-High | High (backlash on social; younger cohort loses trust; under-35 trial stalls) | Build creator seeding playbook now with authenticity gates. Prioritize organic tier (no payment, natural trial). Performance-based tier structured so payment is transparent but incentive-aligned. Red-team: If creator content looks like brand template, reject. | If creator-driven trial has >10% negative sentiment on social ("they're just getting paid") or repeat purchase <15%, pause creator program; pivot to organic/earned media. |
| **Tone Modernization Alienates Loyalists (Perceived as Quality Downgrade)** | Medium | High (loyalist attrition 8-12%, unraveling revenue base) | Build loyalist transition communication plan. Provide 5-10 tone examples to creative team. Test with both audiences before launch. Brief customer service on expected questions. Monitor social/calls for quality-concern themes. | If >5% of loyalist customer service calls are about "is product different?" or social mentions of "quality downgrade" spike, escalate immediately; shift communications back to formal tone (packaging stays modern, messaging becomes more professional). |
| **Packaging Redesign Feels "Foreign" to Loyalists Despite Lab Testing** | Medium | High (recognition test passes in lab but shelf-scanning fails in-store; hidden attrition) | Expand testing to emotional-safety and in-shelf legibility phases. Test with real loyalists in real retail environment. Do NOT commit to production until all 3 test phases pass. | If in-shelf locate time >5 seconds (vs. current <3 seconds) or emotional safety test fails (>15% don't feel "still my brand"), iterate design before production commit. |
| **Phase 1 Online-Only Testing Doesn't Predict Phase 2 Retail Performance** | Medium | High (metrics look good online, but retail trial stalls; channel behavior is different) | Add parallel loyalist attrition monitoring during Phase 1. Track whether loyalists become aware of repositioning (word-of-mouth, social, media) and perception impact. | If loyalist attrition signals >5% decline in Phase 1 (even online-only), pause expansion; root-cause (is awareness/perception already shifting negatively?) before Phase 2. |
| **Competitor Responds While Brand Is Distracted** | Medium | Medium (competitors gain younger-cohort market share; category dynamics shift; brand's repositioning becomes irrelevant) | Monitor competitor activity quarterly. If competitor launches major initiative targeting under-35 or launches innovation, escalate (may need to accelerate repositioning or defend base). | If competitor gains >3% market share in under-35 segment during Phase 1-2, evaluate urgency (is repositioning moving fast enough? Should we accelerate Phase 2 timeline?). |
| **Core SKU Price Increase Is Used to Fund Repositioning; Loyalists Perceive Betrayal** | Medium | High (attrition spikes; repositioning cost is borne by base, not by business; trust breaks) | Establish pricing governance in writing; cap core SKU increase at inflation + 2%. Require CMO + CFO approval for any exception. Document business justification. | If core SKU price increase exceeds cap without documented approval, CMO flags as governance violation; decision overridden or delayed pending review. |
| **Brand Appears in Influencer Partnerships That Don't Align with Product; Audience Perceives Inauthenticity** | Medium-High | High (backlash; brand credibility damaged; under-35 trial stalls) | Vet creator audience demographic/engagement/content alignment before outreach. Use performance-based model so only effective partnerships continue. Review creator's previous brand partnerships. | If creator partnership generates <$X CAC or repeat purchase <15%, end partnership. If partnership generates social backlash (comments like "selling out"), end immediately and prepare apology/explanation. |
| **"Phase 1 Metrics Look Good but Phase 2 Fails" (Online ≠ Retail)** | Medium | High (time/money wasted; repositioning delayed; confidence erodes) | Phase 1 testing must include retail-simulation or small retail test within Phase 1. Do not rely entirely on online metrics. | Phase 1 gate should include: (1) Online metrics (repeat >20%) + (2) Small retail test metrics (trial >3%, attrition <5%). Both must be met to proceed to Phase 2 scale. |
| **Loyalist Transition Communication Insufficient; Silent Attrition** | Medium-High | High (attrition is invisible until revenue reporting 6+ months later; by then hard to correct) | Build transition communication plan with 5 touchpoints. Monitor loyalist perception monthly during Phase 1. Set attrition-signal threshold (<5% month-over-month decline); escalate if exceeded. | If attrition signals >5% in any month of Phase 1, pause and conduct rapid qualitative research (what triggered concern?) before proceeding to Phase 2. |
| **"Big Bang" Rebranding Happens Despite Sequencing Plan; Suddenly All SKUs Redesigned + Tone Shifts + Media Campaign Launches** | Low-Medium | Critical (all authenticity risks compound; under-35 perceives "corporate trying too hard"; loyalists feel abandoned; both audiences alienated simultaneously) | Enforce phased sequencing in production/media plan. Phase 1 = limited geographies, limited SKUs, organic/creator media only, no large paid campaigns. Phase 2 = select retail only. Phase 3 = expansion. Do not authorize production run or media buy outside sequenced plan. | If "big bang" rollout is proposed by leadership (to accelerate), CMO must escalate to CEO: "This violates sequencing strategy; risk of authenticity backlash; recommend proceeding as planned." Decision made by CEO. |
| **Under-35 Repeat Purchase Stays at 15% Despite Corrective Actions; Repositioning Is Not Converting** | Medium | Critical (reveals that product, positioning, or market fit is fundamentally misaligned; repositioning strategy is not viable) | Phase 2 decision gate: If repeat purchase <20% after 6 months, do NOT expand. Root-cause research: Is it product quality? Pricing? Availability? Messaging mismatch? | If repeat purchase remains <20% despite 6 months of Phase 2 effort, escalate to strategy reset: (1) Consider product innovation, (2) Pivot to affordability/values positioning, (3) Redefine target segment, or (4) Accept that repositioning is not viable; optimize for profitability instead. |
