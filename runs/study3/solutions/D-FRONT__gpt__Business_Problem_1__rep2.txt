# Vietnam-First Southeast Asia Expansion Plan

## Executive decision

**Recommendation:** Approve a tightly controlled Vietnam validation program as the company’s first Southeast Asian market test, while evaluating local manufacturing as a separate investment case.

**Initial cities:** Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi  
**Initial validation period:** 0–90 days  
**Validation budget:** €250,000–€400,000  
**Maximum authorized loss:** €400,000  
**Conditional 18-month program:** €1.1m–€2.6m, released only through stage gates

Vietnam is not necessarily the strongest premium-consumer market in Asia-Pacific. Japan, South Korea, Australia, or Singapore may offer greater purchasing power or simpler operating conditions. Vietnam is recommended because it offers the most balanced combination of:

- Meaningful urban demand
- Strong apparel-manufacturing capability
- Lower operating costs than North Asian alternatives
- High mobile, marketplace, and social-commerce usage
- Regional expansion potential
- A manageable environment for commercial and supply-chain learning

### Approval limitations

The initial approval excludes:

- A permanent subsidiary
- A flagship or permanent store
- Large inventory commitments
- Material production transfer
- A regional headquarters
- Partner exclusivity
- The full 18-month investment program

The commercial case and sourcing case must be approved independently. A successful consumer pilot does not automatically justify Vietnamese production.

---

# 1. Strategic objectives

## Commercial objective

Determine whether the company can acquire and retain Vietnamese customers at localized prices while achieving acceptable contribution economics after:

- Product cost
- Duties and freight
- Taxes
- Marketplace and payment fees
- Fulfillment and delivery subsidies
- Returns and failed delivery
- Customer service
- Creator fees
- Variable advertising

## Sourcing objective

Determine whether Vietnamese suppliers can produce selected products with:

- Required quality and lead-time performance
- Competitive total landed cost
- Verified material traceability
- Acceptable labor and environmental standards
- Adequate capacity and minimum-order quantities
- Supply continuity and dual-source potential

## Evidence standard

Management should prioritize:

1. Paid orders
2. Observed conversion and price response
3. Return and fit behavior
4. Repeat purchase
5. Product- and channel-level contribution
6. Customer reviews and complaints

Surveys, clicks, impressions, and stated sustainability interest are directional evidence only.

---

# 2. Market rationale and alternatives

## Vietnam’s strategic role

Vietnam should be treated as:

- A two-city consumer pilot
- A potential apparel-sourcing base
- A possible platform for later expansion into Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, or Indonesia

Regional expansion is not automatic. Each additional market will require separate validation of demand, price, language, payment, tax, logistics, sizing, and returns.

## Comparative view

| Market | Primary strength | Primary limitation | Strategic role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vietnam | Balanced demand, sourcing depth, and manageable pilot cost | Lower purchasing power and complex execution | **First commercial and sourcing test** |
| Japan | Affluent, quality- and durability-oriented consumers | High localization and competition costs | Later premium-market option |
| South Korea | Excellent digital commerce and fashion influence | Very high competition and CAC | Later trend-led market |
| Singapore | Strong infrastructure and regional connectivity | Small domestic demand and high costs | Potential regional hub |
| Australia | High purchasing power and lower language barriers | Limited sourcing leverage and longer logistics | Alternative consumer market |
| Thailand/Malaysia | Viable urban and premium segments | Require local channel and cultural adaptation | Second-wave candidates |
| Indonesia | Significant long-term scale | Greater operating and localization complexity | Longer-term opportunity |

**Important distinction:** Vietnam’s consumer-market attractiveness and sourcing attractiveness should be assessed separately. Sourcing comparisons should include the company’s existing suppliers and relevant alternatives such as Portugal, Turkey, India, Bangladesh, China, or other current sourcing locations.

# 3. Target customers and local proposition

## Primary target: climate-conscious urban professionals

**Location:** Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi  
**Indicative age:** 25–40  
**Profile:** Middle- to upper-income, digitally active consumers interested in design, wellness, travel, quality, and responsible consumption.

### Customer needs

- Breathable clothing for heat and humidity
- Versatility across work, commuting, travel, and casual use
- Comfort in outdoor heat and indoor air conditioning
- Contemporary design at below-luxury price points
- Clear sizing and reliable delivery
- Specific, credible environmental information

## Secondary target: younger digital sustainability consumers

- More social-media active and price-sensitive
- Interested in capsule wardrobes, repair, resale, and limited drops
- Useful for community and content development
- Not assumed to be the first profitability segment

## Tertiary target: premium B2B

Potential customers include:

- Boutique hotels
- Wellness studios
- Responsible cafés and restaurants
- Coworking operators
- International schools
- Sustainability-oriented businesses

B2B should proceed only where minimum orders, customization fees, payment terms, and margins are acceptable.

## Recommended positioning

> **European-designed clothing for warm, humid everyday life—comfortable, versatile, durable, and supported by specific, verifiable material and production evidence.**

Lead with:

1. Comfort
2. Design
3. Versatility
4. Durability
5. Climate suitability
6. Verified material and production information

“European-designed” must be tested as a customer benefit. It should not imply superiority over local brands.

# 4. Product and assortment strategy

## Initial assortment

Launch with **12–20 SKUs**, limited colors and controlled inventory:

| Product role | Indicative share |
|---|---:|
| Everyday essentials | 50–60% |
| Lightweight travel or active products | 20–25% |
| Lightweight layers for offices and travel | 10–20% |
| Limited or locally relevant products | Up to 10% |

### Prioritize

- Lightweight, breathable construction
- Tested moisture management
- Quick-drying and easy-care performance
- Colorfastness
- Versatile styling
- Durable construction
- Suitability for outdoor heat and indoor air conditioning

### Deprioritize

- Heavy wool and winter products
- Complex tailoring
- High-return occasionwear
- Large seasonal ranges
- Styles with high minimum-order quantities
- Products with uncertain climate relevance

## Product testing

Compare:

- Existing European control products
- Climate-adapted products
- Sustainability-led hero products
- Entry, core, and premium price points

Test whether demand is driven primarily by:

- Comfort
- Design
- Fit
- Climate suitability
- Sustainability evidence
- Durability
- Price
- Travel utility

## Fit and sizing

**Initial policy:** retain the global brand architecture, supported by localized measurement guidance and selective pattern changes.

Actions:

- Conduct fit sessions in both cities
- Use body and garment measurements in centimeters
- Use locally relevant models across body types
- Provide digital fit guidance
- Record returns by product, size, and fit reason
- Change patterns only where return and fit data supports the decision

### Provisional return thresholds

To be recalibrated after 500–1,000 orders:

- Basics: below 18%
- Tailored/fitted products: below 25%
- Fit-related returns: below 10–12% of orders

Targets must ultimately be set by category and channel.

# 5. Pricing and unit economics

Pricing should be localized rather than converted directly from European prices.

| Tier | Role | Initial pricing hypothesis |
|---|---|---:|
| Entry essentials | Trial and acquisition | 70–85% of comparable European price |
| Core collection | Main volume and margin | 80–100% |
| Premium/limited products | Brand elevation | 90–115% |

These are estimates requiring competitor and paid-order validation.

## Required price tests

Test three price levels for comparable products:

1. Trial price
2. Intended base price
3. Premium price

Measure:

- Conversion
- Add-to-cart rate
- Full-price sell-through
- Return rate
- Contribution margin
- Repeat purchase
- Discount dependency

## Illustrative economics

The following are planning examples, not forecasts.

| Per-order item | Entry | Core | Premium |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Net revenue after tax treatment | €45 | €75 | €125 |
| Product cost | €14 | €23 | €40 |
| Duty, freight, insurance, inbound | €5 | €7 | €10 |
| Payment and marketplace fees | €6 | €10 | €17 |
| Fulfillment and delivery subsidy | €5 | €5 | €6 |
| Expected return cost | €3 | €6 | €10 |
| Variable marketing/CAC | €10 | €15 | €23 |
| **Illustrative contribution** | **€2** | **€9** | **€19** |
| **Illustrative contribution margin** | **4%** | **12%** | **15%** |

Contribution includes all variable costs, including marketplace fees, returns, creator fees, and advertising. Fixed local staffing, technology, office, and launch costs are reported separately.

### SKU rules

- **Acquisition SKU:** may have low first-order contribution only with an approved maximum loss and evidence of repeat purchase, bundle attachment, or customer-level contribution.
- **Profit-driver SKU:** provisional requirement of at least 10% contribution margin after all variable costs for two consecutive reporting periods.
- **All SKUs:** must not depend on indefinite discounting.

Finance should produce Vietnam-specific entry, core, and premium models using actual competitor prices, fee schedules, tax assumptions, logistics bids, and observed acquisition costs.

# 6. Entry model and partner structure

## Preferred initial model

Subject to written Vietnamese legal and tax advice, use a **local merchant-of-record/importer partner** for validation and controlled launch.

This approach can reduce initial entity and compliance complexity while avoiding a premature subsidiary.

| Responsibility | Proposed allocation |
|---|---|
| Importer of record | Licensed local partner |
| Seller of record | Local merchant-of-record partner |
| Customer contract and invoice | Local partner, confirmed by counsel |
| Inventory ownership | Company until sale, unless an alternative is approved |
| Warehousing and delivery | Vietnamese 3PL under documented agreement |
| Returns and refunds | Local partner under company service standards |
| Product and claims approval | European parent |
| Customer data | Partner processes under documented lawful instructions |
| Product liability | Contractually allocated and insured |
| Pricing and discounts | Company-approved rules |
| Customs and tax | Named partner responsibility, independently verified |
| Cash settlement and repatriation | Defined contractually and reviewed by tax counsel |

### Fallback model

If the partner structure does not provide adequate legal certainty, data access, pricing control, or customer-service quality, establish a company-controlled Vietnamese entity using a local customs provider and 3PL.

No paid testing should begin until counsel confirms:

- Importer of record
- Seller of record
- Inventory owner
- Customer-contracting entity
- VAT and invoice issuer
- Returns and refund responsibility
- Product and claims liability
- Data-controller and processor roles
- Insurance
- Cash settlement and repatriation

## Partner requirements

Obtain at least two proposals and assess:

- Apparel and marketplace experience
- Import and licensing capability
- Financial stability
- Customer service
- Data security and access
- Returns capability
- Claims discipline
- Conflicts with competing brands
- Service-level commitments
- Insolvency protection
- Exit and transition rights

No exclusivity should be granted before performance is demonstrated.

# 7. Marketing channels and launch plan

## Channel sequence

1. Localized DTC website
2. One or two approved marketplaces
3. Two city-based pop-ups
4. Selective premium wholesale
5. Targeted B2B pilots

## Channel roles

### DTC

Use DTC to control:

- Brand presentation
- Product education
- Sustainability claims
- Pricing
- Customer service
- Lawful first-party CRM development

The site should be Vietnamese-language, mobile-first, locally priced, and compatible with local payment methods.

### Marketplaces

Use marketplaces for discovery and trust, but limit initial SKU exposure and monitor:

- Commissions
- Advertising costs
- Fulfillment costs
- Returns
- Review quality
- Discounting
- Counterfeiting
- Data access
- Seller-of-record obligations

### Creators

Prioritize micro- and mid-tier creators in:

- Sustainable living
- Minimalist fashion
- Wellness
- Travel
- Professional lifestyle
- Environmental education

Selection criteria should include audience geography, engagement authenticity, content rights, disclosure compliance, claim controls, and trackable sales.

### Pop-ups and events

Run two initial pop-ups to test:

- Fit
- Full-price conversion
- Customer objections
- Product education
- Offline acquisition economics

Do not establish permanent retail until store-level economics and repeat demand are demonstrated.

### B2B

Treat B2B as a separate business case. Require:

- Minimum order size
- Approved customization fees
- Deposits or payment terms
- Defined fit and liability arrangements
- Minimum contribution margin

## 90-day channel test

| Channel | Test | Share of activation budget | Primary KPI |
|---|---|---:|---|
| DTC paid social/search | Three audiences, messages, and price points | 30% | Contribution after CAC |
| Marketplace | 5–8 SKUs with controlled pricing | 25% | Net contribution and reviews |
| Creators | 15–25 micro/mid-tier creators | 20% | Independent paid orders and CAC |
| Pop-ups | Two city events | 15% | Full-price conversion and fit learning |
| CRM/content | Vietnamese lifecycle content | 10% | Repeat engagement and assisted conversion |

Pause a channel after two consecutive reporting periods with negative contribution, poor-quality traffic, or unacceptable returns without an approved corrective action.

# 8. Manufacturing and sourcing opportunity

Vietnam should be evaluated as a potential sourcing base independently from the consumer launch.

## Sourcing sequence

- **Months 0–9:** import finished products from existing suppliers
- **Months 6–12:** screen and sample Vietnamese factories
- **Months 10–18:** pilot one or two proven product families
- **After 18 months:** scale only with at least two qualified sources

## Pilot product

Start with a proven, low-complexity product such as:

- Lightweight woven shirt
- Basic jersey top
- Simple travel trouser
- Lightweight overshirt

Avoid complex tailoring, high-risk technical products, and uncertain-demand styles.

## Supplier scorecard

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Product quality and defect performance | 20% |
| Labor standards and worker voice | 20% |
| Environmental and chemical management | 15% |
| Material traceability and certification | 15% |
| Total landed economics | 10% |
| Capacity, MOQ, and lead time | 10% |
| Business continuity and subcontractor control | 10% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

## Mandatory exclusion criteria

A supplier cannot qualify, regardless of aggregate score, if it has unresolved:

- Forced labor, child labor, or recruitment-fee issues
- Serious health and safety violations
- Falsified records
- Uncontrolled subcontracting
- Illegal environmental activity
- Material traceability failure
- Product-safety failure
- Prohibited chemical or wastewater violations

## Provisional sourcing gate

Proceed only if:

- At least two factories score 75/100 or higher
- No mandatory exclusion issue remains unresolved
- Pre-shipment defect rate is below 3%, subject to category-specific inspection standards
- Pilot lead time is within 10% of agreement
- MOQ fits the approved inventory plan
- Total landed cost improves by at least 10%, or
- A quantified resilience, lead-time, or diversification benefit justifies the difference

Any resilience benefit must be translated into measurable value, such as avoided disruption cost, shorter replenishment time, or reduced supplier concentration.

Supplier contracts must cover:

- Corrective-action deadlines
- Suspension and termination rules
- Capacity reservations
- Payment terms
- Intellectual-property and pattern protection
- Subcontractor disclosure
- Testing and inspection
- Business continuity

Local production may be rejected even if the consumer pilot succeeds.

# 9. Operations, logistics, and compliance

## Inventory policy

Initial inventory should be staged:

- Experimental products: 8–10 weeks of expected demand
- Proven core products: 12–16 weeks
- Limited color and size breadth
- Small local safety stock
- Weekly SKU-level replenishment review

> **Reorder point = expected demand during replenishment lead time + safety stock**

The inventory model must include:

- Units by SKU, color, and size
- Unit cost
- Weekly demand assumptions
- Replenishment lead time
- Safety stock
- Selling window
- Markdown trigger
- Liquidation value
- Maximum downside exposure

Use different sell-through rules for evergreen, seasonal, limited, and experimental products.

## 3PL and delivery

Obtain at least two 3PL bids covering Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi. Evaluate:

- Delivery time
- Inventory accuracy
- Failed delivery
- Payment handling
- Damage and loss
- Humidity controls
- Returns collection
- Systems integration
- Insurance
- Cost per order and return

Provisional operating targets:

- Major-city delivery: 2–5 working days after local stock is available
- On-time delivery: above 95%
- Core-stock availability: above 90%
- Return processing: under 10 working days
- Failed delivery: below 5%

These are targets, not evidence, until confirmed by bids and pilot data.

## Returns and service

Establish:

- Vietnamese-language customer service
- Local return collection or address
- Clear statutory and commercial policies
- Timely refunds
- Fit-related return coding
- Product inspection and disposition
- Returns-fraud and wardrobing controls
- Escalation for safety, quality, and claims complaints

## Compliance dependencies

### Before paid testing

- Counsel-approved testing structure
- Trademark filing initiated
- Data-consent and marketing approach confirmed
- Advertising and claims review completed
- Test-product labeling and import requirements confirmed
- Partner, payment, and data responsibilities documented

### Before first commercial sale

- Importer and seller of record confirmed
- E-commerce and trading requirements confirmed
- HS classification and customs valuation confirmed
- Duty, VAT, invoicing, and origin rules confirmed
- Consumer returns, warranties, refunds, and product liability confirmed
- Vietnamese labeling approved
- Data-processing agreements executed
- Insurance in place
- Customer-service procedures live

### Before local production

- Factory and subcontractor diligence complete
- Labor and environmental exclusions cleared
- Certification scope confirmed
- Product testing and inspection approved
- IP protections executed
- Capacity and payment terms documented
- Continuity and dual-source plan established

# 10. Sustainability and claims governance

Sustainability should reinforce product value without becoming an unsupported headline.

## Acceptable claim principles

Claims must be:

- Specific
- Product-level wherever possible
- Quantified or clearly scoped
- Supported by current evidence
- Accurately translated
- Consistent across all channels

Examples:

- “Contains 70% certified organic cotton”
- “Made with 100% certified recycled polyester”
- “Produced at a facility audited against [named standard] in [month/year]”

Avoid:

- “Fully sustainable”
- “Zero impact”
- “Non-toxic”
- “Climate positive”
- Unqualified “eco-friendly”
- Unsupported claims that products reduce washing
- Claims that Vietnamese production is automatically more sustainable

## Claims register

| Required field | Control |
|---|---|
| Exact claim | Approved wording |
| Classification | Material, certification, performance, comparative, process, or corporate |
| Evidence | Certificate, test, record, or audit |
| Scope | Product, facility, collection, or company |
| Limitation | What the claim does not imply |
| Vietnamese wording | Legal and linguistic review |
| Owner | Sustainability/legal/marketing |
| Expiry date | Review or certification date |
| Permitted channels | Website, marketplace, packaging, media, creators |
| Correction process | Takedown, correction, and escalation procedure |

Claims require legal or compliance approval before use in paid media, creator content, marketplace listings, packaging, or retail materials.

Repair, take-back, recycling, and packaging-recovery claims require verified local collection and downstream processing before public launch.

# 11. Roadmap and stage gates

## Phase 0: Days 0–30 — discovery and setup

**Accountable sponsor:** Expansion executive  
**Workstream owners:** Commercial, finance, legal/tax, operations, sourcing, data, sustainability, HR

### Actions

- Confirm separate commercial and sourcing objectives
- Validate country comparisons with dated evidence
- Appoint local research, legal, tax, customs, and logistics advisers
- Begin trademark registration
- Define candidate SKUs
- Build landed-cost and contribution models
- Identify at least two partners and two 3PL candidates
- Define seller, importer, inventory, liability, tax, and data responsibilities
- Prepare the bottom-up validation budget
- Establish claims register and evidence repository

### Deliverable

Gate 0 investment memo.

## Phase 1: Days 31–90 — discovery validation

### Actions

- Conduct 30–50 consumer interviews
- Run local fit sessions
- Benchmark 15–20 competitors
- Test three price points
- Run paid landing-page, preorder, or limited-order tests
- Compare climate-adapted and European control products
- Obtain written legal, tax, customs, data, labeling, consumer-protection, and claims opinions
- Obtain two 3PL bids and two partner proposals
- Screen 10–15 factories; deeply assess only suppliers relevant to products showing demand
- Test packaging and customer-service processes
- Build SKU-level inventory, markdown, and liquidation models

### Gate 1: controlled-launch authorization

Require:

- At least two product categories tested
- At least two hero SKUs per category
- At least 300 paid orders from at least 200 independent customers
- At least 50% of orders at full or near-full price
- At least two price points tested for each hero category
- No employee, family, or non-independent orders
- Return observation completed for the majority of orders
- No category above its provisional return threshold
- Two profit-driver SKUs achieving at least 10% contribution margin after variable costs for two reporting periods, or a preapproved acquisition-SKU case with observed repeat or bundle evidence
- Lawful selling and returns model documented
- Two viable logistics options
- Two credible operating options
- All proposed claims evidenced and approved
- Maximum inventory loss quantified and within authorization

If evidence is insufficient, management may narrow the assortment, revise pricing or proposition, extend validation, or stop.

**Timing note:** If return or repeat-purchase evidence cannot mature within 90 days, Gate 1 should be extended or split into a limited paid-pilot gate and a later commercial-launch gate.

## Phase 2: Months 3–6 — controlled launch

- Launch in Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi
- Offer 12–20 SKUs
- Activate localized DTC and one or two marketplaces
- Run two pop-ups
- Establish Vietnamese customer service and returns
- Test creators and limited B2B prospects
- Report SKU- and channel-level economics weekly
- Begin supplier sampling without material production transfer

### Gate 2: optimization continuation

Require:

- Contribution margin improving for two consecutive reporting periods
- Full-price sell-through meeting category targets
- Returns within category thresholds
- On-time delivery above 95%
- No critical compliance, safety, labor, environmental, or claims incident
- Earliest mature cohorts showing repeat purchase
- Inventory exposure within approved limits
- Customer-service performance within approved standards

## Phase 3: Months 6–18 — conditional scale

- Expand only proven products and channels
- Revise fit and assortment
- Pilot one or two Vietnamese-made product families
- Add selected wholesale and B2B accounts
- Test repair or take-back only with verified operators
- Improve CRM and retention
- Consider local entity formation if justified
- Assess Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, and Indonesia separately

### Gate 3: scale decision

Require:

- Positive contribution after variable marketing and fulfillment for two consecutive quarters
- CAC payback within 12 months, subject to management confirmation
- At least 25% repeat purchase in a defined mature cohort, with improving trend
- Core-stock availability above 90%
- On-time delivery above 95%
- Category-specific returns controlled
- No unresolved critical compliance or supplier issue
- At least two qualified manufacturing sources
- Fixed-cost investment justified by cash generation and operational need

# 13. KPI governance

Each KPI must have a formula, source, owner, reporting frequency, observation window, minimum sample size, threshold, and corrective action.

| KPI | Definition |
|---|---|
| Net revenue | Revenue after discounts, cancellations, refunds, and applicable tax treatment |
| Full-price order | Order with no discount beyond the approved baseline |
| Contribution margin | Contribution divided by net revenue after all variable product, channel, logistics, service, creator, and advertising costs |
| CAC | Variable acquisition spend divided by independent new customers acquired |
| Repeat purchase | Percentage of first-order customers placing another paid order within the defined cohort period, excluding cancellations and fully returned orders |
| Full-price sell-through | Units sold at approved price divided by units available during the defined selling window |
| Core-stock availability | Demand-weighted availability of designated core SKUs in sellable sizes |
| On-time delivery | Orders delivered within the promised window divided by delivered orders |
| Fit-related return | Return attributed to size, length, cut, or fit |
| Mature cohort | Cohort with sufficient time for the relevant repeat-purchase window to elapse |
| Critical incident | Confirmed product-safety, forced-labor, child-labor, serious worker-safety, illegal environmental, data, falsification, or regulatory issue requiring immediate escalation |

### Reporting template

| Metric | Formula | Source | Window | Minimum sample | Threshold | Owner | Action if failed |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|

No qualitative term such as “acceptable,” “controlled,” or “improving” should be used as a gate without a defined numerical threshold.
