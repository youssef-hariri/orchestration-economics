## WALK-AWAY CONDITIONS & CONTINGENCIES

### Go/No-Go Framework for Each Funding Source

---

### **VENTURE DEBT LENDER WALK-AWAY (Bright Lines)**

**If any of these occur, lender will demand action (waiver, acceleration, or repayment):**

| Trigger | Metric | Threshold | Measurement | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue growth miss** | YoY revenue growth | <70% (covenant is 80%; 70% is 90-day cure trigger) | Q4-to-Q4 YoY | If <70% for 2 consecutive quarters, lender demands covenant waiver ($160–240K fee) or accelerated repayment |
| **EBITDA margin collapse** | EBITDA margin | <15% (covenant floor) | Annual measurement | Triggers waiver discussion; if margin stays <12% for 2 quarters, lender may demand accelerated paydown from cash or equity raise |
| **Leverage breach** | Net Debt / EBITDA | >3.5x | Quarterly | Automatic covenant breach; 90-day cure required; if not cured, lender can accelerate amortization or demand full repayment |
| **DSCR erosion** | EBITDA / (Int + Princ) | <1.25x | Quarterly | Lender cannot service debt from operating cash; triggers lender concern; may demand dividend restriction or forced capex cutback |
| **Cash minimum** | Minimum quarterly cash balance | <$5M at quarter-end | Quarterly | Company is illiquid; lender may restrict distributions or require additional covenants |
| **Customer concentration spike** | Top 5 customer % of revenue | >45% | Semi-annual | Signals revenue concentration risk; lender may demand concentration reduction plan or require ABL to cover concentration haircut |

**Lender contingency plan (if breach occurs):**
1. **Day 1–30:** Lender sends breach notice; founder has 90 days to cure.
2. **Day 30–60:** Founder negotiates with lender on cure plan (e.g., "We'll hit 80% growth by Q1 if you give us 60-day extension").
3. **Day 60–90:** If cure plan is credible, lender waives breach for 60–90 days; charges waiver fee (2–3% of loan = $160–240K).
4. **Day 90+:** If metrics don't improve, lender can: (A) accelerate amortization (principal jumps from $133K/mo to $200K/mo), (B) demand full repayment in cash, or (C) demand equity co-investment (founder dilution to lender).

**Founder's walk-away:** If lender demands waiver fee >$300K or equity co-investment >5%, founder can explore refinancing with new lender (difficult if credit profile has deteriorated) or raising equity to pay down debt (more dilution).

### **PREFERRED EQUITY INVESTOR WALK-AWAY (Bright Lines)**

**If any of these occur, investor will become hostile or force strategic action:**

| Trigger | Metric | Threshold | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Growth stall** | YoY revenue growth | <40% CAGR for 2 consecutive quarters (vs. target 80%+) | Investor views company as under-performing; pushes for Series C at lower valuation, CEO coaching, or strategic pivot |
| **Unit economics deterioration** | CAC payback + churn combo | CAC payback >24 months OR monthly churn >8% for 2 quarters | Investor questions product-market fit; may demand go-to-market reset; blocks further capital |
| **Competitive loss** | Market share vs. competitor | Market share falls <40% in core segment | Investor pressure mounts for M&A or pivot; may accelerate Series C (to raise growth capital for counter-attack) or recommend sale |
| **Margin collapse** | EBITDA margin | <12% for 2 consecutive quarters | Investor views company as operationally broken; demands cost restructuring or revenue model change |
| **Customer concentration** | Top 10 customer % of revenue | >45% for 2 consecutive quarters | Investor risk; may demand diversification plan or restrict new debt |
| **Founder departure / key hire loss** | Management team stability | CEO or CRO departure without succession plan | Investor may demand interim CEO or COO; restrict new equity grants; push for M&A |

**Investor contingency plan (if performance misses):**
1. **Month 1–3 of miss:** Investor requests deep dive board meeting; reviews unit economics, competitive landscape, retention data.
2. **Month 3–6:** If miss is temporary, investor accepts corrective action plan (e.g., "We'll hit 60% growth by Q2 with new GTM strategy"). If miss is structural, investor pushes for Series C at lower valuation or M&A.
3. **Month 6+:** If no improvement, investor may push for: (A) Founder replacement with interim/permanent CEO, (B) M&A to strategic buyer, or (C) write-down of investment (company stays independent but at lower valuation).

**Founder's walk-away:** If investor demands founders be replaced or forced M&A at valuation <$300M, founder can: (A) buy out investor at 1x return ($8M) + accrued dividends, (B) refinance investor out with new capital (equity or debt), or (C) accept investor's terms as cost of having failed to execute plan.

---

### **RBF LENDER WALK-AWAY**

**RBF lender has minimal walk-away risk because repayment is automated (6% of revenue).**

| Trigger | Scenario | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue collapse** | YoY revenue falls >30% (e.g., $90M → $63M) | RBF repayment rate drops automatically (6% of $63M = $3.78M vs. budgeted $5.4M); loan repayment slows but lender can still collect from revenue stream |
| **Business failure** | Company enters bankruptcy or liquidation | RBF lender is unsecured; has low recovery (claim is on revenue, which is now $0 in bankruptcy); accepts loss |
| **Accelerated repayment** | If revenue exceeds projections significantly, cap ($5.2M) is hit in 16–18 months instead of 24 | Lender's return is capped; no downside acceleration, but also no upside extension; facility terminates and lender exits |

**RBF lender contingency:** Minimal. Lender's only lever is to demand repayment acceleration if company is obviously failing (revenue is collapsing). But if revenue is declining, founder has bigger problems (refinancing venture debt, managing payroll, etc.). RBF is deprioritized.

**Founder's walk-away:** If RBF repayment is unsustainable (>40% of EBITDA), founder can: (A) refinance RBF with ABL (cheaper), (B) accelerate repayment by cutting costs (hitting cap in 18 mo vs. 24 mo), or (C) request lender forbearance (unlikely to be granted; RBF is tied to revenue, so "forbearance" doesn't make sense).

---

### **ABL LENDER WALK-AWAY**

**ABL is backup liquidity; lender's walk-away is low probability if borrowing base is healthy.**

| Trigger | Scenario | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Borrowing base shrinks** | Customer concentration spikes to >50% of A/R; OR inventory turns slow (60+ days); OR receivables aging extends (DSO >60 days) | Borrowing base calculation drops (e.g., from $3M to $1.5M); company's available credit shrinks; may need to cut capex or hiring |
| **Covenant breach** | Minimum $10M cash balance is not maintained when revolver draws >$1.5M | ABL lender restricts new draws; company must reduce burn or raise capital |
| **Annual renewal risk** | Year 2 or Year 3, bank reviews credit; if company's metrics have deteriorated, bank may decline to renew | ABL facility expires; company loses backup liquidity (but hopefully
