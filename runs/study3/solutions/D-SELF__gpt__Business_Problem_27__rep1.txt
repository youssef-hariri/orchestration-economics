# Executive Brief: Growth Financing Strategy

## Executive recommendation

Pursue a **parallel, staged financing process** combining:

1. **Retained cash and operating cash flow** for the first, fastest-payback initiatives;
2. **Delayed-draw senior debt, equipment finance, and/or receivables financing** for identifiable assets and working capital;
3. **A modest minority preferred-equity investment** for market expansion, senior hiring, product development, systems, and liquidity protection; and
4. **A tightly capped hybrid instrument only as a temporary bridge**, not as core capital.

This structure is preferable because it:

- Preserves founder majority ownership and practical operating control;
- Avoids loading speculative growth onto fixed repayment obligations;
- Provides capital quickly enough to defend customers, talent, and channels;
- Matches debt to assets and visible cash flows, and equity to higher-risk expansion; and
- Preserves disciplined organic growth as a fallback.

Begin lender and investor discussions immediately and in parallel, but **do not draw or deploy the full committed capital at closing**. Release capital against commercial, operating, and cash-flow milestones.

The company should not match the rival’s financing simply for headline parity. Acceleration is justified only where its expected market value exceeds financing cost and probability-weighted downside risk.

---

## Recommended indicative structure

For the capital required over the next 18–24 months:

| Source | Indicative share | Primary uses |
|---|---:|---|
| Retained cash and operating cash flow | 20–30% | Deposits, initial hires, implementation, short-term working capital |
| Senior debt, equipment finance, or receivables facility | 35–45% | Equipment, facilities, inventory, eligible receivables |
| Minority preferred equity | 20–30% | Market expansion, product, senior management, systems, liquidity reserve |
| Convertible or revenue-based capital | 0–15% | Short bridge or contingency only |

These are planning ranges, not commitments. Final amounts must be determined by a **24-month monthly cash-flow model** incorporating revenue conversion, collections, margins, working capital, hiring, capex, debt service, taxes, existing obligations, and downside scenarios.

### Equity parameters

Target an initial issuance of approximately **12–15% of post-money fully diluted equity**. Treat **15–18% as the maximum negotiation range**, absent exceptional strategic value.

A 15% post-money issuance would reduce the founders’ 78% position to approximately 66.3%, before any additional dilution:

\[
78\% \times 85\% = 66.3\%
\]

The actual result must include options, warrants, convertibles, accrued dividends or interest, anti-dilution adjustments, and any option-pool increase.

# Strategic rationale and alternatives

## Senior debt

**Best for:** equipment, facilities, inventory, eligible receivables, and capacity supported by contracted or highly visible demand.

**Advantages**

- Preserves ownership and voting control;
- Typically has a lower explicit cost than equity;
- Avoids selling ownership at a weak valuation; and
- Can be matched to the useful life of financed assets.

**Trade-offs**

- Principal and interest begin before the expansion is fully productive;
- Growth-related working capital can create liquidity pressure despite rising EBITDA;
- Covenants may restrict hiring, acquisitions, distributions, borrowing, or future equity; and
- A delayed ramp or margin decline can create default or refinancing risk.

**Parameters to seek**

- Approximately **1.5–2.0x maximum net leverage**, measured against sustainable trailing or normalized EBITDA—not projected post-expansion EBITDA;
- Base-case cash DSCR of at least **1.5x**, with meaningful covenant headroom;
- At least 12 months of liquidity in the downside case after committed spending;
- Delayed draws and an undrawn revolver;
- Interest-only or sculpted amortization through the ramp;
- Debt maturity at least 12–18 months beyond expected free-cash-flow stabilization;
- Limited mandatory cash sweeps;
- No short bullet maturity; and
- Fixed-rate or capped floating-rate exposure where practical.

Cash DSCR should be calculated after cash taxes, maintenance capex, working-capital investment, interest, and scheduled principal—not from EBITDA alone.

## Minority preferred equity

**Best for:** market expansion, product development, senior hires, sales and implementation capacity, systems, and liquidity reserves.

**Advantages**

- No mandatory repayment;
- Absorbs execution and ramp risk;
- Protects the balance sheet;
- Supports faster expansion than retained cash alone; and
- May provide recruiting, distribution, customer, or industry expertise.

**Trade-offs**

- Dilutes the founders;
- May introduce board and veto rights;
- Low valuation transfers significant future upside;
- Preferences and warrants can create hidden economic leakage; and
- Investor return expectations may create pressure for a sale.

**Preferred terms to seek**

- 1x **non-participating** liquidation preference;
- No multiple or participating preference;
- No cumulative or compounding dividend, or only a modest non-compounding dividend if unavoidable;
- No mandatory redemption before durable free cash flow;
- Broad-based weighted-average anti-dilution only;
- No full-ratchet protection;
- One investor board seat or observer right;
- Founder board control while founders remain above an agreed ownership threshold;
- Narrow protective provisions with thresholds and sunset rights; and
- No strategic exclusivity.

## Hybrid instruments

**Best for:** a short timing gap where a priced equity round cannot close before the competitive deadline.

**Advantages**

- Can close faster than a priced round;
- Defers valuation;
- May bridge a short market window; and
- Can reduce immediate dilution.

**Trade-offs**

- Can combine debt repayment with equity dilution;
- Discounts, caps, warrants, and accrued interest can produce significant hidden dilution;
- Revenue-based repayment drains cash during expansion; and
- Maturity or redemption rights can create a financing cliff.

**Parameters to seek**

- Conversion discount no greater than approximately 15–20%;
- Valuation cap not materially below the expected priced-round range;
- No participating preference;
- No mandatory redemption before durable free cash flow;
- Aggregate potential ownership, including warrants, capped at approximately 10–15% and coordinated with the overall dilution ceiling;
- Company-controlled maturity extensions;
- No cross-default accelerating senior debt; and
- No restriction on a later priced equity financing.

## Disciplined organic growth

**Best for:** the fallback if financing terms fail or the competitive threat proves less significant than expected.

**Advantages**

- Preserves full ownership;
- Avoids financing cost and investor governance;
- Reduces execution risk; and
- Forces prioritization of high-return initiatives.

**Trade-offs**

- The rival may secure customers, talent, suppliers, or channels;
- Capacity constraints may cause lost sales and dissatisfaction;
- Future acquisition costs may rise; and
- Slower growth may weaken valuation and negotiating leverage.

Organic growth is too conservative if capacity constraints are causing measurable customer or channel displacement. It is preferable to an unacceptable financing structure.

# Staged deployment plan

## Stage 0: Prepare and validate — immediately to month 3

Use retained cash for:

- Deposits on modular or high-return equipment;
- Facility preparation;
- A limited number of critical hires;
- Customer implementation for qualified demand;
- Priority-segment sales activity; and
- Systems to measure utilization, backlog, gross margin, churn, collections, and cash conversion.

Do not reduce cash below the greater of:

- Six months of fixed cash operating costs and scheduled debt service; or
- The peak modeled downside cash deficit plus contingency.

During this stage:

- Launch lender discussions;
- Launch the preferred-equity process;
- Seek customer deposits, prepayments, supplier terms, leasing, grants, and other non-dilutive sources;
- Confirm the true bottleneck; and
- Establish the competitive-window timetable.

## Stage 1: Secure commitments and initial capital — months 1–6

Secure:

1. A committed delayed-draw senior facility;
2. Equipment or lease financing;
3. A receivables or working-capital facility where appropriate; and
4. A minority preferred-equity tranche.

A small equity tranche may close before full operating proof if it provides acceptable pricing, founder control, and sufficient capital to reach the next milestone. Do not wait for perfect proof before beginning or completing the process.

## Stage 2: Deploy validated capacity — months 3–9

Draw debt in tranches against:

- Equipment purchase orders;
- Installation or facility milestones;
- Eligible receivables;
- Inventory tied to contracted demand; or
- Other identifiable uses with repayment capacity.

Higher-risk early activity should be funded primarily with cash and equity. Debt should not finance speculative expansion merely because the facility is committed.

## Stage 3: Accelerate, pause, or preserve — months 9–18

At each formal review:

- **Accelerate** if utilization, backlog, margins, collections, and liquidity meet plan;
- **Pause** if demand is below plan but liquidity remains strong; or
- **Preserve** cash, reduce commitments, and focus on high-return activity if stop-loss triggers are reached.

# Uses of capital

Subject to the monthly model:

| Use | Indicative allocation |
|---|---:|
| Equipment, facilities, and capacity | 35–40% |
| Inventory, implementation, and working capital | 20–25% |
| Revenue-generating hiring and sales capacity | 20–25% |
| Technology, product, and operating systems | 10–15% |
| Liquidity reserve | Greater of the modeled downside requirement or an agreed minimum |

Match source to use:

- **Cash:** deposits, initial hires, implementation, and short-payback initiatives;
- **Debt:** equipment, facilities, inventory, and eligible receivables;
- **Equity:** market expansion, product development, senior management, systems, and reserves;
- **Hybrid:** temporary timing gaps only.

Also assess customer prepayments, supplier financing, purchase-order financing, leasing, selective receivables financing, grants, strategic partnerships without exclusivity, outsourcing, and contract capacity.

# Milestones and release conditions

## Commercial

Before a major subsequent draw:

- 70–80% of the relevant **irreversible** capacity is supported by signed contracts, purchase orders, deposits, or highly credible recurring demand;
- Backlog covers at least six months of planned capacity;
- Demand meets minimum contribution-margin and collection standards;
- Customer concentration remains within approved limits;
- Churn, satisfaction, and collections remain within plan; and
- Priority segments, territories, or channels show measurable traction.

A lower threshold may be appropriate for modular, resalable, or easily redeployed capacity.

## Operating

- New capacity reaches approximately 60% utilization within six months;
- Utilization reaches approximately 75–85% within 12 months, adjusted for the business model;
- Gross margin remains within three percentage points of plan;
- Delivery, quality, and support metrics remain on target;
- Key hires are completed on schedule;
- Time to productivity remains within assumptions; and
- No material bottleneck has shifted to implementation, quality, support, or management capacity.

## Financial

- Revenue growth supports the investment case;
- Incremental gross profit covers incremental operating costs within the approved 12–18-month payback period;
- Customer-acquisition payback remains within approximately 12–18 months, where relevant;
- Receivables aging remains within plan;
- Base-case cash DSCR remains above 1.5x;
- Minimum cash remains above the required reserve; and
- The company does not need emergency financing to complete the original plan.

Each milestone should have a named owner, data source, review frequency, reviewer, cure period, and specified consequence.

## Stop-loss actions

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Utilization below 50–60% by the agreed date | Stop irreversible capacity spending; use modular or outsourced capacity |
| Gross margin more than five points below plan | Reprice, renegotiate scope, or suspend low-margin activity |
| Backlog below six months | Reduce procurement, hiring, and fixed commitments |
| Cash DSCR below 1.25x | Freeze nonessential hiring; seek waiver or equity early |
| Receivables materially behind plan | Tighten billing, credit, deposits, and collections |
| Major customer cancellation | Reforecast immediately and test covenant and borrowing-base effects |
| Critical hires more than 90 days late | Use contractors, revise the plan, or add operating leadership |
| Liquidity below nine months | Begin contingency financing |
| Liquidity below 12 months in the downside case | Do not commit further expansion capital |
| Second financing required to complete the original plan | Pause expansion and reassess the structure |

# Founder-control protections

The founders should retain:

- Majority voting power after all options, warrants, and conversions;
- Majority board appointment rights while above an agreed ownership threshold;
- Control of ordinary-course hiring, pricing, product, customers, and capital allocation within the approved plan;
- Consent over a sale, merger, change of control, senior securities, or changes to founder voting rights; and
- The ability to complete a bona fide rescue financing if required to preserve solvency.

Investors may reasonably receive:

- One board seat or observer right;
- Quarterly information rights;
- Standard minority protections;
- Participation rights in future financings;
- Consent over actions materially prejudicing their securities; and
- Broad-based weighted-average anti-dilution.

Avoid:

- Investor board control or casting votes;
- Operating vetoes;
- Participating or multiple preferences;
- Full-ratchet anti-dilution;
- Large option pools unrelated to the hiring plan;
- Early redemption rights;
- Founder personal guarantees without strict limits and burn-off;
- Strategic exclusivity; and
- Confidential-information access by competitors or portfolio companies.

Any drag-along should provide equal treatment, several—not joint and several—founder liability, limited founder representations, and release of founder guarantees.

# Negotiation positions and walk-away conditions

## Senior lenders

**Priority positions**

- Delayed draws;
- Interest-only or sculpted amortization;
- Adequate covenant headroom;
- Long-dated maturity and company-controlled extensions;
- Limited collateral appropriate to financed assets or eligible receivables;
- Cure periods for technical breaches;
- Narrow cross-defaults; and
- Permitted future equity financing and ordinary operations.

**Walk away, materially reduce, or reprice if:**

- Unlimited founder personal guarantees are required;
- Debt sizing assumes projected full utilization;
- Debt service fails the downside liquidity test;
- A short bullet maturity creates a refinancing cliff;
- Covenants have insufficient headroom;
- Technical or reporting breaches trigger immediate acceleration;
- Mandatory cash sweeps prevent essential reinvestment;
- The lender can veto ordinary hiring, pricing, product, or customer decisions;
- Restrictions block a reasonable equity financing;
- Cross-defaults broadly capture junior capital; or
- Borrowing-base availability can be reduced unilaterally in a way that creates sudden liquidity risk.

A capped, temporary guarantee may be considered only as a negotiated fallback after personal legal and financial advice, with burn-off upon repayment or leverage milestones.

## Minority equity investors

**Priority positions**

- 12–15% target initial dilution;
- Founder-controlled board;
- 1x non-participating preference;
- No early redemption;
- Broad-based weighted-average anti-dilution;
- Narrow protective provisions with thresholds and sunset rights;
- No operating vetoes; and
- No strategic exclusivity.

**Walk away or materially renegotiate if:**

- Initial dilution exceeds 15–18% without exceptional strategic value;
- The investor requires a participating or multiple preference;
- Full-ratchet anti-dilution is required;
- Dividends are large, cumulative, or compounding;
- Redemption is possible before durable free cash flow;
- The investor obtains board control or unilateral director rights;
- Ordinary operations require investor approval;
- A forced sale conflicts with founder objectives;
- Exclusivity restricts customers, partners, financing, or future strategic options;
- Investor consent is required for a bona fide rescue financing;
- Competitor access to confidential information is permitted; or
- Total initial economic leakage exceeds approximately 20–25% after dilution, preferences, warrants, option-pool effects, and other priority claims.

Reject an investor whose return expectations depend on a near-term sale inconsistent with the founders’ objectives.

## Hybrid providers

**Priority positions**

- Limited amount and defined purpose;
- Long-dated maturity;
- Capped dilution;
- No senior or participating preference;
- No cross-default to senior debt; and
- Clear conversion or refinancing path.

**Walk away if:**

- High cash coupon is combined with mandatory amortization;
- The valuation cap is low and the conversion discount excessive;
- Warrants are substantial or uncapped;
- Mandatory redemption is required;
- Conversion can occur at a distressed valuation;
- Maturity coincides with senior debt;
- Revenue repayment drains operating liquidity;
- Cross-defaults can accelerate bank debt; or
- The instrument blocks a later priced financing.

## Overall accelerated plan

Do not proceed with the full plan if:

- The downside case preserves less than 12 months of liquidity;
- Debt service depends on aggressive utilization or collections;
- Emergency financing is needed to complete the original plan;
- Funding cannot close before the relevant competitive deadline;
- Management lacks execution capacity;
- Required permits, suppliers, insurance, or customer commitments are unavailable;
- The rival’s activity does not create measurable displacement; or
- Founder voting or board control falls below the agreed threshold.

# Key risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Delayed capacity ramp | Tranche debt; use modular or outsourced capacity; preserve reserve |
| Working-capital squeeze | Monthly cash model; deposits and milestone billing; receivables facility |
| Covenant breach | Cash-based covenants; headroom; cure rights; missed milestones pause draws rather than accelerate debt |
| Refinancing failure | Long maturities; company-controlled extensions; no debt/redemption maturity wall |
| Excessive dilution | Fully diluted cap table; aggregate dilution and leakage caps; exit waterfall |
| Loss of founder control | Founder board majority; narrow protective provisions; sunset rights |
| Investor exit pressure | Align return expectations and holding period before signing |
| Customer concentration | Approved concentration limits; monitor backlog and collections |
| Management execution strain | Role-level hiring economics; operating leadership plan; staged hiring |
| Competitive delay | Quantified cost-of-delay analysis and financing timetable |

# Assumptions versus conclusions

## Confirmed facts

- The company is profitable;
- Revenue has doubled every two years;
- Demand exceeds current capacity;
- The company generates cash but cannot fund all initiatives simultaneously;
- A rival is raising significant capital;
- Founders own 78% of the equity; and
- Founders prioritize independence.

## Assumptions requiring validation

- Demand is profitable, collectible, and sufficiently recurring or contracted to support debt;
- Equipment or receivables provide usable collateral;
- The rival’s fundraising will create measurable customer, talent, or channel displacement;
- The company can recruit and manage the required personnel;
- Debt and equity can be raised within the competitive window;
- Investors will accept founder-controlled governance;
- The existing cap table and corporate documents permit the proposed financing; and
- The expansion can reach cash-flow stabilization within the modeled period.

## Conclusions

- Purely organic growth is likely too slow if competitive displacement is real.
- Debt should fund identifiable assets and working capital, not the entire expansion.
- Minority preferred equity is justified for higher-risk expansion and liquidity protection.
- Hybrids should remain temporary and tightly capped.
- The plan should proceed only after the downside case and fully diluted control analysis are acceptable.

# Implementation sequence

1. **Build the model:** Complete the 24-month monthly cash-flow, debt-service, liquidity, dilution, and exit-waterfall analyses.
2. **Quantify the opportunity:** Establish the competitive timetable and cost of delay by initiative.
3. **Prioritize uses:** Separate reversible from irreversible capacity, and assign each use to cash, debt, equity, or hybrid capital.
4. **Launch financing processes:** Approach lenders, equipment financiers, receivables providers, and preferred-equity investors in parallel.
5. **Set negotiation parameters:** Approve absolute walk-aways, tradable terms, and minimum acceptable economics.
6. **Secure initial capital:** Close only enough equity and debt to reach the next milestones, retaining the required reserve.
7. **Deploy in tranches:** Release capital against commercial, operating, financial, and cash-conversion evidence.
8. **Review formally:** At each milestone, accelerate, pause, or preserve cash.
9. **Initiate contingency action early:** Begin financing or restructuring discussions before liquidity becomes distressed.

# Diligence checklist

## Financial and commercial

- Revenue by customer, product, geography, and channel;
- Gross margin, contribution margin, and EBITDA-to-cash conversion;
- Customer concentration, churn, renewals, backlog, and contract quality;
- Payment terms, deposits, cancellations, and collection history;
- Capacity utilization and the actual operating bottleneck;
- Equipment life, resale value, maintenance, and installation timing;
- Inventory and working-capital requirements;
- Hiring plan, loaded costs, and time to productivity;
- Existing debt, leases, guarantees, liens, and contingent liabilities;
- Taxes and maintenance capex;
- 24-month monthly cash-flow model;
- Base, upside, and downside scenarios; and
- Quantified cost-of-delay analysis.

## Capitalization and governance

- Fully diluted cap table;
- Options, warrants, convertibles, and side letters;
- Option-pool requirements and pre-/post-money treatment;
- Shareholder and voting agreements;
- Board appointment and removal rights;
- Founder employment and transfer arrangements;
- Drag, tag, ROFR, and ROFO rights;
- Management incentive plans;
- Related-party transactions; and
- Investor conflicts or competitor exposure.

## Legal, tax, accounting, and insolvency

Qualified advisers should review:

- Share issuance authority and pre-emption rights;
- Securities-law compliance;
- Preferred conversion, redemption, and liquidation mechanics;
- Debt-equity characterization;
- Interest deductibility and withholding taxes;
- Thin-capitalization and interest limitations;
- Accounting classification and embedded derivatives;
- Lien scope and release mechanics;
- Intercreditor priority;
- Solvency and director-duty implications;
- Founder guarantee exposure;
- Enforceability of drag, conversion, and redemption provisions; and
- Confidentiality and information-sharing restrictions.

## Final decision rule

Proceed with the accelerated plan only if:

1. The downside case preserves at least 12 months of liquidity;
2. Debt remains serviceable after working capital, taxes, maintenance capex, and delayed utilization;
3. Initial dilution and aggregate economic leakage remain acceptable;
4. Founders retain majority voting and effective board control;
5. The financing closes before the competitive deadline;
6. The first tranche is sufficient to reach the next milestone; and
7. No emergency financing is required to complete the original plan.

If any condition fails, pursue a narrower organic-growth strategy focused on the most profitable and defensible segments, using modular, outsourced, customer-funded, or otherwise reversible capacity wherever possible.
