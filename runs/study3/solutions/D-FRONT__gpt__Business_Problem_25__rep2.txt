# Board Decision Brief: Customer-Safe Transition to Subscription Revenue

## 1. Decision requested

Approve a **36–48-month, customer-choice transition** and fund readiness work plus a controlled pilot.

Do **not** yet approve:

- broad rollout;
- final pricing or conversion-credit policy;
- product-specific retirement of new perpetual sales;
- external revenue or ARR guidance.

Those decisions require validated pilot, financial, legal, accounting, and operational evidence.

### Strategic objective

Replace lumpy perpetual-license revenue with durable recurring gross profit while preserving customer trust, protecting liquidity, and avoiding the competitor’s reported 20% installed-base loss.

Success will be measured by **retained ARR and gross profit, customer lifetime value, cash generation, and customer retention—not subscription mix alone**.

---

## 2. Preferred transition and alternatives

| Option | Assessment | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer-choice hybrid transition over 36–48 months** | Preserves rights, limits churn, allows product and sales readiness, but creates a temporary dual-model cost and revenue trough | **Preferred** |
| Abrupt conversion to subscription | Fastest recurring-revenue shift, but high risk of customer attrition, legal disputes, and severe cash disruption; competitor lost approximately 20% of installed base | Reject |
| Perpetual indefinitely with optional subscription | Lowest immediate customer risk, but preserves revenue volatility and may underfund recurring product investment | Contingency if pilot retention fails |
| Delayed transition | Reduces near-term disruption but prolongs dual-model costs and delays ARR recovery | Use only if product readiness or retention requires it |

---

## 3. Commercial model

### A. Preserve existing customer rights

Existing customers retain, subject to their contracts:

- purchased perpetual licenses and versions;
- the right to use those versions indefinitely, subject to ordinary breach remedies;
- license keys, installation, backup, and disaster-recovery rights;
- prepaid support and maintenance through the paid period;
- surviving contractual rights.

Subscription will not be a condition of continued use of a purchased perpetual license.

A standard rights schedule will define:

- activation and download continuity;
- rehosting, virtualization, cloud deployment, and affiliate-transfer rights;
- supported operating systems and dependencies;
- data export;
- security and end-of-support policy;
- product-discontinuation remedies;
- fallback rights after subscription cancellation.

### B. Maintain three offers

| Offer | Positioning and contents |
|---|---|
| **Perpetual license / legacy support** | Existing rights, defined maintenance and security policy, continued access to purchased software |
| **Subscription Standard** | Current version, continuous upgrades, security, standard support, and standard hosted/cloud capabilities |
| **Subscription Premium** | Standard plus advanced analytics, automation/AI, integrations, premium support, expanded capacity, migration, and success services |

### C. Customer-safe conversion bridge

For existing license holders:

- identified perpetual fallback version after cancellation;
- license keys, documentation, APIs, and data-export procedures;
- prepaid maintenance honored or credited;
- standard migration services and training;
- 60–90-day overlap where appropriate;
- defined renewal, cancellation, portability, and price-increase terms.

No broad migration will occur until fallback procedures have been tested with representative customers.

### D. Non-migrator policy

Customers that do not migrate will retain a viable path, including:

- published maintenance availability and pricing;
- version-specific security and support commitments;
- continued access to purchased software and license keys;
- disclosed compatibility boundaries;
- ability to migrate later;
- advance notice of material support or product changes.

The company must define the minimum maintenance period, maximum annual increase, reinstatement rules, security coverage, and end-of-support notice by product.

## 4. Segmented migration strategy

| Segment | Migration path | Primary protections | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic enterprise | 12–24-month phased migration | Executive sponsor, price protection, portability, migration services, fallback | CRO + executive sponsor |
| Mid-market at renewal | Standard conversion at renewal | Three-year price protection, term discount, conversion credit | Account Executive + Customer Success |
| Small/low-complexity | Low-touch or self-service | Simple packaging and standardized onboarding | Inside Sales + Customer Success |
| Product-ready advocates | Early-adopter pilot | Enhanced support, price protection, roadmap access | Product + Customer Success |
| Product-not-ready | Continue perpetual/maintenance temporarily | Published roadmap and support commitment | Product + Support |
| At-risk or dissatisfied | Retention-first plan | No pressure deadline, technical remediation, executive intervention | Chief Customer Officer |
| Contractually constrained | Individual plan | Legal and commercial bridge | Legal + Account Executive |

Prioritize product-ready, low-friction accounts rather than automatically migrating the largest customers first.

## 5. Pricing and migration incentives

### Initial pilot hypotheses

| Segment | Subscription price test versus current maintenance |
|---|---:|
| Small/low complexity | 1.0–1.2x |
| Mid-market | 1.1–1.4x |
| Enterprise/high usage | Value-based pricing |
| New customers | Standard subscription list price |

These are **pilot test points, not approved prices**.

Subscription ACV should reflect:

\[
\text{Maintenance value}
+
\text{Included upgrade value}
+
\text{Cloud/service value}
+
\text{New capability value}
-
\text{Credits and incentives}
\]

### Proposed migration incentives

- 15% credit on eligible maintenance, support, and upgrade payments from the prior 24 months;
- credit capped at 25% of first-year subscription fees;
- 5–10% discount for a three-year commitment;
- 3–5% annual price-increase cap during the initial term;
- standard migration and training services;
- usage or user ramp;
- 60–90-day overlap where needed.

\[
\text{Conversion credit}
=
\min[
\text{eligible prior-24-month payments}\times15\%,
\text{first-year subscription fee}\times25\%
]
\]

Finance must quantify aggregate credit exposure before final approval.

### Pricing controls

- Establish segment-specific realized-price floors.
- Require deal-desk approval for discounts above 10%.
- Prohibit unapproved stacking of discounts, credits, free periods, waived implementation, and price protection.
- Provide one-, three-, and five-year total-cost comparisons to customers.
- Approve exceptions only where lifetime contribution remains positive or the CEO authorizes a documented strategic investment.

## 6. Financial plan and scenarios

The current figures are **illustrative planning cases, not forecasts**. Finance must replace them with a monthly, five-year customer/cohort model reconciled to the CRM and general ledger.

### Required model drivers

- perpetual-license and upgrade revenue;
- maintenance renewal and lapse;
- migration timing and capacity;
- subscription pricing, credits, and discounts;
- new subscription sales;
- churn, contraction, and expansion;
- implementation, support, infrastructure, and customer-success costs;
- commissions;
- billing, collections, and deferred revenue;
- operating expenses and product investment.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Base | $91M | $84M | $83M | $87M | $94M | Material trough; recovery beyond Year 5 in this illustration |
| Delayed adoption | $— | $— | $— | $— | $— | Must be populated from capacity-constrained model |
| Combined downside | $77M | $56M | $48M | $48M | $51M | Stress case: delayed adoption, 20% loss, low price, weak renewals |
| Upside | $— | $— | $— | $— | $— | Must be populated from validated model |

The incomplete cells are intentional: upside and delayed-adoption outputs should not be invented without underlying drivers.

### Required board outputs before broad rollout

For each scenario:

- annual recognized revenue;
- ending ARR;
- gross profit;
- billings and cash collections;
- maximum cumulative FCF deficit;
- minimum unrestricted cash;
- external funding requirement;
- ARR and logo retention;
- ARR and recognized-revenue recovery year;
- LTV/CAC and payback by segment.

Use separate definitions:

\[
\text{Gross liquidity requirement}
=
\text{Maximum cumulative cash deficit}
+
\text{Minimum operating reserve}
+
\text{Contingency}
\]

\[
\text{Net external funding requirement}
=
\text{Gross liquidity requirement}
-
\text{Available cash}
-
\text{Committed financing}
\]

The combined downside must be financeable before scale approval.

## 7. Sales compensation redesign

### Compensation structure

| Component | Share of variable pay |
|---|---:|
| Realized first-year ARR | 60–70% |
| Price realization / gross-margin quality | 15–20% |
| Implementation, collection, or 90-day adoption quality | 10–15% |
| Approved term, expansion, or strategic mix | 5–10% |

### Controls

- Commissionable ARR excludes taxes, pass-through hosting, services, conversion credits, free periods, unapproved discounts, nonrenewable pilots, and uncollected or disputed amounts.
- Hold back a quality component until implementation acceptance and first payment collection or an approved adoption milestone.
- Apply clawbacks for early cancellation, material nonpayment, or misrepresentation.
- Treat three-year agreements with annual opt-outs as one-year commitments for accelerator purposes.
- Require an entitlement and customer-economics checklist for every migrated deal.

For 12–24 months:

- use blended perpetual/subscription quotas;
- apply an ARR multiplier to subscription;
- provide quota relief for converted maintenance;
- provide ramp guarantees and retraining;
- reduce perpetual commissions gradually.

Customer Success owns renewals, with shared credit for material sales involvement.

## 9. Rollout roadmap and decision gates

### Phase 0 — Readiness: Months 0–3

Complete entitlement cleansing, customer segmentation, legal policy, financial model, offer design, compensation, billing, accounting, product readiness, staffing, and communications.

**Gate 1:** Pilot records, contracts, fallback rights, billing, support capacity, and implementation capacity are validated.

### Phase 1 — Pilot: Months 4–9

Test pricing, terms, credits, implementation, customer satisfaction, retention, collections, and sales productivity across representative segments.

**Gate 2:** Two consecutive quarters meet validated pilot thresholds.

### Phase 2 — Segment expansion: Months 10–18

Make subscription the default for new customers and expand only to product-ready segments. Restrict new perpetual sales only where evidence supports the decision.

### Phase 3 — Broad transition: Months 19–36

Migrate suitable customers at renewal, standardize onboarding and success, and reduce legacy costs only as demand permits.

### Phase 4 — Optimization: Months 37–48

Optimize retention, expansion, gross margin, support cost, and product-specific commercial policies while maintaining the non-migrator policy.

## 10. KPI dashboard and provisional triggers

All thresholds below are **internal starting points**, not external guidance. Each must be calibrated against historical baselines, cohort size, and measurement period.

### Customer and commercial metrics

- migration conversion by ARR and logo;
- migration-related ARR churn;
- total GRR and strategic-account GRR;
- NRR;
- realized price and discount leakage;
- implementation time and backlog;
- customer satisfaction and time to value;
- annual upfront billing;
- sales productivity;
- LTV/CAC and payback.

### Starting thresholds

| Metric | Pilot | Scale / pause reference |
|---|---:|---:|
| Eligible ARR conversion | ≥30% | — |
| Migration-related ARR churn | <8% | Pause above 12% quarterly |
| GRR | ≥92% | Pause below 88%; scale target 90–92% |
| Strategic-account GRR | — | ≥95% |
| NRR | ≥98% during transition | 100% by Year 2; 105% internal aspiration by Year 3 |
| Sales productivity | Within 20% of baseline | Pause if below for two quarters |
| Implementation backlog | Within capacity | Pause above capacity for two months |
| Realized price | Within corridor | Pause if >15% below plan |
| Installed-base loss | — | Pause at 15%; emergency response near 20% |
| LTV/CAC | — | ≥3.0x at steady state |

Metrics should use minimum cohort thresholds and rolling measurement periods to avoid unstable decisions from small samples.

### Trigger-action matrix

| Trigger | Action | Owner | Timing |
|---|---|---|---:|
| ARR churn exceeds threshold | Pause affected segment; executive save plan; review offer | CCO | 30 days |
| Price falls below floor | Repackage; tighten deal desk; suspend exceptions | CFO/CRO | 15 days |
| Cash forecast breaches reserve | Slow rollout; activate financing and cost plan | CFO | 10 days |
| Implementation exceeds capacity | Limit migrations; add capacity; standardize delivery | COO/CCO | 14 days |
| Strategic-account loss | CEO-led recovery review | CEO | Immediate |
| Rights complaint substantiated | Remediate customer; correct controls; hold related commissions | General Counsel | Immediate |
| Installed-base loss approaches 20% | Suspend deadline messaging; reset guidance; board review | CEO/CFO | Immediate |

If rollout is paused, signed contracts and offers remain valid; unsigned offers may be withdrawn with written notice; signed price protection survives; customers are not penalized for a company-initiated pause.

## 11. Investor and customer communication

### Customer message

> “We are investing in a subscription model that provides continuous updates, security, support, cloud capabilities, and predictable budgeting. Your existing perpetual rights remain intact. Migration is a choice, and we will provide a clear economic comparison, migration assistance, and defined fallback rights.”

Strategic accounts receive entitlement inventories, one-, three-, and five-year comparisons, implementation and rollback plans, executive sponsorship, and written rights confirmation.

### Investor and board message

> “We are transitioning to a customer-choice subscription model while preserving existing perpetual rights. We expect a temporary revenue and cash-flow trough as upfront license revenue is replaced by recurring revenue. We will measure progress through retention, realized ARR, billings, gross profit, cash generation, and customer adoption—not subscription mix alone.”

Quarterly reporting will include:

- ARR bridge;
- recognized-revenue bridge;
- gross-profit bridge;
- billings-to-cash bridge;
- planned versus actual migration;
- planned versus actual churn;
- planned versus actual cash deficit;
- deferred revenue or remaining performance obligations.

Separate migration, price, volume, expansion, contraction, foreign exchange, acquisitions, and one-time credits.

---

## Final board resolution

Approve:

1. the 36–48-month customer-choice strategy;
2. readiness funding and a controlled pilot;
3. preservation of existing perpetual rights;
4. segmented migration design;
5. development of the offer architecture, non-migrator policy, and conversion-credit framework;
6. legal, accounting, billing, product, service, and compensation workstreams.

Defer until validated:

1. broad rollout;
2. final prices and credit exposure;
3. product-specific restrictions on new perpetual sales;
4. final compensation accelerators;
5. external financial guidance;
6. financing sufficiency for scale.

**Board success criterion:** durable recurring gross profit, adequate price realization, strong customer and ARR retention, validated fallback rights, and sufficient liquidity through the transition trough.
