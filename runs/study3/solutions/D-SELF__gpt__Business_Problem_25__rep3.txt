# Subscription Transition Plan  
## Board-, Leadership-, and Investor-Ready Recommendation

## Executive summary

### Recommendation

Approve a **36-month, gated transition** from perpetual licenses to subscription revenue. Begin design and pilot work immediately; authorize broad commercial rollout only after the company satisfies defined customer, financial, legal, product, capacity, and liquidity gates.

The transition should:

1. Stop routine perpetual-license sales to new customers at commercial launch.
2. Preserve purchased perpetual-use rights for existing customers.
3. Offer SaaS, customer-managed term subscription, hybrid modernization, and legacy-maintenance paths.
4. Price subscriptions against five-year contribution economics, including hosting, implementation, support, customer success, commissions, concessions, and expected churn.
5. Use one primary, capped migration incentive rather than stacked discounts.
6. Redesign direct and channel compensation around net, activated, collected, and retained recurring value.
7. Maintain liquidity through the planned revenue trough and a downside involving approximately 20% installed-base loss.
8. Report ARR, revenue, bookings, billings, collections, cash, retention, and cohort economics separately.
9. Slow, pause, or modify the transition if retention, contribution, implementation capacity, or liquidity gates are missed.

### Strategic objective

Create durable, predictable recurring revenue **without materially damaging the installed base or accepting structurally uneconomic subscription contracts**.

Subscription mix is an input to the strategy—not the definition of success.

### Board decision requested

Approve:

- the 36-month strategy in principle;
- Phase 0 design and readiness work;
- a controlled pilot;
- the customer-rights and offer-design principles below;
- the requirement for separate board approval before broad rollout, accelerated migration, or routine perpetual-sales sunset.

Do not issue precise external revenue or trough guidance until the integrated financial model, accounting review, financing plan, and pilot evidence are complete.

# 1. Planning assumptions and decision hierarchy

## Planning assumptions

The following are **internal planning assumptions**, not commitments:

- Current annual revenue is approximately **$100–110 million**.
- A representative legacy customer has generated:
  - $100,000 in perpetual license revenue;
  - $20,000 in annual maintenance;
  - approximately $40,000 for an upgrade every four years.
- Comparable mainstream subscription pricing may initially fall in the **$48,000–$75,000 annual range**, with lower and higher tiers by segment.
- Subscription revenue is generally recognized ratably over the service term; perpetual license revenue may be recognized earlier, subject to auditor confirmation.
- Customers vary materially in renewal timing, upgrade cycles, cloud readiness, regulatory constraints, product usage, and competitive risk.
- The company can support legacy and subscription deployments concurrently during the transition.
- Sufficient liquidity can be secured to fund the base case and a severe downside scenario.

These assumptions must be validated through customer-level analysis and the pilot.

## Decision hierarchy

When objectives conflict, management should prioritize:

1. **Preserving customer rights and avoiding material installed-base loss.**
2. **Maintaining liquidity above the approved downside threshold.**
3. **Achieving acceptable five-year contribution economics.**
4. **Increasing live, paying subscription adoption.**
5. **Reducing the recognized-revenue trough where the first four objectives are not compromised.**

Strong conversion volume must not override weak retention, poor product readiness, excessive discounting, inadequate implementation capacity, or insufficient liquidity.

# 2. Offer and migration design

## 2.1 Future-state offer architecture

### A. SaaS subscription — strategic default

Includes:

- Software access;
- hosting and infrastructure;
- standard product and security updates;
- backup and disaster recovery;
- standard support;
- standard integrations;
- usage and adoption analytics;
- defined implementation services;
- subscription-only functionality released during the term.

### B. Customer-managed term subscription

For customers with data-residency, regulatory, security, or operating constraints.

Includes:

- One-, two-, or three-year software term;
- customer-managed hosting;
- updates and security releases;
- contracted modules;
- defined support levels;
- documented infrastructure requirements.

This must remain a term product, not a disguised perpetual license. It should be priced below SaaS only to reflect genuinely avoided hosting costs.

### C. Hybrid modernization

For customers not ready for full SaaS adoption.

The customer:

- retains its perpetual deployment and existing rights;
- subscribes to selected cloud services, analytics, security, integrations, collaboration, or new modules;
- receives a formal migration review after 12–24 months;
- receives only the approved hybrid concession.

Hybrid is a bridge, not an indefinite fourth business model. Standard duration should be limited to **24 months or two renewals**. Extensions require executive approval, a documented customer constraint, and an economic rationale.

### D. Legacy perpetual maintenance

Available only to existing perpetual customers.

Subject to the customer’s contract and published policy, it includes:

- continued use of purchased perpetual licenses;
- support for versions within the support period;
- contractual maintenance releases and security patches;
- optional paid upgrades where applicable.

It does not automatically include:

- subscription-only modules;
- hosted functionality;
- unlimited future upgrades;
- functionality developed exclusively for the subscription platform.

The company must publish version-support, security-support, renewal, reinstatement, and end-of-sale policies before launch.

## 2.2 Packaging and pricing

Use three editions and one primary value metric.

| Tier | Target customer | Illustrative annual price | Core inclusions |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Essentials | Smaller or less complex teams | $24,000–$36,000 | Core workflows, standard support, limited integrations |
| Professional | Mainstream customers | $48,000–$75,000 | Full core product, integrations, analytics, priority support |
| Enterprise | Complex or regulated customers | $100,000–$200,000+ | Advanced security, SSO, governance, premium support, dedicated success |

These are **illustrative figures**. The final pricing calculator must define:

- primary value metric;
- minimum annual commitment;
- included users, capacity, or transactions;
- module rules;
- deployment surcharges;
- support levels;
- implementation scope;
- overage rules;
- annual escalation;
- price-lock conditions;
- approval thresholds.

Charge separately for nonstandard implementation, data migration, premium support, unusual environments, and exceptional integrations. Apply a standard annual escalation of approximately **3–5%** after any agreed price-lock period.

Avoid pricing simultaneously by too many dimensions. Customers should be able to estimate their bill without a pricing consultant.

---

## 2.3 Existing customer rights

The company should make the following commitments, subject to legal review:

- Purchased perpetual-use rights will not be revoked.
- Subscription conversion does not require surrender of existing perpetual rights.
- A customer that declines subscription may continue using its purchased perpetual version, subject to applicable license terms and published support policy.
- Maintenance, support, upgrades, hosting, security patches, and new modules are distinct rights.
- Customers receive a defined data-export format and reasonable migration assistance.
- Material maintenance or support changes receive at least **12 months’ notice**, subject to existing contracts and applicable law.

Before customer communications begin, legal, product, support, finance, and customer success must approve an entitlement matrix covering:

- use of the purchased version;
- contractual upgrade rights;
- security patches versus functional updates;
- deployment on replacement infrastructure;
- license transfer within corporate groups;
- acquisitions, divestitures, and restructuring;
- maintenance reinstatement;
- continued use after subscription cancellation;
- third-party integrations;
- data export and post-termination access.

The company should not publish definitive “permanent rights” language until counsel confirms its precise scope and consistency with existing contracts.

## 2.4 Migration offers

### Path 1: Rights-preserving full conversion

For cloud-ready customers:

- Existing perpetual rights remain intact.
- Subscription provides hosted service, current updates, new subscription functionality, and standard support.
- Credit **25–50% of unused prepaid maintenance**, capped at **10–15% of first-year subscription ARR**.
- Credit expires after 12–18 months.
- No automatic credit for historical license payments.

### Path 2: Multi-year conversion

- Two-year term: 3–5% discount.
- Three-year term: 7–10% discount.
- Annual prepayment preferred.
- Price lock for the committed term.
- No additional first-year discount except with executive approval.

### Path 3: Hybrid modernization

- Customer retains perpetual deployment and rights.
- Subscription covers selected new capabilities.
- Credit generally limited to 5–10%.
- Migration assistance is fixed-scope and capped.
- Formal migration review occurs after 12–24 months.

### Path 4: Legacy maintenance

- Customer continues under published maintenance terms.
- Pricing reflects the higher cost of supporting the legacy environment.
- Customer receives a documented migration plan.
- No forced conversion before the published policy date.

---

## 2.5 Incentive guardrails

Customers may receive one primary economic incentive:

- maintenance credit;
- multi-year commitment discount; or
- targeted migration assistance.

All discounts, credits, free periods, waived fees, migration labor, partner-funded concessions, and price protections count toward a common cap:

\[
\text{Total concession rate}
=
\frac{\text{all economic concessions}}
{\text{gross subscription TCV}}
\]

Maximum total concession:

- **10–15% of gross subscription TCV** for standard customers;
- **20% maximum** for approved strategic or competitive-risk accounts.

Additional rules:

- No lifetime price protection.
- No free subscription period longer than 90 days without executive approval.
- Historical-license credits require measurable economic value, such as early migration, a binding multi-year commitment, or adoption of higher-value modules.
- Company and partner discounts count toward the same cap.
- First-year price realization and five-year contribution impact must be reported separately.

# 3. Perpetual-sales policy

The policy is:

- **At launch:** no routine perpetual sales to new logos.
- **Year 1:** existing-account exceptions limited to 25–35% of prior-year perpetual bookings.
- **Year 2:** exceptions limited to 10–20%.
- **Year 3:** less than 10%, only for regulatory, technical, or retention reasons.
- **Year 4:** board-approved exceptions only.

An exception is not a sales target. It requires:

- documented customer need;
- retention-risk assessment;
- migration path;
- contribution analysis;
- finance, legal, product, and customer-success approval.

# 4. Pricing and financial scenarios

## 4.1 Customer-level economic comparison

For every material customer or cohort, compare:

\[
\text{Legacy five-year contribution}
=
\text{license}
+
\text{maintenance}
+
\text{expected upgrades}
+
\text{services}
+
\text{expansion}
-
\text{legacy delivery costs}
\]

with:

\[
\text{Subscription five-year contribution}
=
\text{net subscription revenue}
+
\text{services}
+
\text{expansion}
-
\text{hosting}
-
\text{support}
-
\text{customer success}
-
\text{migration costs}
-
\text{credits}
-
\text{commissions}
\]

The model must include expected churn, contraction, expansion, implementation timing, payment timing, and renewal behavior.

### Illustrative economics

A representative legacy customer produces approximately:

\[
\$100,000 + (4 \times \$20,000) + \$40,000 = \$220,000
\]

over a four-year license and upgrade cycle.

A $45,000 annual subscription produces:

\[
4 \times \$45,000 = \$180,000
\]

before subscription delivery costs. Therefore, $45,000 cannot be assumed to be economically equivalent without evidence of superior retention, expansion, or customer value.

## 4.2 Scenario framework

| Scenario | Pricing posture | Year 1 live conversion | Conversion churn | Illustrative revenue trough | Use |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Adoption-first | 85–95% of legacy annualized value | 30–40% | 3–6% | 8–15% | High trust or competitive risk |
| **Balanced base** | **100–115% of legacy five-year contribution value** | **20–30%** | **4–8%** | **10–18%** | **Planning case** |
| Margin-protection | 115–130% of legacy annualized value | 10–20% | 6–12% | 15–25% | Strong product dependence, lower churn risk |

These are **internal planning scenarios**, not external guidance. Final prices should be set by segment-level pricing floors and pilot evidence.

# 5. Revenue, ARR, bookings, billings, and cash

## 5.1 Metric definitions

### Net live ARR

\[
\text{Net live ARR}
=
\text{active subscription ARR}
+
\text{recurring maintenance ARR}
-
\text{known churn and contraction}
\]

Exclude:

- implementation;
- perpetual license revenue;
- uncommitted pipeline;
- free periods before activation;
- uncollectible or materially disputed amounts.

Report separately:

- gross ARR before discounts;
- net ARR after discounts and credits;
- live ARR from implemented and paying customers;
- migrated ARR versus new-logo ARR;
- SaaS ARR;
- term-subscription ARR;
- legacy maintenance ARR;
- total recurring ARR.

### ARR bridge

\[
\text{Beginning ARR}
+
\text{new ARR}
+
\text{expansion ARR}
-
\text{contraction}
-
\text{churn}
=
\text{Ending ARR}
\]

### Bookings, billings, and revenue

A three-year contract with $120,000 total contract value represents:

- $120,000 subscription bookings;
- $40,000 ARR;
- $40,000 annual billings if invoiced annually;
- $120,000 billings if prepaid;
- approximately $40,000 annual recognized revenue, subject to accounting treatment.

These measures must remain separate in internal and external reporting.

## 5.2 Illustrative trough scenarios

| Case | Revenue trough | Return to growth | Management implication |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Upside | 3–7% | Year 2 | Strong conversion, retention, and prepayment |
| **Base** | **10–18%** | **Year 3** | Planned liquidity and disciplined execution required |
| Slow adoption | 15–25% | Year 3–4 | Cost actions and longer hybrid period likely |
| Competitor-like downside | 20–30% | Year 4 or later | Financing and slower migration required |

These figures are **illustrative planning ranges** until validated by the customer-level model.

Measure separately:

1. GAAP revenue trough;
2. billings trough;
3. operating cash-flow/free-cash-flow trough.

Prepayment may improve cash before recognized revenue, but it does not eliminate retention or contribution risk.

## 5.3 Liquidity policy

Planning assumptions:

- 60–75% annual prepayment;
- 15–25% of contracts prepaid multi-year;
- monthly billing only with a premium or strategic rationale;
- liquidity covering the base trough plus at least 25% contingency;
- ability to survive the 20% installed-base-loss downside;
- at least 18 months of runway after launch.

The CFO must produce a funded plan specifying:

- opening cash;
- monthly cash burn;
- minimum cash floor;
- committed facility size;
- expected draw timing;
- covenants and headroom;
- downside draw requirement;
- financing costs;
- actions if financing is unavailable.

Intervention triggers:

- free cash flow 15% below plan for two quarters;
- collections below 90% of billings;
- concessions 20% above budget;
- subscription GRR below 88%;
- runway below 15 months.

Potential actions include reducing hiring, limiting incentives, reducing discretionary platform spending, extending targeted hybrid arrangements, increasing prepayment pricing, or delaying the next phase.

# 6. Customer adoption and retention

## 6.1 Segmentation

Segment every account into:

1. Ready to convert;
2. Convertible with assistance;
3. Structurally resistant;
4. At risk because of dissatisfaction or competition.

Define:

\[
\text{Eligible ARR}
=
\text{renewing ARR with legal, technical, product, commercial, and capacity eligibility}
\]

Track the migration funnel:

\[
\text{Eligible}
\rightarrow
\text{Contacted}
\rightarrow
\text{Qualified}
\rightarrow
\text{Proposed}
\rightarrow
\text{Signed}
\rightarrow
\text{Live}
\rightarrow
\text{Paying}
\rightarrow
\text{First renewal}
\]

The primary adoption measure is **live, paying, retained ARR**, not signed intent.

## 6.2 Retention targets

| Metric | Initial/base target |
|---|---:|
| Legacy maintenance ARR retention | 86–91% |
| Subscription GRR during transition | >90% |
| Subscription GRR after stabilization | >92% |
| Conversion-related ARR churn | <5% acceptable; 5–8% intervention; >8% pause/slow |
| NRR after stabilization | 105–108% |
| Migration payback | ≤24 months |
| LTV/CAC | ≥3.0x |
| Subscription gross margin | 72–78% |

Each metric must specify its cohort, denominator, measurement period, owner, and consequence.

If conversion-related churn exceeds 5% of renewing ARR, management should extend hybrid terms for affected cohorts and prioritize product, implementation, and trust improvements over faster conversion or deeper discounts.

## 6.3 Retention actions

For each material account:

- Assign an executive sponsor.
- Conduct a migration workshop before renewal.
- Provide a rights-and-value comparison.
- Offer SaaS, term, and hybrid alternatives.
- Provide fixed-scope migration assistance.
- Provide data export and rollback capability.
- Supply security and compliance documentation.
- Reserve implementation capacity.
- Conduct 30-, 60-, and 90-day adoption reviews.
- Maintain an executive escalation process.

The competitor’s approximately 20% installed-base loss is an explicit downside case with a pre-approved retention response team.

# 7. Implementation roadmap and stage gates

## Phase 0: Design and readiness — Months 0–3

Complete:

- customer-level five-year revenue and contribution model;
- monthly 36-month and quarterly five-year forecast;
- pricing calculator and segment pricing floors;
- entitlement matrix;
- legal and contract audit;
- accounting conclusions;
- product, security, hosting, and data-export readiness;
- implementation and customer-success capacity model;
- direct and channel compensation plans;
- financing plan;
- customer, employee, partner, investor, and analyst communications;
- RACI and metric dictionary.

### Board gate

Authorize the pilot only after:

- the model reconciles ARR, revenue, bookings, billings, collections, and cash;
- liquidity is sufficient for the pilot and downside planning requirements;
- legal and accounting policies are complete;
- product and implementation readiness meet minimum thresholds.

## Phase 1: Pilot — Months 4–6

Pilot with 20–30 customers across different sizes, industries, deployment types, renewal timing, cloud readiness, and competitive risk.

### Operational gates

- Median implementation under 90 days;
- acceptable data-migration accuracy;
- successful production go-live;
- acceptable support volume;
- customer satisfaction and reference willingness;
- adequate capacity for planned rollout.

### Economic gates

- Conversion-related churn below 5%;
- effective concession below 15%;
- net price realization above 90% of plan;
- documented 24-month contribution payback;
- no materially negative customer segment economics.

Immediate positive contribution is not required where costs are front-loaded, but the payback path must be evidence-based.

## Phase 2: Controlled launch — Months 7–12

- Stop routine new-logo perpetual sales.
- Launch the migration program.
- Implement revised compensation.
- Establish a migration and renewal command center.
- Publish entitlement and support policies.
- Train partners and implementation teams.

Targets:

- 20–30% of eligible renewing ARR live and paying on subscription;
- legacy retention within the approved range;
- effective concessions within policy;
- implementation backlog within capacity;
- cash runway above the approved minimum.

## Phase 3: Scale — Year 2

- Reduce perpetual exceptions.
- Expand subscription-only new-logo selling.
- Standardize onboarding.
- Increase customer-success coverage.
- Expand modules, users, departments, and premium services.
- Reprice based on measured value and renewal behavior.

Acceleration requires:

- subscription GRR above 90%;
- cumulative live conversion above 45%;
- net price realization above 90% of plan;
- cash runway above 18 months;
- subscription gross margin at least 72%;
- adequate implementation and support capacity.

## Phase 4: Completion — Year 3

- End routine perpetual exceptions.
- Migrate remaining eligible accounts.
- Retain only documented legacy cases.
- Shift reporting toward recurring contribution margin and cohort profitability.

Target outcome:

- 65–80% cumulative live converted ARR;
- at least 70% subscription/term recurring mix excluding maintenance;
- subscription GRR above 92%;
- NRR above 105–108%;
- positive recurring contribution margin.

Installed-base conversion and total subscription mix must be reported independently.

# 8. Sales compensation redesign

## New-logo sellers

- 50% net first-year ARR;
- 20% contribution-adjusted ARR or gross-profit quality;
- 15% contract duration and payment quality;
- 15% objective activation and 90-day customer health.

## Account managers and renewal teams

- 40% retained ARR;
- 30% converted ARR;
- 20% expansion ARR;
- 10% activation and adoption.

## Activation definition

Activation requires:

- production go-live;
- first invoice paid;
- required onboarding completed;
- no material acceptance dispute;
- minimum agreed usage achieved;
- customer-success certification;
- enterprise customer acceptance where applicable.

Finance and customer success certify activation. Disputes go to the CFO and CRO.

## Commission timing and controls

- Pay 50–70% after contract signature and first invoice for standard, low-risk deals.
- Defer the balance until collection and activation.
- Pay monthly-billed commissions over time or at a reduced rate.
- Apply 90–180-day clawbacks for early cancellation, nonpayment, or failure to activate.
- Reduce commission for excessive discounts, free periods, or unprofitable implementation commitments.
- Do not pay full TCV commission at signature.
- Do not count unactivated ARR as fully successful quota credit.

Illustrative modifiers:

- 1.0x for standard economics;
- 0.8x above the discount threshold;
- 0.5x for monthly billing or elevated collection risk;
- deferred or zero commission for high-risk contracts until activation.

## Transition protections

For the first 12 months:

- Rebase quotas to achievable ARR productivity.
- Provide quota relief for mandatory migration work.
- Offer temporary draws or guarantees for critical roles.
- Use a 1.25x migration accelerator in Year 1 and 1.10x in Year 2.
- Exclude deeply discounted or low-quality deals from accelerators.
- Certify sellers in value-based subscription selling.

Guarantees should protect critical talent without preserving perpetual-selling economics indefinitely.

---

# 9. Channel strategy

Partners should receive:

- recurring commissions on renewals and expansions;
- migration bonuses for live, retained customers;
- implementation and managed-service margin;
- account-ownership protection;
- training and certification;
- deal registration and price floors;
- a time-limited transition rebate.

Company and partner discounts count toward the same concession cap. Partner bonuses should be paid on live and retained customers, not merely signed contracts.

The channel program must specify:

- account ownership during conversion;
- treatment of partner rebates in the economic model;
- partner commission after customer conversion;
- approval authority for partner exceptions;
- how channel concessions are captured in the quoting system.

## Core message

> The company is accepting a controlled near-term decline in recognized revenue as upfront perpetual transactions are replaced by ratable subscription revenue. Existing customer rights are protected, and the program will be judged by net live ARR, retention, contribution margin, cash consumption, and customer adoption—not by subscription mix or signed contract volume alone. Billings and cash may recover ahead of recognized revenue through annual and multi-year contracts, but prepayment will not be treated as proof of durable growth until customers are activated and retained.

## Guidance-revision protocol

Management must notify the board and reassess public guidance after two consecutive quarters with any of the following:

- revenue more than 10% below plan;
- live conversion more than 15 percentage points below plan;
- conversion-related churn above 8%;
- subscription GRR below 90%;
- net price realization below 90% of plan;
- concessions above policy;
- free cash flow more than 15% below plan;
- runway below 15 months.

The default response to a missed gate is to slow migration, improve product or implementation, extend targeted hybrid terms, or revise packaging. Deeper discounting is not the default remedy.

Do not issue precise external trough or revenue guidance until the integrated model, accounting review, financing plan, and pilot evidence are complete.

15. Authority to proceed through the pilot, but not to issue precise external revenue guidance or scale broadly until the gates are satisfied.

## Final success test

By stabilization, the company should demonstrate:

- 65–80% cumulative live converted ARR by Year 3;
- subscription GRR above 92%;
- NRR above 105–108%;
- conversion-related churn below 5–8%;
- migration payback within 24 months;
- subscription gross margin of approximately 72–78%;
- positive recurring contribution margin;
- effective concessions within policy;
- liquidity maintained through the trough;
- no competitor-like loss of approximately 20% of the installed base.

This plan replaces an abrupt subscription cliff with a controlled migration. It protects customer rights, prices the new model rationally, rewards durable commercial outcomes, preserves financing flexibility, and gives leadership and the board clear authority to accelerate, pause, or redesign the transition as evidence develops.
