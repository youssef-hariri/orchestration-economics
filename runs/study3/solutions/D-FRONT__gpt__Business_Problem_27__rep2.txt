# Board Memorandum: Staged Growth-Financing Strategy

## Recommendation

Approve a **provisional, staged measured-acceleration plan**, subject to completion of the financial, operating, and financing diligence described below.

The recommended structure for the **first defined operating phase** is:

- **20–30% retained cash**, while preserving the board-approved minimum liquidity reserve;
- **30–40% senior or delayed-draw debt** for visible, recoverable capacity and working-capital needs;
- **30–40% minority preferred equity or a simple hybrid** for higher-uncertainty hiring, market development, and expansion initiatives;
- A **committed but undrawn second tranche**, available only after agreed operating milestones are achieved.

These percentages apply to the first operating phase—not the full multi-year opportunity—and must be reconciled to an approved dollar budget before execution.

This structure is recommended because it:

- Addresses the immediate capacity constraint before the rival can establish durable customer, talent, or channel advantages;
- Preserves more founder ownership than a large equity round;
- Avoids placing all execution risk on fixed debt service;
- Maintains a genuine organic-growth fallback; and
- Ensures the second tranche is an option, not a requirement for solvency or debt service.

Debt should fund predictable cash generation. Equity-like capital should fund uncertainty. The company should not finance the full opportunity with debt or sell a large equity stake solely because the current valuation is unattractive.

---

## Approval conditions

Final approval should be conditional on management delivering:

1. A defined first phase, including budget, duration, projects, expected capacity added, and strategic objective.
2. A 24–36-month monthly model covering base, upside, downside, and severe-downside cases.
3. Debt sizing based on existing-business stressed cash flow, not post-expansion EBITDA.
4. A fully diluted ownership analysis, including option-pool changes, conversion, warrants, and future financing.
5. A financing execution plan with 30-, 60-, and 90-day actions.
6. Final term sheets that satisfy the walk-away conditions below.

The current recommendation is therefore a **capital architecture and negotiating mandate**, not unconditional authorization to draw capital.

---

## Strategic choice

| Option | Benefits | Principal risks | Appropriate role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Senior debt** | Preserves ownership; generally lower explicit cost; can close quickly | Fixed repayments, covenants, collateral, refinancing risk | Equipment, facilities, inventory, receivables, and contract-backed hiring |
| **Minority equity** | No mandatory repayment; strongest liquidity protection; funds uncertainty | Permanent dilution; valuation risk; governance influence | Market development, discretionary hiring, new channels, geographies, and product investment |
| **Hybrid / preferred** | Can defer valuation and stage dilution; potentially flexible | May combine debt-like repayment with equity dilution, seniority, and complexity | Use only where flexibility is genuine and downside is bounded |
| **Disciplined organic growth** | Maximum control and resilience; no financing cost | Lost share, slower learning, higher future acquisition costs | Base-case fallback and funding source for modular, high-confidence expansion |

The rival’s fundraising is not, by itself, sufficient reason to overfinance. Acceleration should target identifiable customers, channels, talent, supplier capacity, or other strategic positions at risk from delay.

---

## Proposed use of proceeds

| Use | Preferred source | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment, facilities, and tooling | Senior debt or equipment finance | Measurable payback and recoverable assets |
| Contract-backed hiring | Debt or delayed-draw debt | Demand visibility supports fixed obligations |
| Inventory and receivables | Revolver, borrowing-base facility, or supplier terms | Matches financing to the conversion cycle |
| Hiring ahead of demand | Minority equity or preferred equity | Avoids fixed repayment burden |
| New geography or channel | Equity, hybrid, or retained cash | Higher execution and timing risk |
| Product development | Retained cash or equity-like capital | Longer and less certain payback |
| Marketing and customer acquisition | Equity or retained cash; revenue-based financing only where payback is highly predictable | Avoids debt-funded speculative spend |
| Strategic reserve | Retained cash and undrawn facility | Preserves independence and optionality |

Customer deposits, annual prepayments, milestone billing, and supplier terms should be used where commercially appropriate, but should not be assumed until validated.

---

## Stage structure

### Stage 0: Preparation

Use retained cash for demand validation, implementation planning, critical systems, selected recruiting, and financing diligence, without reducing cash below the approved minimum reserve.

### Stage 1: Verified bottleneck

Fund only the capacity and related expansion required to address the highest-confidence constraint. The first phase must remain viable if growth slows, capacity is delayed, margins compress, or a major customer defers volume.

### Stage 2: Contingent acceleration

Negotiate the second tranche at the initial closing but draw it only after operating evidence improves confidence and, potentially, the company’s valuation and bargaining position.

The second tranche may be delayed-draw debt, preferred equity, a convertible instrument, or another committed facility. Its draw conditions must be objective, enforceable, and subject to reasonable cure periods.

---

## Financial guardrails

The following are preliminary underwriting ranges, to be validated against the company’s actual cash flow, volatility, customer concentration, and lender definitions.

| Metric | Base-case target | Downside minimum | Walk-away trigger |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| EBITDA / cash interest | At least 5.0x | At least 3.0x | Below 2.5x |
| Fixed-charge coverage | At least 2.0x | At least 1.50x | Below 1.25x |
| Net leverage after initial draw | At or below 1.5x EBITDA | At or below 2.0x EBITDA | Above 2.5x without exceptional visibility |
| Covenant headroom | At least 35% | At least 20–25% | Below 15% |
| Stressed cash runway | 12 months preferred | 9 months absolute minimum | Below 9 months |
| Material refinancing maturity | No cliff within 24 months | At least 12 months to refinance | Maturity before credible cash payback |

Runway must include debt service, taxes, maintenance capital expenditure, working capital, leases, implementation costs, delayed receipts, and financing fees—not only fixed operating costs.

The first-stage financing must not require the second tranche or a refinancing to:

- Meet debt service;
- Preserve minimum cash;
- Avoid a covenant breach; or
- Continue ordinary operations.

## Milestones for the contingent tranche

The company should establish hard gates, measurement periods, tolerances, and cure rights before signing.

### Demand

- Approximately **60–70% of incremental capacity** supported by signed contracts, purchase orders, or validated recurring demand;
- Pipeline conversion at or above the underwriting case;
- Churn, renewal, and payment performance within approved ranges;
- Customer concentration within the board-approved limit;
- Competitive win rates stable or improving.

### Economics

- Gross margin no more than approximately **200–300 basis points below plan**;
- Positive incremental contribution margin after fulfillment, support, and service costs;
- Customer-acquisition payback within the approved range, preferably 12–18 months;
- No material deterioration in pricing or payment terms solely to match the rival.

### Execution

- First capacity expansion operational within the agreed timetable, normally no more than 90 days late;
- Utilization at approximately **70–80%** before the next major capacity commitment, unless demand is contractually secured;
- Critical hires filled and productive;
- Service, quality, safety, and regulatory performance within tolerance.

### Cash flow

- Minimum liquidity reserve intact;
- Free-cash-flow conversion broadly consistent with plan, or variance explained by an approved investment phase;
- Working-capital days within the agreed range;
- Existing debt service covered without the contingent tranche;
- No covenant breach or imminent refinancing requirement.

Testing should generally be quarterly. A temporary variance should not automatically create investor control or a financing default.

## Key terms and control protections

### Debt

Seek:

- Delayed drawdowns aligned with capacity installation;
- Interest-only or light amortization during the build and ramp period;
- Quarterly testing where possible;
- At least 20% downside covenant headroom;
- Equity-cure rights and reasonable cure periods;
- Permitted future equity, equipment debt, leases, and ordinary-course expansion;
- Limited collateral, avoiding a blanket lien over core intellectual property where possible;
- No founder personal guarantees except narrowly for fraud or intentional misconduct;
- No punitive prepayment or refinancing restrictions;
- No subjective withdrawal of committed undrawn capital except for serious events.

### Equity or preferred equity

Seek:

- One board seat or observer right, not control;
- Founder rights to appoint a majority of the board while founders retain majority voting control;
- Reserved matters limited to fundamental transactions;
- **1x, non-participating liquidation preference**;
- Broad-based weighted-average anti-dilution only;
- No mandatory redemption; if unavoidable, no earlier than five to seven years, payable only from legally available funds and over time;
- No veto over ordinary-course budgets, hiring, pricing, customers, products, or financing;
- No forced sale right exercisable by a minority investor;
- No transfer to a competitor;
- Confidentiality and reasonable limits on sensitive information access.

### Hybrids

Every hybrid must be modeled under base, downside, down-round, sale, and no-refinancing cases. Model:

- Cash-pay and PIK obligations;
- Conversion ownership at multiple valuations;
- Caps, discounts, warrants, and preferences;
- Redemption and maturity;
- Change-of-control treatment;
- Ranking relative to present and future lenders.

A security that combines high cash or PIK payments, mandatory redemption, substantial dilution, seniority, warrants, and broad investor controls should be rejected.

## Founder ownership and control

The founders’ current 78% ownership should be assessed on a fully diluted basis.

For a primary equity issuance:

\[
\text{Post-money ownership sold}
=
\frac{\text{New capital}}
{\text{Pre-money valuation}+\text{New capital}}
\]

Illustratively, before option-pool changes:

- A 15% post-money issuance reduces founder ownership to approximately 66.3%;
- A 25% issuance reduces it to approximately 58.5%;
- A 30% issuance reduces it to approximately 54.6%.

The board should establish a control floor before negotiations, including:

- Minimum founder voting ownership;
- Board composition and appointment rights;
- Maximum dilution, including conversion and option-pool effects;
- Limits on investor vetoes;
- Treatment of drag-along, redemption, and change-of-control rights.

Nominal majority ownership will not preserve independence if the investor has operating vetoes, a casting vote, coercive redemption rights, or the ability to force a sale.

## Downside contingencies

If demand, margin, utilization, or cash conversion misses plan:

1. Freeze discretionary hiring and nonessential marketing.
2. Stop the next capacity commitment.
3. Prioritize contracted, high-margin customers.
4. Renegotiate supplier terms and use customer-funded working capital where available.
5. Defer lower-confidence products, channels, or geographies.
6. Preserve service quality rather than pursue unprofitable volume.
7. Reforecast cash frequently until performance stabilizes.
8. Draw contingent capital only if the underlying economics remain sound.
9. Revert to the organic plan if the rival has not produced measurable displacement or if financing terms make acceleration disproportionate to the opportunity.

The company should recognize that leases, equipment orders, and hiring may make a later return to organic growth costly. These irreversible commitments must be included in the downside model.

---

## Walk-away conditions

### Debt

Walk away if debt:

- Depends on exceptional or post-expansion growth for service;
- Produces downside fixed-charge coverage below approximately 1.50x;
- Leaves less than 20% covenant headroom;
- Requires the contingent tranche to service the initial draw;
- Creates a refinancing cliff within 24 months;
- Requires broad founder guarantees or unnecessary core-IP security;
- Restricts necessary future financing or ordinary-course operations;
- Allows acceleration for a temporary, curable miss;
- Includes fees, warrants, or restrictions that eliminate its cost advantage.

### Minority equity

Walk away if the investment:

- Requires selling more than approximately 25–30% without transformative strategic value;
- Gives a minority investor control-like vetoes or board control;
- Includes participating or multiple liquidation preferences;
- Includes coercive or short-dated redemption;
- Requires full-ratchet anti-dilution;
- Allows control of ordinary-course budgets or operations;
- Is too small to fund a complete operating phase;
- Permits transfer to a competitor or creates material strategic risk;
- Falls below the agreed founder ownership or board-control floor.

### Hybrid

Walk away if the instrument:

- Combines debt-like repayment with substantial equity dilution;
- Creates a PIK, redemption, or maturity cliff;
- Produces unacceptable dilution under reasonable future valuations;
- Economically double-counts caps, discounts, warrants, and preferences;
- Ranks ahead of future operating financing in a way that impairs refinancing;
- Grants broader consent rights than conventional minority equity;
- Is too complex to explain or refinance.

### Organic fallback

Revert to organic growth if:

- Demand is not supported by reliable customer evidence;
- The rival has raised capital but has not demonstrated actual displacement;
- Stage 1 would breach the minimum liquidity reserve;
- Expansion would materially impair margin, quality, or service;
- Critical systems, talent, or supply capacity are not ready;
- The expected cost of delay is lower than financing and execution risk.

## Execution plan

The company should run debt and equity processes in parallel where practical.

### First 30 days

- Define the first operating phase and rank initiatives by confidence, payback, strategic importance, and reversibility.
- Complete the integrated cash and debt-capacity model.
- Validate demand, customer concentration, margins, capacity lead times, and hiring dependencies.
- Establish founder control and dilution limits.

### Days 31–60

- Solicit debt and equity or preferred term sheets.
- Negotiate delayed-draw mechanics, milestone definitions, covenants, governance, and economic protections.
- Confirm the fastest credible financing route for each use of proceeds.
- Identify interim measures such as customer prepayments, supplier terms, or equipment financing.

### Days 61–90

- Select financing sources only if walk-away conditions are satisfied.
- Close Stage 1 capital.
- Begin only the verified bottleneck investments.
- Establish quarterly milestone reporting and board approval procedures for Stage 2.

The board should set a maximum acceptable financing delay. If closing is delayed beyond the point at which strategic commitments become necessary, the company should implement the organic fallback rather than incur irreversible costs in anticipation of financing.

---

## Final decision rule

Proceed with Stage 1 only if:

- The investment addresses a verified bottleneck;
- A substantial majority of incremental capacity has credible demand support;
- The downside case preserves at least 9–12 months of runway;
- Downside fixed-charge coverage remains at least approximately 1.50x;
- Covenant headroom remains at least 20%;
- The company does not need Stage 2 to remain solvent or service debt;
- Founder ownership and board control remain above the agreed floor;
- The financing preserves future refinancing and strategic flexibility; and
- The measurable cost of delay exceeds the financing cost, dilution, and execution risk.

**Conclusion:** pursue controlled speed. Fund visible capacity with retained cash and appropriately sized debt, fund uncertainty with limited equity-like capital, and release further capital only as evidence confirms demand, economics, execution, and liquidity. This gives the company a credible opportunity to defend the market window without making uninterrupted hypergrowth—or loss of founder independence—a condition of survival.
