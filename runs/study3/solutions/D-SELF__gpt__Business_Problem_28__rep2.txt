# Industrial-Services Unit  
## Final Turnaround-or-Exit Assessment and Board Plan

## 1. Executive assessment and recommendation

### Established facts

- The unit has lost money for three consecutive years.
- Two restructurings reduced costs but did not restore profitability.
- The order book is shrinking.
- High-performing managers are transferring to profitable group divisions.
- Some customers also buy from those divisions.
- The Board has granted one final year to demonstrate a credible path to profitability or exit.

### Diagnosis

The losses are likely **mixed, with structural weakness currently the greater risk**. The evidence is not sufficient to conclude that the unit is inherently unprofitable, but repeated cost reductions without recovery indicate that overhead is not the sole problem.

The likely causes include:

- Uneconomic pricing or contract terms;
- Poor customer and service-line mix;
- Low utilization or insufficient geographic density;
- Estimating and project-control failures;
- Unbilled changes, rework, overtime, and subcontractor leakage;
- Weak collections and excessive working-capital requirements;
- Loss of critical management capability; and
- A portfolio too broad for its scale and differentiation.

### Recommended course

Approve **one final, 12-month, stage-gated turnaround of a smaller focused portfolio**, while preparing sale, carve-out, partnership, asset-light restructuring, and controlled wind-down options immediately.

This is not approval to preserve the current unit or repeat a broad cost-reduction program. The objective is to prove whether a focused business can generate:

1. Positive contribution;
2. Positive operating cash flow after maintenance capex;
3. Cash returns above an approved hurdle;
4. Adequate margin-adjusted backlog;
5. Stable critical management and technical capability; and
6. Greater value through retention than through exit.

If the Month 4 commercial gate or Month 7 execution gate is missed, exit preparation should automatically become exit execution, subject only to one tightly limited Board-approved proof period.

## 2. Facts, assumptions, and required tests

| Item | Current status | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Three years of losses | Fact | Reconcile P&L, cash flow, margin, and restructuring effects |
| Two unsuccessful restructurings | Fact | Complete a post-mortem before approving further structural cost cuts |
| Shrinking order book | Fact; cause unknown | Separate demand decline, customer churn, lost bids, cancellations, capacity constraints, and deliberate rejection of poor work |
| Manager transfers | Fact; cause unknown | Conduct confidential retention interviews and an independent capability assessment |
| Group customer relationships | Fact; value unquantified | Measure actual incremental group contribution and retention value |
| Profitable service lines | Unknown | Establish profitability by customer, contract, service line, geography, and delivery team |
| Structural versus execution losses | Hypothesis | Complete contract-level estimate-to-actual analysis and market benchmarking |
| Exit value | Unknown | Obtain buyer, partner, carve-out, and wind-down indications |

Management must label conclusions as **fact, validated analysis, or hypothesis**. Unvalidated pipeline, synergies, savings, or strategic value must not support further funding.

# 3. Diagnostic framework

## 3.1 Classify every material loss

Each loss should be assigned to one or more categories:

1. **Bad work selected** — uneconomic at award.
2. **Good work underpriced** — viable service sold at inadequate price or terms.
3. **Good work poorly delivered** — operational leakage destroyed margin.
4. **Viable work burdened by excess fixed cost** — portfolio works, but the footprint is too large.
5. **Group-structure loss** — allocations, transfer pricing, or mandated work distort results.

The response must match the cause. Cost reduction is relevant mainly to excess fixed cost; it will not repair structurally uneconomic contracts.

## 3.2 Indicators of structural weakness

A service line or segment should be considered structurally challenged if analysis confirms that:

- Market prices do not cover normalized full cost;
- Customers reject economically necessary repricing;
- Existing contracts lose money even at budgeted productivity;
- Attractive work is insufficient to support required management, equipment, compliance, and geographic costs;
- The unit lacks differentiation, switching costs, or density;
- Capital and working-capital requirements produce inadequate cash returns;
- Customer concentration creates sustained pricing pressure; or
- Low-margin volume is required merely to absorb fixed costs.

## 3.3 Indicators of execution failure

A turnaround remains credible if:

- Comparable operators earn acceptable returns in similar work;
- Losses are concentrated in identifiable contracts, branches, crews, or managers;
- Actual hours, overtime, materials, or subcontractors exceed estimates;
- Change orders are performed but not approved, billed, or collected;
- Utilization is low despite available demand;
- Results vary materially by delivery team or location;
- Invoicing, collections, scheduling, or job costing are weak; and
- Critical managers and customer relationships can be retained.

## 3.4 Prior-restructuring post-mortem

By Week 6, the Group CFO and Turnaround Director should establish:

- Expected versus realized savings;
- Recurring versus one-time savings;
- P&L and cash impact;
- Costs shifted to other group entities;
- Productive capabilities removed;
- Changes in customer retention, quality, safety, and delivery;
- The price-volume-mix and execution causes of continued losses.

No further broad cost reduction should be approved before this review is complete.

# 4. Financial framework and decision measures

The Board should use one consistent financial waterfall.

## 4.1 Financial waterfall

1. Revenue;
2. Direct labor;
3. Materials and subcontractors;
4. Travel, mobilization, standby, and other delivery costs;
5. Contract contribution;
6. Service-line and branch controllable overhead;
7. Segment EBITDA;
8. Cash taxes, where applicable;
9. Change in operating working capital;
10. Maintenance capex;
11. Operating cash flow after maintenance capex;
12. Operating capital employed;
13. Cash return on capital.

\[
\text{Cash return on capital}
=
\frac{\text{Operating cash flow after maintenance capex}}
{\text{Average operating working capital}+\text{net operating assets}}
\]

The analysis must separately show:

- Operating viability;
- Restructuring and turnaround funding;
- Strategic subsidies;
- Separation or wind-down costs; and
- Actual group liquidity requirements.

Working-capital costs must not be deducted twice in contribution and cash-return calculations.

## 4.2 Return hurdle

Group Finance and the Board Investment Committee must approve a numerical hurdle before the Month 4 gate. It should specify:

- Return metric;
- Pre- or post-tax basis;
- Nominal or real basis;
- Risk premium by service line;
- Treatment of customer concentration, capital intensity, and liability exposure; and
- Treatment of strategic subsidies.

Until approved, “acceptable return” remains undefined and cannot be used as a decision criterion.

## 4.3 Value comparison

\[
V_T =
PV(\text{turnaround operating cash flows})
-\text{turnaround investment}
-\text{restructuring costs}
-\text{risk adjustment}
\]

\[
V_E =
\text{probability-weighted sale, carve-out, or recovery proceeds}
-\text{separation costs}
-\text{stranded costs}
-\text{legal, tax, employee, pension, and liability costs}
\]

The model must include:

- Base, downside, and severe-downside scenarios;
- Monthly cash flows through Month 12;
- Liquidity, covenant, and solvency constraints;
- Timing and probability of exit;
- Cost of preserving exit optionality;
- Buyer-specific synergies;
- Transaction certainty; and
- Residual liabilities after sale or carve-out.

A positive expected value is insufficient if the severe downside breaches the approved liquidity or solvency limits.

# 5. Required fact base

The fact base should be substantially complete by Week 8. Residual uncertainty must be quantified, assigned, and reflected in the downside case.

## Financial and cash

- Monthly P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow for 36 months;
- Revenue, contribution, EBITDA, and cash flow by customer, contract, service line, branch, and geography;
- Direct and allocated overhead;
- Prior restructuring savings and costs;
- EBITDA-to-cash bridge;
- Receivables, disputes, retentions, work in progress, and unbilled revenue;
- Payables and supplier terms;
- Maintenance and growth capex;
- Equipment ownership, leases, rentals, and utilization;
- Insurance, tax, pension, environmental, safety, and litigation exposure;
- Guarantees, intercompany funding, and group support.

## Contracts and backlog

For every material contract:

- Signed and remaining value;
- Execution probability;
- Completion, renewal, or rebid date;
- Margin at award and current estimate-to-complete;
- Payment terms and collection record;
- Change-order rights and recovery;
- Liability caps, penalties, warranties, and service levels;
- Required labor, equipment, and working capital;
- Assignment and customer-consent requirements.

Backlog must be reported as:

1. Unweighted value;
2. Probability-weighted value; and
3. Margin- and cash-adjusted value.

Pipeline should use historical conversion rates or independently reviewed probability haircuts. It should not support the base case merely because management considers it likely.

## Commercial, operational, and people data

- Market size, pricing, growth, and comparable operator economics;
- Win/loss, retention, churn, and price-realization data;
- Customer willingness to reprice;
- Utilization and contribution per productive hour;
- Estimate-to-actual variance;
- Overtime, subcontracting, rework, warranty, claims, and safety;
- Equipment utilization and downtime;
- Attrition, internal transfers, vacancies, and critical certifications;
- Management capability, succession, and customer relationships dependent on individuals.

### Validation standard

By Month 2, at least **90–95% of revenue and backlog by value** should have validated economics, with:

- Source data reconciled to the general ledger;
- Direct and shared-cost allocation rules documented;
- Confidence rating for each major assumption;
- Residual exposure quantified;
- Independent reviewer sign-off.

“Validated” does not mean management has merely assigned an estimated margin.

# 6. Customer and service-line portfolio

## 6.1 Profitability cube

Profitability must be reported by:

- Customer;
- Contract;
- Service line;
- Branch or geography;
- Contract type; and
- Delivery team or manager where material.

Each combination should show:

- Revenue;
- Contract contribution;
- Segment EBITDA;
- Operating cash flow;
- Working-capital requirement;
- Capital employed;
- Renewal probability;
- Price realization;
- Safety and claims exposure; and
- Measured group strategic value.

Direct costs should be attributed directly wherever possible. Shared costs should use operational drivers such as productive hours, jobs, dispatch events, equipment hours, headcount, or revenue. Both contribution before shared overhead and fully burdened economics should be shown, including stranded costs after exit.

Profitability should also be tested by:

- New versus mature contracts;
- Current versus prior twelve months;
- Renewal cohort;
- Seasonal or shutdown cycle; and
- Customer and service-line trend.

## 6.2 Customer treatment

| Customer type | Recommended treatment |
|---|---|
| Recurring, profitable industrial accounts | Retain and selectively grow |
| Specialist or regulated accounts | Prioritize only if price, cash, risk, and return hurdles are met |
| Project and shutdown accounts | Accept selectively under risk-adjusted bid controls |
| Spot or commodity accounts | Harvest or exit unless genuine differentiation supports price |
| Group-strategic accounts | Retain only with quantified group value or explicit funding |
| High-risk or late-paying accounts | Reprice, renegotiate, transfer, harvest, or exit |

## 6.3 Service-line treatment

| Classification | Required evidence | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Invest and grow | Positive cash return, attractive demand, differentiation, manageable risk | Targeted investment |
| Fix and test | Specific remediable failure and quantified benefit | 90–180-day remediation |
| Harvest | Positive cash contribution but limited strategic value | Minimal investment; selective work |
| Exit | Structural loss, high risk, weak differentiation, or poor cash return | Stop bidding, transfer, sell, or wind down |

Recurring maintenance, inspection, compliance, reliability, specialist, and project services are all hypotheses until supported by actual price realization, contribution, renewal, cash conversion, and capital-return data.

---

# 7. Group strategic value

For customers that also buy from profitable divisions, report separately:

1. Unit contribution;
2. Incremental contribution to other divisions;
3. Revenue or customer retention protected;
4. Cost and risk of external replacement;
5. Probability the customer remains with the group after a sale;
6. Cost of achieving the benefit; and
7. Required subsidy.

The Group CFO should independently review cases where profitable divisions claim strategic value while resisting arm’s-length pricing.

Any loss-making group-mandated work must have:

- An explicit strategic payment or subsidy;
- A maximum amount;
- An expiry date;
- A named beneficiary and owner;
- A measurable benefit metric; and
- Separate reporting before and after subsidy.

Strategic work must not be treated as standalone profitability, and incentives must not reward subsidized revenue as if it were economic profit.

# 8. Turnaround plan and milestones

## Governance

| Responsibility | Owner |
|---|---|
| Gate decisions and funding releases | Board Turnaround Committee |
| Overall delivery | Turnaround Director |
| Economics and cash | Unit CFO / Group CFO |
| Pricing, customers, and backlog | Commercial Director |
| Operations and productivity | COO |
| Talent and retention | HR Director |
| Group account value | Group Chief Commercial Officer |
| Exit alternatives | Corporate Development / Strategy Director |
| Legal, solvency, and contract exposure | General Counsel / restructuring counsel |
| Independent validation | External adviser or independent reviewer |

The Board Turnaround Committee should include at least one independent non-executive director. It should approve gate outcomes, funding releases, strategic subsidies, exceptions, valuation assumptions, and exit activation.

The Turnaround Director should have authority to stop uneconomic bids, suspend unsafe work, restrict discretionary spending, redeploy resources, and escalate liquidity or solvency risks immediately.

## Phase 1 — Stabilize and protect value: Weeks 1–2

### Actions

- Establish a weekly 13-week cash forecast;
- Freeze nonessential hiring, growth capex, and discretionary expenditure;
- Stop or escalate low-margin, cash-negative, and high-liability bids;
- Register all contracts, guarantees, leases, claims, and obligations;
- Review backlog quality and top-customer exposure;
- Identify critical managers, specialists, licenses, certifications, and equipment;
- Conduct retention interviews;
- Begin sale, carve-out, asset-light, and wind-down preparation;
- Complete preliminary liquidity and solvency review.

### Week 2 deliverables

- Contract register covering all material contracts;
- Backlog classification;
- Top-20 customer and contract risk review;
- 13-week cash forecast;
- Critical-role retention plan;
- Maximum cash exposure through Month 4;
- Initial liquidity floor and emergency triggers.

Funding through Month 2 should be released in a defined tranche, not as an open-ended commitment.

## Phase 2 — Build the fact base: Weeks 3–8

### Actions

- Complete the profitability cube and contract bridge;
- Complete the restructuring post-mortem;
- Normalize group allocations and subsidies;
- Reconcile EBITDA to cash;
- Review market pricing and comparable operators;
- Quantify group strategic value;
- Classify activities as retain, fix, harvest, reprice, transfer, or exit;
- Model base, downside, and severe-downside cases;
- Assess leadership and execution capability.

### Month 2 gate

| Metric | Standard |
|---|---|
| Revenue and backlog economics | 90–95% covered by validated economics; residual exposure quantified |
| Material contract treatment | 100% assigned a proposed treatment |
| Cash visibility | Weekly 13-week forecast with variance analysis |
| Claims and liabilities | Known exposure quantified; unresolved items have maximum exposure estimates |
| Core viability | At least one profitable or specifically fixable core identified |
| Data confidence | Major assumptions rated high, medium, or low confidence |
| Funding | Maximum cash requirement through Month 4 approved |
| Capability | Independent assessment completed for leadership and critical roles |

**Failure response:** no broad reinvestment; discretionary capex and hiring remain restricted; formal exit preparation begins.

## Phase 3 — Portfolio and commercial reset: Months 3–4

### Actions

- Implement formal bid/no-bid approval;
- Reprice, renegotiate, transfer, harvest, or exit loss-making contracts;
- Improve scope, payment, change-order, liability, and mobilization terms;
- Concentrate sales on validated attractive segments;
- Dispose of underutilized equipment;
- Test branch and support-function consolidation;
- Formalize funding for group-mandated strategic work;
- Restore essential estimating, scheduling, supervision, and technical capability where prior cuts damaged performance.

### Month 4 gate

The focused portfolio must demonstrate, against a Month 2 baseline:

- Contribution positive or improved by a pre-approved absolute amount and percentage;
- Customer evidence supporting repricing or economically viable renewal;
- Backlog meeting approved probability, margin, and cash standards;
- Fixed costs supportable without low-margin volume;
- Critical-role retention meeting the approved threshold;
- No material new loss-making awards;
- No reliance on unvalidated synergies or uncommitted pipeline.

Customer repricing should be measured separately as engagement, proposal, agreement, effective price, and contract exit. Timing constraints must be reflected, but unresolved exposure must be quantified.

**Failure response:** automatically activate exit execution, restrict discretionary investment, and schedule a Board decision on sale, carve-out, asset-light restructuring, partnership, or wind-down within 60–90 days.

## Phase 4 — Operational and cash stabilization: Months 4–7

### Actions

- Review all material contracts weekly;
- Enforce time capture, job costing, and estimate-to-complete discipline;
- Improve scheduling, utilization, and geographic density;
- Reduce avoidable overtime, rework, warranty, and subcontracting;
- Capture, approve, bill, and collect change orders;
- Resolve overdue and disputed receivables;
- Tie incentives to contribution, cash, safety, and retention—not revenue;
- Provide targeted retention packages for critical roles.

### Month 7 gate

The focused portfolio must show:

- Positive contribution for three consecutive months, adjusted for seasonality and project timing;
- Positive normalized contribution after required safety, maintenance, and capability costs;
- Forward backlog meeting the same economics hurdle;
- Reduced estimate-to-actual leakage;
- Improving operating cash conversion;
- No material new loss-making awards;
- Stable critical talent;
- No deterioration in safety or compliance.

Temporary collections, delayed maintenance, under-accrued claims, or deferred training must not be used to pass the gate. A quality-of-earnings review is required.

**Failure response:** controlled exit becomes the default. One additional proof period of no more than 60–90 days may be granted once only, with full Board approval, maximum funding, specific deliverables, independent validation, and an automatic exit consequence.

## Phase 5 — Sustainability and final decision: Months 8–12

### Actions

- Build a 24-month forecast using actual focused-portfolio results;
- Stress-test demand, price, labor cost, collections, customer loss, claims, and capital needs;
- Validate renewal, backlog, and cash economics;
- Obtain external buyer and partner indications;
- Finalize separation, transitional-services, asset-light, wind-down, and closure plans;
- Compare risk-adjusted retention value with exit value.

### Month 12 continuation standard

Retention requires:

- Positive EBITDA run-rate;
- Positive operating cash flow after maintenance capex;
- Any remaining working-capital need to be quantified, temporary, and Board-approved;
- At least 12 months of credible margin-adjusted backlog or equivalent recurring visibility;
- Cash return on capital above the approved hurdle;
- No recurring undisclosed subsidy;
- Critical-role retention at or above the approved threshold;
- Forecast accuracy within an agreed tolerance;
- Stable safety and compliance;
- Risk-adjusted retention value greater than exit value.

# 10. Exit alternatives

## 10.1 Whole-unit sale

Appropriate where a buyer values transferable customers, licenses, technical capability, geographic coverage, or operating synergies.

Assess:

- Normalized earnings and cash flow;
- Backlog quality;
- Customer concentration;
- Management retention;
- Claims and liabilities;
- Customer consents;
- Separation and transition costs;
- Buyer-specific synergies;
- Probability and timing of closing.

## 10.2 Carve-out or partial sale

Appropriate where value is concentrated in profitable service lines, branches, assets, licenses, or specialist teams.

Include:

- Shared systems, property, insurance, procurement, and management;
- Transferable employees and licenses;
- Pension and employee liabilities;
- Contract assignment;
- Transitional services;
- Stranded overhead;
- Residual liabilities and wind-down costs.

A service line is not saleable merely because it is profitable; transferability must be demonstrated.

## 10.3 Partnership or strategic investor

Appropriate only where a partner brings material scale, capital, technical capability, geographic density, customer access, technology, or superior field management.

Required terms:

- Committed funding;
- Governance and control;
- Quantified synergies;
- Performance milestones;
- Treatment of future losses;
- Customer and employee responsibilities;
- Exit rights.

## 10.4 Management buyout

Potentially suitable where a focused business is viable outside the group and key managers control customer or technical relationships.

Require independent validation of:

- Normalized earnings;
- Funding capacity;
- Working capital;
- Employee, pension, and contract liabilities;
- Customer continuity;
- Management capability outside the group.

## 10.5 Asset-light restructuring

Evaluate where commercial economics may be sound but the owned operating model is not. Options include:

- Outsourcing or subcontractor conversion;
- Equipment rental instead of ownership;
- Sale-and-leaseback;
- Transfer of contracts to another group entity;
- Licensing or managed-service arrangements;
- Shared dispatch or facilities;
- Contract-by-contract novation.

Proceed only if the model improves cash return and reduces fixed-risk exposure without compromising safety, quality, or retention.

## 10.6 Controlled wind-down

Appropriate where no buyer or focused model creates sufficient value but orderly recovery can preserve value.

The plan should cover:

- Contract completion, termination, or novation;
- Receivable collection;
- Equipment and asset sales;
- Employee consultation and redundancy;
- Pension and benefit obligations;
- Lease termination;
- Warranty and claims;
- Customer, supplier, regulator, and employee communications;
- Environmental and safety obligations.

## 10.7 Closure or formal restructuring

If the unit cannot meet essential obligations or may be unable to pay debts as they fall due, the Board must obtain restructuring-counsel advice immediately. Formal insolvency or creditor-protection options may be required and should not be treated as an ordinary wind-down.

# 11. Decision criteria

## Favor turnaround when

- A meaningful core is profitable or becomes profitable through specific, testable actions;
- Market prices support normalized full cost;
- The focused portfolio can cover fixed costs;
- Customers accept repricing and improved terms;
- Critical talent can be retained;
- Required investment is bounded and proportionate to expected cash returns;
- Strategic value is quantified and material; and
- Risk-adjusted retention value exceeds exit value.

## Favor divestment, carve-out, or wind-down when

- Most service lines remain structurally loss-making after normalization;
- Attractive activities are too small to support efficient scale;
- Customers reject economic pricing;
- Backlog continues to deteriorate after intervention;
- Key managers continue to leave;
- The unit requires indefinite subsidy;
- The group lacks the capability or appetite to operate it;
- A buyer or partner can create greater value;
- Month 4 or Month 7 gates are missed;
- Exit value exceeds risk-adjusted retention value; or
- Liquidity, solvency, safety, regulatory, or liability triggers are breached.

A failed gate automatically activates exit preparation, stops discretionary growth investment, and requires a defined Board decision timetable. It does not itself authorize a sale or closure.

---

# 12. Board actions requested

The Board should approve:

1. A final 12-month turnaround of a focused portfolio, not the current unit;
2. A Turnaround Director with documented authority to stop uneconomic or unsafe work;
3. Immediate cash, hiring, capital, contract, and bid controls;
4. Completion of the fact base and prior-restructuring post-mortem within eight weeks;
5. Parallel preparation of sale, carve-out, partnership, asset-light, MBO, wind-down, and formal restructuring options;
6. A maximum additional funding envelope and minimum liquidity floor;
7. Funding releases by stage gate rather than an open-ended commitment;
8. A numerical return hurdle before Month 4;
9. An independent Board Turnaround Committee with non-executive participation;
10. Independent validation of economics, valuation, backlog, and gate results;
11. Transparent accounting and funding of strategic group work;
12. One defined 60–90-day exception mechanism after a failed gate;
13. Immediate exit activation and investment restrictions following a failed Month 4 or Month 7 gate.

---

## Final recommendation and contingency

**Recommended course:** proceed with one final, tightly funded turnaround of a smaller, evidence-based portfolio, while preserving and actively developing exit alternatives from Day 1.

Retain only customers and services that demonstrate economic pricing, reliable delivery, positive cash generation after maintenance capex, acceptable capital returns, and sufficient management capability. Do not count theoretical synergies, uncommitted pipeline, temporary collections, or undisclosed subsidies as evidence of viability.

**Contingency:** if the focused portfolio fails the Month 4 commercial gate or the Month 7 execution and cash gate—or breaches liquidity, solvency, safety, or compliance limits—the group should stop funding a general turnaround and execute the highest-value combination of carve-out, sale, asset-light transfer, contract exit, asset disposal, controlled wind-down, or formal restructuring.
