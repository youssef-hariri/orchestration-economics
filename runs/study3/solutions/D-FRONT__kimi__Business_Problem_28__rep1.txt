**TURNAROUND-OR-EXIT ASSESSMENT**
**Industrial Services Unit (ISU)**

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Industrial Services Unit’s three-year loss history and two failed restructurings are not, in themselves, proof of structural failure. They prove that **cost-led overhead reductions executed by the same leadership team cannot fix a revenue-side problem.** ISU is trapped in a group portfolio conflict: profitable divisions have used it as a captive loss-leader to win equipment sales, while parent overhead allocations and internal talent poaching have obscured whether a viable core exists.

This assessment treats ISU not as a standalone turnaround, but as a **group portfolio optimization problem.** The board must decide whether the group is willing to let ISU charge sustainable prices to shared customers—even if that risks equipment-division attach rates—or whether the highest group value is achieved through a divestiture coupled with arm’s-length service agreements.

We recommend a **12-Month “Performance or Exit” Protocol** with a non-negotiable precondition: **new turnaround leadership must be in place within seven days of board approval.** The plan runs in parallel with exit preparation from Day 1. Because industrial-services restructuring is governed by contract tails, WIP wind-downs, and license portability, the unit will look worse in Months 4–7 before it looks better. The board must therefore judge progress on **core-line contribution margin and talent retention**, not unit-level reported EBITDA during the J-curve.

Hard decision triggers are set at Months 3, 6, and 9. If ISU cannot demonstrate a self-sustaining profit engine by Month 10, the default path becomes an orderly carve-out sale or wind-down—not a third restructuring.

**Recommendation:** Approve the protocol immediately. Fund Phase I retention and working capital for the Sustain core only. Insist on clean-room economics by Month 3 and enforce the hard triggers below. ISU has one credible attempt left to prove it is a business worth keeping.

## 1. DIAGNOSIS: STRUCTURAL VS. EXECUTION-DRIVEN LOSSES

**Rule:** No structural verdict before clean-room economics. The prior two restructurings failed because they were overhead reductions applied across a portfolio by a leadership team that had already lost credibility. That is a **restructuring execution failure**, not evidence the underlying business is unviable.

### 1.1 The Clean-Room Requirement (Months 0–90)
Before the board declares any line “structural,” ISU must produce a **direct contribution-margin P&L by project and by customer** with:
- **Zero parent allocations** (G&A, IT, HR, finance, legal).
- **Market-rate transfer pricing** for any shared services consumed from the parent.
- **True direct SG&A** assigned to service lines and accounts.

Parent overhead is currently allocated on revenue percentage rather than consumption. A material portion of ISU’s reported “loss” may reflect a **group tax** rather than a unit-economics problem. The clean room will separate what ISU owns from what the parent allocates.

### 1.2 Phased Hypothesis (Not a Verdict)

| Hypothesis | Test | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **A: Execution-Broken Core** | Lines A+B+C generate >15% contribution margin after direct SG&A and proper pricing; losses driven by bid optimism, utilization gaps, procurement leakage, and cross-subsidy policy. | Portfolio is sound. Losses are reversible with leadership change, pricing discipline, and parent policy reset. |
| **B: Structural Impairment** | Lines A+B+C show <10% contribution margin even after pricing discipline and zero-based overhead. | Cost base or market position is irreversible at ISU scale. Default to carve-out or wind-down. |

**Preliminary Finding (Pending Clean Room):**  
Management accounts suggest that after stripping out parent allocations and below-market bundled pricing, the **Predictive Maintenance, Compliance, and Parts/Logistics core** may carry healthy gross profit dollars, while Project Installation (fixed-price), IFM, and General Contracting dilute or destroy value. This hypothesis will be confirmed or rejected by the Month 3 clean-room output.

## 2. VIABLE SEGMENTS & SERVICE LINES

**Objective:** Identify what can sustainably fund its own capital, talent, and direct overhead—and what must be exited.

### 2.1 Service-Line Portfolio Matrix

| Service Line | Strategic Bucket | Profitability Logic | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Predictive Maintenance & Asset Diagnostics** | **SUSTAIN** | High switching costs; sticky contracts; recurring revenue. Synergistic with group equipment base if digitized. Margin potential >20% gross under direct costing, pending clean-room confirmation. | **Invest.** Ring-fence technical talent. Develop IoT-enabled condition-monitoring offering. |
| **B. Regulatory Inspection & Compliance** | **SUSTAIN** | Mandated spend; oligopolistic supplier set in niche certifications. Recurring, low-capex profile. | **Defend & expand.** Cross-sell through group installed base only if pricing is market-rate. |
| **C. Spare Parts & Logistics** | **FIX / HOLD** | Thin margins, but strategic glue for shared customers. Keep **only if** it secures Equipment Division attach rates >30%. Parent allocation currently masks true direct profitability. | **Automate warehousing;** renegotiate vendor terms; move to consignment. Exit if clean room shows it cannot cover direct cost plus capital. |
| **D. Project Installation & Commissioning** | **FIX or EXIT** | Erratic margins driven by bid optimism and scope creep (execution pathologies). Not automatically structural. T&M or cost-plus commissioning tied to group equipment may be viable. | **Exit all fixed-price projects >$2M.** Retain only T&M/cost-plus work. If clean room shows irreversible cost base even with perfect bidding, full exit. |
| **E. Integrated Facilities Management (IFM)** | **EXIT** | Pure commodity; national FM providers hold ~40% cost advantage. No route to sustainable EBITDA. | **Divest contracts or run off.** Do not renew. |
| **F. General Contracting / Construction Support** | **EXIT** | Cyclical, low barriers, highest working capital drag. Loss-making for 8 consecutive quarters per management accounts. | **Immediate wind-down.** Stop bidding. |

### 2.2 Customer Segment Filter & Shared-Customer Political Economy

| Segment | Profitability | Strategic Value to Group | Imperative |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shared Group Accounts** | Often breakeven or loss due to bundled discounting and below-market transfer pricing. | **Critical.** ISU retention protects an equipment and solutions revenue stream that must be quantified account-by-account. | **Shared Customer Council** must rule on approved loss-leader contracts, cross-divisional compensation splits, and blocked internal poaching. |
| **Independent Industrial Accounts** | Mixed; must pass strict contribution margin hurdle. | Low. | **Ruthless profitability filter.** Exit accounts that cannot deliver 15%+ gross margin after direct SG&A. |
| **Public Sector / Infrastructure** | Poor; extended payment cycles; low-margin tendering. | Neutral. | **Exit** unless sole-source or premium-priced framework agreements. |

**The Shared-Customer Scenarios (Board Must Model These):**

1. **Status Quo:** ISU continues to subsidize equipment sales through bundled below-market services. Group profit is locally optimized, but ISU bleeds out.
2. **Market Pricing:** ISU charges standalone market rates. Equipment division win rates may decline 15–30% if customers were buying primarily for the subsidized service wrap.
3. **Divestiture + Arm’s-Length Service Agreement:** ISU core is sold to a third party or PE platform; equipment division pays market rates for aftermarket services via long-term contract. Often the **highest group NPV** because it crystallizes ISU value and removes cross-subsidy distortion.

**Decision Rule:** If the equipment division refuses to adjust transfer pricing, approve formal loss-leader contracts with explicit caps, and change cross-divisional compensation plans by **end of Month 3**, the default path must shift to **divestiture.** The parent will have proven it is structurally unwilling to let ISU be profitable.

## 3. TURNAROUND PLAN & MILESTONES

**Precondition for All Funding:** If the incumbent CEO/COO led the prior two failed restructurings, an interim turnaround director (external or internal) must be appointed in **Week 1.** The organization will not believe a third plan led by the same cast.

### Talent Retention & Leadership Reset

- **Parent-Sponsored Transfer Embargo:** The Group CHRO must issue a 6-month moratorium on internal transfers out of ISU without turnaround committee approval. Profitable divisions have been poaching ISU’s best managers because they offer career trajectory; a unit-level retention bonus cannot compete without structural protection.
- **Retention Funding:** Packages funded 50/50 by ISU and parent to align incentives.
- **“Project Keep” Expanded Pool:** Target **50–60 mission-critical roles**:
  - Account directors, project managers, compliance inspectors, diagnostic engineers, lead field supervisors.
  - Cash retention: 25–50% of base, vesting 50% at Month 6 and 50% at Month 12 or upon sale.
  - **Carve-out equity kicker:** Phantom or real equity tied to sale premium or turnaround EBITDA target.
- **Rebuilding the Bench:** By Month 3, engage 2–3 retired industry veterans or specialist consultants as interim project directors to backfill capability while permanent hiring ramps.

### Phase I: Stabilize & Clean Room (Months 1–3)
*Theme: Stop the bleed. Protect shared customers. Prove the math.*

| Initiative | Action |
|---|---|
| **Cash & Control** | Implement 13-week rolling cash forecast with weekly CEO/Board treasury reviews. Freeze all non-essential capex and hiring. |
| **Clean-Room Economics** | By Week 8, produce direct contribution-margin P&L by service line and by top 20 customers. Zero allocations; market-rate transfer pricing. |
| **Contract Triage** | Renegotiate or terminate bottom-quartile contracts. Issue stop-bid on all new IFM and general contracting; issue non-renewal notices on E/F contracts where legally permissible. |
| **Shared Customer Council** | Chaired by Group CEO or designated board member. Must resolve by Month 3: (i) formal transfer-pricing protocol; (ii) cross-divisional comp splits (e.g., equipment reps receive 2% on attached service revenue); (iii) capped, time-bound loss-leader approvals; (iv) blocked talent poaching. |
| **Carve-Out Readiness** | Begin Month 1 assessment of change-of-control clauses, license/certification portability (ASME, NDT, state contractor licenses), and estimated separation costs for IT/HR/payroll (typically 2–4% of revenue). |

**Month 3 Milestones**
- [ ] Monthly cash burn reduced by ≥40% versus Q3 annualized run-rate.
- [ ] Clean-room P&L presented to board; structural-vs.-execution hypothesis confirmed by data.
- [ ] “Sustain / Fix / Exit” lines formally board-approved based on direct costing.
- [ ] Retention packages signed by ≥75% of critical talent; parent transfer embargo in effect.
- [ ] Zero shared-customer anchor account defections.
- [ ] Shared Customer Council operational with signed protocol **or** board notified that divestiture default is triggered.

### Phase II: J-Curve & Core Build (Months 4–6)
*Theme: Build the core. Absorb exit costs. Reset commercial discipline.*

**Critical Warning to Board:** Unit-level reported EBITDA will likely deteriorate in this phase due to severance, lease breakage, and WIP run-off from exited lines. Judge progress on **core-line contribution**, not consolidated reported profit.

| Initiative | Action |
|---|---|
| **Exit Execution** | Complete divestment or orderly closure of Service Lines E & F. Redeploy/sever staff. Close or sublet 2–3 surplus regional depots. Board pre-approves a restructuring reserve. |
| **Pricing & Governance** | Implement dynamic pricing floors on Lines A–D. All fixed-price projects >$2M require independent risk-review gate; T&M becomes default for installation work. |
| **Cost Rebase** | Reduce SG&A run-rate by 20% (vs. Month 0), achieved primarily through exit of non-core lines, not salami-slicing the core. |
| **Shared-Customer Win-Back** | Launch bundled “Equipment + Diagnostics + Compliance” offering **only if** cross-divisional compensation and pricing governance were fixed in Phase I. |

**Month 6 Milestones**
- [ ] Core lines (A+B) achieve **positive segment EBITDA** (direct costs only, zero parent allocation) for at least one month.
- [ ] Sustain/Fix lines represent ≥50–55% of **run-rate** revenue (not absolute revenue, acknowledging contract tails).
- [ ] Gross margin on continuing portfolio ≥20% under clean-room direct costing.
- [ ] Shared-customer order book flat or positive quarter-on-quarter.
- [ ] Leadership refresh complete (turnaround director fully empowered or permanent CEO in seat).
- [ ] Restructuring reserve fully provisioned; E/F wind-down underway.

### Phase III: Sustainability Proof Points (Months 7–9)
*Theme: Demonstrate that the core can generate cash and retain customers.*

| Initiative | Action |
|---|---|
| **Selective Investment** | Deploy modest capex into digital diagnostics platform (Line A) and automated parts warehousing (Line C). |
| **Commercial Hiring** | Hire 3–4 senior account managers exclusively for Predictive Maintenance and Compliance. |
| **Margin Expansion** | Enforce standard SLAs with penalty/charge-back clauses. Reduce subcontractor dependence by 15% through retained talent. |
| **Exit Parallel Track** | Shortlist 2–3 strategic and private-equity buyers signed to NDAs; receive indicative bids. PE platforms are likely buyers for maintenance/recurring revenue assets. |

**Month 9 Milestones**
- [ ] Core lines generate **positive operating cash flow** for two consecutive months.
- [ ] Gross margin on A+B ≥20% sustained for two consecutive quarters.
- [ ] Core order backlog covers ≥4 months of forward revenue.
- [ ] Cash conversion on core lines (EBITDA to Operating Cash Flow) ≥75%.
- [ ] Shared-customer NPS at or above group average.
- [ ] Indicative bids received for core carve-out **or** wind-down path approved by board.

### Phase IV: Strategic Optionality & Board Decision (Months 10–12)
*Theme: Lock in the future or execute exit. No ambiguity.*

- **Parallel Track:** Maintain data room-ready Information Memorandum from Month 6 onward. Update carve-out readiness assessment.
- **Long-Term Customer Lock:** Negotiate 3-year framework agreements with top 10 shared customers, priced at sustainable market rates.
- **Capital Structure:** If turnaround is selected, secure standalone working capital facility to replace parent funding.

**Month 10–12 Milestones**
- [ ] **Month 10:** Board receives recommendation—Turnaround vs. Exit—based on **reviewed** (not audited) Q2 and Q3 financials, exit options, and shared-customer stability.
- [ ] **Month 11:** If Turnaround: Approve Years 2–3 plan targeting unit-level EBITDA breakeven by **Month 15–18**, then 4–5% EBITDA margin at Year 2, and 8–10% at Year 3. Appoint permanent leadership and capital allocation.
- [ ] **Month 12:** If Exit: Signed **LOI with exclusivity and breakup terms** (binding SPA target: Month 15–18). **OR** Board resolution to initiate orderly wind-down. No further extensions.

## 4. EXIT ALTERNATIVES

| Alternative | When to Pursue | Key Considerations | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Trade Sale (Strategic or PE Platform)** | Core lines (A/B) are viable but parent unwilling/unable to fund reinvestment; buyer can realize synergies (geography, customer base, PE back-office scale). | Market the “Sustain” lines as a carve-out; wind down the rest pre-close to avoid stranded liabilities. Value is tied to talent retention—if >30% of key managers depart, multiple contracts severely. Comparable maintenance-services transactions have ranged 3.5–6.0x EBITDA; project-heavy assets trade at 1.5–3.0x. Assume 3–4x for conservative planning. | Engagement letters by Month 2; teaser by Month 4; LOI by Month 10; close Month 15–18. |
| **B. Management Buyout (MBO)** | New turnaround management has credible plan; unit is overburdened by parent G&A/allocations; financing available via vendor note or small-cap PE. | Requires clean separation of shared-customer contracts and IP. Parent must secure 3–5-year service agreements with shared accounts to protect group sales. Separation costs for IT/HR/payroll often 2–4% of revenue. Management equity check required (1–2x salary). | 4–7 months to LOI; close Month 12–15. |
| **C. Managed Wind-Down** | Unit unsaleable as a whole, but customer contracts and WIP have recoverable value; avoids distressed fire-sale discount. | Stop all new bids except high-margin extensions. Run off WIP aggressively. Liquidate working capital. Redeploy shared-service staff to parent. Receivables recovery typically **60–70%** after retentions (5–10%), defect liabilities, WIP disputes, and performance bond releases are accounted for. | 12–18 months. |
| **D. Immediate Closure / Liquidation** | Cash burn exceeds wind-down recovery; existential liabilities (litigation, environmental, pension) discovered; no credible buyers by Month 9. | Nuclear option. Preserve customer relationships by novating essential contracts to competitors with parent-mandated transition fees. Minimize director/officer liability. | 1–3 months to cease operations. |

## 5. DECISION CRITERIA

### 5.1 Scored Assessment Matrix (0–100)
A graded scorecard replaces binary checklists, allowing the board to weigh trade-offs and act decisively.

| Criterion | Points | Scoring Guide |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Core Market Position & Growth** | 20 | Sustain lines in growing/stable TAM with ISU holding ≥15% defensible niche share = 20; shrinking TAM or <5% share = 0. |
| **2. Core Gross Margin Trajectory** | 20 | Lines A+B sustaining >20% gross margin (direct cost) and expanding = 20; <12% or declining = 0. |
| **3. Talent Retention & Leadership** | 20 | ≥75% critical roles retained, turnaround director effective, zero unplanned exits of anchor managers = 20; <50% retained or simultaneous top-leadership departure = 0. |
| **4. Shared-Customer Stability** | 20 | Zero anchor shared-customer loss, churn <5%, order book flat or growing = 20; loss of any anchor account = 0. |
| **5. Parent Capital Efficiency & Payback** | 20 | Incremental parent funding to core breakeven within board-approved hard cap and ≤24-month payback = 20; requires open-ended or uncapped funding = 0. |

**Thresholds**
- **≥70:** Continue turnaround. Approve multi-year plan.
- **50–70:** Pursue divestiture. Default path becomes trade sale or MBO.
- **<50:** Initiate managed wind-down or orderly closure.

### 5.2 Hard Triggers

| Date | Trigger Event | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Month 3** | Contribution margin by segment not clarified; OR cash burn not reduced by 40%; OR >25% critical talent departed; OR equipment division refuses transfer-pricing reset. | **Accelerate exit prep.** Engage advisors for trade sale or wind-down planning. Divestiture default triggered. |
| **Month 6** | Core lines fail to achieve positive segment EBITDA (direct cost); OR shared-customer order book declined further; OR continuing portfolio gross margin <18% under clean-room costing. | **Activate formal divestment process.** Default path becomes trade sale or MBO. |
| **Month 9** | Core lines fail to generate positive operating cash flow; OR turnaround requires funding materially above board-approved cap; OR no advanced buyer interest at acceptable terms. | **Move to wind-down or closure.** Do not advance to Month 12 on hope. |
| **Month 10** | Board review of reviewed Q2–Q3 financials, exit options, and scorecard. | Render final verdict: **Turnaround, Divest, or Liquidate.** |

## RECOMMENDATION TO THE BOARD

Approve the **12-Month Performance or Exit Protocol** immediately. Release Phase I funding exclusively for the Sustain core: retention packages for 50–60 critical roles, working capital stabilization, and clean-room analytics. Mandate that new turnaround leadership be seated within seven days, that the Group CHRO enforce a six-month transfer embargo, and that the Shared Customer Council render a binding resolution on transfer pricing and cross-divisional compensation by end of Month 3.

The ISU has one credible attempt left to prove it can sustain profit. It will not be saved by a third cost-cutting exercise. Enforce the hard triggers. If the core cannot generate positive direct contribution, operating cash flow, and a stable talent base by Month 9, the board must have the discipline to move to exit without extension.
