# International Cultural-Marketing Crisis Response Plan

## Objective

Stop further harm, acknowledge responsibility, support affected stakeholders, and rebuild trust through visible operational change. The response should be globally coordinated but locally led. No universal template can replace qualified, issue-specific in-market expertise.

---

## 1. Contain the issue immediately  
### 0–6 hours

**Priority actions**

- Pause and remove the campaign across paid, owned, retail, influencer, partner, and automated media channels.
- Issue written takedown instructions and track completion, including physical and partner-controlled materials.
- Preserve campaign files, research, translations, approvals, media records, and complaint data.
- Activate a 24/7 crisis team led by the country or regional general manager.
- Appoint:
  - One incident lead
  - One communications owner
  - One authorized spokesperson
  - One campaign-removal owner
  - One employee and partner-support contact
- Assess threats to people or facilities, regulatory exposure, commercial disruption, and whether related assets or markets are affected.

**Ownership**

Country or regional leadership; local and global communications; marketing and agencies; legal, HR, security, customer care, and digital teams.

**Success measures**

- All company-controlled placements paused
- Partner takedown confirmations tracked
- No contradictory public statements
- Employee and partner inquiries acknowledged promptly
- Initial risk assessment completed

---

## 2. Acknowledge the concern without speculating  
### Within 6–24 hours

If the issue is public, issue a short, locally reviewed holding statement once containment is underway:

> “We are aware of the reaction to our recent campaign in [market]. We have paused the campaign and are reviewing the content with qualified local advisers and relevant stakeholders. Responsibility for publishing the campaign rests with us. We are listening carefully and will provide a further update by [date/time].”

Use the relevant local language and adapt the tone and formality to local expectations. Do not rely on mechanical translation.

Avoid:

- “We are sorry if anyone was offended.”
- “The audience misunderstood the joke.”
- “It was only intended as humor.”
- “A small number of people complained.”
- “This does not reflect who we are.”
- Generic claims about respecting all cultures
- Descriptions of the country or culture as a single, uniform group

Do not suppress good-faith criticism. Moderate threats, doxxing, impersonation, and unlawful abuse under clear, consistently applied rules.

---

## 3. Establish what happened and why  
### 24–72 hours

### Build a reliable fact base

Document:

- The campaign’s development, localization, testing, and approval process
- All distribution channels and derivatives
- The specific words, images, symbols, references, or humor involved
- Warnings or objections raised before publication
- Verified criticism, speculation, and unknowns separately
- Stakeholder, employee, partner, legal, regulatory, and security impacts
- Whether similar assets exist in other markets

### Obtain independent local expertise

Engage advisers with demonstrated knowledge of the specific issue—not simply general market familiarity. Where perspectives differ, use more than one adviser or stakeholder group.

Advisers should assess:

- What the content communicates locally
- Which groups may feel mocked, stereotyped, erased, or exploited
- Whether the issue varies by region, language, religion, generation, class, or community
- Which terms accurately describe the failure
- What apology and remedy would be credible
- Whether proposed gestures could create further offense

Advisers should be independent of the original creative approval chain, properly compensated, and free to challenge the brand. Their participation must not be presented as endorsement.

### Map stakeholders specifically

Do not assume that one online group, organization, or individual represents everyone affected. Consider:

- People directly represented or referenced
- Relevant cultural, religious, historical, disability, academic, or civil-society experts
- Employees and frontline customer-care teams
- Customers, retailers, distributors, franchisees, creators, and agencies
- Regulators, industry bodies, and local-language media
- Stakeholders who may face safety or privacy risks

---

## 4. Issue a direct, locally credible apology  
### Normally within 24–72 hours

Once the core issue is confirmed, publish a fuller statement reviewed by local communications professionals, an issue-specific cultural adviser, and an independent local-language editor.

The statement should:

1. Name the campaign.
2. Describe the failure accurately and plainly.
3. Accept responsibility for publishing it.
4. Apologize without conditional language.
5. Confirm withdrawal and review of related material.
6. Commit to specific corrective action.
7. State when progress will next be reported.

A possible structure is:

> “We published a campaign in [market] that treated [subject] in an insensitive and disrespectful way. That was our responsibility, and we are sorry. We have withdrawn the campaign and are reviewing related materials. We are consulting independent local advisers and relevant stakeholders to understand the impact and determine appropriate corrective action. We will strengthen our local review and approval process and report on the steps we complete.”

Use stronger or more specific language—such as “stereotyping,” “trivializing,” or “inappropriate”—only when it accurately reflects the findings and is validated in-market. Do not claim complete understanding before the review supports it.

A global executive may need to accept accountability, but should not displace a credible local voice. Brief all global spokespeople to prevent inconsistent explanations.

---

## 5. Engage affected stakeholders responsibly  
### Days 3–7 and ongoing

### Communities and advisers

- Hold private conversations where appropriate and safe.
- Use trusted local intermediaries when advised.
- Ask what corrective action would be useful, not what would make the brand look better.
- Obtain consent before identifying, recording, quoting, or photographing participants.
- Compensate people for substantial expertise and time.
- Do not ask anyone to defend the brand, forgive it, endorse the response, or appear in recovery advertising.

Some stakeholders may prefer no engagement. Respect that decision.

### Employees

Brief local employees before or alongside the public apology. Provide:

- Approved customer-response language
- A confidential channel for concerns
- Security and legal support where needed
- Clear guidance on confidentiality and media inquiries
- Assurance that they are not expected to defend the company publicly

Do not impose a blanket ban on lawful personal criticism. Protect employees who raise legitimate concerns.

### Partners and agencies

Provide retailers, distributors, franchisees, licensees, creators, media partners, and agencies with:

- Takedown and replacement instructions
- Affected-asset lists
- Customer-response guidance
- A named escalation contact
- Arrangements for media, inventory, or cancellation issues

Do not assign public blame before the investigation is complete. The brand remains accountable for approving and publishing the work.

---

## 6. Implement tangible remediation  
### First week to 90 days

### Immediate action

- Permanently withdraw the creative where the issue is substantive.
- Remove captions, translations, edits, influencer content, and point-of-sale derivatives.
- Review related assets globally for similar risks.
- Provide refunds, replacements, or other remedies where customers experienced a concrete impact.
- Support employees, creators, and partners facing harassment or threats.
- Review agency and supplier responsibilities fairly and based on evidence.

### Process reform

Implement:

- Mandatory issue-specific local review for humor and content involving identity, religion, ethnicity, nationality, gender, disability, politics, history, social conflict, sacred symbols, or vulnerable groups
- Testing in the relevant language and cultural context
- Documented resolution of local objections
- Independent review separate from the original creative chain
- Binding local authority to modify or stop sensitive global creative
- Formal escalation where advisers disagree
- Updated agency briefs, contracts, and performance requirements
- Scenario-based training and testing for marketers, executives, agencies, translators, social teams, and customer care

Local review must be a meaningful decision right, not a checkbox.

### Community initiatives

Do not lead with donations, advisory panels, or branded cultural programs. Consider them only after local consultation confirms they are relevant and wanted.

Any initiative should have:

- A legitimate connection to the issue
- An independent or established local partner
- A defined budget, owner, duration, and outcome
- No requirement for public endorsement
- Fair compensation for participants
- Proportionate, non-promotional disclosure

---

## 7. Resume marketing carefully  
### Approximately 30–180 days

Return to marketing only after:

- The offending campaign and derivatives are removed or controlled
- The apology has been issued
- Relevant stakeholders have been approached appropriately
- Corrective actions have owners and funding
- Local teams have stop authority
- New creative has undergone meaningful local review and testing

Begin with work that is locally informed and clearly distinct from the controversy—not work simply labeled “culturally neutral.” Do not immediately revisit the same humor, subject, or creative device.

Local creators should have genuine creative influence and fair compensation. They should not be used as symbolic proof of cultural credibility or asked to defend the brand.

Avoid:

- “We learned” redemption campaigns
- Hashtags urging people to move on
- Influencer activity designed to drown out criticism
- Publicizing private meetings without consent
- Using the controversy as a brand-story asset

---

## 8. Govern recovery and prevent recurrence  
### 30 days onward

Assign a senior executive sponsor for remediation, with regional or board-level oversight for serious incidents. Every commitment should have:

- An accountable owner
- A deadline
- Confirmed funding
- A defined deliverable
- Evidence of completion
- An independent or locally credible reviewer
- A decision on what will be reported publicly

For serious incidents, commission an independent after-action review covering:

- Whether local warnings were ignored
- Whether employees could challenge global creative safely
- Whether approval incentives contributed to the failure
- Whether similar risks exist in other markets
- Whether the apology and remedy were credible

Maintain a market-level dashboard tracking:

- Campaign-related complaints
- Local-language media and social discussion
- Threats and safety issues
- Partner and employee concerns
- Regulatory inquiries
- Trust, respect, favorability, and consideration
- Completion of local reviews and training
- Repeat cultural-risk incidents

Use continuous operational monitoring during the acute phase, weekly reviews in the first month, and broader assessments at 30, 60, 90, and 180 days. Combine quantitative data with qualitative feedback from local advisers, employees, partners, and relevant stakeholders.

Do not declare the issue resolved solely because media attention or negative sentiment declines. Closure requires completed commitments, credible evidence of process change, and no continuing harm.

---

## Leadership success standard

The response is working when:

- The campaign is no longer generating new harm.
- The brand has accepted responsibility in locally credible language.
- Employees and partners are supported and equipped.
- Relevant stakeholders believe their concerns were heard, even if they do not endorse the brand.
- Corrective actions are completed on schedule.
- Local markets can challenge or stop sensitive global work.
- Trust and respect indicators begin improving without suppressing legitimate criticism.
- No comparable incident recurs in the affected market or related markets.
