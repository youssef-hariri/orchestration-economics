# RASA Everyday India  
## Modern snacks built around familiar Indian flavors

### Target-market assumption

Because the target country was not specified, this proposal assumes **urban and semi-urban India** as the initial market hypothesis. A first test should focus on a defined multi-city pilot—such as **Delhi NCR, Mumbai/Pune, Bengaluru, and Hyderabad**—rather than assuming nationwide appeal.

The line is designed for kiranas, modern grocery, convenience stores, e-commerce, and quick-commerce. Products should compete primarily on **taste, texture, convenience, and price**, not unverified health claims.

# Product details and launch guidance

## 1. RASA Achari Masala Crunch Mix

### Recommended positioning

**“Achari Masala — Pickle-Spice Crunch.”**

The flavor should draw from a **mild North Indian mixed-pickle profile**, with amchur-led tang, aromatic mustard and fennel, controlled fenugreek, and moderate heat. It should not be excessively bitter, garlic-forward, or hot.

### Pack architecture

- **₹10–₹15:** 18–22 g impulse pouch  
- **₹30–₹40:** 45–60 g standard pouch  
- **₹70–₹100:** 120–150 g resealable pack  

These are planning targets, not confirmed MRPs. Final pack weight depends on ingredient costs, trade margins, and consumer value perception.

### Principal risks

- Hard roasted pulses
- Ingredient settling
- Excessive fenugreek bitterness
- Insufficient product volume in small packs
- Generic rather than recognizable achari flavor

**Launch status:** Proceed to formulation and multi-city testing.

---

## 2. RASA Nimbu Pudina Masala Puffs

### Recommended positioning

**“Lemon-Mint Masala Puffs.”**

The pulse percentage should be meaningful and accurately declared. The product should be sold for its flavor and texture rather than positioned as a health snack.

### Pack architecture

- **₹10:** 12–15 g impulse pouch  
- **₹20–₹25:** 25–35 g standard pouch  
- **₹50–₹60:** 70–90 g sharing pouch  

High-moisture-barrier packaging is essential. Nitrogen flushing should be used only if testing shows a meaningful shelf-life benefit relative to its cost.

### Principal risks

- Artificial lemon or mint notes
- Excessive acidity, salt, or heat
- Rapid softening in humid conditions
- Low perceived value at the entry price
- Overstating the nutritional value of the pulse base

**Launch status:** Proceed to formulation and testing.

---

## 3. RASA Tandoori Masala Makhana

### Recommended positioning

**“Roasted Makhana — Tandoori Masala Flavor.”**

The seasoning should taste like Indian tandoori masala, not generic barbecue. Smoke should be subtle. A dairy-free core is preferable, with any milk, whey, or ghee-derived ingredients declared accurately.

### Pack architecture

- **₹20–₹30:** 12–18 g trial pouch  
- **₹60–₹80:** 35–45 g resealable pouch  
- **₹120–₹160:** 80–100 g family or e-commerce pack  

Makhana should not be the price leader. Small trial packs can reduce the barrier created by its higher raw-material cost.

### Principal risks

- High ingredient cost
- Broken or undersized makhana
- Uneven seasoning adhesion
- Humidity-related softening
- Excessive smoke flavor
- Consumer perception that the pack contains too little product

**Launch status:** Proceed as the accessible-premium hero, subject to cost and shelf-life validation.

---

## 4. RASA Curry Leaf Tadka Crisps

### Recommended positioning

**“Curry Leaf Tadka — Curry Leaf and Mustard Crisps.”**

The recipe should reflect one coherent tempering profile rather than combining multiple regional cuisines. A peanut-free core may broaden potential placement, but the final choice should follow sensory and cost testing.

### Pilot markets

Begin in selected southern urban markets, particularly:

- Bengaluru
- Chennai
- Hyderabad
- Kochi

### Principal risks

- Overly broad or inauthentic regional positioning
- Product breakage
- Excessive mustard, chili, or asafoetida
- Allergen issues from peanut or wheat-based asafoetida carriers
- Narrow appeal outside southern markets

**Launch status:** Regional pilot after the three core products have been validated.

# Later-stage concepts

## RASA Coconut Jaggery Crunch Bites

A sweet snack made with puffed rice, coconut, sesame, cardamom, and a standardized jaggery binder.

- **Format:** Individually wrapped 15–20 g bites and multipacks
- **Occasions:** Tea accompaniment, afternoon sweet craving, travel
- **Target:** Families, students, and consumers seeking an alternative to chocolate bars
- **Differentiation:** Portable format inspired by chikki and puffed-rice sweets
- **Main risks:** Stickiness, hardness, coconut rancidity, jaggery variability, and shelf life

Position as a sweet snack—not a protein bar, breakfast replacement, sports product, or inherently healthier alternative.

**Status:** Develop after the savory core.

## Chaat Crunch

The original hollow, filled format is technically risky because of breakage, moisture migration, and loss of crispness. A more practical version would be:

- Tamarind-mint seasoned crisps
- A chaat-flavored roasted mix
- Crisps with roasted gram and sev-style pieces

Select one clear reference—**Pani Puri Crunch**, **Bhel Crunch**, or **Tamarind-Mint Chaat**—rather than combining several distinct street-food experiences.

**Status:** Redesign as a limited-edition innovation.

## Masala Chai Crunch Bites

Hold the granola-style concept initially. It may be perceived as a premium breakfast product rather than an impulse snack, and strong clove or ginger notes could taste medicinal.

**Status:** Deprioritize unless research identifies a clear tea-time occasion and strong repeat interest.

# Portfolio and communication safeguards

- Use precise flavor names rather than broad labels such as “Indian” or “South Indian.”
- Do not imply that one product represents an entire region.
- Clearly declare vegetarian status and allergens, including peanut, sesame, milk, whey, and wheat-based flavor carriers where applicable.
- Do not use Jain, fasting-friendly, vegan, high-protein, healthy, low-sugar, guilt-free, or energy claims without formulation and regulatory validation.
- Treat “light” as a texture description only; “Puffs” is safer as the consumer-facing platform name.
- Use ingredient and texture imagery instead of religious symbols, stereotypes, monuments, or caricatured street-food scenes.
- Localize language by pilot city; test English alongside relevant regional languages.
- Validate all products for heat, humidity, oxidation, crispness, breakage, and seal integrity before scale-up.

---

# Recommended next-step research

1. **Confirm the market scope:** Validate India as the target market and finalize pilot cities, channels, and price tier.  
2. **Benchmark competition:** Compare chips, puffs, namkeen, roasted mixes, makhana, millet snacks, and regional crisps by price per 100 g, pack size, flavor, and positioning.  
3. **Run sensory testing:** Test mild, medium, and hotter versions across regional consumer groups, measuring liking, name comprehension, and texture acceptance.  
4. **Conduct repeat-use trials:** Measure repeat purchase after several consumption occasions rather than relying on novelty-driven first trial.  
5. **Cost the products:** Confirm ingredient, packaging, manufacturing, trade-margin, and promotional costs before approving ₹10–₹15 packs.  
6. **Complete technical testing:** Establish shelf-life, moisture, oxidation, crispness, breakage, segregation, and transport standards.  
7. **Review claims and packaging:** Confirm regulatory, allergen, language, cultural, and trademark requirements for RASA and each product name.

## Recommendation

The strongest launch direction is a focused three-product pilot:

1. **RASA Achari Masala Crunch Mix** — value and sharing anchor  
2. **RASA Nimbu Pudina Masala Puffs** — mass impulse and youth-oriented product  
3. **RASA Tandoori Masala Makhana** — accessible-premium hero  

Follow with **Curry Leaf Tadka Crisps** as a southern regional pilot. Defer sweet and chaat extensions until consumer repeat purchase, product stability, retail reorders, and unit economics are demonstrated.
