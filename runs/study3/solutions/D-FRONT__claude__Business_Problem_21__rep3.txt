# AUTOMATION AND WORKFORCE-TRANSITION ROADMAP
## Five-Warehouse Regional Distribution Network

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Situation:** Five regional warehouses (132,000 lines/day aggregate, 764 FTE picking/support staff) face rising labor costs, 35% annual turnover, and error rates damaging key customer relationships. A competitor's botched big-bang automation cutover (500-person site, 6-week deployment, supply-chain failure, national news coverage) illustrates the execution risk.

**Five Headline Decisions:**

1. **Phased, Site-Differentiated Automation (36–48 months):** 
   - Site B (Tier 1): $314K capex, 1.1-year payback, proof-of-concept pilot
   - Site E (Tier 1): $220K capex, 1.6-year payback, error-reduction focus
   - Site C (Tier 1+2 with AMRs): $1,420K capex, 4.2-year payback, production pilot
   - Site D (Tier 1 + process redesign): $358K capex, 2.7-year payback, seasonal optimization
   - Site A (Tier 1.5): $944K capex, 4.1-year payback, last-mover (town-dominant site)
   - **Total: $4,256K capex** (vs. competitor's $8–10M rip-and-replace)

2. **Zero Involuntary Layoffs via Disciplined Attrition:**
   - 35% annual turnover + hiring freeze = 276 natural exits over 36 months
   - 105 FTE redeployed to technician/QA/ops roles (6–12 week retraining, $4K subsidy per person)
   - 30 FTE external hire for specialist roles (technicians, data auditors)
   - Remaining 86 FTE reduction absorbed by attrition; no separations without voluntary severance offer
   - **Investment:** $2.22M retraining + wage guarantees + EAP expansion

3. **Three-Wave Execution with Hard Stage Gates:**
   - **Wave 1 (Mo 1–12):** Sites B & E deployed in parallel; gate: uptime >95%, error <1,200 ppm, safety <1 incident/week, staff satisfaction ≥6/10
   - **Wave 2 (Mo 10–34):** Site C phased deployment (Phase 1A: PTL/voice; Phase 1B: AMR pilot; Phase 2: scale; Phase 3: pack-line integration); kill-switch at each phase if gates missed
   - **Wave 3 (Mo 12–42):** Sites D & A using proven playbooks; Site A last-to-go-live (proven success at sisters mitigates town-dominant-employer risk)
   - **Kill Triggers:** Uptime <92%, pick rate <80% baseline, error >1,500 ppm ×24 hrs, safety >2 incidents/week → immediate rollback protocol

4. **Parallel-Run & Contingency Operating Procedures:**
   - 2–3 week parallel-run (automation picks 30% of orders, manual 70%; scale if successful to 50/50 by day 8)
   - Contingency staffing reserve: $146K labor + $664K buffer inventory across network
   - Kill-switch SLA: rollback live within 30 minutes (automated data recovery, manual picking resumed, WMS sync validated)
   - Customer SLA amendments signed (98% on-time → 95% on-time during cutover window, 2 weeks duration)

5. **Town-Dominant Site Workforce & Community Protection (Site A):**
   - **Commitment:** Public zero-layoff pledge (CEO-signed, Month 1 town hall, 200+ attendees, local media invited)
   - **Career Pathways:** Picker → Lead → Technician → Supervisor, with wage bands and credential requirements published on intranet
   - **Local Hiring:** 100% of 30 technician roles from region; $50K annual subsidy to community college for automation tech certificate
   - **Wage Floor:** $20/hr minimum by Month 24 (from $18 current); technician entry $24/hr, pathway to $28–32/hr over 5 years
   - **Quarterly Communication:** Town halls (Months 4, 7, 10, 13…42), public dashboard (automation progress at all sites, local hiring pipeline, wage increases), press releases timed to wins at sister sites
   - **Competitor Inoculation:** Document 5 differences from competitor failure (36-month phased vs. 6-week big-bang, modest capex, pilots first, manual fallback, transparent comms)

## WHAT WOULD MAKE US STOP

**Explicit Kill Triggers (Program Halt, Capex Pause, Board Re-Vote):**

1. **WMS Audit Reveals ≥20% Data Error Rate or Integration Incompatibility:** If SKU master data error >20% or WMS cannot feed real-time data to automation control systems without $400K+ custom development, pause capex approval. Redirect $1M to data cleanup & WMS replacement feasibility study (6-month delay acceptable). Board re-vote required.

2. **Wave 1 Pilot (Site B) Fails Any Gate by Month 12:**
   - Uptime <92% for 8+ weeks, or
   - Pick rate <80% of manual baseline sustained, or
   - Error rate >1,400 ppm at ramp end, or
   - Safety incidents >2 per week, or
   - Staff satisfaction survey <5/10
   - **Action:** Pause Wave 2 (Site C) capex approval; conduct 8-week root-cause investigation; mandatory remediation + retry. If retry fails, board decision to scale back (Tier 1 only at C, no AMRs; reprice capex & payback).

3. **Site C AMR Pilot (Phase 1B) Fails at Month 24:**
   - Uptime <95% (rolling 24-hr) for 4+ weeks, or
   - Pick accuracy <98.8%, or
   - Cost per pick >$1.50 (plan $1.30), or
   - Vendor cannot commit to live rollback SLA (<30 min downtime recovery)
   - **Action:** Kill AMR fleet purchase. Repurpose $650K capex to Site A Tier 1.5 acceleration (earlier go-live, no AMRs). Replan Sites D & A as Tier 1 only.

4. **Retraining Pass Rate Falls <65% at Any Site:**
   - If first cohort of 20–30 staff shows <65% certification pass rate, pause remaining cohorts. Conduct root-cause analysis (trainer quality, candidate selection, material difficulty, motivation collapse). If root cause unfixable, revert to external hiring model (increase technician external hire from 30 to 50 FTE; cost overrun $600K, payback slip 9 months).

5. **Competitor or Major Account Public Pressure Exceeds Mitigation Capacity:**
   - If national media covers automation announcement negatively (e.g., "another warehouse gearing up for mass layoffs like competitor") AND at least 2 of top-8 accounts publicly express concern (press quote or formal escalation), trigger emergency board session. Options: (a) accelerate communication timeline (town halls monthly instead of quarterly), (b) increase wage guarantees or retraining subsidy, (c) pause capex 6 months pending reputational recovery, (d) bring in external change-management consultant.

6. **Union Organizing Activity Detected at Any Site During Months 1–12:**
   - If union card-check or organizing petition filed, immediately engage labor counsel. Prepare effects-bargaining offer (wage floor +$2/hr, retraining subsidy +$1K, 5-year job security guarantee). If bargaining stalls >12 weeks, pause capex pending labor agreement. Estimated cost of effects bargaining: $300–500K; contingency budget must cover.

7. **Payback Assumptions Deteriorate >25% by Month 18 (Mid-Project Revalidation):**
   - By Month 18, first two waves should show actual data (uptime, ramp time, utilization, maintenance cost). If actual 3-year cumulative payback projects to >4.5 years (vs. 3.1-year plan), conduct re-forecasting. If re-forecasting confirms deterioration and reveals no near-term recovery path, board may elect to slow capex investment (defer Sites A & D, focus on steady operation of B, C, E). Decision triggers CFO+COO presentation to board with revised NPV model.

### B. Master Data Cleanup Project (Parallel to Vendor RFP, $120–150K, 12–16 Weeks, Starts Week 2)

**SKU Master Data Consolidation:**
- Identify duplicates (same item, multiple SKU codes): Site A 28K SKUs likely 3–5% duplicates = 840–1,400 records.
- Deactivate obsolete records (inactive >12 months, zero picks in past year): Site A likely 8–12% = 2,240–3,360 records.
- Validate core fields (UPC, weight, dimensions, case-qty, hazmat flag): 100% audit of records touching, random sampling of unchanged records (5% spot-check).
- Cost: Consultant + data validation staff, $3–6K per 1,000 records touched. Site A: $8–20K. Network: $30–50K.

**Inventory Accuracy Initiative:**
- Monthly cycle counts targeting 99%+ accuracy in 3 months (Months 1–3 of program).
- Focus on high-error zones (mezzanine, archive, low-velocity slow-mover racks).
- Hire temp staff for dedicated cycle-count work ($8–12K/month × 3 = $24–36K).
- Output: Inventory accuracy report by location type; publish to leadership weekly.

**Order/EDI Data Validation:**
- Validate customer data feeds (EDI, API, manual entry): test for format errors, mapping failures, qty/SKU mismatches.
- Site E (metro retailers, EDI-heavy): likely 1–3% errors; implement WMS validation checkpoint (orders flagged if confidence <95% before pick-release).
- Cost: $15–25K consulting; $10K WMS configuration.

**WMS Database Optimization:**
- Index tuning, archival of 12+ months old transactions, test data load cleanup.
- Prepare WMS for real-time automation control system integration (API endpoint testing, data format alignment, latency testing).
- Cost: $10–15K vendor professional services.

**Total Data-Cleanup Capex: $120–150K** (budgeted separately from automation capex; board approval required).
