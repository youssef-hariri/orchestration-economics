# Board Recommendation: Fund the Market Window Without Creating Solvency or Control Risk

## Executive recommendation

**Proceed with a first-phase acceleration funded by internal cash, downside-sized senior debt, and minority preferred equity.**

- **Internal cash** should fund preparation, customer retention, and low-risk bottleneck removal.
- **Senior debt** should fund only assets and working capital with demonstrable repayment capacity.
- **Minority preferred equity** should absorb uncertain ramp-up, market-entry, strategic hiring, and contingency risk.
- **Hybrid capital** should remain outside the base case and be considered only for a specific, value-protecting timing gap.
- **Disciplined organic growth** should remain the fallback if competitive urgency or financing terms do not justify acceleration.

The company should fund the **minimum credible acceleration plan**, not the maximum plan. Later investment must be optional and milestone-gated. A delayed ramp should lead to a slower growth plan—not a covenant breach, forced refinancing, excessive dilution, or loss of founder control.

This is conditional strategic approval, not approval of a final financing amount. Final sizing requires the company-specific operating model, competitive analysis, fully diluted capitalization table, and definitive financing terms.

---

## 1. Strategic decision rule

Proceed beyond internally funded preparation only if management demonstrates that delay creates a material and difficult-to-reverse risk involving specific:

- Strategic customers or contracts;
- Distribution channels;
- Scarce talent or supplier capacity;
- Customer switching or lock-in;
- Pricing position or market access.

Management should provide a competitive-window memorandum covering:

- Named accounts, channels, suppliers, or talent pools at risk;
- Current relationship status and strategic importance;
- The competitor’s expected deployment timeline;
- The company’s organic expansion timeline;
- Customer switching and lock-in dynamics;
- The expected impact of a six- and twelve-month delay;
- Whether the harm would be temporary or difficult to reverse.

If this evidence is not persuasive, restrict the program to internally funded bottleneck removal and reassess.

## 2. Comparison of financing alternatives

| Option | Principal advantage | Principal risk | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Senior debt** | Preserves ownership and founder upside; potentially lower nominal cost | Fixed payments, covenants, collateral, refinancing, and default risk during an uncertain ramp | Use selectively for identifiable asset and working-capital needs |
| **Minority equity** | Strongest liquidity protection; funds uncertain expansion without mandatory repayment | Dilution, liquidation preferences, governance rights, and exit economics | Make this the principal risk-bearing capital |
| **Hybrid instruments** | Speed and valuation deferral for a short bridge | PIK accrual, warrants, conversion dilution, maturity cliffs, and refinancing risk | Use only for a defined bridge; not core capital |
| **Organic growth** | Maximum control and balance-sheet resilience | Lost customers, talent, channels, pricing power, or market share | Retain as the fallback and use if external terms fail |

The blended structure is preferable because it avoids both extremes:

- An all-debt plan could convert a delayed ramp into a solvency event.
- An all-equity plan could crystallize unnecessary dilution at an unattractive valuation.
- Organic growth alone could surrender the market window.
- Hybrid capital may defer rather than solve the underlying funding requirement.

## 3. Proposed capital stack

Define the requirement before assigning sources:

\[
\text{Gross funding need}
=
\text{Expansion capex}
+\text{Incremental working capital}
+\text{Hiring and launch costs}
+\text{Contingency}
\]

\[
\text{External funding need}
=
\text{Gross funding need}
-\text{Cash available above the approved reserve}
\]

| Source | Appropriate uses | Allocation principle |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal cash** | Preparation, retention, initial hiring, and low-risk working capital | Use only cash above the approved liquidity reserve |
| **Senior debt** | Equipment, facilities, and predictable working capital | Lower of verified downside debt capacity or eligible uses with identifiable repayment |
| **Minority preferred equity** | Uncertain ramp, market entry, strategic hires, and contingency | Fund the residual risk-bearing requirement |
| **Hybrid capital** | A defined bridge before a priced round or cash-flow inflection | Preferably zero in the base case |
| **Organic growth** | Ongoing reinvestment and fallback execution | Preserve as a credible alternative |

Debt should ordinarily remain a minority of external funding unless revenue is highly recurring or contracted, margins are strong, customer concentration is manageable, and cash conversion is predictable. This is a planning principle, not a fixed ceiling.

Debt must be the lower of:

1. Cash-service capacity under the approved downside case; and  
2. Debt required for assets and working capital with a credible repayment path.

Equity must be sufficient to fund the uncertain portion of the first-phase plan and the modeled contingency. If that amount cannot be raised within acceptable dilution and governance terms, **reduce the plan rather than automatically adding debt or hybrid capital**.

### Dilution framework

The initial equity objective is approximately **10–15% post-money dilution**, calculated on a fully diluted basis. The calculation must include options, warrants, convertibles, and any option-pool refresh.

If the residual equity requirement cannot be raised within the target range:

1. Reduce or narrow the plan;
2. Improve valuation or financing terms;
3. Seek demonstrable strategic value;
4. Consider additional dilution only through a new board approval;
5. Add debt only if downside capacity supports it;
6. Revert to disciplined organic growth.

Any strategic value must be supported by specific customer, distribution, hiring, supply, or execution benefits.

## 4. Financing sequence and authority

### Phase 0: internal preparation

Management may proceed with:

- Demand validation and customer commitments;
- Capacity design, permitting, and supplier work;
- Customer-retention and service-quality measures;
- A limited number of high-leverage hires;
- Deposits and preparatory work required to secure capacity.

The board must approve a Phase 0 spending budget and minimum liquidity reserve before material commitments are made.

### Financing sequence

1. Complete the monthly 24–36-month base, downside, and severe-downside model.
2. Complete the competitive-window memorandum.
3. Obtain competing debt and equity term sheets.
4. Secure equity sufficient to absorb uncertain ramp risk.
5. Close debt simultaneously with or after equity unless the board approves otherwise.
6. Draw Tranche 1 only after all conditions are satisfied.
7. Require separate approval for Tranches 2 and 3 unless authority is later delegated against objective metrics.

Later equity may be relied upon only if it is:

- Legally committed and objectively drawable;
- Escrowed;
- Subject to an enforceable subscription option with defined pricing and expiry; or
- Already closed and available for staged deployment.

Uncommitted equity, undrawn debt subject to lender discretion, and financing dependent on subjective conditions do not count as liquidity or support binding expansion commitments.

### Source-failure rules

| Event | Required response |
|---|---|
| Debt closes but equity does not | Do not incur commitments dependent on equity; limit activity to internal cash and legally committed funds |
| Equity closes but debt does not | Re-size the plan; use equity for the highest-return and highest-risk-bearing uses; do not automatically replace debt with hybrid capital |
| Only partial funding is available | Fund only a self-contained bottleneck phase that preserves the liquidity reserve |
| Later financing becomes unavailable | Stop optional expansion, preserve cash, and return to organic growth |
| Tranche 1 succeeds but later milestones fail | Withhold subsequent tranches; do not increase leverage to compensate |

The company must be able to stop after Tranche 1 without default, mandatory redemption, penalty pricing, investor control escalation, or inability to maintain essential operations.

## 5. Milestone-based tranching

The tranche percentages below are planning placeholders applied to **external funding for the first-phase plan**. Actual amounts must be determined by the use-of-proceeds schedule, minimum viable bottleneck investment, contingency requirement, and ability to stop safely after each stage.

### Tranche 1: remove the bottleneck

**Uses**

- Capacity directly limiting current revenue;
- Essential operating, implementation, and technical hires;
- Customer-service protection;
- High-confidence working capital;
- Initial contingency.

**Release conditions**

- Demand is supported by signed contracts, backlog, or pipeline weighted by historical conversion;
- Equipment, facilities, and suppliers are contracted on acceptable terms;
- Key hires and implementation owners are identified;
- Capacity payback remains acceptable under delay and margin-stress cases;
- The downside model preserves the approved liquidity floor;
- Customer, supplier, and channel concentration remain within approved limits.

### Tranche 2: validate commercial scale

**Uses**

- Additional delivery capacity;
- Proven sales and marketing channels;
- Selected market or channel expansion;
- Further implementation resources.

**Release conditions**

Over the board-approved measurement period:

- Commissioned capacity reaches the utilization level required by the operating model;
- Incremental revenue tracks within the approved range;
- Contribution margin remains consistent with the investment case;
- Retention, churn, quality, and service levels remain within target;
- Working-capital consumption is consistent with the model;
- Customer-acquisition payback remains within the approved hurdle;
- No covenant breach is forecast under the base case.

### Tranche 3: optional acceleration

**Uses**

- Additional market-share capture;
- Adjacent products or geographies;
- Opportunistic hiring;
- Competitive-response initiatives.

**Release conditions**

- Core capacity economics are validated;
- Adequate downside liquidity remains after the draw;
- Growth, margin, and retention support further acceleration;
- Competitive conditions continue to justify speed;
- Expected returns exceed the board-approved hurdle.

A milestone miss defaults to withholding the next tranche. Management must submit a corrective plan before any exception is considered.

For each metric, management must document the definition, baseline, target, measurement period, data source, responsible executive, certification process, and consequence of a miss.

## 6. Financing terms and walk-away conditions

### Senior debt

**Required terms**

- Delayed draws aligned with milestones;
- Interest-only treatment through commissioning and early ramp where justified;
- Amortization beginning after the financed asset generates cash;
- Covenant headroom tested at the tightest forecast point;
- Cure rights, including equity cure where practical;
- Permitted additional debt and equity baskets;
- Prepayment flexibility;
- Limited collateral tied principally to financed assets;
- No founder personal guarantees;
- No broad material-adverse-change acceleration right;
- No cash sweep or cross-default that prevents continued investment or rescue financing.

**Hard walk-away conditions**

- Founder personal guarantees are required;
- Mandatory amortization begins before the funded asset generates cash;
- The facility prevents rescue equity or an orderly slowdown;
- A manageable operating setback can accelerate the facility without a realistic cure;
- Repayment requires refinancing in the approved downside case.

**Model-dependent limits**

Reject or reduce the facility if downside cash flow, peak leverage, maturity leverage, liquidity, or covenant headroom fail the thresholds approved after review of the operating model.

Initial negotiation benchmarks may include approximately:

- 1.5× base-case fixed-charge coverage;
- 1.25× downside coverage;
- 20% covenant headroom.

These are benchmarks, not final limits, until calibrated to company-specific data.

### Minority preferred equity

**Required terms**

- Approximately 10–15% post-money dilution as the initial target;
- 1× non-participating liquidation preference;
- No preference multiple above 1×;
- No mandatory or compounding dividend;
- No early redemption right;
- Broad-based weighted-average anti-dilution;
- One minority board seat or observer;
- Consent limited to fundamental matters;
- Sunset of enhanced rights below an agreed ownership threshold.

**Hard walk-away conditions**

- Participating preference;
- Full-ratchet anti-dilution;
- Investor board majority;
- Investor control over ordinary-course hiring, pricing, product, customer, or approved-budget decisions;
- Redemption or forced-sale rights during the ramp;
- Loss of founder voting control without separate board approval.

Any dilution above the target requires a new board approval supported by:

- Alternatives analysis;
- Strategic-benefit assessment;
- Fully diluted cap table;
- Founder-control analysis;
- Exit waterfall analysis.

### Hybrid capital

Use only for a documented timing gap where the value of speed exceeds its all-in cost.

Reject if:

- It cannot be repaid or refinanced in the downside case;
- Maturity or redemption precedes cash break-even;
- Conversion exceeds approved dilution limits;
- PIK, fees, warrants, conversion, and redemption economics are disproportionate;
- It has forced-sale rights or excessive seniority;
- It merely postpones a financing problem better addressed with equity.

Hybrid capital requires separate board approval regardless of size.

### Disciplined organic growth

Use the fallback if:

- The rival cannot deploy capital before the company can expand organically;
- Demand is not durable or is highly price-sensitive;
- Capacity utilization lacks credible support;
- Expansion payback is too uncertain or long;
- External terms create unacceptable solvency, dilution, or governance risk;
- Key accounts, talent, and channels can be preserved through incremental investment.

## 7. Liquidity safeguards and contingencies

The board must approve a minimum unrestricted liquidity reserve as both a dollar amount and a formula covering:

- Essential fixed costs;
- Taxes and leases;
- Mandatory debt service;
- Unavoidable working capital;
- The time required to reduce discretionary expenditure.

Management should test the reserve on the monthly forecast. Undrawn debt, uncommitted equity, and financing subject to subjective conditions do not count toward it.

Required stress testing should be at least as severe as historical volatility and identified exposure, including:

- Material revenue shortfall;
- Three-, six-, and twelve-month capacity delays;
- Margin compression;
- Major customer loss;
- Hiring delays;
- Working-capital deterioration;
- Higher rates or unavailable refinancing.

### Escalation triggers

- **Liquidity floor forecast to be breached:** stop discretionary spending and debt draws.
- **Covenant breach forecast:** engage lenders before breach and activate the cure plan.
- **Commercial milestone missed:** withhold the next tranche.
- **Severe downside:** suspend acceleration and return to organic growth.

Potential contingency tools include delayed-draw facilities, committed equity, equity cure rights, customer deposits, milestone billing, receivables financing, supplier-term negotiations, and pre-approved cost reductions.

---

## 8. Founder control and governance

Founders currently own 78%. A 15% post-money issuance would leave them with approximately 66% economic ownership before other dilution, but practical control depends on voting rights, board composition, class votes, and investor protections.

Before closing, counsel must provide a post-transaction governance schedule showing:

- Founder economic and voting ownership;
- Investor voting ownership;
- Board appointment and removal rights;
- Preferred-class votes;
- Reserved matters;
- Future financing consent rights;
- Option-pool treatment;
- Drag-along and forced-sale mechanics;
- Deadlock resolution;
- Rights-sunset thresholds.

The financing must preserve:

- Founder voting control;
- Founder appointment rights while above the agreed threshold;
- No investor board majority;
- No ordinary-course operating vetoes;
- Investor consent limited to fundamental matters;
- Ability to raise rescue equity or additional capital;
- No investor-controlled sale process.

Legal, tax, accounting, and fiduciary review are conditions to closing.

---

## 9. Required information before final sizing

Management must provide:

1. Current cash, debt, leases, taxes, and fixed obligations;
2. Fully diluted capitalization table;
3. Monthly 24–36-month revenue, EBITDA, free-cash-flow, and liquidity forecast;
4. Downside cash available for debt service;
5. Capacity cost, lead time, commissioning, utilization ramp, and payback;
6. Backlog, contracts, pipeline conversion, retention, churn, and customer concentration;
7. Gross and contribution margins by product, customer, and channel;
8. Hiring plan, compensation, and time to productivity;
9. Customer-acquisition cost, payback, and channel economics;
10. Competitor funding, deployment timing, customer overlap, and pricing response;
11. Founders’ minimum ownership, voting, board, and exit requirements;
12. Existing financing and shareholder restrictions;
13. Legal, tax, accounting, regulatory, and fiduciary implications of each instrument.

---

## Proposed board action

The board should:

1. **Approve Phase 0** within a specified internal-cash budget and approved liquidity floor.
2. **Approve the financing strategy** of internal cash, downside-sized senior debt, and minority preferred equity.
3. **Authorize management to solicit and negotiate term sheets only.**
4. Set the final debt ceiling from the approved downside cash-flow model.
5. Treat 10–15% fully diluted post-money dilution as the initial negotiation target; any exception requires a new board vote.
6. Prohibit hybrid capital without separate board approval.
7. Approve Tranche 1 only after the competitive-window, demand, liquidity, capacity-payback, governance, and financing conditions are certified.
8. Require separate approval for Tranches 2 and 3.
9. Require monthly reporting on cash, liquidity, capacity, utilization, revenue, margins, retention, working capital, covenants, and competitor activity.
10. Require legal, tax, accounting, and fiduciary sign-off before closing.
11. Direct management to revert to disciplined organic growth if competitive evidence, financing terms, or milestone performance do not justify acceleration.

## Final recommendation

**Act now, but finance only the first credible bottleneck. Use equity to absorb uncertainty, debt only where repayment is demonstrable, and preserve the right to slow down.** This approach best balances the competitive window with founder control, independence, and downside resilience.
