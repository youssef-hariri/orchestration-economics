**CONFIDENTIAL BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Executive Leadership Team / Turnaround Office  
**SUBJECT:** Industrial-Services Unit: Turnaround-or-Exit Assessment and 12-Month Strategic Plan  
**DATE:** October 26, 2023  

---

### **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**
The Industrial-Services Unit is trapped in a structural and commercial death spiral. The failure of the previous two restructurings demonstrates that this is not an operational efficiency problem; it is a fundamental business model and commercial alignment failure. Cutting costs within a shrinking revenue base, without fixing the underlying commercial engine, has only accelerated the decline—degrading service quality, driving away top talent, and further eroding the order book.

This memorandum provides a definitive, metrics-driven 12-month roadmap. It shifts the mandate from *internal cost reduction* to *portfolio rationalization, commercial restructuring, and group-level realignment*. We will execute a 6-month stabilization and pivot plan, culminating in a hard "Go/No-Go" decision gate at Month 6. This gate will determine whether to accelerate the turnaround or initiate a managed exit, ensuring enterprise value is preserved before further capital incineration.

---

### **PART 1: DIAGNOSTIC ASSESSMENT – ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS**

The persistent losses are **structurally driven** by a flawed group-level commercial model and **execution-driven** by a degraded commercial engine and internal talent cannibalization. 

**1. Structural Losses (The Root Cause)**
*   **Broken Transfer Pricing & Cross-Subsidization:** The unit serves customers who also buy from the Group’s profitable divisions. The unit has been forced to offer discounted, below-market rates to secure group-wide contracts, effectively acting as a loss-leader that subsidizes the profitable divisions. The unit’s P&L reflects this structural cross-subsidization.
*   **Negative Operating Leverage in a Commoditized Core:** The baseline services offered by the unit have become commoditized. As the order book shrinks, fixed overhead (management, IT, fleet) is spread across fewer dollars, mathematically guaranteeing margin destruction regardless of operational cost-cutting.
*   **The "Revenue Trap":** Previous restructurings maintained a focus on top-line revenue to absorb fixed overhead. Bidding on unprofitable, highly customized, or low-margin work simply to keep utilization high has trapped the unit in a cycle where every new contract destroys cash.

**2. Execution-Driven Losses (The Accelerant)**
*   **Internal Talent Cannibalization:** Top managers are transferring *within the Group* to the profitable divisions. The unit’s broken P&L makes it impossible for local managers to achieve their bonus targets, and the Group has lacked a mandate to stop internal poaching. This drains the unit of its most critical client relationships and operational expertise.
*   **Commercial Discipline Collapse:** A shrinking order book indicates a broken bid/no-bid discipline. The sales team has failed to pass through inflationary cost increases and is losing bids to more agile, specialized competitors.

**Verdict:** *Further pure cost-cutting is strictly prohibited.* The turnaround must focus exclusively on fixing the commercial engine, realigning group transfer pricing, and ruthlessly pruning the portfolio.

---

### **PART 2: PORTFOLIO RATIONALIZATION & TARGET SEGMENTS**

To achieve profitability, Management will abandon the "growth at all costs" mindset and focus exclusively on segments and service lines with high switching costs and clear paths to margin expansion.

**1. Customer Segment Strategy**
The customer base will be segmented using a "Switching Cost vs. Group Synergy" matrix:

*   **Segment A: Strategic Synergistic Accounts (High Profit Potential).** Overlapping customers where the unit provides mission-critical, integrated services alongside the profitable divisions. 
    *   *Action:* Renegotiate internal transfer pricing to ensure the unit receives fair market value for its contribution to group wins. 
    *   *Risk Mitigation:* To prevent anchor client churn during repricing, Management will bundle services and leverage the profitable divisions' relationships to lock in multi-year agreements, emphasizing integrated value over standalone price.
*   **Segment B: High-Switching-Cost Standalone Accounts (Core Profitable).** Customers exclusive to this unit where the unit has deep operational integration (e.g., embedded technicians, proprietary software integration, strict compliance requirements). 
    *   *Action:* Protect at all costs. Implement immediate price increases to inflation + margin. These are the foundation of the standalone turnaround.
*   **Segment C: Transactional/Price-Sensitive Accounts (Divest/Exit).** Overlapping or standalone customers who only buy commoditized, easily substitutable services. 
    *   *Action:* **Immediate exit.** Issue a "price-to-margin" notice. If the client refuses a +15% margin adjustment, initiate off-boarding.

**2. Service Line Rationalization**
*   **Scale & Invest (The Profitable Core):** Predictive/preventative maintenance, specialized compliance and safety services, and integrated facility management. These leverage economies of scale, feature high switching costs, and yield recurring, high-margin revenue.
*   **Divest & Kill (The Value Destroyers):** Reactive "break-fix" maintenance, low-tier commoditized labor supply, and highly customized, low-volume engineering projects. These consume management bandwidth, carry high liability, and destroy capital.

### **PART 3: 12-MONTH TURNAROUND PLAN & MILESTONES**

This plan is executed in three phases, governed by strict, measurable milestones. 

#### **Phase 1: Triage & Commercial Reset (Months 1–3)**
*Objective: Stop the cash bleed, lock in critical talent, and enforce commercial discipline.*
*   **Key Actions:**
    *   Implement immediate, ring-fenced retention packages (cash + guaranteed bonuses) for the top 15% of critical managers. *Note: These packages will be funded directly by Group HQ, not the unit’s depleted P&L, and enforced via a binding Group-level HR mandate prohibiting internal poaching.*
    *   Institute a strict, centralized "Bid/No-Bid" committee. Halt bidding on all new loss-making or highly customized RFPs.
    *   Audit the top 50 contracts. Invoke price-escalation clauses or initiate renegotiations.
*   **Month 3 Milestones:**
    *   [ ] Monthly cash burn reduced by 25% from baseline.
    *   [ ] 100% retention of the top 15% critical talent cohort.
    *   [ ] Zero new contracts signed below the newly established minimum gross margin threshold (25%).

#### **Phase 2: Portfolio Pruning & Operating Model (Months 4–6)**
*Objective: Rebuild the operating model around the rationalized portfolio and achieve cash break-even.*
*   **Key Actions:**
    *   Officially sunset Segment C customers and unprofitable service lines. Reallocate field technicians to Segment B and specialized services.
    *   Shift from a decentralized, project-based delivery model to a centralized, productized service delivery model to improve technician utilization rates.
    *   Finalize the new transfer pricing agreement with the Group’s profitable divisions for Segment A customers.
*   **Month 6 Milestones (The "Go/No-Go" Gate):**
    *   [ ] **Gross Margin Inflection:** Blended gross margin improved by a minimum of 400 basis points from baseline.
    *   [ ] **Order Book Stabilization:** Book-to-bill ratio ≥ 0.95 (bleeding has stopped).
    *   [ ] **Cash Break-Even:** Unit achieves cash-flow break-even on a monthly run-rate basis.

#### **Phase 3: Margin Expansion & Sustainment (Months 7–12)** *(Contingent on Month 6 "Go")*
*Objective: Prove the new model, expand margins, and transition to permanent leadership.*
*   **Key Actions:**
    *   Launch targeted cross-selling campaigns to Segment A clients, leveraging the profitable divisions' relationships to sell high-margin, specialized services.
    *   Implement strict weekly margin-tracking dashboards and tie 30% of management compensation directly to EBITDA margin expansion.
    *   Transition from interim turnaround leadership to permanent operational leadership.
*   **Month 12 Milestones:**
    *   [ ] Achieve a sustained **15% EBITDA margin**.
    *   [ ] Generate positive Free Cash Flow (FCF) for three consecutive months.
    *   [ ] Order book consists of >80% recurring, high-margin revenue.

---

### **PART 4: EXIT STRATEGY, ALTERNATIVES, AND DECISION CRITERIA**

If the turnaround fails to gain traction, Management will pivot to an exit to preserve residual value. The sunk-cost fallacy will not be tolerated.

#### **1. Hard Exit Triggers**
The Board will evaluate the following hard metrics. Failure to meet these triggers will immediately abort the turnaround and initiate an exit.
*   **Month 3 Trigger:** Monthly cash burn is not reduced by at least 15%, OR >15% of the critical talent cohort resigns/transfers.
*   **Month 6 Trigger:** Gross margin improvement is <300 bps, OR Book-to-bill ratio is <0.90.
*   **Month 12 Trigger:** EBITDA margin is <10%, or the unit fails to generate positive FCF.

#### **2. Exit Alternatives**
If an exit trigger is breached, leadership will execute one of the following alternatives, ranked by value preservation:

*   **Alternative A: Strategic Carve-Out & Trade Sale (Value Maximization).**
    *   *Mechanism:* Carve out the profitable core (Segment B customers + specialized/predictive maintenance service lines) and sell to an industry competitor or private equity roll-up. 
    *   *Rationale:* Strategic buyers will pay a premium for the high-switching-cost customer base and specialized technical talent, maximizing residual cash return to the Group.
*   **Alternative B: Internal Absorption & Liquidation (Synergy Capture).**
    *   *Mechanism:* Carve out Segment A (Strategic Synergistic) customers and absorb their contracts directly into the Group’s profitable divisions. Liquidate the remaining standalone assets, terminate leases, and wind down the legal entity.
    *   *Rationale:* Captures the cross-selling synergies for the Group while eliminating the unprofitable standalone overhead and commoditized service lines.
*   **Alternative C: Managed Harvest & Wind-Down (Last Resort).**
    *   *Mechanism:* If no internal integration or trade sale is viable, execute a controlled liquidation. Fulfill existing contracts to avoid penalty clauses, harvest remaining cash flows, and sell hard assets. 
    *   *Financial Requirement:* Management will establish a pre-funded terminal cash reserve (sourced from Group liquidity) to cover anticipated severance, lease breakages, and contract fulfillment penalties, ensuring the wind-down does not create secondary liabilities for the Group.

---

### **CONCLUSION & RECOMMENDATION**

The Industrial-Services Unit cannot be cost-cut its way to profitability. The previous restructurings treated a commercial and structural disease with an operational bandage. 

**Recommendation:** The Board should immediately approve the 12-Month Turnaround-or-Exit Plan. Management will execute the 90-day triage to stop the cash bleed, ring-fence critical talent from internal poaching, and enforce ruthless commercial discipline. By establishing hard, metrics-driven Go/No-Go gates at Months 3, 6, and 12, the Board will protect the Group from endless capital incineration while giving the unit a highly focused, commercially driven path to survival. If the unit fails to meet these gates, Management will immediately execute a carve-out sale or internal absorption.
