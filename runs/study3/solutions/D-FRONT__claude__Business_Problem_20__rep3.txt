## LEVER A: PROCESS IMPROVEMENT (Batch Elimination, SMED, Cellular Layout, Standard Work)

### A.1 Per-Order Release Policy

**Current Reality:** Orders batch by product line (5–20 units per campaign); early orders wait **0–14 days (median 7 days)** for batch fill.

**New Policy:** Release every order to fabrication **immediately** upon engineering approval and long-lead PO issue (or supplier component arrival). **Batch size = 1 (lot-of-1).** Finite-capacity scheduler optimizes batch size dynamically (see A.2).

**Setup Cost Impact:**
- Setup labor increases: ~$30/order (if 1-hr setup × $30/hr). **Payoff:** Each 1-week lead-time improvement ≈ +1 deal closed/month @ $50K margin = **$600K annual revenue uplift.** Setup cost is <1% of margin. ✓ ROI positive.

**Lead-Time Recovery: −7–14 days (median −7 days)** per order from elimination of batch-wait.

---

### A.2 Finite-Capacity Scheduler with Dynamic Batch Optimization

**Implementation Spec:**

| **Component** | **Specification** |
|---|---|
| **Software** | MES module (Parsable, Tulip, Plex) or ERP scheduler (SAP PP/DS, NetSuite PPM). Mid-market: $50–110K (license + implementation). |
| **Capacity Model Inputs** | Work-center capacities (fab, assembly, test, inspection); setup time per product-line transition; process time per unit; hand-off SLA max 4 hrs. |
| **Prioritization Logic** | (a) Order due date − lead-time-to-constraint = earliest release window. (b) Batch consecutive same-line orders within window. (c) Expedite flag: >3 days past due = priority. (d) If constraint conflicts, sort by due date (earliest first). |
| **Visibility Dashboard** | Real-time: orders queued per work center, WIP heatmap, long-lead component forecast, constraint status, SLA compliance. |
| **Pilot (Weeks 4–12 Phase 1)** | Top 3 SKUs (60% revenue); parallel-run with manual schedule; ramp to all SKUs if metrics green. |

## LEVER B: PROCUREMENT STRATEGY (Supplier Lead-Time Reduction, Dual-Source, Consignment/VMI)

### B.1 Early Forecast-Based PO (6–8 Weeks in Advance)

**Current Reality:** Customer order → Config lock → Procurement issues PO → Supplier 42-day lead time = 45+ days from order entry.

**New Strategy:** Sales/Operations forecasts high-probability opportunities (80%+ confidence, 6–8 weeks pre-customer-order); Engineering issues "speculative PO" for long-lead items; at customer order confirm, expedite or cancel PO.

**Lead-Time Recovery:**
- **P50:** Reduce supplier delivery from 42 days (post-PO) to **28 days (pre-positioned stock or partial delivery).** = **−14 days recovery.**
- **P95:** Reduces variability; P95 goes from 60 days to ~48 days (tighter tail).

**Risk Management:**
- Inventory risk: If customer order does NOT materialize, speculative buy becomes overstock (solution: cancel PO within cancellation window; negotiate with supplier ~5% cancellation fee).
- Forecast discipline: Requires S&OP accuracy >85% in rolling 12-week forecast (builds organizational discipline; high value regardless of lead-time project).

**Implementation Timeline:** Phase 2 (Months 4–9). Start with 50% of monthly demand (lower risk); ramp to 100% once forecast accuracy proven.

---

### B.2 Dual-Source Qualification (2nd Supplier for Critical Long-Lead Items)

**Purpose:** Create expedite fallback; reduce lead-time variability (P95 tightens if supplier 1 delays, can draw from supplier 2).

**Qualification Timeline (6–8 weeks, Phase 0 parallel to main work):**

| **Week** | **Activity** | **Owner** |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Supplier 2 selection (capability, references, ISO 9001 cert) | Procurement Lead |
| 1–2 | Design review + sample ordering | Quality + Engineering |
| 2–3 | Sample receiving & inspection (FAI-equivalent) | Quality |
| 4–6 | Pilot lot production (100 units); dimensional & performance test | Quality + Manufacturing Engineer |
| 7–8 | Sign-off; add to approved-supplier list | Quality Manager |

**Condition:** Supplier 2 brought online **only after full approval**, not before. Prevents unqualified supplier from causing field defects.

**Lead-Time Impact:** P95 supplier variability (60 days max vs. 42 days P50) tightens to ~48 days if dual-source fallback used. Doesn't improve P50 (both suppliers similar) but reduces worst-case exposure.

---

### B.3 Consignment Stock / Vendor-Managed Inventory (VMI) Pilots

**Mechanism:** Supplier holds finished-goods inventory (castings, motors, electronics) at customer facility or in supplier warehouse. Customer "pulls" from consignment stock on-demand; pays only for what's used; supplier replenishes automatically.

**Pilot Structure (Phase 2, Months 6–9):**
- Start with 1 supplier (e.g., Model A castings supplier) + 1 product family.
- Contract specifies: Supplier holds $50–100K inventory; payment on delivery (not upfront); Buyer has lot-tracking procedure; recall risk: Supplier reimburses if traceability lost.
- Pilot duration: 3–6 months; measure: stockout frequency, obsolescence rate, cost vs. standard PO.

**Lead-Time Benefit:** Access consignment stock faster than "order → build-to-order → deliver" cycle. Effective lead time becomes **"pull-from-consignment (1–2 days) + integrate into production (3–5 days)"** vs. **"order → 42-day supplier lead time → receive → integrate (48+ days)."**

**Working-Capital Impact:** Consignment shifts inventory from balance sheet to supplier (or reduces carrying cost via supplier rebate, e.g., 2–3% rebate on consignment-held inventory cost). **Net working-capital reduction: −$50–100K (Phase 2).**

---

### B.4 Demand Forecast Visibility to Suppliers

**Simple, High-Impact Action:** Share 6–12 month rolling demand forecast with long-lead suppliers.

**Benefit:** Supplier can produce to forecast (not wait for PO); smoother production planning on supplier side = more predictable delivery.

**Implementation:** Quarterly forecast update (Sales → Ops → Procurement → Supplier). Template: Expected monthly volume per SKU, 12 months ahead, with confidence bands (high/medium/low probability).

**Lead-Time Recovery:** −3–7 days (stabilizes variability; P95 tightens).

## LEVER C: SCHEDULING & INVENTORY MANAGEMENT (Batching Policy, CONWIP, Postponement)

### C.1 Eliminate Campaign-Batch Scheduling (Move to Per-Order Release)

**Combined with Lever A.1–A.2.** Per-order release + finite-capacity scheduler replaces campaign batching. **Recovery: −7–14 days.**

---

### C.2 CONWIP (Constant Work-In-Process) Discipline

**Mechanism:** Cap WIP in each cell/work center. When cap is reached, halt downstream fab release until work clears.

**Example (Model A Cell):**
- WIP cap: 3 units max in queue before mill.
- If queue reaches 3, fab shop stops releasing new orders to Model A cell (prioritize downstream completion).
- Result: Tighter inventory control; faster feedback on defects.

**Lead-Time Benefit:** Reduced queue depth (from 5–10 units typical to 3–5 units). Each day of queue reduction = **−1 day lead time (at low-throughput make-to-order rates).**

---

### C.3 Customer Order Decoupling Point (CODP) Segmentation (Phase 3, Optional Expansion)

**Not Required for 10-Week Target, but Optional for Further Compression:**

**Standard Variants (40–50% of volume):** Pre-position sub-assemblies or final-assembly "shells" in finished-goods buffer. Upon customer order, complete final configuration (paint, label, packaging) only. **Lead time: 7–10 days (skips long-lead supplier wait).**

**Custom Variants (50–60% of volume):** Make-to-order from customer order. Lead time: 10–14 weeks.

**Benefit:** If standard variants are pre-positioned, Sales can quote 7–10 weeks for these, capturing deals lost on lead time *without* requiring full-item inventory pre-build.

**Risk:** Requires demand forecast accuracy (>75% confidence on which variants to pre-position); inventory write-off risk if forecast misses. Not recommended unless forecast discipline is strong.

### D.3 Inspection Parallelization (No Rigor Loss, Only Queue Elimination)

**Mechanism:**

| **Gate** | **Current (Serial)** | **Proposed (Parallel)** | **Touch Time Impact** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In-Process Checkpoints** | Unit finishes assembly → waits for inspector → inspected → released | Inspector stationed at checkpoint; unit arrives → signed off immediately | Queue eliminated (4 hrs max SLA vs. 2–5 days wait) |
| **Functional Test & Burn-in** | 1–2 test stations; units serialize (1 unit per 72 hrs per station) | 3-station test lab; 2–3 units burning in parallel (start times staggered) | Wall-clock time reduced (3 units × 72 hrs each = 216 station-hrs over 3 stations = 72 hrs calendar time if sequential arrival) |
| **FAI & Final Cert** | Quality schedules; batch arrivals cause queue | Quality pre-schedules inspection windows (daily calendar); orders flow through in scheduled slot (no arrival randomness) | Predictable timing; no queue |

### D.5 Rollback Triggers: When to STOP Compression & Revert

**Hard Stops (Executed Immediately):**

| **Condition** | **Threshold** | **Response** | **Authority** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quality Escape (Assembly Defects)** | FPY drops >10% at any work center in 1-week average | HALT all compression work; revert to pre-compression process until FPY recovers to baseline; 5-day root-cause investigation mandatory | Quality Manager (with Ops Manager notification) |

| **Function**
