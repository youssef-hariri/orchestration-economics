## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The transition from perpetual licenses to subscription revenue is a reinvention of the Company’s economic engine. The primary failure mode—demonstrated by the competitor that hemorrhaged 20 percent of its installed base—is forced entitlement revocation. This plan rejects abrupt sunsetting in favor of a dual-run, opt-in architecture that honors historical purchases.

**The Hard Truth:** Under the validated Base Case, GAAP revenue will trough at **20–30 percent below the current $100 million baseline** for approximately 24–30 months. Recovery to the prior $100 million revenue peak occurs in **Year 5** in the Base Case (Year 3 under Conservative assumptions, Year 5+ under Aggressive). Operating cash flow, after funding sales-transition pools, migration incentives, and debt-forbearance costs, will decline **15–20 percent** despite multi-year prepays. During the transition, the Company will almost certainly trade in a **valuation penalty box** at a hybrid multiple (3–5x revenue) until subscription mix exceeds 60 percent and GAAP growth re-accelerates.

**What Makes This Executable:** Five structural commitments separate this plan from the competitor’s failed abrupt switch:
1. **Anti-Gaming Sales Economics:** Perpetual exceptions for new logos require CEO approval, carry a **160 percent price premium**, and pay reps only a **2 percent tombstone commission**. A rep earns more on a standard subscription deal than on a gamed exception.
2. **Funded Income Protection:** An explicit **$5 million Transition Income Protection Pool** (sized for 75 reps, scalable) guarantees that no performing rep falls below 85 percent of prior trailing-twelve-month earnings for 18 months.
3. **Sales Capacity Model:** The plan explicitly recognizes that reps must close **35–40 subscription deals per year** versus 12–15 perpetual deals historically, and it mandates inside-sales expansion, 3x demand-generation throughput, and a 60-day cycle target to make the math feasible.
4. **Sustainable Customer Economics:** Loyalty credits are capped at 12 months of current maintenance and 50 percent of first-year ACV, eliminating the $150 million+ cumulative-liability risk. A dilutive-converter cohort (accounts where maintenance exceeds the ACV cap) is flagged and monitored.
5. **Hard Governance Triggers:** If GAAP revenue trough breaches 35 percent, perpetual churn exceeds 5 percent, or the hybrid technical bridge slips beyond 90 days, the Board Transition Committee is empowered to pause acceleration and diagnose.

## 1. OFFER STRUCTURE & MIGRATION INCENTIVES: THE CUSTOMER SOVEREIGNTY MODEL

**Core Principle:** Customers who bought perpetual licenses own them in perpetuity. The Company will earn the migration through superior value, not enforce it through coercion.

### 1.1 Perpetual-Fallback Rights
Any customer who migrates to subscription and later lapses retains a perpetual license to the last on-premise version they held prior to subscribing. Data export in non-proprietary formats and read-only API access are guaranteed in perpetuity. This eliminates vendor-lock-in anxiety; subscription adoption must be a continuous value decision, not a hostage scenario.

### 1.2 Loyalty Credit Program (Capped, Non-Cumulative)
Customers receive a credit equal to **12 months of their current annual maintenance rate**, applied dollar-for-dollar against the first 12 months of subscription fees. The credit is **hard-capped at 50 percent of first-year ACV** (i.e., the Founders’ Rate amount, not list price).

*Typical Example:* A customer paying $15,000 maintenance converting to a $40,000 list / $30,000 Founders’ Rate subscription receives a $15,000 credit, paying $15,000 net in Year 1—flat to maintenance.

*Dilutive Edge Case:* Approximately 20 percent of the installed base pays maintenance above the 50 percent ACV cap (e.g., $25,000 maintenance on a $30,000 Founders’ Rate deal, credit capped at $15,000). These accounts generate net revenue roughly 40 percent below prior maintenance and deepen the trough by an estimated 2–3 percentage points. They are flagged as a discrete “high-maintenance converter” cohort in forecasting.

The Founders’ Rate is the only other discount. The previously proposed 1.5× multiplier is eliminated. Credits are recorded as contra-revenue. **Gross ARR** reports full subscription value to show adoption velocity; **Net ARR** and GAAP revenue reflect the credit economically.

### 1.3 Dual-Run (Hybrid) Options
Customers keep their perpetual core and subscribe to cloud-native modules, AI layers, advanced analytics, or premium support. Three dedicated engineering sprint teams will deliver:
- **Month 4:** API gateway and entitlement microservices live in production.
- **Month 6:** Closed beta with 10 friendly enterprise accounts.
- **Month 9:** General availability of the hybrid connector.

Total engineering budget is **$4.0 million** ($2.5 million base + $1.5 million contingency). If the bridge slips beyond Month 10, the Company pivots to a **managed-edge architecture** (subscription modules in a VPC adjacent to the on-prem core) with a contractual performance SLA of ≥99.5 percent uptime for hybrid data sync.

### 1.4 Grandfathered Maintenance Pathways
- **Years 1–5:** Maintenance (bug fixes, security patches, compliance updates) available at inflation-indexed rates (CPI + 1 percent). No punitive hikes.
- **Year 6+ (Soft Landing):** Maintenance transitions to **Extended Security Support (ESS)**. Price resets to **125 percent of the Year 5 rate**. Scope narrows to critical security patches and regulatory-mandated updates only. No feature access, no non-critical bug fixes. Customers may remain indefinitely, but the value gravity shifts decisively to subscription.

**Customer Communication Timeline:**
- **Month 6:** Publicly publish the Year 6+ ESS pathway so no customer is surprised.
- **Month 30:** Direct outreach to all maintenance-only accounts reminding them of the timeline and offering conversion consultations.
- **Month 54:** Final ESS opt-in renewal (first ESS contracts begin).

**Maintenance Respect Program:** To prevent resentful abandonment sentiment, maintenance-only customers receive quarterly security briefings, transparent view-only roadmap webinars, and priority support escalation rights. They are valued customers choosing a stability path, not a stranded cohort.

### 1.5 Active Choice Architecture at Renewal
At every maintenance or support renewal event, present three explicitly neutral options:
1. **Renew Maintenance / ESS** (status quo, grandfathered rights).
2. **Subscribe with Loyalty Credit** (migrate with capped, time-bound discount).
3. **Adopt Perpetual+ Hybrid** (core stays, new capabilities subscribe).

No automatic conversions. Sales and Customer Success guide; the customer decides.

### 1.6 Data & Entitlement Guarantees
- Perpetual licenses retain API access at historical levels.
- Customer data remains fully exportable in non-proprietary formats.
- Legal documentation of these rights is published publicly to counter any “bait and switch” market narrative.

## 2. PRICING DESIGN: BALANCING TROUGH DEPTH AGAINST ADOPTION VELOCITY

### 2.1 Introductory “Founders’ Rate” Pricing
During the first 18 months, early movers lock in subscription pricing at **75 percent of long-term target ACV** (a 25 percent discount). The rate is contractually protected for 36 months, after which pricing steps up to standard rates. This is the single, time-bound price incentive; it does not stack with unsustainable credit multipliers.

### 2.2 Sustainable Conversion Economics
The Loyalty Credit (1:1 of 12 months current maintenance, capped at 50 percent of Year 1 ACV) remains available for 36 months; after Month 36, new conversions receive standard commercial discounts only (Finance approval required).

A typical converter generates Year-1 GAAP revenue roughly flat to their prior maintenance stream. High-maintenance converters are the exception and are modeled separately.

### 2.3 Hybrid Pricing Anchors
Subscription modules in Dual-Run are priced as **incremental ACV**—new money, not replacement revenue. No credit applies to the perpetual core. Customers perceive additive value while retaining sunk-cost protection.

### 2.4 New-Logo Policy (Anti-Gaming Architecture)

| Phase | Timeline | Policy | Exception Criteria |
|-------|----------|--------|-------------------|
| **Phase 1** | Months 1–12 | Subscription-default. Perpetual available **only by written CEO approval**. | Customer provides written regulatory or legal justification; deal priced at **160 percent of historical perpetual list**; commission reduced to 2 percent. Hard cap: 5 percent of new logos. |
| **Phase 2** | Month 13+ | **Hard subscription-only.** No perpetual licenses for new logos. | None. |

Because a subscription deal pays 10 percent ACV plus new-logo bounty, while a perpetual exception pays 2 percent of a hard-to-approve deal, the rational rep aggressively sells subscription.
