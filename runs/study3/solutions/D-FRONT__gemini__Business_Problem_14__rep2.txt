# Global Inclusivity & Pluriversal Design Guidelines

## 1. Executive Vision
To achieve success in a global market, our product must move beyond "Universal Design"—which often centers on Western norms—and embrace **Pluriversal Design**. Our goal is to create a digital ecosystem that is technically accessible to all, yet locally resonant and culturally sensitive to the diverse social, linguistic, and communal structures of our users.

---

## 2. Governance: Localizing Decision-Making
We reject centralized, Western-led bias definitions. Our development lifecycle now prioritizes local expertise.

*   **Local Advisory Circles (LACs):** Before deployment in any new market, local experts (educators, social scientists, and community leaders) must validate the product’s core logic.
*   **The "Technological Imperialism" Test:** Every feature is subject to the following mandatory audit: *“Does this feature require the user to change their fundamental behavior or social values to match our platform’s logic?”* If the answer is yes, the feature must be redesigned for local compatibility or made optional.
*   **Privacy-First Flexibility:** In regions where personal disclosure (gender, religion, identity) poses security risks, users must have the agency to opt-out of data fields without sacrificing core functionality.

---

## 3. Communicative & Linguistic Nuance
We recognize that "Plain Language" is a Western preference that does not always suit high-context, formal cultures.

*   **Adaptive Formality Toggles:** Implement UI architectures that allow for shifting levels of formality. In regions where honorifics are essential (e.g., Japan, Korea, Arabic-speaking nations), the interface should default to respectful, formal tones rather than direct, casual ones.
*   **Contextual Translation:** Avoid "forced neutrality." In languages with strictly gendered grammar, work with local linguists to find natural, non-offensive phrasing rather than applying awkward, break-the-grammar symbols.

---

## 4. Structural Design: The Communal Shift
We move away from the "Autonomous Individual" as the only user type.

*   **Communal Account Architecture:** For markets where household devices are shared, prioritize **Profile Switching** as a core, high-visibility feature, allowing different users to maintain personal history and preferences within a single login environment.
*   **Fluid Navigation:** Avoid "Western-linear" progress flows. In cultures favoring holistic, cyclical, or non-linear information gathering, provide "Explorer" interfaces that support intuitive, non-sequential content discovery.

---

## 5. Technical Accessibility Standards
Accessibility is our technical bedrock; cultural adaptation is our design skin.

*   **Universal Bedrock:** All products must meet **WCAG 2.2 Level AA** to ensure baseline usability for individuals with sensory, physical, or cognitive disabilities.
*   **Semantic Token System:** Rather than hard-coding color or symbol meanings, use a **Semantic Token System.** This allows the system to automatically swap colors (e.g., changing "mourning" or "success" colors) and icons based on the user's regional style guide.
*   **Accessibility vs. Bloat:** To ensure performance in low-bandwidth regions, we will maintain a lean code architecture, prioritizing high-impact accessibility features (Screen reader support, CC, transcriptions) while ensuring that cosmetic "customization" settings do not compromise load times.

## 7. Closing Directive
A truly inclusive product does not just translate its language; it adapts its **logic** to respect the diverse ways humans organize their societies and families. By empowering local stakeholders to guide our design, we create a product that is not just globally accessible, but locally native.
