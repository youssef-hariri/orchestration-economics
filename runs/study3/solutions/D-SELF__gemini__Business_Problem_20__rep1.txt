**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Executive Leadership Team
**FROM:** Operations Transformation Task Force
**DATE:** October 26, 2023
**SUBJECT:** Operations Improvement Plan: Lead-Time Reduction & Quality Stabilization

---

### 1. Executive Summary
To restore market competitiveness, we are transitioning from a linear, serialized production model to a **Decoupled "Configure-to-Order" (CTO) framework**. This plan targets a reduction in lead times from 14 weeks to 6 weeks, protected by a hard-logic quality governance system. By synchronizing Sales, Production, and Quality through shared KPIs and systemic process constraints, we will eliminate the "blame culture" that has historically compromised our delivery speed and product integrity.

### 2. Operational Strategy: The Decoupling Point
We will shift the manufacturing critical path by decoupling sub-assembly from final customization:
*   **Inventory Buffers:** We will shift to building pre-certified "Base Modules," allowing us to assemble final customer configurations on demand. 
*   **Parallel Processing:** Engineering and software validation will occur concurrently with mechanical assembly, removing the traditional "wait-for-finish" bottleneck.
*   **Sales Guardrails:** A standardized "Configurator Menu" will govern sales quotes. Customizations outside this menu will trigger a "Complexity Surcharge" and extended lead times, ensuring the core 6-week flow remains undisturbed.

### 3. Quality Assurance: "Hard-Logic" Governance
To avoid the warranty spikes of the past, we are transitioning from end-of-line inspections to an **In-Process Quality Model**:
*   **MES Hard Logic:** We are deploying Manufacturing Execution System (MES) software that locks subsequent assembly stages if the preceding quality verification (e.g., torque check, software flash) is not logged in the system.
*   **Burn-In Preservation:** The final stress-test/burn-in phase is declared a "non-negotiable" process step. Time saved through parallel processing will be reinvested into more rigorous, longer-duration quality testing.
*   **Supplier Synchronization:** We will move critical suppliers to a Vendor Managed Inventory (VMI) model, requiring Certificate of Conformance (CoC) documentation for all shipments to prevent the introduction of unverified components.

### 4. Cross-Functional Alignment & Incentives
To break the cycle of inter-departmental finger-pointing, we are restructuring the performance management framework:
*   **Shared Metrics:** Departmental silos will be dissolved in favor of a unified **"Order-to-Cash Velocity with Zero Defects"** scorecard.
*   **Governance:** An S&OP Steering Committee will meet bi-weekly. All process deviations will be addressed using **A3/5-Whys Problem Solving**, explicitly shifting the focus from individual accountability to systematic root-cause elimination.
*   **Cultural Shift:** Empowering employees with "Stop-Work Authority"—supported by a "Quality Champion" incentive—ensures that the drive for speed never supersedes the necessity of compliance.

### 5. Implementation Roadmap (90-Day Sprint)

| Phase | Timeline | Critical Milestone |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase I: Stabilization** | Days 1–30 | Launch "Configurator Menu," finalize Surcharge policy, and audit/re-certify key suppliers. |
| **Phase II: Process Shift** | Days 31–60 | Deploy MES "Hard Logic" in pilot assembly stations and initiate Process Capability ($C_{pk}$) testing. |
| **Phase III: Execution** | Days 61–90 | Full cutover to 6-week cadence; establish bi-weekly S&OP Steering Committee cadence. |

### 6. Closing Statement
This plan does not simply speed up our current process; it redesigns our operational DNA. By implementing these constraints, we ensure that our velocity is a result of **process efficiency** rather than **rushed execution**. We are prepared to execute this transition immediately to reclaim our market position.
