# COMPREHENSIVE OPERATIONS IMPROVEMENT PLAN
## Industrial Equipment Manufacturer | 14-Week to 8-Week Lead-Time Compression
### Executive Leadership & Cross-Functional Implementation Strategy

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### The Business Opportunity
- **Current State:** 14-week quoted lead time; 1-in-3 deals lost on delivery speed alone ($4.2M–$4.9M annual revenue loss)
- **Competitor Baseline:** 6-week lead time
- **Target State:** 8-week lead time (realistic, competitive, addresses customer pain threshold)
- **Expected Impact:** +40–50% improvement in deal-win rate; +$1.8M–$2.5M incremental annual revenue
- **Quality Commitment:** ASME/ISO 9001 certification maintained; zero regulatory compromise; rework prevention (not gate removal)

### Root Cause Analysis
The 98-day cycle comprises:
- **42 days (43%):** Supplier procurement (structural; partially compressible via inventory + planning)
- **8 days (8%):** Production queue waste (fully compressible via FIFO scheduling)
- **5–12 days (embedded):** Handoff friction (fully compressible via concurrent processes)
- **18 days (18%):** Fabrication value-add (inherent; minimal compression opportunity)
- **6 days (6%):** Quality gates (correctly sequenced; not a bottleneck)
- **3–14 days (variable):** Rework cycles from quality failures (preventable via upstream design review, supplier SLA, process capability)
- **12 days (12%):** Logistics (partially compressible via expedited shipping)

**Critical Finding:** The previous compression attempt removed FAI steps (quality gate shortcuts) → $720K warranty cost over 3 years. This plan eliminates waste *around* quality gates, not gates themselves.

### Compression Strategy: Four Parallel Initiatives

| Initiative | Current State | Target State | Lead-Time Savings | Owner | Timeline |
|-----------|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Supplier Lead-Time Compression** | 42 days | 28–31 days | 7–10 days | Procurement | Months 1–6 |
| **Queue Elimination (FIFO + Expedite Protocol)** | 8 days | 1–2 days | 6–8 days | Production | Weeks 2–12 |
| **Handoff Friction Removal** | 5–12 days | 1–2 days | 5–7 days | Product Manager | Weeks 1–8 |
| **First-Pass Yield Improvement** | 6–9% rework rate | 4–5% target | 1.5–3 days (avg) | Quality | Weeks 4–16 |
| **Logistics Optimization** | 12 days | 3–5 days (expedited) | 7–9 days (customer option) | Logistics | Weeks 8–12 |

**Net Lead-Time Compression:** 98 days → 54–60 days realistic (targeting 56 days = 8 weeks)

### Investment & Financial Impact
- **Capital Investment:** $180K–$300K (inventory buffer, scheduling tools, rework cell setup)
- **Annual Operating Cost:** +$110K–$130K (staffing, training, supplier engagement)
- **Annual Revenue Recovery:** +$1.8M–$2.5M (from deal-loss baseline)
- **Gross Margin Improvement:** +$400K–$550K/year (at 22% standard margin)
- **Payback Period:** 9–12 months (Year 2 achieves full ROI)
- **Warranty Risk Mitigation:** Rework prevention strategy estimated to reduce warranty costs by $15K–$30K/year (vs. prior risk of $720K over 3 years)

## PART A: ROOT CAUSE & TIME-LOSS DEEP DIVE

### A1. Time-Loss Bucket Analysis (Where the 98 Days Vanish)

**BUCKET #1: SUPPLIER PROCUREMENT (42 days = 43% of cycle)**

**Root Causes:**
- ProGear gearing (longest supplier): 38–42 days manufacturing + ocean transit + customs (structural dependency)
- TierOne forgings (Germany): 35–40 days (geographic distance + ocean logistics)
- FlexHydro manifold: 28–32 days (design-lock requirement delays PO by 3–5 days)
- No buffer stock strategy (each order triggers full supplier cycle)
- No rolling forecast (suppliers receive POs reactively, after design sign-off)
- FAI prototype orders for new designs add 5–12 days to first-order lead time

**Controllable Opportunities:**
- Design-lock to PO latency: 3–5 days (can eliminate via concurrent BOM development) ✓
- FAI pre-qualification: 5–12 days on first orders (can eliminate for repeat designs by pre-qualifying tooling) ✓
- Supplier quality rework: 1–3 days per incident on ~6% of orders (addressable via SLA enforcement) ✓
- Safety stock investment: Holding 2–3 units of critical components would reduce effective lead time from 42 days to 7–10 days (tradeoff: capital tie-up vs. delivery predictability) ✓

**Compression Target:** 42 days → 28–31 days (7–10 day savings)

---

**BUCKET #2: PRODUCTION QUEUE TIME (8 days = 8% of cycle)**

**Root Causes:**
- Batch-size scheduling: Orders grouped into 3–5 unit batches; single-unit orders wait 2–4 days for batch completion
- Work-center capacity constraints: Specific machines (CNC, test stands) have full queues; jobs stack up
- No dedicated rework path: Failed units rejoin main production queue, adding 4–6 day delays
- Manual ad-hoc scheduling: No optimization algorithm; no FIFO discipline
- No visibility between Sales and Production: Emergency rush orders preempt planned schedule, cascading delays

**Why This Is 100% Waste:**
- No value-added work occurs during queue time
- Units sit idle waiting for work-center availability
- Queue time is pure scheduling inefficiency (not inherent to processes)

**Compression Target:** 8 days → 1–2 days (6–8 day savings via FIFO scheduling)

---

**BUCKET #3: FABRICATION VALUE-ADD (18 days = 18% of cycle)**

**Breakdown:**
- Machining (engine block, shafts): 7 days (inherent equipment speed; little compression opportunity)
- Sub-assembly (manifold, wiring): 6 days (some pre-assembly possible with supplier; 1–2 day potential)
- Final assembly (integration, calibration): 5 days (custom per variant; modularity could save 0.5–1 day)
- Embedded in-process inspection: 0.5–1 day (overlapped; no sequential delay)

**Compression Target:** 18 days → 17–18 days (minimal; 0.5–1 day realistic)

---

**BUCKET #4: QUALITY GATES (6 days cumulative = 6% of cycle)**

**Gates (correctly sequenced):**
- Incoming Material QC (Gate #1): 1 day
- First-Article Inspection (Gate #2, new designs only): 2 days
- Hydrostatic Pressure Test (Gate #3): 1.5 days
- Final Performance Validation (Gate #4): 1 day
- Certification Sign-Off (Gate #5): 0.5 day

**Critical Finding:** Gates themselves total only 6 days and are largely embedded (not purely sequential). However, **rework cycles triggered by gate failures add 3–14 days per incident (6–9% of orders experience rework).**

**Quality-Safe Compression Strategy:**
- Parallelize documentation prep during pressure test cool-down: Save 0.5–1 day
- Move FAI to design phase for new designs; waive manufacturing FAI for repeat orders: Save 2 days on first order; 2 days on repeat orders
- Deploy SPC to detect process drift early: Prevent rework incidents

**Compression Target:** 6 days sequential gates + rework buffer → 3–4 days effective (via parallelization + prevention)

---

**BUCKET #5: HANDOFF & ORGANIZATIONAL FRICTION (5–12 days embedded)**

**Root Causes:**
- Sequential design → BOM → procurement workflow: 3–5 day latency before supplier clock starts
- No pre-approved supplier list: Each RFQ requires manual sourcing; selection takes 2–3 days
- Manual change-order loops: No standard change process; escalation adds 3–5 days per change (~20% of orders affected)
- No expedite protocol: Each rush request requires manager escalation (1–2 day decision delay)
- Quality & Production operate independently: No integrated scheduling of quality gates with production timeline

**Compression Target:** 5–7 day baseline + 3–5 day change/expedite overhead → 1–2 days (via concurrent engineering, change control board, expedite protocol)

---

**BUCKET #6: LOGISTICS & PACKAGING (12 days = 12% of cycle)**

**Breakdown:**
- Packaging & documentation: 2–3 days (non-value-add)
- Carrier pickup scheduling: 1 day (dock coordination)
- Outbound transit (ground freight baseline): 9 days
- Finished-goods hold (batch shipment strategy): 2–5 days (cost optimization; extends lead time)

**Compression Target:** 12 days → 3–5 days (via expedited shipping when customer needs faster delivery; 15–25% cost premium)

## PART B: CROSS-FUNCTIONAL IMPROVEMENT STRATEGY & IMPLEMENTATION

### B1. QUALITY ASSURANCE FRAMEWORK: PROTECTING CERTIFIED GATES

**Critical Principle:** This plan does NOT remove quality gates. All ASME/ISO 9001 requirements remain hard stops.

#### Quality-Gate Validation Matrix

| **Gate** | **Regulatory Requirement** | **Current First-Pass Rate** | **Failure Consequence** | **Compression Strategy** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Incoming Material Verification** | ISO 9001 document control | 96% | Supplier non-conformance discovered in field (warranty: $50K–$150K per incident) | Move to in-process during material staging (parallelize with receiving; no delay added) |
| **First-Article Inspection (FAI)** | ASME for new designs | 91% | Supplier tooling defect cascades to all units; design margin insufficient | MOVE to design phase (pre-production) for new designs; WAIVE for repeat orders if pre-qualified; maintain same rigor, earlier timing |
| **Dimensional Inspection (Mid-Cycle)** | Internal control point (not regulatory) | 98% | Tolerance stack-up propagates to pressure test | Keep embedded in-process; already non-sequential |
| **Hydrostatic Pressure Test** | ASME safety-critical performance validation | 94% | Unit released with inadequate safety margin; field failure possible; liability exposure | KEEP as hard gate; NON-NEGOTIABLE; cannot compress or sample; every unit must pass rated pressure |
| **Final Performance Curve Validation** | Certification requirement | 93% | Performance deviation not documented; customer dispute | KEEP as hard gate; optimize: parallelize documentation prep during pressure-test cool-down (0.5 day saved) |
| **Certification Sign-Off** | ISO 9001 traceability requirement | 99.5% | Audit failure; production halt | KEEP as hard gate; optimize: pre-stage documentation during testing; final sign-off same-day |
| **Third-Party Audit** | ASME/ISO regulatory oversight | 100% (if cert valid) | Audit failure cascades; customer notified; reputational damage | KEEP as hard gate; external timeline; no compression possible |

**Quality-Gate Conclusion:**
- All gates remain; none removed or weakened
- Optimization opportunities: Move FAI pre-production, parallelize documentation prep, enhance supplier quality (SLA) to reduce failure rates
- Rework prevention (via design review, SPC, supplier SLA) reduces gate-failure incidents, not gate time itself

#### Quality Rework Root Causes & Prevention Strategy

| **Rework Scenario** | **Frequency** | **Current Cycle Impact** | **Root Cause** | **Prevention Strategy** | **Target Compression** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FAI rejection (new design)** | 9% of new designs | +6 days (design/tooling revision) | Design not validated; supplier tooling defect | Pre-design FAI prototype (move to design phase); design review checklist (tolerance stack-up, FEA validation, supplier risk assessment) | Eliminate from manufacturing cycle; move to pre-production |
| **Pressure test failure** | 6% of orders | +5 days (disassembly/diagnosis/repair/re-test) | Assembly error (1–2%), material defect (1–2%), design sensitivity (2–3%) | Process capability study (identify root causes), mistake-proofing in assembly (poka-yoke fixtures), SPC monitoring, supplier SLA enforcement | Compress via dedicated rework cell from 5 days to 2–3 days; prevent via upstream controls |
| **Incoming material defect** | 4% of orders | +1–3 days (supplier contact, rework decision, material resourcing) | Supplier process drift; dimensional/material non-conformance | Supplier SLA enforcement (<2% defect rate); annual supplier audits; enhanced incoming inspection for high-risk suppliers | Reduce frequency; compress resolution cycle to <1 day |
| **Certification/documentation failure** | <1% of orders | +14 days (audit remediation, re-submission) | Audit finding; missing documentation; design non-compliance | Pre-audit checklist; documentation template; quality review before third-party audit submission | Prevent via upstream gate discipline; target: 0 occurrences |

**Quality Risk Mitigation Plan:**
1. **Design Review Gate (Pre-Release):** Tolerance stack-up analysis, FEA validation, pressure-margin confirmation, supplier risk assessment (identifies design flaws before manufacturing)
2. **Supplier SLA Enforcement:** Defect-rate target <2%; on-time delivery 98%; annual audits; escalation for deviation (prevents supplier-sourced failures)
3. **Process Capability Study:** Analyze last 50 pressure-test failures; root-cause each; implement corrective actions (prevents repeat failures)
4. **Statistical Process Control (SPC):** Monitor pressure-test results, assembly rework incidents; detect trends early (prevents cascade to field failures)
5. **Rework Cell:** Dedicated technician diagnoses failures; documents root cause; feeds continuous improvement (prevents repeat incidents)

**Expected Quality Outcome:**
- First-pass yield improvement: 91–99% baseline → 95%+ target (via prevention)
- Warranty claim costs: <$150K/year (within normal 0.5–1% product cost allowance; vs. prior risk of $720K over 3 years if prevention fails)
- Regulatory compliance: 100% maintained; zero audit findings

### B2. FOUR-PART IMPROVEMENT STRATEGY

#### INTERVENTION #1: SUPPLIER LEAD-TIME COMPRESSION (42 days → 28–31 days)

**Strategy Pillars:**

**1.1 Strategic Inventory Buffer for Long-Lead Components**
- **What:** Hold 2–3 units each of three critical components (ProGear gearing, TierOne forgings, FlexHydro manifold)
- **Why:** Eliminates effective supplier lead-time from 42 days to 7–10 days (materials in-house; only downstream queue + fabrication remain)
- **Investment:** $180K–$250K additional inventory (carrying cost ~15% annually = $27K–$37K/year)
- **Revenue Justification:** +$1.8M–$2.5M annual revenue recovery from improved deal-win rate >> carrying costs
- **Quality Impact:** None (same materials, same suppliers)
- **Risk Mitigation:** Design stability SLA (no major design changes <quarterly); quarterly inventory rotation (FIFO); annual component testing

**1.2 Rolling 12-Week Demand Forecast to Suppliers**
- **What:** Sales provides weekly rolling forecast to Procurement; Procurement shares with top 3 suppliers; suppliers commit to capacity priority allocation
- **Why:** Suppliers can pre-stage materials, prioritize tooling, batch ocean shipments → reduces manufacturing lead time from 42 days to 32–35 days
- **Process:** Weekly forecast update (Friday); monthly summary to suppliers; quarterly demand-review meeting
- **Supplier Benefit:** Volume visibility enables their capacity planning; reduces their rush-order risk
- **Your Benefit:** Priority queue access; potential 5–7% material cost reduction from ocean consolidation (TierOne)
- **Risk Mitigation:** Forecast accuracy penalty; start with soft forecast (weeks 1–4 firm, 5–8 soft, 9–12 directional); escalation if variance >30%

**1.3 FAI Pre-Qualification (Eliminate Repeat-Order FAI Delays)**
- **What:** For repeat product designs (60–70% of orders), complete FAI during design phase; subsequent orders reference pre-qualified supplier tooling; manufacturing FAI waived
- **Why:** Eliminates 2-day FAI delay from manufacturing cycle on repeat orders; first new-design order saves time by moving FAI pre-production
- **Process:** Design-release gate includes FAI certification (supplier tooling validated); repeat orders cite design release package; quality audits supplier tooling annually
- **Quality Protection:** FAI rigor unchanged (same 3-unit prototype testing); just moved earlier; supplier tooling audit frequency maintains compliance
- **Risk Mitigation:** Supplier tooling drift detection via annual audits + SPC monitoring on repeat-order pressure tests; if drift found, FAI re-triggered

**1.4 Supplier Capacity Negotiation & Tiered Contracts**
- **What:** Multi-year agreement with ProGear (longest supplier) establishing three service tiers:
  - Standard lead time: 38–42 days, baseline cost
  - Premium lead time: 32–35 days, cost + 5%
  - Expedite: 28–30 days, cost + 12%
- **Why:** Allows sales to quote "Premium" lead times as baseline; expedite available without ad-hoc negotiation
- **Benefit to Supplier:** Volume commitment + long-term relationship = capacity planning visibility + margin improvement
- **Benefit to You:** Capacity reserved; no last-minute surprises
- **Timeline:** Negotiation Weeks 2–10; contract signed by Month 3
- **Risk Mitigation:** If ProGear refuses, dual-source evaluation begins immediately (6–12 month qualification)

**Supplier Compression Summary:**
- Design-lock to PO latency elimination: 3–5 days
- Rolling forecast + supplier pre-staging: 2–3 days
- FAI pre-qualification (repeat orders): 2 days
- Inventory buffer (structural compression): 7–10 days
- **Total:** 14–21 days compression potential; realistic target: 10–15 days (accounting for negotiation friction, phased implementation)
- **Residual supplier time:** 42 → 28–31 days

#### INTERVENTION #2: PRODUCTION QUEUE ELIMINATION (8 days → 1–2 days)

**Strategy Pillars:**

**2.1 FIFO Scheduling & Eliminate Batch-Processing Delays**
- **What:** Replace batch-based scheduling (3–5 units per run) with First-In-First-Out (FIFO) single-unit flow
- **Why:** Eliminates 2–4 day batch-wait delay for 60% of orders; reduces queue time from 8 days to 1–2 days
- **Process:**
  - Demand stratification: Identify high-volume repeats (>3/month) for batch runs; standard variants (<3/month) for FIFO flow
  - FIFO algorithm: Jobs scheduled in sequence; each assigned start date based on work-center queue depth
  - High-volume batch cell: Separate dedicated cell/machine for batch families (no conflict with FIFO)
  - Scheduling discipline: Daily FIFO queue update; visual kanban cards showing job sequence
- **Quality Impact:** No gate changes; inspection timeline earlier (faster completion)
- **Utilization Trade-Off:** Equipment utilization may drop from 95% to 85–90% (acceptable; trade-off for lead-time compression and on-time delivery)
- **Risk Mitigation:** Production staff may resist (batch scheduling is familiar); engage in pilot design; measure queue-time reduction; use pilot success to drive rollout

**2.2 Segregate Rework Path & Dedicated Rework Cell**
- **What:** Create dedicated rework area (1 FTE technician + 0.2 FTE QA inspector) where failed units are diagnosed and repaired in parallel to main line
- **Why:** Failed units don't rejoin main queue (eliminating 4–6 day rework delay); diagnosis completed within 24 hours; repair within 3 days total
- **Process:**
  - Pressure test failure → tag unit; move to rework cell within 1 hour (parallel to main line)
  - Technician diagnosis (24h): Assess feasibility; document failure mode (assembly error, material defect, design sensitivity)
  - Rework schedule: Target same-week completion (3 days)
  - Root-cause documentation: Logged for continuous improvement (feeds process capability study)
- **Investment:** $15K setup (tools, test equipment); $65K/year labor (1 FTE tech + 0.2 FTE QA)
- **Benefit:** Faster rework; root-cause learning prevents repeat failures; zero customer impact if rework completes pre-shipment
- **Risk Mitigation:** If rework exceeds capacity (>3 incidents/month), cross-train second technician or use external contractor

**2.3 Sales-Production Visibility & Expedite Protocol**
- **What:** Implement shared master schedule (accessible to Sales, Production, Quality) showing available capacity by date; establish pre-approved expedite decision rules
- **Why:** Eliminates 1–2 day decision latency for expedite orders; prevents unplanned schedule preemption
- **Expedite Decision Rules (Pre-Approved; No Escalation):**
  - Deal >$500K, AND available capacity within 3 days of request, AND <5 days delay to downstream jobs, AND Quality confirms pressure-test availability
  - **Decision turnaround:** 24 hours
- **Escalation Rules (VP Operations decides within 48 hours):**
  - Deal $250K–$500K OR >5 days delay to downstream jobs OR pressure-test capacity tight
- **Denial Rules (Not Approved):**
  - Deal <$250K OR >10 days delay to downstream jobs OR Quality veto (safety/test-capacity concern)
- **Process:** Sales flags order in shared system; Production + Quality respond within 24 hours with capacity assessment; Production lead approves/denies
- **Quality Protection:** Quality lead has veto power (if test capacity unavailable, expedite denied; no quality compromise)
- **Risk Mitigation:** Cap expedite approvals at 15% of orders/month (prevents chaos); escalation if exceeded

**Queue Elimination Summary:**
- FIFO scheduling: 2–4 days saved (batch wait eliminated)
- Rework cell (parallel path): 2–3 days saved per rework incident (6% of orders = 0.12 days average)
- Expedite decision protocol: 1–2 days saved (decision latency eliminated)
- **Total:** 6–8 days compression; residual queue time: 8 → 1–2 days

#### INTERVENTION #3: HANDOFF & FRICTION REMOVAL (5–12 days → 1–2 days)

**Strategy Pillars:**

**3.1 Concurrent Engineering: Parallel Design-to-BOM-to-PO**
- **What:** BOM development begins during design phase (while design 70%–80% complete); supplier RFQs issued pre-design-completion; PO drafted ready to place on design sign-off
- **Why:** Eliminates 3–5 day sequential handoff delay (design → BOM → Procurement → PO issuance)
- **Process:**
  - Assign Procurement engineer (0.3–0.5 FTE) to design team during design phase
  - Week 2 of design: Procurement issues RFQs to pre-approved suppliers (living BOM basis)
  - Design 80% complete: Suppliers provide quotes; Procurement negotiates
  - Design complete: Final BOM finalized; PO issued same-day (no delay)
- **Pre-Approved Supplier List & Standing Specs:** Reduce RFQ turnaround; suppliers pre-quote standard configs
- **Quality Impact:** Earlier supplier engagement improves FAI planning; design review includes supplier risk assessment
- **Timeline:** PO issuance moves from Day 5 (post-design) to Day 0; supplier lead time clock starts immediately on design release

**3.2 Change Control Board with Delegated Authority**
- **What:** Lightweight board (Product Manager + Production Lead + Quality Lead) with pre-delegated authority to approve low-risk changes same-day (no escalation)
- **Why:** Eliminates 3–5 day per-change decision loops (~20% of orders affected); average change decision compressed from 3–5 days to <24 hours
- **Low-Risk Changes (Auto-Approve, Same-Day):**
  - Customer logo/cosmetic markings, delivery location change, documentation language, labeling, paint finish variation
  - **Rationale:** No design/fabrication impact; accommodated within baseline lead time
- **Medium-Risk Changes (48-Hour Approval):**
  - Sensor sub-type swap (pre-approved alternative), rating variation ±5%, material substitute (pre-qualified), manufacturing process change
  - **Rationale:** May affect supplier schedule or fabrication; engineering review required
- **High-Risk Changes (Escalation to VP/Chief Engineer):**
  - Pressure rating change >5%, new supplier component, design rework, material change to safety-critical part
  - **Rationale:** Requires FEA, design validation, or tooling changes; 1-week decision
- **Process:** Daily 15-minute change review; same-day/48-hour approvals documented; high-risk escalations assigned owner with deadline
- **Quality Authority:** Quality lead has veto power (if change poses risk, escalation required; no pressure to approve risky changes)

**3.3 Integrated Quality Planning into Production Schedule**
- **What:** Quality gates (incoming QC, pressure test, final cert) scheduled into Production master schedule; Quality lead reviews weekly; capacity never compressed
- **Why:** Quality team aware of production timeline; can pre-stage equipment, prep documentation; no quality schedule surprises
- **Process:**
  - Production schedule includes manufacturing dates + Quality gate dates
  - Pressure-test SLA: Every order allocated dedicated 2-day test window; results available pre-shipment
  - Documentation pre-staging: During manufacturing, QA fills template; final data entered post-test; sign-off same-day
- **Quality Impact:** Gates never slip; documentation ready when needed; no scheduling shortcuts taken

**Handoff Friction Summary:**
- Design-lock to PO latency: 3–5 days (eliminated via concurrent engineering)
- Change-order decision delays: 2–3 days (compressed to <24 hours via change control board)
- Quality-schedule friction: 1–2 days (eliminated via integrated planning)
- **Total:** 5–7 days compression; residual handoff delays: 5–7 days → 1–2 days

#### INTERVENTION #4: FIRST-PASS YIELD IMPROVEMENT (6–9% rework rate → 4–5% target)

**Strategy Pillars (Upstream Prevention, Not Gate Removal):**

**4.1 Pre-Manufacturing Design Review Checklist**
- **What:** Gate to design release; design must pass checklist before manufacturing can begin
- **Checklist Items:**
  - **Tolerance Stack-Up:** All dimensions contributing to pressure seal/function verified; margin >15% design spec
  - **FEA Analysis:** Pressure-vessel stress analysis; material safety factor >2.0; results documented
  - **Supplier Risk Assessment:** Supplier complexity rating (medium/high-risk items flagged for FAI prototype)
  - **Pressure-Test Margin:** Design margin >20% at design min/max conditions
  - **Assembly Sequence Risk:** Critical assembly steps identified (e.g., hydraulic seal seating); work instructions prepared; operator certification required
- **Approval:** Product Manager + Chief Engineer + Quality Lead sign off; no approval without checklist complete
- **Escalation:** If checklist finds risk, change order issued; design revised; no release until resolved
- **Why:** Prevents FAI rejections (9% of new designs) and pressure-test failures (6% of orders) by catching design flaws upfront
- **Timeline:** 2–3 day review (lightweight; not bureaucratic); allows conditional design release if preliminary FAI pending

**4.2 Supplier SLA Enforcement & Audit Program**
- **What:** Formal SLA with Tier-1 suppliers (ProGear, TierOne, FlexHydro) requiring:
  - Defect rate: <2% for standard, <1% for repeat/pre-qualified
  - On-time delivery: 98% (within ±3-day window acceptable)
  - Quality audits: Annual 3-day facility audits; tooling inspection, material certs, process controls verified
  - Escalation: 1st incident → corrective action plan (2 weeks); 2nd incident → capacity allocation reduced 25%; 3rd incident → supplier replacement evaluation
- **Quarterly Business Review:** Discuss quality trends, cost reduction, design changes; supplier improvement initiatives
- **Why:** Reduces incoming material defects (currently 4% defect rate observed; target <2%); ensures supplier tooling stability (prevents tooling drift → pressure-test failures)

**4.3 Process Capability Study & Root-Cause Analysis**
- **What:** Analyze last 50 pressure-test failures; root-cause each failure; identify systemic issues
- **Failure Categories:**
  - Assembly error (hydraulic seal misaligned, gasket not seated, bolt torque incorrect): ~1–2% of failures
  - Material defect (supplier spec non-conformance, batch issue): ~1–2% of failures
  - Design sensitivity (design margin insufficient for tolerance variation, environmental sensitivity): ~2–3% of failures
  - Other (equipment malfunction, operator error): <1%
- **Corrective Actions:** Per failure category, implement upstream prevention (mistake-proofing, enhanced incoming inspection, design revision)
- **Timeline:** 6–8 week study; results inform design review checklist refinements and assembly procedure updates
- **Why:** Identifies systemic root causes; prevents repeat failures; feeds SPC monitoring

**4.4 Statistical Process Control (SPC) Monitoring**
- **What:** Deploy control charts on pressure-test results, assembly rework incidents, supplier defect rates; monitor ongoing for trends
- **Process:**
  - Quality staff trained in SPC basics (2-day course; ~$5K investment)
  - Control chart thresholds established (±3σ from baseline)
  - Daily chart updates (automated via lab equipment or manual data entry)
  - Alert protocol: If control limit exceeded, Quality lead initiates process investigation (within 48 hours)
- **Why:** Detects process drift before cascade to field failures (early warning system)
- **Timeline:** Training complete by Month 4; charts deployed by Month 5

**First-Pass Yield Improvement Summary:**
- Design review prevents FAI rejections (9% of new designs → target 2–3%): Saves 6 days on 9% = ~0.5 days average
- Supplier SLA + audit reduces incoming defects (4% → 2%): Saves 1 day on 2% = ~0.02 days average
- Process capability study + mistake-proofing reduces pressure-test failures (6% → 3–4%): Saves 5 days on 3% = ~0.15 days average
- SPC monitoring prevents late-stage surprises (early detection = faster diagnosis + prevention)
- **Total:** 1.5–3 days average per order (accounting for rework frequency); residual rework: 6–9% rate → 4–5% target
