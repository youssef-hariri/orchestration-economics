**OPERATIONS IMPROVEMENT PLAN: PROJECT VELOCITY**
*Industrial Equipment Manufacturer – Lead-Time Compression with Certified Quality Integrity*

---

### 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The Crisis.** The company currently quotes **14-week lead times** while our fastest competitor quotes **6 weeks**. Sales is losing **33% of qualified deals solely on delivery speed**, and a prior attempt to compress the schedule triggered a warranty spike that required two years of reputation recovery. The status quo is unsustainable; the gap is widening.

**The Diagnosis.** This is not a capacity problem. It is a **flow problem**. Value-added touch time across the value stream is approximately **4.6 weeks**. The remaining **9.4 weeks** are pure organizational waste—interdepartmental queues, inspection backlogs, engineering rework, procurement latency, and batching inertia. The competitor is not machining or welding faster; it is **waiting less**. Our quoted lead time is a mirror of our internal friction, not our physical capability.

**The Strategic Objective.**
*   **Phase 1 (Months 1–4):** Reduce quoted lead time from **14 weeks to 8 weeks**, stabilize flow, and validate quality integrity.
*   **Phase 2 (Months 5–9):** Reduce quoted lead time from **8 weeks to 6 weeks**, achieving competitive parity.

**The Quality Constraint.** Zero increase in 30-day field failure rate (hold at or below **0.3%**). No certified inspection stage will be shortened in duration. All compression will come from the elimination of non-value-added wait time, never from the thinning of quality gates.

**Investment & Returns.** The plan requires approximately **$XM** in strategic raw-material positioning, cellular re-layout, and digital workflow infrastructure. Expected outcomes: recapture **50% of speed-driven deal losses**, add **$YM in annual margin**, and structurally reduce warranty exposure through upstream prevention and hardened governance.

### 2. DIAGNOSTIC: THE CURRENT-STATE TIME-LOSS MAP

The following diagnostic deconstructs the 14-week quote into its constituent stages, distinguishing value-added (VA) activity from true time loss (wait, rework, and overprocessing).

| Stage | Quoted Calendar Weeks | Value-Added Hours | Wait / Rework / Loss Hours | Diagnostic Root Cause of Time Loss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Order Entry & Contract Review** | 1.0 | 4 hrs | 36 hrs | Sales submits incomplete specs; credit holds loop in Finance; Engineering clarification requests sit in queue for days. |
| **Engineering & BOM Release** | 2.0 | 30 hrs | 50 hrs | **80% of orders trigger custom redraws** because no standard module library exists; BOM errors discovered late by Procurement. |
| **Procurement & Receiving** | 4.0 | 8 hrs | 152 hrs | Long-lead castings/forgings are **not ordered until drawing release**; no forecast shared with suppliers; incoming inspection queue adds 5–7 days. |
| **Fabrication / Machining** | 2.0 | 50 hrs | 46 hrs | Functional departmental layout forces **batching by machine type**; parts wait for NC programmer availability; material kits are incomplete. |
| **Assembly** | 1.0 | 35 hrs | 13 hrs | Sub-assemblies queue in wire baskets; wrong revision parts picked from stock; fasteners missing due to kitting errors. |
| **In-Process Certified Quality** | 1.5 | 28 hrs | 32 hrs | **Certified inspector bottleneck**: one hydro/NDT lab, two inspectors, first-come-first-served queue; 8% of units fail hydro and loop back. |
| **Final Test & Customer Witness** | 1.5 | 12 hrs | 48 hrs | Test fixture conflicts; **customer witness schedules drive the bottleneck**; punch-list rework requires re-booking. |
| **Paint, Pack & Ship** | 1.0 | 16 hrs | 24 hrs | Batch painting by color to "save setup"; freight consolidation waits 3–5 days for full truckload. |
| **TOTAL** | **14.0 weeks** | **~185 hrs** (≈4.6 wks) | **~375 hrs** (≈9.4 wks) | **Time is actually lost in queues, not operations.** |

**Key Insight.** The 6-week competitive gap is explained almost entirely by the **375 hours of organizational waiting time** that this enterprise treats as normal. Compress the queues, and we compress the quote without compromising the caliper.

### 3. IMPROVEMENT LEVERS

#### A. Process Levers
*Target: Compress front-end and manufacturing flow by 4.0 weeks.*

*   **Configure-to-Order (CTO) Library.** Build pre-approved mechanical and electrical modules covering **70% of order volume**. Eliminate custom redraws on standard configurations.  
    *Target:* Engineering release from **2 weeks to 72 hours**.
*   **Product-Family Cells.** Re-layout Fabrication and Assembly from functional departments (all lathes together) into **three dedicated product-family cells**. Parts move point-to-point within the cell, not through central WIP stock.  
    *Target:* Machining/Assembly WIP queue cut from **46 hours to 16 hours**.
*   **Single-Piece Machining Flow.** Set batch size to **one** for assembly-sequenced parts. Right-size tooling and apply quick-changeover discipline to eliminate batch justification by "setup cost."  
    *Target:* Fabrication lead time from **2 weeks to 5 days**.
*   **Digital Birth Certificate & Hard-Stop Routing.** Every serialized component carries a barcode/RFID record. No physical move to the next value-stream stage is possible without electronic sign-off from the prior stage. The ERP enforces routing; paper travelers are abolished.  
    *Target:* Eliminate 100% of "lost in the system" WIP.

#### B. Inventory Levers
*Target: Remove procurement and kitting delays by 3.5 weeks.*

*   **Raw Material Supermarket.** Pre-position **six months of demand** for the top 15 long-lead SKUs (steel plate, forgings, castings) based on a statistically frozen forecast.  
    *Target:* Procurement lead time for these items from **4 weeks to 48 hours**.
*   **Postponement Strategy.** Build standard base units to the final-test stage and hold. Customize only control packages, paint color, and nameplates in the last **10 days**.  
    *Target:* 40% of orders ship from advanced WIP rather than raw stock.
*   **Point-of-Use Consignment.** Suppliers manage fasteners, seals, gauges, and consumables in bins at the assembly cells; invoiced on consumption.  
    *Target:* Zero kitting shortages on Class C parts.

#### C. Scheduling Levers
*Target: Eliminate inspection and test queues by 1.5 weeks.*

*   **Finite Scheduling with Inspector-as-Resource.** Load certified inspectors into the ERP as capacity-constrained work centers with **pre-booked 48-hour windows**. Inspection is no longer a "when available" activity.  
    *Target:* Inspection queue from **5 days to 0.5 days**.
*   **Frozen Schedule Horizon.** Once material is kitted and released to the floor, the production sequence is **frozen for 3 weeks**. Sales cannot change delivery dates, configurations, or sequence inside the horizon.  
    *Target:* Schedule volatility from 35% to **<5%**.
*   **Protective Capacity Planning.** Load cells to **85% of demonstrated capacity**, leaving 15% headroom to absorb variance without breaking promise dates.  
    *Target:* On-time delivery to promise shifts from 68% to **95%**.

#### D. Supplier Levers
*Target: Compress supplier and inbound latency by 2.0 weeks.*

*   **Supplier-Managed Inventory (SMI) for Long-Lead Items.** Place forecasted demand at the foundry/mill and take ownership only upon pull signal. We will pay carrying cost; it is cheaper than lost deals.  
    *Target:* Casting availability from **6 weeks to 24 hours**.
*   **Certified Supplier Fast-Track Program.** Top-tier suppliers (Class A) may bypass traditional receiving inspection **only after** satisfying four non-negotiable conditions:
    1.  **MSA Correlation:** A formal Measurement System Analysis confirms their gauging correlates to our internal standards within accepted tolerances.
    2.  **SPC Baseline:** The supplier demonstrates **Cpk ≥ 1.67** on critical characteristics for 90 consecutive days.
    3.  **Unannounced Audits:** Quarterly unannounced audits of their final inspection and shipping processes.
    4.  **Escrow Containment:** Any lot released under fast-track is electronically tagged. If a single defect escapes, automatic containment triggers 100% inspection of the next three lots and immediate suspension of fast-track status until root cause is closed.  
    *Target:* Incoming inspection time cut by **60%** with verified, governed risk reduction.
*   **Local Surge Network.** Qualify **two machine shops within a 50-mile radius** for overflow machining on standard features.  
    *Target:* Absorb demand spikes without extending the 8-week quote.

### 4. CERTIFIED QUALITY GATE PROTECTION & WARRANTY PREVENTION

**Core Doctrine: Compress the Queue, Not the Caliper.** Inspection duration, certified sample sizes, and acceptance criteria remain unchanged. Wait time *before* inspection is abolished.

**Prerequisite: Forensic Escape Analysis (Weeks 1–2).**  
Before any process change is commissioned, Quality and Operations will conduct a serial-by-serial trace of the prior warranty spike. The analysis will identify exactly which units bypassed which certified gates, what failure modes resulted, and which managerial decisions or system gaps enabled the escape. Findings will be fed into a revised Process FMEA so that the new flow structurally prevents the same latent failure modes.

**Non-Negotiable Protections:**

1.  **Upstream Source Inspection.** All critical castings, weldments, and pressure-retaining components are inspected **at the supplier** by our certified personnel or an approved third-party inspector **before** they ship. Warranty data indicates **60% of field failures originate from supplier material defects** caught too late.
2.  **Operator-Owned First-Pass Quality.** Each assembly cell is equipped with **go/no-go fixtures and torque-sealed tooling**. A wrong part cannot be physically assembled. First-pass yield target is **>96%** at final hydro test. Any cell dropping below 94% triggers a **48-hour Quality Engineering lockdown**, not a Production workaround.
3.  **Statistical Process Control (SPC) Gate.** Real-time SPC is deployed on the five critical-to-quality (CTQ) characteristics.
    *   If **Cpk ≥ 1.33**, certified skip-lot sampling is authorized per the existing quality plan.
    *   If **Cpk < 1.33**, automatic 100% inspection is triggered, and the **prior lot since the last proven-acceptable sample is quarantined** pending process requalification.
4.  **Digital Chain of Custody.** No unit moves past a certified gate without an electronic signature from a qualified inspector. The ERP physically enforces this; unauthorized moves trigger automatic alerts to Quality and Operations leadership.
5.  **Warranty Canary & Production Line Stop.** A weekly automated dashboard feeds field failure data back to Quality. If the **30-day field failure rate exceeds 0.3%** for two consecutive weeks, all suspect lots are quarantined and the affected quality gates are re-audited. **Production stops** until root cause is documented.
6.  **Independent Quality Authority & Dual-Sign Deviation Protocol.** The **Chief Quality Officer (CQO)** maintains independent veto authority over any schedule-driven deviation, with dotted-line reporting to the CEO. Under no circumstances may a certified hold point be bypassed. Any emergency deviation requires **simultaneous signatures from the VP of Operations, VP of Quality, and the CQO**. A single signature is invalid and grounds for disciplinary action. To prevent mutual accommodation driven by shared P&L pressure, **Quality leadership incentive metrics are insulated**: 50% of the Quality bonus pool is tied to field failure rate and first-pass yield, independent of shipment volume or revenue recognition.

**Quantified Quality Targets:**
*   First-pass yield ≥ **96%**
*   30-day field failure ≤ **0.3%**
*   Customer witness test pass on first attempt ≥ **90%**

### 5. CROSS-FUNCTIONAL ALIGNMENT & GOVERNANCE

Interdepartmental conflict between Production, Quality, and Sales is a **structural artifact** of misaligned metrics and sequential handoffs. The remedy is forced integration through process design, not culture rhetoric.

**A. Deal Qualification Gate (Sales ↔ Operations)**
*   Sales cannot issue a quote with a delivery promise without Operations confirming configuration status and material availability in the CTO configurator.
*   **Standard product:** Auto-approved at **8 weeks**.
*   **Non-standard product:** Requires a **24-hour joint review** (Sales + Engineering + Production + Quality); quote defaults to **12 weeks** unless all four sign a 10-week exception.
*   *Metric:* **Clean Order Ratio** (% of orders requiring zero post-entry clarification). Target: **>95%**.

**B. Integrated Control Tower (Daily 8:15 AM, 12-Minute Stand-Up)**
*   **Attendees:** Production Superintendent, Quality Manager, Sales Operations Analyst (non-negotiable attendance).
*   **Location:** Physical room with live digital board.
*   **Agenda:**
    1.  Next 20 orders at risk of missing the frozen schedule (2 min).
    2.  Inspection backlog by cell (2 min).
    3.  Supplier late list with mitigation owner (2 min).
    4.  Sales order amendments attempted inside frozen zone (2 min).
    5.  Warranty canary status (2 min).
    6.  Action register with owner and deadline (2 min).
*   *Ground rules:* No narratives, no PowerPoint, no blame. Data only.

**C. Unified Metrics & Shared Consequence**
*   A single dashboard tracks one shared leading metric: **Quality-Adjusted On-Time Delivery (QA-OTD)**. An order is counted as "on time" only if it ships within **±3 days of promise** *and* passes final test with zero punch-list items.
*   **Cost of Poor Quality Pool:** Warranty and field failure costs are deducted proportionally from the annual bonus pools of **all three functions**—Production, Quality, and Sales. The functions succeed or fail together.
*   Individual metrics are visible to all:
    *   **Sales:** Clean Order Ratio.
    *   **Production:** Schedule Adherence (hourly).
    *   **Quality:** First-Pass Yield and Inspector Queue Time.

**D. Rotational Embedding**
Each function embeds one person in another function for **one week per quarter**:
*   Quality Engineer rides with Sales to witness how delivery quotes are constructed.
*   Sales Representative works the inspection bench to understand why a hydro test cannot be "hurried."
*   Production Supervisor attends a customer site acceptance to see the economic consequence of a punch-list item.

**E. 24-Hour Escalation Protocol**
*   Disputes over priority, rework responsibility, or quote feasibility are escalated to the Plant Manager within **24 hours**.
*   The format is a one-page A3: Problem Statement, Data, Proposed Countermeasure, Experiment. No email chains, no narrative accusations.
*   The Plant Manager decides in a **15-minute huddle** with the three function heads.

### 7. FINANCIAL & RISK SUMMARY

**Capital Requirement.** Approximately **$XM**, allocated to strategic raw material, cellular re-layout, ERP hard-stop modules, and supplier SMI prepayments.

**Expected Return.** Recapture of **50% of speed-driven losses** yields an estimated **$YM annual margin expansion**. Additional benefits include reduced expediting costs, lower WIP carrying cost, and decreased warranty exposure.

**Primary Risk & Mitigation.**
*   *Risk:* Quality drift during speed transition.  
    *Mitigation:* Independent CQO authority, automated warranty canary with line-stop authority, forensic escape analysis, and the 8-week stabilization gate. We will not advance to 6 weeks until the 0.3% field failure ceiling is held for 90 consecutive days.

**The Result.** A **6-week competitive lead time** built on faster flow, not thinner inspection. A quality system that is structurally harder to bypass than the one it replaces. And an organization aligned by shared metrics, shared consequence, and shared data—not by shared blame.
