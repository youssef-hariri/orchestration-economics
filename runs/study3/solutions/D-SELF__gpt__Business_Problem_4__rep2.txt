# Michi Snacks Japan  
## Localized Snack Product-Line Proposal

## Executive recommendation

Develop a Japan-focused snack range built around:

> **Familiar Japanese flavors, thoughtfully adapted for everyday snacking.**

Authorize **Stage 1 market validation and limited formulation work** for three core products:

1. **Michi Umami Arare Bites – Nori Soy**
2. **Yuzu Shio Soybean Crisps**
3. **Miso Sesame Soy Bites**

Develop **Matcha Azuki Soft Bites** as a controlled seasonal pilot after the savory concepts pass initial sensory and cost screening. Hold **Shichimi Crunch Mix** for phase two and keep **Ume Kombu Konjac Chews** in R&D pending specialist safety validation.

---

# 1. Market rationale and assumptions

## Working market assumptions

These are planning hypotheses, not validated market facts.

- **Market:** Japan, initially Tokyo metropolitan area, Osaka/Kyoto, and Nagoya
- **Primary consumer:** Urban office workers aged 25–39 who purchase individual snacks during weekday breaks or commuting
- **Secondary consumers:** Students, consumers aged 40–59, regular rice-snack buyers, plant-forward shoppers, and premium sweet buyers
- **Channels:** Convenience stores, supermarkets, drugstores, e-commerce, and selected specialty retailers
- **Primary occasions:** Desk breaks, commuting, afternoon hunger, study or gaming, tea breaks, and evening relaxation
- **Positioning:** Premium-mass—more distinctive than a standard snack but affordable for repeat purchase
- **Pilot scope:** Approximately 60 stores across the three target regions, subject to retailer access

The line should not be positioned as representative of all Japanese cuisine or as uniformly healthy. The commercial hypothesis is that consumers will respond to recognizable flavors in convenient, contemporary formats if taste, texture, price, and portability are competitive with established snacks.

## Preliminary price assumptions

| Format | Indicative retail range |
|---|---:|
| 25–30 g single pack | ¥180–¥280 |
| 30–40 g substantial snack | ¥220–¥320 |
| 70–100 g sharing pack | ¥350–¥600 |
| Three- to five-piece multipack | ¥450–¥750 |

These ranges require validation against current competitor prices, retailer margins, trade promotion, manufacturing route, and consumer willingness to pay.

# 2. Brand platform

**Brand:** Michi Snacks  
**Descriptor:** *Modern Japanese-inspired snacks for everyday breaks*  
**Supporting line:** *ひとくち、いつもの道* — provisional and subject to native Japanese review

## Brand principles

- Familiarity before novelty
- Taste and texture before health messaging
- Specific ingredient stories rather than generalized cultural imagery
- Portable, low-mess, portion-appropriate formats
- Clear flavor, allergen, and dietary communication
- Regional or ingredient-origin claims only where sourcing is genuine and traceable

Packaging should avoid generic cultural symbols such as geisha, temples, faux calligraphy, or indiscriminate “traditional Japan” imagery. Japanese consumers, copywriters, packaging specialists, and regulatory advisors should review the brand and product language before final artwork.

# 3. Product concepts

## 1. Michi Umami Arare Bites – Nori Soy

### Product concept

Compact, crisp rice pieces inspired by arare and senbei, seasoned with nori and soy. The bites should be crunchy but not excessively hard, crumbly, oily, or powdery.

**Consumer-facing description:**  
> Crispy rice bites with nori-soy flavor

### Key ingredients

- Rice or rice flour
- Soy sauce or soy seasoning
- Nori
- Vegetable oil
- Sesame, if used
- Optional kombu or shiitake seasoning

A later **Kombu-Shiitake Umami** variant could offer a vegetarian-positioned flavor, subject to complete ingredient and facility review. A bonito version should be separately formulated and clearly identify fish-derived ingredients.

### Format and occasion

- 25–30 g single-serve pouch for convenience stores
- 70–100 g resealable pouch for supermarkets and home sharing
- Suitable for commuting, desk breaks, study, lunch accompaniment, and light evening snacking

### Target consumers

Broad savory-snack buyers, especially consumers familiar with senbei, arare, or other rice snacks.

### Differentiation

The innovation is a compact, low-mess, portable format—not a granola substitute. The product should compete on:

- Crispness
- Compact bite geometry
- Seasoning clarity
- Reduced mess
- Contemporary packaging

### Preliminary development targets

- 110–150 kcal per 25–30 g pack
- Sodium target at or below approximately 300 mg per pack
- Minimum nine-month shelf-life objective
- Less than 5% visibly damaged units in simulated distribution
- Crispness retained through shelf life

### Main risks

Competition from established rice snacks, excess saltiness, breakage, and confusion between fish-based and vegetarian variants.

### Gate

Advance if the product performs at least comparably to selected rice-snack benchmarks in blind testing, at least 70% of consumers understand the product type, and cost, sodium, breakage, and shelf-life targets are met.

## 2. Yuzu Shio Soybean Crisps

### Product concept

A dense, satisfying baked or extruded soybean crisp with roasted soybean flavor, light salt, kombu umami, and restrained yuzu aroma.

**Consumer-facing description:**  
> Soybean crisps with fresh yuzu-salt flavor

Use “edamame” only if the finished product contains meaningful edamame content and delivers recognizable edamame flavor.

### Key ingredients

- Roasted soybean flour
- Edamame powder, if materially present
- Rice flour or starch
- Vegetable oil
- Salt
- Kombu seasoning
- Yuzu peel or natural yuzu flavor

Japanese-origin yuzu should be highlighted only where its sourcing is genuine, traceable, and commercially meaningful.

### Format and occasion

- 25–30 g single-serve pouch for convenience stores and drugstores
- Potential 60–80 g pack after repeat-purchase validation
- Suitable for afternoon breaks, desk snacking, commuting, and tea or nonalcoholic-drink occasions

### Target consumers

Convenience-store shoppers, consumers seeking alternatives to potato chips, plant-forward shoppers, and consumers interested in soybean-based foods.

### Differentiation

This is the range’s likely **hero product**, but it must win on taste and texture rather than merely sounding novel or healthy. It should be benchmarked against soybean snacks, pea crisps, potato chips, baked crisps, and protein extrusions.

### Preliminary development targets

- 110–160 kcal per 25–30 g pack
- Preliminary protein target of 4–6 g per pack, subject to formulation and claim review
- Sodium target at or below approximately 280 mg per pack
- Minimum nine-month shelf-life objective
- Dense, crisp texture without a chalky finish
- Yuzu noticeable but not sour, bitter, or perfumed

### Main risks

Generic protein-extrusion flavor, excessive yuzu, insufficient protein for a formal claim, and soy allergen or cross-contact controls.

### Gate

Advance if:

- At least 50% of tasters indicate likely repeat purchase
- At least 60% rate yuzu intensity as appropriate
- Fewer than 15% describe the flavor as artificial, bitter, or overly perfumed
- Blind liking is within 0.3 points of the preferred category benchmark on a nine-point scale
- Target cost supports the planned retail price and margin

Initial communication should favor factual language such as “soybean crisp” or “made with soybeans” unless a protein claim is legally substantiated.

## 3. Miso Sesame Soy Bites

### Product concept

Soft-baked, slightly chewy bites made with roasted soybean, white miso, black sesame, and restrained sweetness.

**Consumer-facing description:**  
> Soft-baked soy bites with white miso and black sesame

### Key ingredients

- Soybean flour or soy protein
- Rice flour or oats
- White miso
- Black sesame
- Kinako, if used
- Syrup or sugar
- Vegetable oil

### Format and occasion

- 30–40 g single pack
- Three- to five-piece multipack for supermarkets and e-commerce
- Suitable for mid-morning hunger, afternoon desk breaks, after-school use, and post-work snacking

Gym distribution should be exploratory rather than part of the core channel plan.

### Target consumers

Busy professionals, students, consumers seeking a more substantial snack, and e-commerce shoppers buying portioned foods.

### Differentiation

This product must be visibly and sensorially distinct from the crisp products:

| Attribute | Soybean Crisps | Miso Sesame Soy Bites |
|---|---|---|
| Texture | Dense and crisp | Soft and slightly chewy |
| Flavor | Fresh, salty, citrus-led | Roasted, mildly sweet, miso-led |
| Occasion | Light savory break | More substantial between-meal snack |
| Format | Single crisp pouch | Single bite pack or multipack |

### Preliminary development targets

- 130–190 kcal per 30–40 g pack
- Preliminary protein target of 5–8 g per pack, subject to texture and claim review
- Sodium target at or below approximately 280 mg per pack
- Six- to nine-month shelf-life objective
- Soft texture retained through shelf life
- Water activity sufficiently low for stability without hardening

### Main risks

Dry or chalky texture, excessive saltiness, higher packaging cost, and overlap with the soybean crisps.

### Gate

Advance if:

- At least 65% rate the texture acceptable or better
- Fewer than 15% describe the product as dry or chalky
- At least 50% perceive it as more substantial than the soybean crisps
- Shelf-life texture remains within specification
- The selected format supports viable production and margin

Do not position it as a breakfast replacement, meal replacement, or sports-performance product.

## 4. Matcha Azuki Soft Bites

### Product concept

A small soft sweet with matcha flavor and an azuki center.

Use **Soft Bites**, not **Mochi**, unless the product genuinely delivers the expected elastic rice-cake texture.

**Consumer-facing description:**  
> A small matcha-and-azuki treat for tea breaks

### Key ingredients

- Rice-based soft-bite exterior
- Azuki bean paste
- Matcha
- Sugar or syrup
- Vegetable oil
- Optional kinako

### Format and occasion

- Two-bite pouch
- Three- to four-piece multipack
- Suitable for tea breaks, study, afternoon treats, evening relaxation, and gifting

### Target consumers

Matcha and azuki buyers, students and younger adults, small-treat purchasers, and seasonal or specialty-retail shoppers.

### Differentiation

This is the line’s sweet and gifting complement, not a health product. Matcha and azuki provide familiar flavor references, while the small format supports portioned indulgence.

### Preliminary development targets

- 80–140 kcal per serving
- Moderate sweetness relative to benchmark confectionery
- Initial six-month shelf-life objective
- Stable matcha color and aroma
- No filling migration or excessive stickiness

### Launch approach

Run as a controlled seasonal pilot after the savory concepts pass initial screening. Broader distribution should require:

- Acceptable seasonal sell-through
- At least 50% likely repurchase outside gifting
- Successful filling and shelf-life validation
- Clear consumer understanding of the soft texture

# 4. Phase-two and experimental concepts

## Shichimi Crunch Mix

Develop as a second-wave evening and sharing snack.

### Recommended base

- Arare or rice crackers
- Roasted soybean or broad bean
- Roasted corn puffs
- Nori, sesame, citrus, chili, and shichimi-style seasoning

Avoid very hard whole corn kernels.

### Flavors

- Mild Citrus Shichimi
- Standard Shichimi

A hotter variant should follow only if heat tolerance supports it.

### Formats

- 35–45 g single-serve pouch
- 90–120 g sharing bag

### Gate

Proceed only if the product demonstrates acceptable liking, clear heat-level understanding, viable cost, and repeat-purchase potential for both household evening use and optional alcohol-adjacent occasions.

## Ume Kombu Konjac Chews

Keep in R&D only.

Potential formats include short coins, flattened pieces, or small cubes; long slippery strips should not be the default.

Specialist review must assess:

- Firmness and slipperiness
- Piece geometry
- Ease of biting
- Chewability
- Swallowing risk
- Instructions and warnings
- Shelf life
- Repeat purchase after initial novelty

Any unresolved safety concern is an automatic no-go. If the concept fails, replace it with **Ume Kombu Soy Chews** or **Ume Kombu Rice Bites**.

# 6. Feasibility and operating considerations

## Preferred manufacturing routes

| Product | Preferred initial route |
|---|---|
| Arare Bites | Japanese rice-snack co-manufacturer |
| Soybean Crisps | Japanese or hybrid co-manufacture |
| Miso Sesame Bites | Japanese soft-bite specialist |
| Matcha Azuki | Japanese confectionery co-manufacturer |
| Shichimi Mix | Established Japanese snack-mix producer |
| Konjac | Specialist konjac manufacturer only |

Compare at least three suppliers per priority SKU for minimum order quantities, unit cost, tooling, yield, lead time, freight, allergen controls, shelf life, and changeover costs.

## Shared ingredients

Potentially shared inputs include:

- Rice and rice flour
- Soybean ingredients and kinako
- Sesame
- Nori
- Kombu seasonings
- Miso
- Vegetable oils
- Standard sweeteners and syrups

Japanese-origin yuzu, matcha, rice, and other ingredients should be used where their sensory or provenance value justifies the additional cost.

## Packaging

Packaging should be compact, durable, easy to open, and resistant to crushing. Resealable formats should be reserved for packs intended for repeated consumption. Individual wrapping should be used only where freshness, hygiene, gifting, or portion control justifies the additional material and cost.

Before final selection, compare:

- Material weight
- Barrier performance
- Recyclability or disposal guidance
- Unit cost
- Product protection
- Retailer requirements

# 7. Regulatory and claim controls

Before commercialization:

1. Verify Japanese allergen labeling for soy, sesame, wheat, dairy, fish, and other applicable allergens.
2. Clearly declare bonito, fish extract, gelatin, dairy, or other animal-derived ingredients.
3. Do not imply vegetarian, vegan, halal, or allergen-free suitability without full formula and facility review.
4. Verify claims such as plant-based, protein, fiber, low calorie, reduced sugar, and baked against the finished product and serving size.
5. Establish cleaning and cross-contact procedures for shared facilities.
6. Review product names, flavor terms, origin claims, instructions, and package copy with Japanese regulatory counsel.
7. Obtain native Japanese review of the brand name, tagline, and flavor terminology before consumer testing.

Factual descriptions such as “soybean crisp,” “yuzu-salt flavor,” “soft-baked soy bites,” and “matcha and azuki” are preferable starting points to broad wellness claims.

# 8. Validation plan and decision gates

## Stage 1: Market and competitor audit

Complete before full formulation investment. Benchmark each concept against at least two direct or substitute products, recording:

- Pack size
- Price
- Calories and sodium
- Claims
- Texture
- Flavor intensity
- Packaging
- Channel placement
- Promotional frequency

## Stage 2: Consumer research

Conduct approximately six to eight qualitative groups or paired interviews across office workers, students, consumers aged 40–59, regular rice-snack buyers, soybean or nutrition-snack buyers, and sweet-snack buyers.

Then conduct quantitative blind and branded testing with approximately 150–300 consumers per major decision. Include quotas for the three pilot regions, core and secondary ages, gender balance, and relevant snack behaviors.

Evaluate:

- Overall liking
- Texture
- Saltiness and sweetness
- Yuzu and miso intensity
- Product-type understanding
- Purchase intent
- Repeat-purchase intent
- Value perception

## Indicative consumer gates

- Blind liking equal to, or within 0.3 points of, the preferred benchmark on a nine-point scale
- Product-type comprehension of at least 60%
- Likely repeat purchase of at least 50% for core savory products
- Target-price acceptance of at least 60%
- No critical texture, allergen, or safety concerns

These thresholds should be finalized before fieldwork.

## Stage 3: Technical validation

For each priority SKU:

1. Develop three to five prototypes
2. Screen flavor and texture
3. Conduct pilot production
4. Test shelf life, moisture migration, oxidation, and color stability
5. Test package drop, crush, and distribution performance
6. Confirm yield, line speed, and cost
7. Complete regulatory review

## Stage 4: Retail pilot

Pilot approximately 60 stores:

- 30 in the Tokyo metropolitan area
- 15 in Osaka/Kyoto
- 15 in Nagoya

Use a mix of central urban, commuter, and suburban locations. Run for approximately 8–12 weeks.

Track:

- Units per store per week
- Trial-to-repeat conversion
- Promotion versus non-promotion sales
- Markdown rate
- Damage and returns
- Complaints
- Inventory availability
- Price response
- Flavor-specific performance

Indicative commercial gates:

- Repeat purchase of at least 25% among identified trial buyers during the pilot
- Markdown below 15% of units
- Damage and returns below 5%
- At least 70% of sales not dependent on deep discounting
- Retailer-agreed sales-per-store target achieved by week six

## Immediate Stage 1 authorization gate

Formulation investment for the three core SKUs should proceed only if the audit confirms:

- A credible gap versus existing products
- Feasible target retail prices
- Sufficient expected margin
- Available manufacturing capacity
- No unresolved regulatory or allergen barrier
- A clear consumer occasion for each product

# Final prioritization

1. **Michi Umami Arare Bites – Nori Soy**  
   Best combination of familiarity, broad appeal, technical feasibility, and channel flexibility.

2. **Yuzu Shio Soybean Crisps**  
   Strongest differentiated hero proposition, subject to taste, texture, and repeat-purchase validation.

3. **Miso Sesame Soy Bites**  
   Promising extension into softer, more substantial snacking, but dependent on texture, shelf life, and cost.

4. **Matcha Azuki Soft Bites**  
   Appropriate as a controlled seasonal product with limited initial volume.

5. **Shichimi Crunch Mix**  
   Hold for phase two until heat preference and sharing behavior are validated.

6. **Ume Kombu Konjac Chews**  
   R&D only until specialist safety, technical, regulatory, and repeat-purchase requirements are satisfied.

## Final recommendation

Proceed with Stage 1 validation and limited formulation for the three core savory products. Develop Matcha Azuki only after the savory prototypes pass initial sensory and cost screening. Do not authorize full pilot production until taste, texture, repeat purchase, cost, shelf life, packaging, regulatory compliance, and retail economics meet the agreed gates.

The line’s success should depend on a repeatable consumer benefit:

> **Good taste, satisfying texture, convenient portions, and credible value—delivered through familiar Japanese flavors in formats suited to everyday life.**
