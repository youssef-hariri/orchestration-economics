## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Situation
A five-site distribution network (900 pickers, 232M annual picks) faces rising fully-loaded labor costs ($0.169/pick), 35% annual picker turnover, and error rates (4.2–9.2 errors/1K picks) damaging key accounts. The largest warehouse is the primary employer in a single-employer town. Automation capital is available ($20M+ budget headroom) but execution risk is severe—a competitor's public automation failure makes flawed implementation costly in reputation and talent.

### Recommendation
**PROCEED with restructured roadmap, phased over 48 months (not 36), reversed sequencing, explicit payback reset, and workforce transparency.**

**Core strategy:**
- **Pilot at Site E** (lowest risk: 22% turnover, highest tenure, simplest full-case profile, fastest payback). Move Site B to Phase 2. Defer Site A (flagship, community exposure) to Phase 3B (month 30+).
- **Capex: $20.3M** (revised up 28% from draft; includes 22% contingency, facility mods, integration labor, spares, WMS upgrade options).
- **Expected return:** Year 1 savings $1.2M (40% realization); Years 2–3 $2.4M/year (75% realization); 3-year cumulative $6.0M.
- **Blended payback: 5.5–6.0 years** (vs. 2.8 years claimed; realistic after financing and working-capital costs).
- **Workforce reduction: 126 FTEs** (−11.5%) absorbed via 50% attrition, 35% retraining, 10% voluntary severance, **5% involuntary severance (budgeted $1.2M–$1.8M, legal-vetted)**.
- **Single-employer town (Site A):** Slower automation profile, economic-impact mitigation, government engagement, option to preserve net headcount via insourced services (reverse logistics, returns processing, contract warehousing for third parties).

### Investment & Decision Required
- **Capex: $20.3M** staged across 48 months (M1–M48)
- **Working capital: +$0.6M** (spares, licensing, project overhead)
- **Financing: $4M incremental debt @ 4.5%, $2.6M equipment leasing @ 8%**
- **Total program cost: $23.5M** (including financing)

**Expected financial return (5-year horizon):**
- Cumulative labor-cost savings: $6.0M (Years 1–3 only; Years 4–5 yield additional $4.8M if payback extends to 5.5–6.0 years)
- NPV (at 8% discount rate): ~$1.8M positive over 5 years
- IRR: 8.2% (below cost of capital by ~200–250 bps; modest but defensible given non-financial returns: safety, quality, reputation, turnover reduction)

**Non-financial returns:**
- Error rate: −35% (7.2 → 4.7 errors/1K picks), protecting key accounts
- Staff safety: −35% injury incidents via ergonomic improvements
- Turnover: −20% (35% → 28% annual rate) via skill-uplifting and career-path creation
- Community reputation: preserved at flagship (Site A) via transparent, phased approach

### What We Ask of Leadership

1. **Authorize 48-month program** (not 36 months); approve restructured phase sequence (E → B → C/D → A).
2. **Commit to stage-gate capital discipline:** Release capex based on leading-indicator tripwires (uptime, training completion, attrition tracking), not calendar dates. Build contingency to 22% ($3.5M); hold Phase 2 capex until Site E proves success (M11 gate).
3. **Embed external program governance:** Hire external program manager (0.5 FTE, $250K–300K for 48 months). Monthly steering-committee deep-dives with board participation and third-party KPI audit.
4. **Adopt vendor performance guarantees:** No capex disbursement without contractual guarantees (design specs, minimum performance thresholds at 85% of promised uplift, milestone-based holdbacks, explicit rollback rights). Reject standard vendor T&Cs.
5. **Fund workforce transition responsibly:** Allocate $1.2M–$1.8M for involuntary severance (if attrition falls short) + retraining + community investment. Legal review of severance terms (WARN Act, state labor laws). Communicate honestly to workforce (no false "attrition-only" narrative).
6. **Protect Site A (flagship) reputation:** Commit publicly to no involuntary layoffs before month 30; slower automation profile; economic-impact study; government and labor-council engagement. This requires board-level messaging and patience.
7. **Publish and defend payback reset:** Communicate internally and to investment community: "True payback is 5.5–6.0 years, not 2.8 years; we have realistic cost models and vendor guardrails in place."

**Decision gate:** Approve restructured roadmap (proceed) OR pivot to Site-E-only pilot (defer network rollout 12–18 months, validate payback thesis before expanding). **Do not proceed with original draft roadmap as designed.**

### 1.2 Automation Archetypes: Technology, Uplift & Rationale

#### **Archetype A: Process + Sorter + Dock Ergonomics (Site E)**
- **Why this site:** Full-case B2B profile has no fast-pick labor optimization opportunity. Value is in sorter efficiency and dock ergonomics.
- **Technology:** Upgraded sorter control (wave prioritization), dock conveyor, ergonomic dock stations
- **Picks/hour uplift:** +7% (220 → 235)
- **Error reduction:** −8% (via better wave logic)
- **Capex:** $1.2M
- **Payback:** 2.8–3.2 years (fastest; lowest capex, proven architecture)
- **Risk level:** LOW

#### **Archetype B: G2P + Conveyor + RF Hybrid (Site B)**
- **Why this site:** High turnover (40%), worst error rate (9.2/1K), ecommerce sensitivity. Pain point justifies capex; 35–40% error reduction directly addresses account risk.
- **Technology:** Mobile goods-to-person (6–8 units, 40–50% of SKUs), upgraded sortation conveyor, RF tablets, WMS integration
- **Picks/hour uplift:** +18% (220 → 260)
- **Error reduction:** −35% (9.2 → 6.0/1K)
- **Capex:** $5.7M (includes hardware, conveyor, RF, integration, training, spares, contingency)
- **Payback:** 4.8–5.2 years
- **Risk level:** MEDIUM (proven architecture; Site E proves conveyance logic; main risk is ecommerce order complexity and G2P slotting)

#### **Archetype C: Process + Targeted G2P Lite (Site C)**
- **Why this site:** Stable, medium-sized, good labor market. G2P for slow-pick zones only (25% SKUs, 40% of labor).
- **Technology:** 2–3 mobile shelving units, wave-optimization algorithm, ergonomic carts
- **Picks/hour uplift:** +14% (220 → 250)
- **Error reduction:** −22%
- **Capex:** $1.6M
- **Payback:** 3.8–4.2 years
- **Risk level:** LOW-MEDIUM

#### **Archetype D: Pick-to-Light + RF + Exception Handling (Site D)**
- **Why this site:** Highest SKU count (22.1K), highest line-count orders (6.2 lines/order). Exception handling becomes competitive differentiator for specialty/high-touch orders.
- **Technology:** Pick-to-light (8 zones, 60% of fast picks), RF tablets, integrated exception-handling team (6–8 new FTEs), quality-audit workflow
- **Picks/hour uplift:** +16% (220 → 255)
- **Error reduction:** −30% (via pick-to-light + exception catching)
- **Capex:** $2.2M
- **Payback:** 4.4–4.8 years
- **Risk level:** MEDIUM (novel integration; new role creation; exception-handling ROI depends on staff quality)

#### **Archetype E: Light G2P + Quality Scan + Integrated Quality Team (Site A — Flagship)**
- **Why this site & why last:** Error rate drives account risk (8.5/1K, highest in network). Quality scan + quality team (12–15 FTEs) address root cause. Deferred to Phase 3B (month 30+) to allow community confidence-building and most mature playbook.
- **Technology:** G2P for medium-velocity SKUs (50%, 8–12 units), inline quality scan (barcode, weight, label), RF tablets, WMS quality-prediction logic, integrated quality team
- **Picks/hour uplift:** +25% (220 → 275)
- **Error reduction:** −38% (8.5 → 5.3/1K)
- **Capex:** $4.2M
- **Payback:** 4.2–4.8 years (highest capex but full-site scale + quality ROI justify)
- **Risk level:** HIGH (flagship status, community exposure, union sensitivities if applicable, largest staff, most complex integration) — **mitigated by 18+ month delay**

### 1.3 Sequencing Rationale: Reversed from Draft

**Original draft proposed:** Site B pilot (M4–M12) → Sites C/D (M13–M24) → Site A (M25–M36).

**Revised sequence:** **Site E pilot (M4–M12) → Sites B/C/D (M13–M36, phased) → Site A (M30–M48)**.

**Why reversed:**

| Dimension | Original (B as Pilot) | Revised (E as Pilot) | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Turnover Risk** | Site B: 40% turnover makes attrition forecasts unreliable; 20–25 pickers may quit unexpectedly during ramp, creating service gaps | Site E: 22% turnover is predictable; low absolute numbers; attrition is manageable. Payroll planning is certain. | **Learning is cleaner; no "emergency staffing" distractions**. Success is more repeatable. |
| **Technical Simplicity** | G2P + conveyor integration is complex; ecommerce order variety (SKU mix changes frequently) makes WMS configuration fragile | Sorter + conveyor is deterministic; full-case orders are predictable; WMS is unchanged (sorter is offline control system, not integrated with picking) | **Faster learning curve; 8-week dual-running is realistic vs. 12–16 weeks for complex G2P**. |
| **Payback Speed** | G2P payback 4.8–5.2 years; benefits are deferred while learning occurs (M12+ ramp-up) | Sorter/dock payback 2.8–3.2 years; benefits accrue faster (dock labor reduction is immediate); financial proof-of-concept happens by M12 | **Board sees tangible savings by end of Phase 1; confidence in program increases.** Funds Phase 2 expansion with internal cash flow. |
| **Staffing Retention** | High-turnover site (Site B) experiences 15–20 picker exits during ramp; morale drops; training effectiveness suffers; error rate may spike before improving | Low-turnover site (Site E) loses only 3–5 staff during M4–M12; stable team learning together; peer mentoring works; error rate stays flat or improves | **Psychological safety for staff; change-management success. Staff spread word to other sites: "This works, not scary."** |
| **Replication Risk** | Site B playbook may not transfer to Sites C/D (different order profiles, SKU density); learning is site-specific | Site E playbook (sorter + conveyor) is highly transferable to all sites (all have sorters; all have docks). Sites B, C, D benefit from proven template. | **Phase 2 designs are validated, faster, cheaper.** No re-learning per site. |
| **Community Risk** | No direct benefit; Site B is medium-exposure region. No reputational upside in hometown. | Site E's dock ergonomics improve safety; local news covers "injury reduction & worker care" narrative. Builds goodwill in lower-pressure region before tackling flagship. | **Reputational proof-of-concept. Site A sees: "Company cares about worker safety."** Sets tone for community engagement. |

## SECTION 2: PHASED TIMELINE, CAPITAL TRANCHES & GO/NO-GO GATES

### 2.1 Program Phases & Milestones (48-Month Timeline)

```
PHASE 0 (M1–M3):        Foundation & Pilot Design
PHASE 1 (M4–M12):       Site E Pilot + Site A Prep + Board Confidence Building
PHASE 2 (M13–M36):      Sites B, C, D Phased Rollout (Waves 1, 2, 3)
PHASE 3A (M25–M29):     Phase 2 Stabilization + Site A Final Prep
PHASE 3B (M30–M48):     Site A Phased Rollout (18-month ramp)
POST-GO-LIVE (M49+):    Steady-State & Payback Validation
```

**Rationale for 48-month timeline (vs. 36-month draft):**
- Extended dual-running windows: 8 weeks per site minimum; 12–16 weeks for complex sites (vs. 4–8 weeks draft). Reduces service-level collapse risk.
- Phased Phase 2 deployments (not concurrent): Avoids simultaneous training chaos and PSL surge-capacity strain.
- 18-month Site A ramp (M30–M48, not 6 months): Community stakeholder engagement requires slower cadence; multiple ramp windows; measured narrative.
- Learning pipeline: Each site's go-live informs next site's design; cost/schedule is optimized, not compressed.
