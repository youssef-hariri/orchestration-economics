# 2. Market insight

The campaign should be designed around informed choice rather than aggressive health promises.

Relevant market considerations include:

- Consumers may seek clear information about ingredients, amounts, intake, manufacturing, and safety.
- Moderate, practical messaging is more credible than miracle claims or dramatic transformation.
- Supplements should be presented as optional additions to ordinary food and personal lifestyle habits.
- Trust can be strengthened through concrete, verifiable information rather than broad claims such as “premium,” “natural,” “pure,” or “safe.”
- Japanese consumers are not homogeneous. Claims about cultural preferences for moderation, family well-being, or skepticism should be treated as hypotheses and validated through local research.
- The total impression matters: wording, images, experts, testimonials, packaging, search terms, disclaimers, and landing pages must be reviewed together.

**Local validation required:** Japanese consumer research should test naturalness, trust, distinctiveness, perceived pressure, optionality, product-category comprehension, and unintended medical interpretations.

# 3. Audience

## Primary pilot audience

> **Health-conscious adults aged 30–59 in Japan who value clear product information and may want an optional addition to an existing nutrition routine.**

Initial targeting should use contextual or non-sensitive interest signals.

## Initial exclusions

Do not initially target:

- Children
- Pregnant or breastfeeding consumers, unless specifically supported by the safety assessment
- People with diagnosed conditions
- People taking medication where interactions have not been assessed
- Audiences inferred to have disease, symptoms, medication use, fertility concerns, mental-health conditions, frailty, or cognitive decline
- Adults aged 60+ until age-related safety and communication requirements have been confirmed

The campaign must not imply that people who do not use supplements are irresponsible or neglectful.

## Business objectives

Final numerical targets must be set after baseline research and pilot-scale confirmation.

- First-time purchasers
- Conversion rate

- Average order value
- Retail sell-through

“Qualified traffic” should mean non-bot traffic showing meaningful engagement with product, ingredient, or safety information.

## Communication objectives

- Explain what the product contains and how it should be used.
- Improve understanding of the product’s actual category and role.
- Build trust through clear information and documented product attributes.
- Ensure consumers understand that the product is not a medicine.
- Encourage label-compliant use without creating pressure or fear.

## Ethical objectives

- Zero unresolved prohibited or unsupported claims.
- 100% pre-publication asset approval.
- 100% prominent disclosure for paid creator content.
- No misleading pricing, subscription, scarcity, or review practices.
- Prompt identification and escalation of adverse events and safety questions.

# 6. Ethical messaging framework

## The campaign will

- Explain ingredients, amounts, use, precautions, and evidence clearly.
- Make product use visibly optional.
- Encourage consumers to follow the stated daily intake.
- Position the supplement alongside—not instead of—ordinary food and lifestyle habits.
- Use concrete and verifiable quality information.
- Make advertising relationships prominent.
- Provide accessible Japanese information across all formats.
- Review claims in text, images, testimonials, expert content, metadata, comments, and landing pages together.

## The campaign will not

- Promise or imply disease diagnosis, treatment, cure, or prevention.
- Guarantee rapid, universal, or dramatic results.
- Suggest medication replacement or delayed medical care.
- Encourage excessive intake or unassessed combinations with other supplements.
- Exploit fear of illness, aging, weight, appearance, or social rejection.
- Use before-and-after body imagery or shame-based testimonials.
- Use medical settings, symbols, white coats, or laboratory theatrics to imply endorsement.
- Use fake scarcity, misleading countdowns, hidden renewals, or cancellation barriers.
- Present supplementation as equally necessary as food, movement, sleep, or medical care.

# 7. Claims policy and substantiation

## Candidate factual copy

The following is not pre-approved; each use requires product-specific verification:

> **[商品名]は、[成分名]を含む食品です。原材料、含有量、1日摂取目安量、注意事項をご案内しています。**  
> **[Product Name] is a food containing [ingredient]. We provide the ingredients, amounts, recommended daily intake, and precautions.**

Optional format statement:

> **毎日の習慣に取り入れやすい形状です。**  
> **It has a format that is easy to include in a daily routine.**

Use this only where objectively supported by the product format. It must not imply superior adherence, absorption, efficacy, or safety.

## Claims requiring heightened review

Do not use without product-specific evidence and confirmation of the applicable Japanese claim route:

- **免疫力を高める** — strengthen immunity
- **疲労回復** — recover from fatigue
- **腸内環境を改善する** — improve the intestinal environment
- **睡眠の質を改善する** — improve sleep quality
- **血糖値を下げる** — lower blood glucose
- **コレステロールを下げる** — lower cholesterol
- **若々しさを保つ** — maintain youthfulness
- **脂肪を燃焼する** — burn fat
- **健康を維持する** — maintain health
- **体調を整える** — regulate physical condition
- **サポートする** — support, where it conveys a physiological benefit

## Prohibited unless specifically authorized and substantiated

- Disease diagnosis, treatment, cure, or prevention
- Medication replacement
- Guaranteed, rapid, or universal results
- Weight-loss or anti-aging promises
- **医師が推奨する** — doctors recommend it
- **臨床的に証明された** — clinically proven
- **副作用がない** — no side effects
- **誰でも実感できる** — everyone can feel the results
- **飲むだけで変わる** — change simply by taking it

Unsupported claims must be removed, not softened with a disclaimer.

## Claims register

Maintain a signed register for every consumer-facing statement.

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Exact wording | Japanese copy and any English equivalent |
| Claim type | Product fact, nutritional, functional, testimonial, lifestyle, or implied claim |
| Evidence | Study, specification, test, or record |
| Product relevance | Ingredient-level or finished-product evidence |
| Dose and population | Studied amount and applicable audience |
| Limitations | Duration, sample size, comparator, uncertainty |
| Japanese status | Applicable category or claim route |
| Required qualifier | Context or required disclosure |
| Approvers | Regulatory, safety, nutrition, copy, and business owners |

Review evidence for dose, formulation, human relevance, population, study quality, safety, limitations, and whether the Japanese wording overstates the findings. Foreign or ingredient-level research does not automatically substantiate a claim for the finished Japanese product.

If the product is a Foods with Function Claims or FOSHU product, use only the applicable exact wording. Do not imply that notification or designation constitutes government approval of efficacy or medicine endorsement.

# 8. Safety and disclaimer framework

Use exact wording approved for the final product and channel.

## General statement

> **本品は、疾病の診断、治療、予防を目的としたものではありません。**  
> **This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, or prevent disease.**

Where applicable:

> **ご利用前に、原材料、1日摂取目安量、注意事項をご確認ください。服薬中の方、妊娠中・授乳中の方、疾病のある方、アレルギーが気になる方は、医師または薬剤師にご相談ください。**  
> **Before use, check the ingredients, recommended daily intake, and precautions. If you take medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, have a medical condition, or have allergy concerns, consult a doctor or pharmacist.**

Also, where applicable:

> **本品はバランスのよい食事に代わるものではありません。**  
> **This product is not a substitute for a balanced diet.**

The product-specific safety review must identify concrete warnings relating to:

- Allergens
- Medication interactions
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding
- Age restrictions
- Relevant medical conditions
- Maximum daily intake
- Storage and accidental ingestion
- Combination with other supplements

A general consultation statement is not a substitute for required warnings and does not guarantee safety. Customer-service staff must provide product information and escalation, not individualized medical advice.

Health-related information must be collected only through secure, approved channels with data minimization, access controls, retention limits, and appropriate privacy review.

# 11. Influencer and expert guidance

## Suitable partners

- Japanese registered dietitians or accurately described nutrition professionals
- Food, cooking, walking, and balanced-lifestyle creators
- Creators with no history of extreme dieting or unsafe supplementation
- Creators with predominantly Japan-based audiences
- Partners willing to follow approved claims, disclosure, monitoring, and correction requirements

Experts must not be presented as providing medical endorsement. Credentials, compensation, and review scope must be accurate.

## Required disclosure

Use prominent, relationship-specific disclosure:

> **広告｜この投稿は[会社名]から依頼を受けた広告です。**  
> **Advertisement | This post was commissioned by [Company].**

For gifted products, affiliate commissions, employee advocacy, livestreams, and reposts, use wording that accurately describes the relationship. Do not bury disclosures among hashtags.

For video and stories:

- Keep disclosure visible on screen.
- Repeat it in each relevant story frame.
- Use verbal disclosure where practical.
- Use platform disclosure tools where appropriate.
- Test visibility on mobile.
- Define minimum duration, placement, contrast, and text size.

## Approved influencer script

> **広告｜この投稿は[会社名]から依頼を受けた広告です。**  
> 私は、食事などの生活習慣も大切にしながら、パッケージに記載された1日摂取目安量で[商品名]を取り入れています。ご利用前に、原材料や注意事項をご確認ください。服薬中の方や、利用について気になることがある方は、医師または薬剤師にご相談ください。  
>   
> **本品は、疾病の診断、治療、予防を目的としたものではありません。**

Creators may discuss genuine experiences with taste, packaging, format, or routine convenience. They may not claim disease relief, symptom improvement, medication compatibility, rapid results, weight loss, anti-aging effects, superior efficacy, or higher-than-label intake.

Preapprove guidance for questions about efficacy, side effects, medication, pregnancy, missed doses, and suitability. Medical questions should be directed to official support or a healthcare professional.

Monitor captions, comments, replies, livestreams, affiliate pages, and reposts throughout the campaign.

| Claims | Signed approved/prohibited claims register |

| Governance | Named owners, RACI, escalation matrix, and pause rules |
| Inventory | Stock, lot consistency, expiry, storage, and advertising-pause plan |

The launch date depends on completion of these workstreams, not merely the passage of eight weeks.

## Controlled launch: weeks 1–2

- Use limited paid media and a restricted audience.
- Monitor search terms, complaints, comments, replies, ad rejections, safety inquiries, and subscription confusion daily.
- Test product-category, optionality, intake, and warning comprehension.
- Pause any asset creating a medical, guaranteed, fear-based, unsafe, or misleading impression.

## Optimization: weeks 3–10

Test only compliant variations:

- Ingredient-information layout
- Product-format description
- Educational versus lifestyle creative
- Short versus detailed product page
- Informational CTA wording

Do not test borderline claims to improve conversion.

## Evaluation: weeks 11–12

Assess:

- Incremental sales
- Conversion and acquisition cost
- Appropriately timed repeat purchases
- Returns and cancellations
- Subscription complaints
- Ingredient and safety-page engagement
- Category, intake, optionality, and warning comprehension
- Disclosure compliance
- Complaints and adverse events per unit sold
- Time to detect and remove misleading content
- Customer-service response times

Do not scale if conversion improves while comprehension declines, complaints increase, consumers infer treatment benefits, or unsafe-use questions increase.

# 15. Risk mitigations

## Claims and regulatory risk

- Maintain a signed claims and evidence register.
- Require product-specific approval for every health-related statement.
- Review the total impression, including visuals, experts, testimonials, search terms, SEO, metadata, alt text, comments, and retailer copy.
- Remove unsupported claims rather than relying on disclaimers.
- Preserve records of evidence, approvals, translations, and final assets.

## Product-safety risk

- Complete ingredient-specific interaction, allergen, age, pregnancy, breastfeeding, and maximum-intake reviews.
- Include concrete warnings where required.
- Add dose-stacking guidance where relevant.
- Establish adverse-event escalation, pause, withdrawal, and relaunch rules.
- Pause advertising if credible safety concerns or lot-quality issues arise.

## Consumer-protection risk

- Display total price, ongoing price, shipping, renewal, cancellation, refund, and return terms before checkout.
- Prohibit fake scarcity, hidden renewals, preselected subscriptions, and cancellation barriers.
- Use genuine reviews and disclose incentives.
- Do not suppress legitimate negative reviews.

## Influencer and disclosure risk

- Use contracts, approved claims, relationship-specific disclosures, monitoring, correction, and takedown procedures.
- Apply the same rules to affiliates, employees, gifted products, livestreams, retailers, marketplace sellers, replies, and reposts.
- Suspend payments or activity for repeated violations.

## Privacy risk

- Use secure channels for health-related inquiries and adverse-event reports.
- Collect only necessary information.
- Define consent, retention, access, and deletion controls.
- Do not request detailed medical information in public comments or ordinary social messages.

## Cultural and reputational risk

- Avoid fear, shame, body criticism, aging anxiety, and family-responsibility pressure.
- Use realistic and varied people and routines.
- Avoid implying that supplements are superior to food.
- Test whether the campaign feels generic, paternalistic, overly scripted, or medically suggestive.
- Do not continue promotion after credible safety concerns without documented review.

## Accessibility risk

- Adopt a documented accessibility standard.
- Test mobile, video, web, retail, and screen-reader experiences.
- Use readable disclosures, captions, transcripts, adequate contrast, keyboard access, and claim-neutral alt text.
- Provide telephone or printable support where feasible.
- Test comprehension, not only technical conformance.

# Final campaign direction

> **毎日の健やかさを、無理なく。**  
> **成分と摂取目安を確認して、サプリメントを利用する場合は、ご自身の生活習慣とともに、表示に従ってご利用ください。**

This campaign builds persuasion through clarity, verifiable information, convenience, and respect for consumer choice. It avoids unsupported efficacy, medical implication, vulnerable-audience pressure, covert advertising, cultural stereotyping, unsafe use, and manipulative purchasing practices.

**Final publication remains prohibited until all product-specific regulatory, safety, substantiation, privacy, localization, accessibility, and consumer-protection gates are complete and documented.**
