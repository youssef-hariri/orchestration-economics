# Executive Transition Plan: Perpetual Licensing to Subscription Revenue

## Executive recommendation

Adopt a **36–48-month, customer-led hybrid transition** to recurring revenue. Do not abruptly end perpetual licensing or require customers to subscribe to retain licenses they have already purchased.

The company should:

1. Preserve existing perpetual-license rights under each customer’s applicable contract.
2. Launch four clearly differentiated offers:
   - Perpetual Essential.
   - Perpetual Advantage.
   - Term Enterprise.
   - Cloud Complete.
3. Migrate customers by segment and renewal event, prioritizing active-maintenance and cloud-ready accounts.
4. Retain perpetual and customer-managed options for regulated, air-gapped, technically constrained, and economically unsuitable customers.
5. Use targeted, time-limited migration credits—not permanent discounts.
6. Redesign sales compensation around collected, implemented, retained, quality-adjusted ARR.
7. Fund the revenue trough through a staged investment envelope with explicit cash, financing, retention, margin, and capacity gates.
8. Approve Phase 0 and a tightly controlled pilot now; approve broad migration only after the financial, legal, operational, product, and compensation requirements below are validated.

**Success is not universal conversion.** It is higher recurring gross profit, stronger customer lifetime value, improved cash visibility, and retention of the installed base.

---

# 1. Planning basis

## Known facts

- The current model relies on perpetual licenses and paid upgrades.
- Revenue is lumpy and receives a lower investor valuation than predictable recurring revenue.
- Sales compensation favors large upfront transactions.
- Existing customers believe they own licenses already purchased.
- Management expects a multiyear revenue trough during the transition.
- An abruptly transitioning competitor reportedly lost approximately 20% of its installed base.

## Illustrative planning assumptions

The following figures are **illustrative planning inputs, not forecasts**:

| Item | Illustrative value |
|---|---:|
| Current reported annual revenue | $100m |
| Perpetual licenses and upgrades | $65m |
| Maintenance/support | $25m |
| Services and other | $10m |
| Existing recurring ARR | $30m |
| Historical upgrade cycle | Three years, to be validated |
| Transition period | 36–48 months |
| Initial cloud gross margin | 65%–70% |
| Potential mature cloud gross margin | 72%–78% |

The $65 million of current-year license and upgrade revenue is not equivalent to $65 million of recurring annual revenue. If those transactions recur every three years, their normalized annual contribution may be approximately $21.7 million before retention, timing, services, and growth effects.

Before setting targets, management must reconcile:

- Reported revenue.
- Normalized economic revenue.
- Existing recurring ARR.
- Eligible migration ARR.
- Annualized upgrade economics.
- Support and services revenue.

All targets must identify whether they use the **reported-revenue baseline** or the **normalized economic baseline**.

# 2. Customer rights and offer structure

## Customer-rights principle

Previously purchased perpetual rights must remain governed by the applicable contract. The company must not unilaterally convert those rights into expiring access.

A perpetual right to use an acquired version may not automatically include:

- Future major upgrades.
- Technical support.
- Security patches.
- Compatibility updates.
- Cloud hosting.
- Subscription-only functionality.

Those entitlements must be determined contract by contract and stated clearly in every proposal.

## Four commercial offers

### 1. Perpetual Essential

For customers requiring ownership, offline deployment, capital-budget purchasing, infrequent upgrades, or a non-cloud environment.

**Includes, subject to contract:**

- Existing perpetual license rights.
- Optional technical support.
- Defined maintenance releases and security updates while support is active.
- Paid professional services.

**Does not automatically include:**

- Future major versions.
- Cloud hosting.
- Subscription-only features.
- Support after an applicable support term expires.

### 2. Perpetual Advantage

A bridge offer for customers that want to retain ownership while receiving regular releases.

**Includes:**

- Existing perpetual license.
- Annual support.
- Defined upgrades during the paid period.
- Security and compatibility updates as specified.
- Defined service levels.
- Selected functionality as contractually specified.

**Illustrative price hypothesis:** 18%–25% of current equivalent license value annually, subject to product-level margin and willingness-to-pay validation.

### 3. Term Enterprise

For customers willing to adopt recurring commercial terms but unable or unwilling to use public cloud.

**Includes:**

- One-, three-, or five-year term rights.
- Customer-managed, private-cloud, or dedicated deployment.
- Current-version rights during the term.
- Support, security, and agreed upgrades.
- Deployment assistance.
- Explicit exit and data-export terms.

A customer’s existing perpetual license remains separate unless the customer voluntarily agrees to a documented exchange.

### 4. Cloud Complete

The strategic offer for cloud-ready customers.

**Includes:**

- Managed hosting.
- Continuous upgrades.
- Security monitoring.
- Backup and disaster recovery.
- Service-level commitments.
- Analytics, automation, integrations, and other genuinely incremental capabilities.
- Migration assistance.
- Expansion through users, capacity, transactions, or modules.

Cloud pricing may exceed support-only pricing when it includes managed operations and new value, but the company must demonstrate a credible customer total-cost or productivity benefit.

## Rights-preservation controls

Before broad migration, the company must complete:

- A product- and contract-specific rights matrix.
- Standard rights-preservation language.
- A formal non-coercion policy.
- Minimum support, security, and interoperability commitments.
- Tested migration, reversion, and data-export procedures.
- Accounting and tax treatment for migration credits and contract modifications.

The company must not preserve a license legally while making it commercially unusable through punitive support, compatibility, pricing, or product-maintenance practices.

# 3. Customer segmentation and migration paths

| Segment | Recommended path |
|---|---|
| Strategic enterprise | Executive-sponsored coexistence plan; Term Enterprise or Cloud Complete; two- to three-year migration roadmap |
| Active-maintenance commercial | Perpetual Advantage, Term Enterprise, or Cloud Complete; renewal-linked migration credit |
| Cloud-ready commercial | Cloud Complete; bundled migration; 60–120-day dual run; adoption plan |
| Regulated or restricted | Perpetual Essential, Perpetual Advantage, or private Term Enterprise; no forced public-cloud migration |
| Low-engagement or price-sensitive | Standardized perpetual/support or self-service subscription; limited migration resources |
| Lapsed maintenance | Paid reinstatement, paid upgrade path, or forward-looking subscription reactivation |
| At-risk or competitor-exposed | Retention remediation before migration; executive sponsor and recovery plan |

Before scale approval, management must quantify by segment:

- Customers and current revenue.
- Eligible and technically migratable ARR.
- Expected conversion and churn.
- Renewal timing.
- Cost to migrate.
- Gross margin.
- Channel ownership.
- Product readiness.
- Account risk.

The analysis must also show top-10, top-25, and top-50 ARR concentration and ARR renewing over the next eight quarters.

# 4. Migration incentives

## Active-maintenance customers

Use targeted, temporary credits based on documented prior investment.

| Timing of eligible prior purchase | Illustrative credit hypothesis |
|---|---:|
| Within 12 months | 75%–100% of approved eligible value |
| 13–24 months | 40%–75% |
| More than 24 months | Smaller credit or first-year concession |

Credits must:

- Apply only to the first one or two subscription years.
- Require a minimum two- or three-year term.
- Be capped by documented economic value.
- Preserve contribution-margin and cash thresholds.
- Exclude sunk implementation and third-party costs unless approved.
- Be excluded from renewal pricing.
- Receive consistent Legal, Finance, Tax, and channel treatment.

## Other incentives

- 60–120-day dual-run.
- Bundled or discounted migration services.
- Training and adoption support.
- Three-year price protection.
- A 3%–7% prepayment discount where cash value justifies it.
- Early access to genuinely incremental functionality.
- Selective 12–18-month reversion rights for design partners and high-risk strategic accounts.

Reversion may be offered only where data export, restoration, support, cost allocation, and technical feasibility have been tested.

## Lapsed-maintenance customers

Lapsed customers retain their perpetual rights but should not receive free retroactive support or upgrades. Offer:

- Paid reinstatement.
- Forward-looking subscription reactivation.
- Paid upgrade and security services.
- Standardized, low-touch support.

Measure each path by conversion, two- and three-year gross profit, renewal, usage, and cash collection—not signature volume alone.

# 5. Pricing strategy

Use the pricing metric that best reflects customer value:

- User or seat pricing for collaboration products.
- Capacity or transaction pricing for usage-driven products.
- Module or platform pricing for enterprise systems.
- Hybrid pricing only where it remains understandable.

Every offer must be evaluated on both sides of the economics.

### Customer economics should include

- Historic license and support cost.
- Internal infrastructure and administration.
- Security and compliance.
- Downtime.
- Migration disruption.
- Integration work.
- Exit and reversion costs.
- Renewal-price risk.

### Company economics should include

- Hosting and infrastructure.
- Support and customer success.
- Implementation.
- Product investment.
- Sales commissions.
- Payment processing and bad debt.
- Partner economics.
- Renewal probability.
- Cost of maintaining perpetual alternatives.

## Pricing controls

- 0%–10% discount: standard approval.
- 10%–20%: sales leadership and Finance approval.
- Above 20%: CFO and deal-desk approval.
- Nonstandard credits, SLAs, termination, or reversion: Legal, Finance, and deal-desk approval.
- Credits and discounts are cumulative for approval purposes.
- Renewal pricing must be disclosed at signing.
- Three-year annual increases should generally be capped at 3%–5%, subject to validated usage and infrastructure provisions.

The four-offer portfolio should be simplified through standard pricing metrics, limited credit structures, and strict controls on custom terms.

# 6. Revenue trough and cash management

The transition must be modeled through separate schedules for:

- Bookings.
- Billings.
- ARR.
- Recognized revenue.
- Deferred revenue.
- Collections.
- Gross profit.
- Cash flow.

\[
ARR \neq Bookings \neq Billings \neq Revenue \neq Cash
\]

For example, a three-year subscription with $270,000 total contract value may produce:

- Bookings at signing: $270,000.
- Annual contract value: $90,000.
- Annual billings: approximately $90,000.
- Upfront billings: potentially $270,000.
- Recognized revenue: approximately $90,000 per year, subject to accounting treatment.
- Deferred revenue after one year of upfront billing: approximately $180,000, subject to contract terms.

## Required financial model

Finance must produce a reconciled 60-month customer-cohort model, monthly for the first 24 months, covering:

- Customers and ARR by segment and offer.
- New, migrated, expanded, contracted, and churned ARR.
- Perpetual, support, term, cloud, usage, and services revenue.
- Bookings, billings, collections, and deferred revenue.
- Go-live timing and implementation delays.
- Gross profit by offer.
- Implementation, cloud, support, customer-success, and commission costs.
- Operating expense, capital expenditure, debt service, financing, cash, and covenant headroom.

## Required scenarios

The model must include:

1. Base case.
2. Upside case.
3. Downside case.
4. Severe downside.
5. 20% installed-base-loss case.
6. Two-year recovery delay.
7. Low-prepayment and weak-collections case.
8. Cloud-margin underperformance case.

The 20% loss case must show ARR, revenue, gross-profit, cash, covenant, financing, recovery-time, and cost-reduction implications.

Illustrative revenue and ARR tables should not be presented as forecasts until they are generated by this reconciled model.

## Board funding guardrails

The board should approve:

- Maximum reported-revenue trough.
- Minimum cash balance.
- Minimum downside-case runway.
- Base-case investment envelope.
- Contingency reserve.
- Financing trigger.
- Hiring and implementation-capacity ceiling.
- Mandatory cost-reduction trigger.

Illustrative starting points, subject to company data:

- At least 18 months of downside-case runway.
- Cash floor equal to six months of fixed operating costs.
- No acceleration below 15 months of downside-case runway.
- Monthly cash review for the first 24 months.

# 7. Sales compensation redesign

## Principles

- Pay for collected, implemented, retained ARR.
- Do not give full credit for the entire value of a multiyear contract.
- Reward renewal and expansion.
- Preserve incentives for appropriate perpetual exceptions.
- Keep the plan transparent and automated.
- Protect sellers during the transition.

## Recommended structure

| Transaction | Treatment |
|---|---|
| New subscription | 100% quota credit for approved Year 1 ARR |
| Multiyear subscription | Year 1 ARR plus limited credit for Years 2–3 |
| Expansion | Credit on incremental ARR |
| Renewal | Commission on collected renewal value |
| Active-maintenance migration | Full credit on approved converted ARR |
| Lapsed reactivation | Credit on new recurring value only |
| Term Enterprise | High credit, subject to margin and implementation |
| Unsupported perpetual | Reduced or approval-only credit |
| Perpetual plus support | Greater credit than unsupported perpetual |

Illustrative commission rates:

- New subscription ARR: 8%–12% of first-year ARR.
- Expansion ARR: 4%–6%.
- Renewal ARR: 2%–4% of collected value.

Pay 50%–75% at signature or first invoice and hold back 25%–50% until go-live, first payment, or month 12.

Use product-level margin bands and approved price floors rather than frequently changing account-level estimates.

Clawbacks should:

- First affect unpaid holdbacks.
- Be capped.
- Focus on seller-controlled mis-selling, nonpayment, or defined early cancellation.
- Exclude company-caused implementation failure, product defects, customer bankruptcy, and documented customer-caused delays.
- Include a formal dispute process.
- Comply with applicable wage and commission laws.

Before launch, model old and new seller earnings at 80%, 100%, and 120% quota. Provide two quarters of partial quota protection, migration bonuses, and quota relief for migration-heavy territories. Taper protection over four to six quarters.

Customer-success and channel plans must separately reward implementation, adoption, first renewal, GRR, expansion quality, partner delivery, and collected recurring revenue.

# 8. Retention and adoption program

Retention is the primary transition control.

For each strategic or material account, document:

- Contractual rights and renewal dates.
- Products and versions in use.
- Critical workflows.
- Usage and adoption.
- Open incidents.
- Executive sponsor.
- Migration complexity.
- Data and compliance requirements.
- Competitor activity.
- Budget and procurement timing.
- Channel ownership.

During migration, provide:

- A named migration plan.
- Executive sponsorship for strategic accounts.
- A 60–120-day dual run.
- Data-conversion testing.
- Training and adoption support.
- Migration health scoring.
- 48-hour escalation for critical incidents.
- Defined service credits for agreed failures.
- Tested data export and reversion.

Track renewal readiness at 180, 120, and 90 days. The evidence hierarchy should be:

1. Usage and health.
2. Renewal intent.
3. Proposal acceptance.
4. Signed renewal.
5. Collected renewal.
6. Post-renewal usage and expansion.

Illustrative targets, subject to baseline validation:

| Metric | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Gross revenue retention | ≥88% | ≥89% | ≥90% |
| Migrated logo retention | ≥90% | ≥91% | ≥92% |
| First-renewal rate | ≥85% | ≥88% | ≥90% |
| On-time migration | ≥80% | ≥85% | ≥90% |
| Mature-cohort NRR | ≥100% | ≥105% | ≥108% |

These targets must be compared with current maintenance retention, upgrade renewal, implementation performance, and support satisfaction.

# 9. Phased rollout

## Phase 0: Validate and design — days 0–90

Complete:

- Contract and rights inventory.
- Non-coercion policy.
- Five-year revenue normalization.
- Segment economics and account-risk analysis.
- 60-month cohort model.
- Monthly 24-month cash model.
- Offer-specific accounting memos.
- Product and cloud-readiness assessment.
- Implementation and customer-success capacity model.
- Pricing and incentive hypotheses.
- Sales, customer-success, and channel compensation plans.
- Investor metric policy.
- Pilot design and budget.

**Entry to pilot requires:**

- At least 90% of material contracts classified.
- Legal-approved rights matrix and customer language.
- Finance reconciliation of reported and normalized baselines.
- Acceptable downside-case runway.
- Funded product, cloud, billing, implementation, and support capacity.
- Pilot customers confirming measurable value.

## Phase 1: Pilot — months 4–9

Use a capped, representative group of approximately 25–50 customers across:

- Strategic enterprise.
- Active-maintenance commercial.
- Cloud-ready.
- Regulated or customer-managed.
- Lapsed maintenance.
- At-risk accounts.

Test all four offers, pricing, credits, billing terms, dual-run, reversion, migration services, and compensation holdbacks.

**Pilot success criteria:**

- At least 85% logo retention.
- At least 90% on-time go-live.
- No material rights disputes.
- Gross margin within 10 percentage points of plan.
- Discounts within guardrails.
- Demonstrated usage and renewal evidence.
- Written customer reference approval where applicable.
- No critical capacity bottleneck.

## Phase 2: Controlled scale — months 10–24

- Prioritize renewal-linked migration.
- Maintain justified perpetual exceptions.
- Cap quarterly migration by the scarcest operational resource.
- Expand capacity only after demand is validated.
- Standardize packages and approval bands.
- Report ARR, bookings, billings, revenue, deferred revenue, collections, and cash separately.
- Review retention monthly and cohort economics quarterly.

## Phase 3: Optimize — months 25–48

- Reduce credits for proven segments.
- Increase prices where value and retention support it.
- Move suitable Term Enterprise customers toward Cloud Complete.
- Expand modules, users, capacity, and integrations.
- Retire uneconomic new-sale packages only after viable alternatives, notice, and contractual obligations are satisfied.
- Continue honoring existing perpetual rights and contractual commitments.

# 10. Governance and decision rights

| Workstream | Accountable executive |
|---|---|
| Financial model, cash, accounting | CFO |
| Offers and pricing | CRO |
| Customer retention and migration | Chief Customer Officer |
| Product roadmap | CPO |
| Cloud, security, and capacity | CTO/COO |
| Rights and contracts | General Counsel |
| Compensation | CHRO/CRO |
| Billing and systems | COO/CFO |
| Channel transition | CRO or channel leader |
| Investor communication | CFO/IR lead |

Each workstream must have a defined budget, deliverables, deadline, acceptance criteria, dependencies, and escalation path.

### Pause and escalation triggers

Pause or slow migration if:

- GRR falls below 85% for two consecutive quarters.
- Migration-related churn exceeds 5% of beginning ARR in a quarter.
- First-renewal rate falls below 85%.
- More than 15% of implementations miss target dates.
- Cloud gross margin remains below 60% beyond the approved scale period.
- Credits exceed 10%–15% of contract value without measurable retention benefit.
- Discounts exceed 20% without corresponding improvement in NRR or strategic justification.
- Bookings rise while collections, usage, or renewal evidence do not.
- Cash runway falls below 15–18 months.
- Material rights, security, product, or customer-trust issues emerge.

The CEO may pause a migration immediately for legal, customer, security, product, or cash reasons. Acceleration beyond an approved phase requires board approval.

# 11. Board and investor communication

## Board reporting

Quarterly materials should show:

- Reported and normalized baselines.
- ARR bridge:
  \[
  Beginning\ ARR + New\ ARR + Migration\ ARR + Expansion - Churn - Contraction = Ending\ ARR
  \]
- Migration by segment, cohort, and offer.
- Bookings, billings, collections, revenue, and deferred revenue.
- Gross profit by offer.
- GRR, NRR, logo retention, and first-renewal results.
- Discounts and credits.
- Implementation backlog and capacity.
- Cloud gross margin.
- Sales attrition and commission expense.
- Cash, runway, debt, covenants, and financing capacity.
- Perpetual exception volume.
- 20% installed-base-loss scenario status.

## Investor metric policy

Define consistently:

- Whether support is included in ARR.
- Whether term licenses are included.
- Treatment of usage-based revenue.
- Exclusion of professional services unless genuinely recurring.
- Treatment of credits, free periods, prepaid cancellations, and reversion.
- Channel-generated ARR.
- Acquisitions and divestitures.
- Historical recasts.
- Reconciliation to financial statements.

## Recommended investor message

> We are executing a staged, customer-led migration that preserves previously purchased perpetual rights. Reported revenue may be temporarily pressured because subscription revenue is recognized over time, whereas historical license transactions were recognized largely at delivery. We are managing the transition against explicit retention, implementation, margin, cash, and conversion gates, and will report ARR, bookings, billings, deferred revenue, recognized revenue, gross retention, net retention, and cash flow separately.

Do not present illustrative revenue or ARR tables as forecasts, and do not commit to a specific recovery year until actual migration and renewal cohorts support that conclusion.

# Immediate decisions for leadership

Leadership and the board should decide now:

1. Approve a **36–48-month phased hybrid transition**, not an abrupt conversion.
2. Approve Phase 0 and a capped pilot, subject to the stated entry criteria.
3. Approve the four-offer architecture, including perpetual and customer-managed paths.
4. Confirm that previously purchased perpetual rights will not be unilaterally revoked or converted.
5. Set the maximum acceptable revenue trough and minimum cash/runway thresholds.
6. Approve development of the 60-month reconciled financial model and required downside scenarios.
7. Approve targeted migration-credit and discount guardrails, pending Finance, Legal, and Tax validation.
8. Approve redesign of sales compensation around collected, implemented, retained ARR.
9. Require a funded product, cloud, billing, implementation, support, and customer-success capacity plan before scale.
10. Approve quarterly board gates for retention, conversion, margin, implementation, customer rights, and cash.
11. Approve the investor metric policy and communication framework.
12. Define the long-term acceptable mix of cloud, term, perpetual, support, and services revenue.

## Final recommendation

Proceed with a **controlled diagnostic and pilot**, not full-scale conversion. The company should earn the right to accelerate through evidence: durable customer retention, successful implementation, validated pricing, improving cloud economics, reliable billing and accounting, acceptable seller behavior, and sufficient cash runway.

The transition should be judged successful only when recurring gross profit and cash visibility improve **without violating customer rights, repeating the competitor’s installed-base loss, or replacing durable economics with discounted short-lived ARR**.
