# Japan Dietary Supplement Campaign Deliverable  
**Status: Pre-launch development only — not approved for public release**

## 1. Executive summary

The proposed campaign introduces `[Product Name]` in Japan using an informed-choice platform:

> **具体的な情報を、選ぶ人にわかりやすく。**  
> “Clear, concrete information for people making a choice.”

The campaign should build awareness and consideration through accurate product information, transparent evidence, understandable precautions, responsible pricing, and prominent advertising disclosures. It must not present the supplement as a medicine or as a substitute for balanced food, rest, movement, prescribed treatment, or professional care.

The campaign is **not launch-ready**. The product, classification, ingredients, dosage, intended use, evidence, safety profile, price, manufacturing details, and sales model remain unconfirmed. All claims, visuals, testimonials, influencer content, Japanese copy, labels, offers, and purchase flows require product-specific Japanese regulatory, safety, privacy, and native-language review.

**Release gate:** No public campaign until all mandatory checks are documented and approved by the relevant Japanese reviewers.

---

## 2. Market rationale

The campaign should test—not assume—that relevant Japanese consumers may value:

- Clear ingredients, dosage, directions, and precautions
- Verifiable quality and manufacturing information
- Credible, specific evidence
- Convenient formats that fit existing routines
- Transparent pricing, subscriptions, cancellation, and refunds
- Respectful, non-coercive communication
- Accessible Japanese customer support
- Information that allows consumers to decide independently

The campaign should avoid broad claims about “Japanese consumers” as a single group. Test differences by:

- Age, including directly addressing active older adults rather than only family purchasers
- Existing supplement use versus category skepticism
- Region and urbanity
- Product motivation, such as convenience, nutrition, beauty, daily condition, or another verified purpose
- Retail, search, marketplace, and social-media behavior

Do not rely on stereotypes, including assumptions that Japanese consumers universally prefer understated communication, family-oriented decision-making, discreet self-care, or traditional Japanese imagery.

## 3. Audiences

Audience definitions are hypotheses pending Japanese-language research and product-safety confirmation.

### Primary: informed supplement users

Potential characteristics:

- Already compare supplements, functional foods, or nutrition products
- Seek ingredient, dosage, format, price, quality, and evidence information
- Use search, e-commerce, retail websites, and reviews

**Communication need:** Specific, verifiable product differences and evidence limitations.

### Secondary: routine-oriented working adults

Potential characteristics:

- Balance work, commuting, household, or caregiving responsibilities
- Value convenience and clear directions
- May prefer e-commerce, drugstores, pharmacies, or specialty retailers

**Communication need:** Verified format, preparation, serving, storage, price, and pack-size information.

### Tertiary: active older adults

Potential characteristics:

- Adults approximately `[age range—confirm]` who maintain hobbies, work, exercise, or social activities
- May value legibility, pharmacy access, telephone support, and clear interaction guidance

**Controls:** Do not portray aging as a defect, weakness, dependency, or condition to be reversed. Confirm age suitability before targeting.

### Conditional: younger wellness or beauty consumers

Use only if the product’s composition, evidence, and intended use support this audience. Test whether the relevant motivation is nutrition, convenience, beauty, sport, price, sustainability, or another verified need.

### Conditional: family or household purchasers

Do not use guilt, fear, or implied obligation, such as suggesting that purchasing demonstrates care for a parent or partner. Provide recipient-facing safety information where gifting is tested.

### Exclusions or restrictions

Do not target or make suitability claims for children, pregnant or breastfeeding people, people taking medication, people with medical conditions, or vulnerable consumers unless product-specific safety and legal reviews support doing so.

Do not target based on inferred disease, symptoms, medication use, menopause, infertility, depression, insomnia, frailty, or weight-related distress.

This positioning is a working framework only. It must be revised after the following are confirmed:

- Legal category: `[ordinary food / dietary supplement / FFC / FOSHU / other—confirm]`
- Ingredients and dosage: `[ingredient(s), amount per serving—verify]`
- Format: `[tablet / capsule / powder / beverage—verify]`
- Intended use: `[general nutritional support / other—confirm]`
- Permitted communication scope
- Evidence and safety profile

### Core communication platform

> **具体的な情報を、選ぶ人にわかりやすく。**  
> “Clear, concrete information for people making a choice.”

Alternative working platform:

> **毎日の選択を、確かめやすく。**  
> “Make everyday choices easier to check.”

Both are communication principles, not efficacy claims. They require native-copywriter review.

### Reason to believe

The campaign must add one or more specific, verified reasons to choose the product, such as:

- Ingredient amount
- Format or preparation
- Traceability
- Documented manufacturing process
- Specific testing
- Verified pack size or convenience feature
- Legally permitted and substantiated function claim

Do not rely on generic terms such as “quality,” “premium,” “scientifically advanced,” or “trusted” without defining and documenting them.

## 5. Message framework

### Pillar 1: Product facts

Communicate only verified information:

- Product category
- Ingredients and quantities
- Serving size and directions
- Format, flavor, texture, or preparation
- Pack size and number of servings
- Manufacturer or importer
- Country of origin or manufacturing location
- Documented testing and quality processes
- Allergen and caution information

**Working Japanese example — native-copywriter review required:**

> **原材料、成分量、召し上がり方を確認しやすく表示しています。**  
> “The ingredients, quantities, and directions are displayed clearly for review.”

Use only if the information is genuinely clear and prominent.

### Pillar 2: Routine fit

Use factual usability language without implying a health outcome.

**Working Japanese example — native-copywriter review required:**

> **毎日の生活に取り入れる前に、召し上がり方をご確認ください。**  
> “Before incorporating it into your daily life, please check the directions.”

Avoid implying that continued use produces a specific result.

### Pillar 3: Evidence clarity

**Working Japanese example — native-copywriter review required:**

> **どの情報が、どのような根拠に基づくものかをご説明します。**  
> “We explain which information is supported by which evidence.”

State whether evidence relates to:

- The finished product
- An ingredient
- A specific dose
- A particular population
- Quality or identity testing only

Include limitations in understandable Japanese.

### Pillar 4: Responsible use

**Working Japanese example — native-copywriter review required:**

> **ご使用前に、表示・召し上がり方・注意事項をご確認ください。**  
> “Before use, please check the labeling, directions, and precautions.”

Any warning for medication, medical conditions, pregnancy, breastfeeding, age, or interactions must be product-specific and medically reviewed.

### Pillar 5: Broader well-being

The supplement may be presented as one optional part of a broader routine, subject to review. Do not suggest it replaces balanced food, sleep, movement, prescribed treatment, or professional care.

## 6. Creative platform

### Creative idea

Show consumers independently checking information before choosing a product. Use varied, realistic everyday settings rather than stereotypical cultural imagery.

Potential scenes, subject to audience and product relevance:

- A commuter reviewing ingredients
- An older adult checking directions before a hobby
- A household purchaser reviewing suitability
- A younger consumer comparing product information online
- A person asking an appropriate professional a general product question

Avoid:

- Miracle transformations
- Before-and-after images
- Medical settings, white coats, hospital cues, or prescription-like design
- Fear of aging, decline, illness, or inadequacy
- Body shame or weight-loss pressure
- Family guilt
- Cherry blossoms, tea ceremonies, salarymen, or multigenerational scenes used merely as cultural shorthand
- Visual implications stronger than the approved wording

### Design principles

- Calm but not vague
- Informative but not overloaded
- Persuasive through verified product differences
- Clear product-category identification
- Readable warnings and labels
- Sufficient contrast and font size
- Captions and text alternatives
- Mobile-first presentation
- No candy-like presentation where dosing requires care
- No medical visual cues implying treatment

## 7. Channel plan

### Owned website and landing page

Recommended sections:

1. Product category and intended use  
2. Ingredients, quantities, and allergens  
3. Directions, serving limits, storage, and precautions  
4. Manufacturing, sourcing, and quality information  
5. Evidence and limitations  
6. Appropriate professional-consultation guidance  
7. Price, shipping, tax, subscription, cancellation, and refund terms  
8. Customer-service and safety contacts  
9. Privacy notice and marketing preferences  

The page should use plain Japanese and clearly separate factual information from promotional content.

### Search and publisher advertising

Use non-sensitive, product-relevant intent such as verified ingredient, category, or general label-information searches.

**Working Japanese example — native-copywriter review required:**

> **[Verified product category]｜成分・召し上がり方を確認**  
> `[Product Name]`の原材料、成分量、品質管理、利用方法をご案内します。  
> “`[Verified product category]` | Check ingredients and directions. We provide information about `[Product Name]`’s ingredients, amounts, quality controls, and use.”

Replace placeholders only after verification.

### YouTube and video

Use:

- Ingredient education
- Label-reading guidance
- Preparation demonstrations
- Directions and precaution explanations
- Routine footage

Avoid symptom-to-recovery narratives, product-result measurements, unqualified expert claims, and medical imagery.

### Instagram and TikTok

Use only with:

- Prominent sponsorship disclosure
- Pre-approved claims and scripts
- Platform-specific monitoring
- No medical or guaranteed-result statements
- No request for public disclosure of health conditions

### LINE and email

Use only with valid, understandable opt-in consent.

Possible uses:

- Transactional updates
- Product education
- Opt-in reminders
- Customer support
- Clearly labeled promotions

Separate transactional and promotional consent. Provide a simple opt-out process and avoid exposing sensitive product information in shared-device notifications where feasible.

### Retail, pharmacies, and drugstores

Provide:

- Approved staff script
- Prohibited-claims list
- Product category and intended-use guidance
- Ingredient and warning guide
- Escalation route for safety questions

Staff must not diagnose, recommend the product for a condition, advise stopping medication, or imply purchase is necessary for responsible health management.

### E-commerce marketplaces

Keep the following consistent:

- Product title
- Images
- Bullet points
- Reviews
- Price and shipping
- Subscription terms
- Warnings
- Manufacturer or importer details

Do not purchase positive reviews, suppress legitimate negative reviews, or repeat prohibited claims found in user-generated content.

### Retargeting and CRM

Use only lawfully obtained, appropriately consented, non-sensitive signals. Do not imply that the company has inferred a disease, symptom, medication use, or body concern.

> “Please review the product labeling and precautions before use.”

### Evidence-page language

> **利用できる根拠、その対象、限界についてわかりやすくご説明します。**  
> “We explain the available evidence, what it relates to, and its limitations.”

### Influencer example

> **#PR｜[Company Name]から商品提供を受けています。**  
> 私が確認したのは、[format]で、1回分に[verified amount]が含まれること、そして表示や召し上がり方が確認しやすい点です。  
> 商品が自分に適しているか、使用前に表示と注意事項をご確認ください。  
>
> “#PR | I received this product from `[Company Name]`. I checked that it is in `[format]`, contains `[verified amount]` per serving, and that the labeling and directions are easy to review. Please check the labeling and precautions before deciding whether it is suitable for you.”

Use only if the creator has genuinely received the product, the disclosure is prominent, the statement is accurate, and the Japanese wording is approved.

### Avoid by default

- “Prevents,” “treats,” “cures,” or “relieves”
- “Boosts immunity”
- “Detoxes”
- “Burns fat”
- “Reverses aging”
- “Feel the difference in seven days”
- “Safe for everyone”
- “No side effects”
- “Clinically proven”
- “Japan’s No. 1,” “best,” or “most trusted”
- “The only supplement you need”
- Before-and-after claims
- Medical testimonials
- “If you care about your family, buy this”
- Fake scarcity or health-related urgency

## 10. Claims-and-evidence controls

### Claim governance

Maintain a Japanese claims matrix containing:

1. Exact claim  
2. Claim type  
3. Intended audience  
4. Context and asset  
5. Product or ingredient evidence  
6. Evidence limitations  
7. Legal status  
8. Required warning or qualifier  
9. Approval owner  
10. Version and date  

### Evidence file

For every express or implied claim, retain:

- Exact Japanese wording
- Product and formulation tested
- Ingredient identity and dosage
- Study population
- Study design and endpoints
- Results and limitations
- Relevance to the target audience
- Whether evidence concerns the finished product or an ingredient
- Safety information
- Source and date
- Scientific or regulatory approver

Do not treat ingredient studies, laboratory findings, traditional use, professional opinion, or foreign evidence as automatic proof of a finished-product consumer outcome.

### Claim-type controls

- **Product facts:** Verify against formulation, label, packaging, and commercial records.
- **Quality claims:** Document the specific process or test; avoid vague superiority language.
- **Safety warnings:** Use product-specific food-safety and medical review.
- **Health claims:** Confirm classification, legal scope, evidence, dosage, population, and Japanese wording.
- **Price and offer claims:** Verify against actual commercial terms and records.
- **Origin claims:** Verify manufacturing and supply-chain facts.
- **Testimonials:** Moderate and substantiate any health implication; do not treat “results vary” as sufficient protection.
- **Comparisons:** Define the market, date, methodology, comparison set, and evidence.
