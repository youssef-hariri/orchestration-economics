# Industrial-Services Unit: Turnaround-or-Exit Assessment and Plan

## 1. Executive recommendation

The board should authorize **one final, 12-month prove-or-exit program**, with turnaround and exit preparation running in parallel.

This is **not another general cost-reduction exercise**. The unit must prove that a focused portfolio of customers and service lines can:

- Win work at acceptable prices.
- Deliver it reliably and safely.
- Recover scope changes and invoice promptly.
- Convert earnings into cash.
- Retain the talent required to operate.
- Generate an acceptable return after working capital, maintenance capital, risk, and full cost-to-serve.

### Recommended posture

> **Protect and grow the profitable core, reprice or exit structurally unattractive work, and prepare a sale, carve-out, selective disposal, partnership, or wind-down from the outset.**

The board should release funding in tranches, subject to decision gates at **Day 30, Day 90, Month 6, and Month 9**. No extension beyond the one-year deadline should be granted without evidence of sustainable positive free cash flow or a signed, executable exit outcome.

---

## 2. Diagnosis

### What is established

The unit has:

- Lost money for three consecutive years.
- Failed to restore profitability after two restructuring programs.
- A shrinking order book.
- Experienced managers transferring to profitable divisions.
- Customers that also purchase from other group divisions.

These facts establish that the current operating model is not working. They do **not** yet establish the proportion of losses caused by market structure, service mix, pricing, execution, management capability, or group-level cost allocation.

### Working diagnosis

The most likely diagnosis is **mixed**:

| Potential cause | Current indication | Required test |
|---|---|---|
| Structural market or service pressure | Shrinking order book; possible commoditization or subscale operations | Market growth, competitor economics, customer willingness to pay |
| Poor pricing and contract selection | Prior cost reductions did not restore profit | Quote-to-cash margin bridge, price realization, contract profitability |
| Delivery and productivity failure | Manager transfers may have weakened planning and control | Utilization, labor variance, rework, overtime, schedule adherence |
| Portfolio mix problem | Profitable and loss-making work may be blended | Customer and service-line contribution analysis |
| Weak cash conversion | Industrial services often lose value through delayed billing and working capital | Contract-level cash margin, receivables, unbilled work |
| Overstated group strategic value | Shared customers are not necessarily incremental or profitable | Attributable contribution and retention counterfactual |
| Leadership and governance failure | Best managers are leaving; prior interventions may have lacked commercial accountability | Talent data, decision rights, benefits verification |

### Structural-loss test

The problem is structural if losses remain after normalizing:

- Pricing and inflation recovery.
- Utilization and labor productivity.
- Scope and change-order recovery.
- Rework, warranty, and claims.
- Management coverage.
- Working-capital and billing discipline.
- One-time restructuring effects.

It is also structural if the market will not support prices sufficient to cover full cost, risk, maintenance capital, and an acceptable return.

### Execution-loss test

The problem is primarily execution-driven if:

- Quoted margins are acceptable but completed-job margins are not.
- Actual hours materially exceed estimates.
- Utilization is weak despite adequate demand.
- Losses are concentrated in particular contracts, locations, customers, or managers.
- Repricing and operating controls produce rapid, repeatable margin improvement.
- Customers remain willing to renew or expand if service reliability improves.

### Required loss bridge

By Day 45, the CFO should reconcile the last 36 months into:

1. Volume and market decline.
2. Price realization versus cost inflation.
3. Customer and service-line mix.
4. Labor productivity and utilization.
5. Overtime and subcontracting.
6. Rework, warranty, claims, and penalties.
7. Unrecovered change orders and scope creep.
8. Travel, mobilization, and standby.
9. Controllable overhead and corporate allocations.
10. Restructuring and other one-time costs.
11. Bad debt and working-capital effects.
12. Maintenance capital expenditure.
13. Quantified cross-divisional benefits.

The bridge must show both the reported unit and a normalized, controllable operating result.

## 3. Evidence, assumptions, and hypotheses

### Known facts

The facts available are limited to the loss history, failed restructurings, order-book decline, manager transfers, and shared customers described above.

### Hypotheses requiring validation

The following are **hypotheses, not established conclusions**:

- **H1:** Pricing is below full cost-to-serve or does not recover operational risk.
- **H2:** Losses are concentrated in a limited number of contracts, customers, branches, or service lines.
- **H3:** Utilization, scheduling, estimating, or delivery control is materially below benchmark.
- **H4:** The order book is shrinking because of weak competitiveness, service reliability, or customer confidence.
- **H5:** Some recurring or specialist services have sufficient pricing power to sustain profit.
- **H6:** Cross-selling creates incremental group contribution that would be at risk without the unit.
- **H7:** Manager transfers are both a cause and a symptom of deteriorating performance.
- **H8:** The unit is subscale in some geographies or services and should use a partner or asset-light model.
- **H9:** A buyer could create more value through synergies than the group can realize internally.

### Planning assumptions

Until validated, the plan assumes:

- Labor and subcontracting are major variable costs.
- Customer and service-line profitability is not yet sufficiently reliable.
- Corporate allocations may obscure controllable performance.
- Contracts, employees, systems, and assets may be separable for an exit.
- The group can provide limited, staged funding through the proof period.

### Definitions required before target approval

Management must define and reconcile:

- Contribution margin.
- Cash contribution.
- Normalized EBITDA.
- Free cash flow.
- Maintenance versus growth capital.
- Material customer, contract, and service line.
- Critical talent.
- Weighted backlog.
- Strategic subsidy.
- Acceptable return on capital.

## 4. Attractive customer segments and service lines

## 4.1 Customer segmentation

Each material customer should be assessed on standalone profitability, cash contribution, retention, strategic value, risk, and incremental group contribution.

| Segment | Characteristics | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Core protect** | Profitable, recurring, retainable, strategically important | Protect service and renew early |
| **Expand** | Profitable, underpenetrated across the group | Pursue targeted, measured cross-selling |
| **Reprice and retain** | Valuable relationship but below hurdle | Reset price, scope, indexation, or service model |
| **Selective gateway** | Below-hurdle unit economics but proven incremental group value | Retain only with explicit, capped, time-limited subsidy |
| **Harvest** | Positive cash contribution but limited growth | Minimize investment and maximize cash |
| **Exit** | Negative contribution and weak future economics | Stop bidding, renegotiate, or exit |
| **Risk-controlled** | Credit, legal, safety, claims, or concentration exposure | Add risk premium, tighten terms, or exit |

A customer should not be labeled strategic solely because another group division sells to it.

## 4.2 Shared-customer economics

For every shared customer:

**Incremental group value =**  
incremental contribution attributable to the unit relationship  
× probability that it would be lost without that relationship  
− incremental account-management and delivery costs.

The analysis must exclude independently won revenue, unweighted opportunities, and internal transfers without external contribution.

Any below-hurdle customer retained for group reasons must have:

- A named beneficiary division.
- Transparent transfer pricing or an explicit subsidy.
- A quantified counterfactual.
- A stated amount, duration, owner, and exit condition.
- Quarterly review by the Group CFO and board committee.

If the benefit is not evidenced by Month 6, the account must meet standalone economics or enter an exit plan.

## 4.3 Service-line categories

Each service line should be classified by market attractiveness, margin, cash conversion, capital intensity, capability, and risk.

| Category | Test | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Invest/grow** | Differentiated, attractive demand, sustainable margin | Invest selectively |
| **Fix** | Attractive market but correctable pricing or execution leakage | Reprice and repair |
| **Harvest** | Positive cash contribution but limited growth | Minimize investment |
| **Partner/asset-light** | Viable demand but uneconomic owned footprint or assets | Use partners selectively |
| **Exit** | Persistent negative contribution, weak differentiation, high risk, or inadequate scale | Stop bidding, sell, transfer, or wind down |

**Hypothesis requiring validation:** recurring maintenance, specialist technical services, and differentiated field services may be attractive. No service line should receive priority until its market, price, capacity, and capability economics are demonstrated.

## 5. Turnaround choices and operating plan

### Commercial reset

Effective immediately:

- 100% of material bids require bid/no-bid approval.
- No new contract may have negative expected lifecycle cash contribution without executive and CFO approval.
- Margin hurdles must be set by service line after full cost, risk, working capital, and capital requirements are known.
- Strategic gateway work requires separate approval and a documented subsidy.
- Price risk for remote, emergency, accelerated, complex, or high-liability work.
- Recover travel, mobilization, standby, overtime, materials, and approved change orders.
- Use indexation and milestone billing where commercially feasible.
- Review every completed material job from quoted margin through cash margin.

An initial screening threshold may be used during the first 30 days, but a generic gross-margin target must not become a substitute for service-line economics.

### Legacy contract reset

Within 90 days:

- Identify all materially loss-making contracts.
- Reprice, rescope, renegotiate, complete, transfer, or exit them.
- Recover approved change orders and aged claims.
- Stop renewing work whose economics cannot be corrected.
- Obtain legal, customer, and group-value review before termination or nonrenewal.

Every exit decision must assess termination cost, customer impact, stranded overhead, warranty and safety obligations, utilization effects, buyer value, and impact on profitable group divisions.

### Operational recovery

The COO should implement:

- Weekly capacity planning by skill, geography, and customer.
- Improved scheduling, dispatch, routing, and sales-to-operations handoffs.
- Daily or weekly job-cost reporting on material work.
- Estimate-to-actual labor tracking.
- Change-order and customer-delay logs.
- Root-cause reduction of rework and warranty.
- Faster job closeout and invoicing.
- Selective use of subcontractors based on total delivered cost and flexibility.
- Asset utilization and footprint review.
- No growth capital until the Month-6 proof gate; mandatory safety and compliance capital remains protected.

### Talent actions

Within 30 days:

- Identify roles essential to customer continuity, estimating, delivery, safety, collections, and project control.
- Provide targeted retention arrangements.
- Fill or replace critical vacancies.
- Establish a successor or external replacement plan for every critical role.
- Conduct structured transfer and exit interviews.
- Align incentives with margin, cash, delivery, safety, and retention—not revenue alone.

**Target:** retain at least 90% of designated critical roles through Month 6, subject to the approved target portfolio. Any unplanned loss of an indispensable specialist triggers an immediate customer and delivery-risk review.

## 6. One-year milestones and dashboard

Targets below are provisional and must be reset against validated baselines by Day 30. A target is not a decision criterion until its formula, owner, data source, tolerance, and consequence are approved.

### Milestone plan

| Timing | Required outcomes | Funding / decision consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Day 30: Fact base** | Reconciled three-year P&L and cash; active contracts classified; at least 80% of material revenue and all major loss/risk exposures mapped; 13-week cash forecast live; critical-role plan approved; preliminary exit valuation and liability map | Release only approved stabilization funding. Unreconciled data triggers independent review and restricted commitments |
| **Day 90: Reset** | 100% of material bids under approval; at least 50% of identified loss-making contract revenue actioned; target portfolio proposed; measurable improvement in pricing, utilization, leakage, and cash burn | Continue only if evidence shows controllable leakage is improving and customers accept economic terms |
| **Month 6: Economic proof** | Focused core at or near normalized EBITDA break-even; positive contribution in the majority of future revenue; new work above hurdle; weighted backlog at or above hurdle; improved utilization, delivery, and collections; critical talent stable | If improvements are only one-time savings or the core remains structurally negative, begin formal exit |
| **Month 9: Strategic decision** | Positive normalized EBITDA for three consecutive months, positive operating cash flow or a fully funded and evidenced near-term path, stable/improving weighted backlog, adequate leadership, and continuation value exceeding exit value | Board selects continuation or exit. No further discretionary turnaround funding without a new decision |
| **Month 12: Outcome** | If retained: positive free cash flow after maintenance capex and normal working capital, quality backlog, sustainable new-book margins, stable talent. If exited: signed transaction, binding transfer agreement, or board-approved executable wind-down plan | Deadline met only by sustainable economics or an executable exit outcome—not by a reduced loss |

### Dashboard

The board should receive a monthly dashboard, segmented by branch, customer, service line, and material contract.

| Area | Measures |
|---|---|
| **Financial** | Normalized EBITDA, free cash flow, cash burn, contribution margin, margin leakage, working capital, receivables, maintenance capex |
| **Commercial** | Book-to-bill, weighted backlog, backlog margin and cash contribution, win rate, price realization, renewals, below-hurdle wins |
| **Operations** | Billable utilization, productive hours, labor variance, on-time completion, first-time fix, rework, warranty, overtime, subcontracting, safety |
| **People** | Critical-role retention, regretted attrition, vacancies, succession coverage, absence, engagement |
| **Strategic** | Attributable cross-divisional contribution, subsidies, customer concentration, exit readiness |
| **Exit readiness** | Buyer interest, separation issues, contract assignability, stranded costs, liabilities, data-room completeness |

### Illustrative operating targets

Subject to Day-30 validation:

- 10–15% utilization improvement by Month 3 and 15–20% by Month 6.
- 20% reduction in avoidable overtime, rework, and emergency subcontracting by Month 3.
- 30% reduction in estimate-to-actual labor variance by Month 6.
- On-time completion above 90% by Month 6.
- Invoice cycle time reduced by 25% by Month 3.
- At least 75% of overdue receivables either collected or under a board-approved recovery plan by Month 6; collection, not reclassification, is the principal measure.
- Cash burn reduced by 50% by Month 3, unless the validated baseline shows a different liquidity priority.

These are leading indicators. They do not substitute for positive free cash flow.

## 7. Funding, governance, and talent

### Funding envelope

Before Day 30, management must present:

- Stabilization funding through Day 90.
- Conditional funding for Months 4–6.
- Maximum cumulative turnaround support.
- Minimum liquidity floor.
- Separate reserve for warranty, claims, redundancy, tax, pension, environmental, and other exit liabilities.
- Funding required to complete profitable backlog.
- Funding required under downside scenarios.

Any amount above the approved envelope requires board approval.

### Governance

| Role | Accountability |
|---|---|
| **Board turnaround committee** | Monthly oversight through Month 6; approves scope, funding, strategic subsidies, major contract exits, and transition to exit |
| **Group CEO** | Executive sponsor; resolves conflicts with profitable divisions and enforces authority |
| **Turnaround leader** | Single accountable owner with authority across commercial, operations, finance, and people |
| **CFO** | Financial baseline, cash, customer economics, benefits verification, funding control |
| **COO** | Utilization, delivery, quality, safety, footprint, and assets |
| **Commercial Director** | Pricing, bid discipline, renewals, customer segmentation |
| **General Counsel** | Contract resets, liabilities, assignments, employee and regulatory issues |
| **CHRO** | Critical-role retention, succession, transfers, and workforce actions |
| **Transaction lead** | Sale, carve-out, partnership, selective disposal, or wind-down execution |
| **Independent validator** | Reviews major customer economics, strategic benefits, turnaround benefits, and exit valuations |

The turnaround leader must be able to prevent unplanned internal transfers from critical roles and must have authority to decline uneconomic work.

### Incentive safeguards

Incentives must not reward:

- Delayed cost recognition.
- Uncollectible invoicing.
- Asset sales treated as recurring performance.
- Rejection of all difficult work.
- Revenue growth without margin and cash.
- Safety or customer-service deterioration.

## 8. Exit alternatives

| Alternative | When attractive | Principal risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Trade sale** | Buyer can realize customer, geographic, capability, or cost synergies | Low valuation, selective asset purchase, customer or employee disruption |
| **Financial sale** | Business is separable and near break-even with a credible plan | Seller financing, earn-outs, retained liabilities, low valuation |
| **Group transfer or carve-out** | Strategic value exists but standalone scale is inadequate | Concealed losses, stranded costs, unclear ownership |
| **Joint venture or partnership** | Demand exists but owned assets or footprint are uneconomic | Quality, safety, margin dilution, partner dependence |
| **Selective sale** | Profitable teams, contracts, locations, or assets are identifiable | Residual entity retains liabilities and overhead |
| **Orderly wind-down** | Losses are structural and no buyer will assume the risk | Termination, redundancy, warranty, tax, safety, environmental, and reputational costs |
| **Managed or asset-light model** | Customers value access or expertise more than owned delivery capacity | Reduced control, margin dilution, service-quality risk |

The board should also assess asset sale versus share sale, management buyout, licensing, and a run-off vehicle for receivables, claims, warranties, and other residual obligations.

### Exit timing

Exit preparation begins immediately:

- Buyer universe and valuation by Day 45.
- Separation, liability, contract, employee, tax, pension, environmental, and IT mapping by Day 90.
- Decision on a formal dual-track sale process by Day 90.
- Indicative buyer, partner, or internal-transfer proposals by Month 6.
- Binding transaction, transfer agreement, or executable wind-down plan by Month 12 if exit is selected.

A market sounding should be confidential, supported by retention arrangements and customer-continuity plans.

## 9. Decision criteria and trigger dates

### Continue turnaround only if, by Month 9 and confirmed by Month 12:

1. The focused core generates sustainable positive EBITDA.
2. Free cash flow is positive after maintenance capex and normal working capital.
3. New and renewed work meets approved margin and cash hurdles.
4. Most future revenue comes from profitable or demonstrably repairable activities.
5. Weighted backlog is stable or improving and meets the required margin.
6. Losses are not concentrated in unresolved contracts.
7. Critical leadership and technical capability are stable.
8. Cross-divisional value is incremental, quantified, and separately reported.
9. Required funding is within the approved envelope.
10. Risk-adjusted continuation value exceeds the best exit alternative by a board-approved margin.

### Accelerate exit if any of the following occurs:

- Liquidity falls below the approved floor.
- Material data cannot be reconciled by Day 30.
- No measurable improvement in controllable leakage by Day 90.
- Customers reject repricing and below-hurdle work continues to expand.
- EBITDA remains materially negative at Month 6.
- Weighted backlog falls by more than 10% for two consecutive months or falls below the approved margin hurdle.
- New-book contribution remains below hurdle.
- Critical roles cannot be retained or replaced.
- Cross-divisional benefits remain unproven by Month 6.
- Downside cases require funding above the approved envelope.
- Continuation value no longer exceeds the best exit alternative.

### Valuation methodology

The board should compare net, risk-adjusted value to the group under:

- Base, downside, and severe-downside turnaround cases.
- Trade sale.
- Financial sale.
- Group transfer or carve-out.
- Partnership or asset-light model.
- Selective sale.
- Wind-down.

Each case must include:

- Probability of success.
- Revenue attrition and repricing effects.
- Working capital.
- Maintenance and growth capex.
- Restructuring and transaction costs.
- Stranded overhead.
- Taxes.
- Pension, warranty, environmental, legal, and employee liabilities.
- Parent funding.
- Customer and group-relationship effects.
- Terminal value and residual obligations.

Strategic value should be shown separately from financial value. A decision to retain the unit for strategic reasons below the financial hurdle must be approved explicitly as a group investment.

## 10. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Customers reject repricing | Segment accounts, use phased resets, redesign scope, and provide alternatives |
| Exiting work damages profitable group relationships | Require group-value, customer, legal, and stranded-cost review before exit |
| Capex freeze harms safety or service | Protect mandatory maintenance, compliance, and customer-critical investment |
| Branch consolidation reduces coverage | Model route density, response times, service levels, and customer retention first |
| Subcontractor reduction creates capacity or quality problems | Use total delivered cost, flexibility, and quality—not invoice cost alone |
| Retention package accelerates cost without results | Limit payments to designated critical roles and evidence of continuity |
| Sale process causes attrition or customer concern | Use confidentiality protocols, retention plans, and a controlled communication plan |
| Selective sale leaves a residual loss-maker | Complete residual-entity, stranded-cost, and liability analysis before approval |
| Cross-selling value is overstated | Use a documented counterfactual and independent validation |
| Management incentives distort results | Tie incentives to recurring cash margin, safety, quality, and return on capital |
| Downside exceeds liquidity | Maintain a funding ceiling and exit-liability reserve |
| Safety or compliance deteriorates under pressure | Give HSE independent escalation rights and protect required spending |

## 11. Immediate next steps

### Within 10 business days

1. Appoint the turnaround leader, executive sponsor, transaction lead, and board committee.
2. Establish the 13-week cash forecast and minimum liquidity floor.
3. Freeze nonessential commitments while protecting safety, compliance, and customer-critical work.
4. Implement bid/no-bid approval for all material opportunities.
5. Identify critical roles and begin retention actions.
6. Create a single group owner for each major shared customer.

### By Day 30

1. Deliver the reconciled three-year P&L and cash bridge.
2. Classify every material active contract.
3. Map customer and service-line economics, including risk-weighted loss exposures.
4. Establish the focused-core and discontinued-activity views.
5. Produce the initial funding envelope and downside cases.
6. Complete preliminary sale, carve-out, partnership, and wind-down valuations.
7. Approve the customer-continuity and contract-exit playbooks.

### By Day 90

1. Approve the target portfolio.
2. Complete the first wave of repricing, rescoping, contract exits, and operating changes.
3. Decide whether to launch a formal dual-track sale or carve-out process.
4. Confirm whether leadership capability is sufficient or requires replacement.
5. Release or withhold the next funding tranche based on evidence, not narrative.

## Final conclusion

The unit should receive one final opportunity, but only as a **focused, commercially led prove-or-exit program**. The board should not preserve loss-making revenue, count unproven cross-selling as profitability, or treat cost reduction and working-capital releases as a turnaround.

The unit earns the right to continue only by proving **repeatable, cash-generative economics** in a defined core. If that proof is not available by the Month-9 decision gate and confirmed by Month 12, the group should execute the highest-value exit route without extending the deadline.
