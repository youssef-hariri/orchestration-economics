# Executive Recommendation: Subscription-First Hybrid Transition

**Recommendation:** Approve a **subscription-first hybrid transition** over approximately 36 months, with a five-year path to the target state.

- Sell subscription to substantially all new logos and for all new products immediately.
- Preserve every valid perpetual right already granted.
- Migrate existing customers voluntarily, by segment and renewal/upgrade event.
- Retain time-limited term-license or private-deployment exceptions for regulated, disconnected, and highly customized customers.
- Use declining migration credits and controlled dual-use periods—not permanent price reductions.
- Redesign sales compensation before broad rollout.
- Fund the transition against an integrated revenue, billings, cash, gross-margin, and liquidity model.
- Require pilot results and board stage gates before each expansion.

This approach sacrifices some speed of ARR conversion versus an abrupt switch, but materially reduces the risk of repeating the competitor’s approximately 20% installed-base loss. It is expected to produce a shallower, more manageable trough while preserving the option to accelerate if retention, adoption, and cash performance exceed plan.

All figures below are **illustrative assumptions**, not forecasts.

---

## 1. Strategic principles

### 1.1 Protect the installed base before optimizing conversion speed

The company’s most valuable asset during transition is not reported ARR; it is the installed customer relationship. The decision rule should be:

\[
\text{Expected subscription value}
=
\text{conversion}
\times
\text{retention}
\times
\text{expansion}
\times
\text{gross margin}
-
\text{discounts}
-
\text{migration cost}
-
\text{incremental churn cost}
\]

A rapid conversion that causes substantial churn can destroy more long-term value than a slower migration with higher retention.

### 1.2 Separate owned rights from new value

Customers retain valid perpetual-use rights for the versions they purchased, subject to existing contractual terms. Subscription pricing must clearly purchase **incremental value**, such as:

- Current and future releases
- Security updates
- Premium support
- Cloud or managed operations
- Backup and recovery
- New integrations
- Usage and seat flexibility
- Analytics and administrative capabilities
- Service-level commitments
- Migration and customer-success services

The company should never imply that customers must repurchase rights they already own.

### 1.3 Make subscription the default, not an abrupt mandate

Subscription should be the default for:

- New logos
- New products
- Materially new modules
- Customers whose technical and regulatory requirements are already supported

Existing customers should migrate through a defined path, usually at renewal, maintenance anniversary, or next upgrade event.

### 1.4 Optimize total economic value, not headline ARR

ARR, bookings, billings, GAAP revenue, cash collections, deferred revenue, and gross profit are different measures.

The company should not approve a deal solely because it increases ARR. Each transaction must satisfy:

\[
NPV(\text{subscription gross profit})
-
\text{migration subsidy}
-
\text{implementation cost}
-
\text{incremental retention cost}
>
NPV(\text{legacy alternative})
\]

### 1.5 Use temporary incentives with a visible end state

Migration credits, introductory discounts, and dual-use rights should:

- Be time-limited
- Decline over time
- Require a meaningful subscription commitment
- Be capped
- Be included in total-concession reporting
- Avoid establishing a permanently lower price

### 1.6 Treat cash preservation and customer trust as design constraints

The transition must be managed to an approved:

- Maximum revenue trough
- Minimum cash balance
- Minimum covenant headroom
- Maximum installed-base churn
- Minimum subscription gross margin
- Maximum average migration concession

### 1.7 Use evidence-based expansion

The company should begin with pilots, measure cohort results, and expand only when adoption, retention, discounting, delivery capacity, and cash outcomes meet predetermined gates.

## 2. Target offer architecture

### 2.1 Subscription packages

Use a limited number of packages to avoid commercial and product fragmentation.

| Package | Target customer | Core contents | Commercial structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Essential | Small and straightforward customers | Current product, standard support, security updates, standard integrations | Annual or three-year term; monthly option at premium |
| Professional | Default commercial offer | Essential plus premium support, advanced integrations, reporting, migration tools, and enhanced usage limits | One- or three-year term; annual upfront billing preferred |
| Enterprise | Strategic and complex customers | Professional plus enhanced SLA, security/compliance features, sandbox, analytics, dedicated success and executive reviews | Two- or three-year term; minimum annual commitment |
| Private/Regulated Deployment | Disconnected, regulated, or highly customized environments | Subscription rights deployed on customer infrastructure or private cloud | Two- or three-year term; price premium versus SaaS |

Add-ons should be separately priced where practical:

- Migration and implementation
- Data conversion
- Premium support
- Additional environments
- Storage or usage
- Compliance modules
- Custom integrations
- Training

### 2.2 Deployment options

Offer:

1. SaaS
2. Private cloud
3. Customer-managed term deployment
4. Hybrid deployment where technically necessary

Customer-managed deployments should be **term subscriptions**, not new perpetual licenses, and should carry a premium reflecting:

- Additional support complexity
- Release management
- Security obligations
- Lower operating leverage
- Infrastructure-related cost

### 2.3 Perpetual-rights policy

Existing customers may:

- Continue using the perpetual version they purchased.
- Maintain legacy support or upgrade rights where contractually provided.
- Convert to a subscription for current and future value.
- Run a perpetual version while subscribing to support, cloud, security, or selected add-ons.
- Use a defined dual-use period while migrating.

If support for obsolete versions is eventually retired, provide:

- 12–24 months’ notice
- A published lifecycle policy
- Data export
- Migration assistance
- A commercially reasonable conversion path

This policy directly reduces installed-base-loss risk by removing the perception that subscription is a forced repurchase.

### 2.4 Standard contract terms

- Essential: one year, with three-year option
- Professional: one or three years
- Enterprise and private deployment: two or three years
- Annual renewal after initial term
- Annual price increase cap of 3–5% for protected customers
- Annual payment in advance as the preferred billing method
- Quarterly or monthly billing available by segment, generally at a 5–10% premium
- Defined data-export and transition-assistance obligations
- Termination for material breach and persistent SLA failure
- No cancellation of previously granted perpetual rights

## 3. Segmented migration incentives for existing license holders

A single offer should not be applied across the installed base.

### 3.1 Strategic enterprise customers

**Characteristics:** High ACV, substantial customization, strategic relationships, complex integrations.

**Offer:**

- Two- or three-year Enterprise subscription
- 12–24-month dual-use period, subject to milestones
- 3–5% price cap for the initial term and one renewal
- Credit for eligible recent maintenance and upgrade spend
- Migration services and executive sponsorship
- Quarterly migration governance

**Migration approach:**

1. Joint value and architecture assessment
2. Conversion agreement
3. Parallel operation
4. Milestone-based implementation
5. Acceptance testing
6. Subscription go-live
7. Legacy retirement only after agreed acceptance criteria

### 3.2 Current-maintenance customers

**Characteristics:** Already paying annually for support or updates; generally lowest-friction cohort.

**Offer:**

- 50–100% credit for unused maintenance value
- Waived or discounted standard migration fee
- One-year price hold
- 5–15% commitment discount for a two- or three-year term
- Immediate access to current releases

Convert at maintenance renewal or earlier if the customer has a clear value case.

### 3.3 Upgrade-cycle customers

**Characteristics:** Purchase major upgrades periodically and may not see value in paying every year.

**Offer:**

- Credit for a recent upgrade, with the credit declining with age
- Three-year total-cost comparison against historical ownership
- Subscription start aligned to expected upgrade timing
- Option to retain the last perpetual version during transition
- Free or discounted first-year access to the current release

Do not force conversion between upgrade cycles without a compelling product or economic benefit.

### 3.4 Dormant or lapsed customers

**Characteristics:** No active maintenance, old versions, uncertain usage.

**Offer:**

- Re-entry package
- Limited, not automatic, migration credit
- Paid discovery or technical assessment
- Defined supported-version baseline
- Standardized migration package

Require account qualification before granting significant concessions.

### 3.5 Small and price-sensitive customers

**Offer:**

- Essential package
- Simple annual subscription
- Monthly option at a premium
- Self-service migration tools
- Fixed-price data import
- Strict discount floors

Use digital campaigns and in-product messaging rather than costly bespoke sales processes.

### 3.6 Regulated, disconnected, and highly customized customers

**Offer:**

- Private or customer-managed term subscription
- Longer transition period
- Security and compliance documentation
- Explicit release and support obligations
- Premium pricing for nonstandard deployment

Exceptions should have an expiry date and be reviewed annually.

### 3.7 Migration-credit formula

For customer \(i\):

\[
C_i=
\min\left[
C_{\max},
\alpha M_i+\beta U_i+\gamma L_i+\delta E_i
\right]
\]

Where:

- \(M_i\) = eligible maintenance paid in the prior 24 months
- \(U_i\) = unused value of recent upgrade or license purchase
- \(L_i\) = eligible recent perpetual-license spend
- \(E_i\) = approved migration or implementation cost
- \(C_{\max}\) = maximum allowed total credit

Illustrative policy:

- 50–100% of unused maintenance value
- 25–50% of a recent upgrade purchase, depending on age
- 10–25% of recent perpetual-license spend
- Approved migration costs where necessary
- Total credit capped at 25–50% of first-year subscription ACV

Example:

- First-year subscription ACV: $100,000
- Eligible maintenance: $20,000
- Recent upgrade credit: $15,000
- Approved migration cost: $10,000
- Calculated credit: $45,000
- Credit cap: 35% of ACV, or $35,000
- Final credit: $35,000

Credits should generally reduce invoices rather than be paid in cash.

### 3.8 Dual-use policy

- Standard accounts: 90–180 days
- Complex accounts: up to 12 months
- Exceptional strategic accounts: up to 24 months with executive approval

Dual use must be documented, milestone-based, and nontransferable. It should not become an indefinite free entitlement.

## 4. Pricing and discount policy

### 4.1 Pricing framework

For each package establish:

1. List price
2. Target transaction price
3. Floor price

The starting reference should be the customer’s historical total cost of ownership, adjusted for incremental subscription value:

\[
P_{\text{subscription}}
\approx
\left(P_{\text{license annualized}}+
M+
U\right)
\times
(1+\text{incremental value adjustment})
\]

However, historical spend must be annualized correctly. A customer that bought a perpetual license and used it for six years should not be treated as though it purchased that license every year.

### 4.2 Illustrative pricing ranges

- Year-one effective migration discount: 15–35%
- Year-two discount: 5–20%
- Steady-state discount: 0–10%
- Multiyear commitment discount: 5–15%
- Annual price cap: 3–5%
- Monthly billing premium: 5–10%

These are starting ranges only. Pilot results should determine the optimal package.

### 4.3 Total-concession control

The company must combine all concessions:

\[
\text{Total concession}
=
\text{price discount}
+
\text{migration credit}
+
\text{free period}
+
\text{free users}
+
\text{waived services}
+
\text{payment-term concession}
\]

A transaction must satisfy both:

\[
\text{Total concession} \leq \text{approved segment ceiling}
\]

and:

\[
NPV(\text{subscription gross profit})
-
\text{total concession}
-
\text{implementation cost}
\geq
\text{required return}
\]

### 4.4 Approval matrix

| Effective concession | Required approval |
|---|---|
| 0–10% | Account executive within standard rules |
| 10–20% | Regional or sales leader |
| 20–30% | Deal desk and finance |
| Above 30% | Executive committee |
| Below margin floor or outside credit policy | CFO approval |

The CRM and quoting system must show price discounts and credits together.

### 4.5 Trade-off management

| Decision | Adoption | Retention | Cash flow | Trough depth | Long-term economics |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deeper year-one discount | Higher | Potentially higher | Lower per customer | Deeper recognized-revenue trough | Worse if permanent or excessive |
| Longer dual-use period | Higher for complex accounts | Higher | Delayed collections | Longer trough | Justified only for high-risk accounts |
| Annual upfront billing | May reduce adoption slightly | Neutral to positive if value is clear | Better | Does not eliminate GAAP trough | Better working capital, higher renewal cliff |
| Monthly billing | Higher initial adoption | May increase flexibility | Worse | Potentially deeper cash trough | Use selectively and price at premium |
| Retaining perpetual options | Protects retention | Higher | Supports near-term cash | Shallower | Creates complexity and cannibalization |
| Faster legacy retirement | Faster ARR transition | Higher churn risk | Variable | Potentially deeper | Simpler end state |

## 5. Sales compensation redesign

The existing plan rewards large upfront perpetual transactions and is therefore structurally opposed to the desired model.

### 5.1 Transition plan: first 12–18 months

#### New-logo sellers

Illustrative quota credit:

\[
\text{Quota credit}
=
100\%\times ARR_1
+
35\%\times ARR_{2,3}
+
\text{approved term/upfront bonus}
\]

- 100% credit for first-year subscription ACV
- 25–50% credit for years two and three of a signed multiyear term
- Additional credit for annual upfront billing
- Reduced credit for monthly billing
- No credit for unapproved discounts below floor

#### Installed-base migration sellers

- 100% credit for new subscription ARR
- 50% credit for expansion ARR
- Separate fixed migration bonus for targeted accounts
- No quota credit for the migration credit itself
- Accelerators for two- or three-year commitments
- Reduced credit for deals below margin or concession floors

#### Legacy perpetual sales

- Permitted only in approved exception segments
- Lower quota credit than subscription sales
- Commission based on collected cash or approved gross profit
- No credit toward subscription quota
- Sunset date defined by segment

### 5.2 Steady-state plan

Illustrative variable-compensation weighting:

- 55–65% new ARR or subscription ACV
- 15–20% gross retention and renewal quality
- 10–20% expansion ARR
- 5–10% cash collection, term, or gross-margin quality

Account managers should have greater retention and expansion weighting than new-logo sellers.

### 5.3 Accelerators and quality conditions

Illustrative accelerators:

- 1.0x below 80% of quota
- 1.25x at 100–110%
- 1.5x at 110–125%
- 2.0x above 125%

Accelerators apply only if:

- Gross retention exceeds the minimum threshold
- Discounts are within policy
- No material implementation failure occurs
- Early cancellation remains within limits
- Forecast accuracy is acceptable

### 5.4 Holdbacks and clawbacks

Hold back 10–25% of commission for 90–180 days, or until a defined payment/implementation milestone.

Apply clawbacks for:

- Early cancellation
- Nonpayment
- Unapproved discounting
- Material misrepresentation
- Failure to meet contract-quality requirements

### 5.5 Transition protection

For the first two plan years:

- Guarantee 70–90% of prior-year target variable compensation for eligible reps
- Provide migration-account quota protection
- Offer a draw or recoverable advance for high-transition territories
- Provide specialist migration support and enablement

This protection should expire on a published schedule.

### 5.6 Account and channel rules

- Assign one primary account owner.
- Establish overlay split-credit rules.
- Credit customer-success-led expansions.
- Define dormant-account ownership.
- Prevent territory changes designed solely to gain credit.
- Give partners first-year subscription commission plus smaller renewal/residual economics.
- Tie partner compensation to retention and collection quality.
- Apply partner clawbacks and discount floors.

## 6. Phased timeline, pilots, and stage gates

### Phase 0: Preparation — Months 0–3

Complete:

- Customer-level entitlement and contract audit
- Cohort segmentation
- Package and product readiness review
- Pricing and margin model
- Five-year integrated financial model
- Accounting memorandum
- Sales compensation and channel amendments
- Migration-capacity plan
- Investor and board communication materials
- Liquidity and covenant stress tests

**Gate 0:** No pilot until legal rights, accounting, pricing floors, systems, compensation, and downside liquidity are approved.

### Phase 1: Pilot — Months 3–6

Pilot across:

- Current-maintenance customers
- Upgrade-cycle customers
- Small customers
- Strategic accounts
- Regulated/private-deployment customers
- Direct and channel routes

Use a control group where feasible and test:

1. Standard subscription with maintenance credit
2. Three-year stepped pricing
3. Subscription with dual-use
4. Private deployment
5. Subscription with implementation credit

**Gate 1 targets:**

- 25–40% conversion in targeted low- and medium-risk cohorts
- At least 90% gross retention
- Average discount within approved ceiling
- Sales productivity at least 70–80% of legacy baseline
- Implementation cost within 10–15% of plan
- Stable customer satisfaction
- No material rights, accounting, or product issue

### Phase 2: Controlled expansion — Months 6–18

- Subscription mandatory for new products and new logos, subject to approved exceptions
- Broaden migration to current-maintenance customers
- Align conversions with renewals and upgrade events
- Maintain legacy sales only for defined exceptions
- Report monthly to executives and quarterly to the board

**Gate 2:** Expand only if pilot results are repeatable across multiple cohorts and cash collections, gross margin, implementation capacity, and churn remain within plan.

### Phase 3: Subscription-first — Months 18–36

- Retire most perpetual new-sale options
- Retain private/term exceptions
- Reduce migration credits
- Move compensation toward renewals and expansion
- Increase price realization toward standard rates

**Gate 3:** Proceed only if the revenue trough and liquidity remain within the approved range and retention remains stable.

### Phase 4: Steady state — Month 36 onward

- Subscription is the default model
- Perpetual versions remain usable under existing rights
- Exceptions reviewed annually
- Legacy commercial variants reduced to the minimum necessary
- Reporting emphasizes retention, expansion, margin, cash, and contracted revenue

### Acceleration and stop rules

**Accelerate if:**

- Conversion exceeds plan by 10–15%
- Churn is at or below plan
- Discounts are below ceiling
- Annual upfront billing is strong
- Sales productivity is stable
- Implementation capacity is available

**Slow or segment if:**

- Churn exceeds plan by 3–5 percentage points
- Discounts exceed ceiling for two consecutive periods
- Sales productivity falls more than 20%
- Implementation backlog exceeds capacity
- Gross margin falls below floor

**Pause or redesign if:**

- Installed-base loss approaches 15–20%
- Early cancellations materially exceed legacy levels
- Liquidity falls below the approved contingency threshold
- Customers challenge the treatment of perpetual rights
- Subscription gross margin is materially below plan

## 7. Financial model and trough-management approach

### 7.1 Required integrated model

Build a monthly model for at least five years, by customer cohort and product, showing:

- Perpetual license revenue
- Upgrade revenue
- Maintenance revenue
- Subscription bookings
- Subscription billings
- GAAP revenue
- ARR
- Deferred revenue
- Cash collections
- Gross margin
- Hosting and support cost
- Implementation expense
- Sales commissions
- Capitalized commission amortization
- Operating expenses
- Capital expenditure
- Taxes
- Debt service
- Free cash flow
- Liquidity and covenant headroom

### 7.2 Revenue bridge

\[
\Delta R
=
-\text{lost perpetual timing}
-\text{lost upgrade-event timing}
-\text{migration credits/free periods}
-\text{churn}
+\text{recognized subscription revenue}
+\text{expansion}
+\text{services revenue}
\]

Subscription cash collections differ from revenue:

\[
\text{Cash collections}
=
\text{billings}
+\Delta \text{accounts receivable}
-\text{bad debt}
-\text{refunds}
\]

Deferred revenue:

\[
\text{Ending deferred revenue}
=
\text{Opening deferred revenue}
+\text{billings allocated to future obligations}
-\text{revenue recognized}
-\text{refunds/releases}
\]

### 7.3 Illustrative planning assumptions

Assume, for planning only:

- Subscription gross margin: 75–85%
- Five-year installed-base conversion: 65–80%
- Migration churn: 6–10% in the base case
- Year-one conversion: 15–25%
- Average year-one concession: 15–25%
- Gross retention after stabilization: 92–95%
- Net retention: 100–110%
- Annual upfront billing: 60–75%
- Revenue trough: 10–25% below a realistic legacy comparator
- Recovery: Year 3–4

Illustrative scenario ranges:

| Scenario | Five-year conversion | Churn | Revenue trough | Recovery |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Upside | 80–90% | 3–6% | 5–15% | Year 2–3 |
| Base | 65–80% | 6–10% | 10–25% | Year 3–4 |
| Downside | 50–65% | 10–15% | 25–40% | Year 4–5 |
| Severe downside | <50% | 15–20%+ | 40%+ | Uncertain |

The model must compare these outcomes with an optimized legacy case, not simply the current year.

### 7.4 Trough-management actions

To reduce trough depth and cash pressure:

1. Prefer annual upfront billing.
2. Align conversion starts with renewal and upgrade dates.
3. Use term commitments rather than permanent discounts.
4. Credit invoices rather than paying cash rebates.
5. Retain approved perpetual or term sales for high-risk cohorts.
6. Control migration pace according to implementation capacity.
7. Reduce discretionary hiring during the trough.
8. Stage product and infrastructure investment.
9. Use committed financing before liquidity becomes constrained.
10. Maintain a 13-week cash forecast during the transition.

### 7.5 Liquidity guardrails

Illustrative board thresholds:

- Minimum cash equal to 12–18 months of downside operating burn
- Minimum covenant headroom of 20–25% above required levels
- Minimum subscription gross margin of 75–80%
- Maximum quarterly migration subsidy approved in absolute dollars and as a percentage of eligible ACV
- Maximum DSO and minimum collection rate
- Maximum implementation backlog

If liquidity approaches the floor:

- Slow low-return migrations
- Reduce credits
- Require annual upfront payment
- Retain approved legacy offers
- Defer discretionary spending
- Draw committed facilities where appropriate
- Begin financing discussions early

## 8. Board and investor communication plan

### 8.1 Board approval package

The board should approve:

- Target end-state and 36-month transition plan
- Perpetual-rights policy
- Package and pricing architecture
- Maximum total concession
- Minimum gross-margin floor
- Maximum acceptable installed-base churn
- Minimum liquidity and covenant thresholds
- Sales-compensation transition budget
- Pilot gates and stop-loss rules
- Legacy-product exception policy

### 8.2 Core investor message

The company should state clearly:

> The transition changes both the timing and the quality of revenue. Perpetual licenses and upgrades were recognized more immediately, while subscriptions are generally recognized over the service period. Therefore, ARR and billings may grow before GAAP revenue fully reflects the recurring base. This is beneficial only to the extent that customers remain, contracts are collected, margins are healthy, and recurring gross profit is realized. We will therefore report revenue, billings, cash flow, ARR, retention, margin, migration cost, and contracted obligations together.

### 8.3 Quarterly reporting

Report consistently:

- GAAP revenue
- Subscription revenue
- Billings
- Cash collections
- Free cash flow
- Deferred revenue
- ARR
- Gross retention
- Net retention
- Installed-base conversion
- Churn and contraction by cohort
- Average discount and total migration concession
- Subscription gross margin
- Sales productivity
- Legacy revenue percentage
- RPO/backlog, where applicable
- Revenue trough versus plan

### 8.4 Required reconciliations

ARR bridge:

\[
ARR_{\text{ending}}
=
ARR_{\text{beginning}}
+\text{new ARR}
+\text{expansion}
-\text{contraction}
-\text{churn}
+\text{FX}
\]

Gross retention:

\[
GRR=
\frac{\text{opening recurring revenue}-\text{churn}-\text{contraction}}
{\text{opening recurring revenue}}
\]

Net retention:

\[
NRR=
\frac{\text{opening recurring revenue}-\text{churn}-\text{contraction}+\text{expansion}}
{\text{opening recurring revenue}}
\]

The company must explain differences among ARR, bookings, billings, revenue, deferred revenue, RPO, and cash.

RPO should be reported only where applicable and should distinguish cancellable from non-cancellable commitments and fixed from usage-based amounts.

### 8.5 Guidance policy

Recommended approach:

- Continue GAAP revenue, gross-margin, operating-expense, and cash-flow guidance if forecast reliability permits.
- Provide ARR, billings, conversion, and retention as operating outlook metrics.
- Use ranges rather than false precision.
- If guidance is suspended or widened, identify the specific uncertainty and provide measurable operational ranges.
- Do not present ARR as a substitute for revenue or cash flow.
- Preserve metric definitions and recast history if definitions change.

## 10. Key risks, triggers, and contingencies

| Risk | Trigger | Contingency |
|---|---|---|
| Customer believes it is repurchasing owned software | Legal complaints, refusal rates, negative sentiment | Reaffirm perpetual rights, extend dual use, add executive sponsorship, revise communications |
| Migration churn | Churn 3–5 points above plan; approaches 15–20% | Slow rollout, protect high-risk cohorts, improve migration support, retain legacy path |
| Excessive discounting | Total concession above ceiling for two periods | Tighten approvals, reduce credits, reprice packages |
| Cannibalization | Subscription gross profit below legacy comparator | Retain selected term/perpetual sales, revise pricing and packaging |
| Renewal cliff | High downsell or churn at end of price protection | Improve adoption, stage price increases, offer term renewals tied to value |
| Sales gaming | Early cancellations, uncollected ARR, unusual free periods | Quality-adjusted crediting, holdbacks, clawbacks, audits |
| Channel conflict | Partner attrition or increased discounting | Renewal economics, split-credit rules, partner enablement |
| Implementation bottleneck | Backlog or time-to-value materially above plan | Pause sales wave, certify partners, standardize migration |
| Recession | Longer sales cycles, lower expansion, higher DSO | Smaller packages, preserve liquidity, reduce fixed costs, favor retention |
| Slower migration | Conversion below plan for two periods | Improve value proposition, extend timeline, avoid automatic deeper discounts |
| Product or security failure | Increased incidents or support escalations | Pause affected cohort, remediate product, extend protections |
| Liquidity pressure | Cash or covenant headroom below threshold | Slow rollout, cut discretionary spend, draw facilities, seek financing |

4. Segment the installed base by risk, value, upgrade timing, and technical complexity.
5. Build the five-year monthly cohort model and 13-week cash forecast.
6. Obtain preliminary accounting and legal conclusions.
7. Define package architecture, pricing floors, credit limits, and approval matrix.
8. Design the compensation and channel transition plans.
9. Identify pilot customers and control cohorts.
10. Prepare board materials and customer-rights messaging.

2. Configure quoting, CRM, ARR, billing, and commission systems.
3. Train sales, customer success, support, and partners.
4. Confirm migration and implementation capacity.
5. Secure or validate liquidity facilities.
6. Establish the executive dashboard and monthly operating review.
7. Obtain audit committee review of accounting treatment.
8. Launch customer advisory sessions and reference accounts.

### Within 90 days

1. Launch the pilot.
2. Begin weekly operating reviews.
3. Track conversion, retention, concession, cash, implementation, and sentiment.
4. Report pilot results to the board at the first formal stage gate.
5. Expand, redesign, or pause based on predetermined criteria—not anecdotal sales optimism.

## Final decision

Approve the **subscription-first hybrid plan**, subject to Gate 0 readiness and funded downside protection. This plan moves the company decisively toward recurring revenue while preserving perpetual rights, reducing the probability of a destructive installed-base loss, and making the revenue trough measurable and manageable. The transition should accelerate only when customer retention, subscription gross profit, sales productivity, implementation capacity, and liquidity support doing so.
