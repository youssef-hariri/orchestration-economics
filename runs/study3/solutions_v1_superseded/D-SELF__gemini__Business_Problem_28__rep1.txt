To finalize the Board’s directive, the following document consolidates the **One-Year Turnaround Plan** with specific operational milestones and establishes the **Decision Matrix** for the ultimate exit strategy.

---

### **Part 1: The One-Year Turnaround Roadmap**

| Milestone | Deadline | Operational Focus | Key Performance Indicator (KPI) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase 1: Stabilization** | Month 3 | Contract Audit & "Bleed" Stoppage | 100% of "Destructive Drag" contracts exited or renegotiated; 90% retention of key technical staff via bridge-bonus. |
| **Phase 2: Rationalization** | Month 6 | Portfolio Narrowing | Unit achieves Gross Margin >15% on narrowed portfolio; successful implementation of internal "Market Rate" billing. |
| **Phase 3: Operational Pivot** | Month 9 | Process Automation & Efficiency | 15% reduction in OpEx via automation; evidence of stable (non-shrinking) order book. |
| **Phase 4: Validation** | Month 12 | Profitability Review | Achievement of sustained, monthly EBITDA-positive run rate (excluding non-recurring restructuring costs). |

---

### **Part 2: Exit Alternatives**

If the unit fails to meet the criteria defined in Part 3, the Board will initiate one of the following:

1.  **Trade Sale:** Best if the unit has successfully offloaded "Drag" segments, leaving behind a clean, specialized, and profitable core. Assets are sold to a competitor seeking to expand into the unit’s now-refined "Niche Expert" segment.
2.  **Management Buyout (MBO):** A viable option if the management team—having successfully executed the turnaround—believes they can drive further value that the Group is not interested in capturing. This provides a "clean exit" for the Group while maintaining continuity for customers.
3.  **Managed Wind-Down (Liquidation):** The default if the unit remains a financial drain. Fulfill high-value contractual obligations to protect the Group’s reputation, then dissolve the legal entity and transition surviving essential services to internal Shared Service Centers.

---

### **Part 3: The Board’s 'Go/No-Go' Decision Matrix**

The Board must assess the unit against these four quantitative and qualitative triggers. **Any two "No-Go" signals at the Month 6 review necessitate an immediate move to divestment planning.**

| Criteria | 'Go' (Proceed with Turnaround) | 'No-Go' (Trigger Exit/Divest) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Unit Profitability** | Achieves or exceeds 15% Gross Margin. | Remains margin-negative or requires Group subsidy. |
| **The "Subsidy" Test** | Profitable at market rates (internal transfer pricing adjusted). | Cannot sustain operations without Group balance sheet support. |
| **Talent Retention** | Key technical lead turnover <10% post-stabilization. | Continued brain drain of Tier-1 managers (>20% attrition). |
| **Strategic Moat** | Order book stabilization (±2% variance). | Shrinkage >5% QoQ; inability to compete on technical merit. |

---

### **Final Governance Recommendation**

To ensure transparency, the Board should appoint a **"Turnaround Monitor"** (a non-executive director or external advisor) to conduct a **"Hard Stop" assessment at Month 6.**

*   **If the Turnaround is successful:** The unit is re-chartered as a specialized, high-margin business line integrated into the Group’s value chain.
*   **If the Turnaround fails:** The "Exit Committee" is immediately empowered to initiate a 6-month wind-down or sale process, ensuring the unit is closed or offloaded by the end of the fiscal year, thereby removing the recurring drain on the Group’s P&L and management focus.

**Board Resolution:** *“Success is not defined by merely cutting costs, but by the unit's ability to operate as a self-sustaining entity at market-competitive prices. Failure to reach the Month 6 KPIs will be treated as an admission that the unit’s structural utility to the Group has expired.”*
